## Editorial decisions

1. Identify the intended buyer, their immediate desire or problem, the emotional payoff, the product proof, and one specific action. Write that arc in one sentence before choosing shots.
2. Screen each source as moving footage. Record what is visibly shown, project identity, rights, prior use, intended role, and the strongest in/out points. Contact sheets help triage; they do not replace watching the clip.
   For a local folder of originals, `python scripts/screen_sources.py <input-folder> <output-folder>` creates early/middle/late wide and 9:16 contact frames plus a CSV ledger with the review fields. Requires FFmpeg; inspect promising clips in motion afterward.
3. Select for meaning and continuity before image quality. Reject footage that suggests a different product or setting. Do not imply that stock lifestyle scenes, workshop footage, and finished worktops show one installation unless identity is verified.
4. Start with an observable action, question, or visual tension. Develop it through a change in scale or perspective: human use, material detail, and a real finished result. End with a specific invitation and the client's approved brand treatment.
5. Show hands, people, food, and ordinary use when the brief calls for a lived-in home; balance illustrative lifestyle stock with clear, genuine client product footage. Keep stock licensing and origin explicit in the source ledger.
6. Pace for a silent mobile viewer. Use readable captions and safe zones, but let visual action carry the story. Avoid repeated decorative pans, duplicate shots, or text that merely narrates the image.
7. Check the actual 9:16 crop through the full duration of every selected shot. A promising wide frame can become ceiling, wall sockets, or decor in a vertical crop. Check the first, middle, and final frame, including the cut into the next shot. If the product is no longer the subject, find another source or in-point.
8. Compare the rough cut against the strongest relevant client exports: opening, transition logic, shot variety, motion, color, sound, and outro. Revise weak beats before presenting it as ready. A static contact sheet is only a screening aid; watch the complete render at phone size and listen to it before release.
9. When a hook feels generic, test a concrete buyer truth against the first moving shots. In the Bloem October case, an abstract sample-choice quote and a line about kitchens being used lacked a vivid action. Specific verbs tied to gestures gave the workshop proof a reason to matter. Do not treat that specific wording as a reusable template.
10. Build a proof ladder from lifestyle illustration to the client's actual premises, material range, human craft, and finished result. A workshop detail is only useful when the shot visibly proves the nearby copy. Avoid suggesting every style, colour, or cut is available unless the verified footage and client offer support that scope.
11. Compare a restrained cut with an effects pass as separate exports. Add ramps or transitions only where source motion and narrative change support them. Check the first, middle and last moving frames of every effect at phone size; avoid effects over the emotional payoff or CTA simply for variety. If the source frame rate is double the timeline rate, a half-speed settle can use native frames. In Premiere, use Time Remapping speed keyframes and ease the handles; if creating processed media externally, label the ramp as baked and preserve the original and recipe for retuning.
12. For an energetic opening, build a short visual sentence from distinct actions in the client's real footage. Match each cut to its own camera sound or a licensed effect, then let the music and story take over. Check the exact portrait crop and readability of any premises sign. Reject flashes that are attractive stills but do not communicate an action, texture, or place.
13. **Verify orientation, then apply a stabilisation pass to every ordinary camera selection by default.** Inspect recognisable text, shelves, floors, people and objects at the first, middle and last frame of the exact chosen span. Set rotation per source file, not per folder; reject any interval that rolls or reads upside down in the delivered portrait film. A stabiliser cannot repair a wrong orientation. Screen the complete selected span, choose a clean in/out and reject a camera whip or rolling frame rather than trying to rescue it with a filter. Compare the original and corrected portrait crop at full and phone size for warped edges, excess crop, floating hands, lost subject or a mechanically locked-off feel; tune the pass so a deliberate smooth pan or push remains natural. Leave a shot rough only when it is an intentional sporty/action beat that connects to a motivated transition, and write that exception in the shot manifest. Record the source in/out, correction settings, crop and linked original so another editor can revise it. Check the final encoded shot in motion, not just still frames.
14. Keep natural production sound on a separate timeline track with per-action fades and gain. Align machine, tool, hand, and workshop sounds to the visible action; let them sit under music rather than masking it. Measure output loudness and true peak, then listen on a phone or headphones before delivery.
15. A run of short clips is not automatically a rhythmic intro. Choose a song-led or SFX-led opening before setting cut times. For an SFX rush, the riser and selected whooshes lead to an impact and brief hold; the song begins with the subsequent story beat and continues without a forced dip. Align each effect's audible peak to its intended action or cut. Check exported stems, then listen to the complete opening. Do not force every later cut onto a beat; honor gestures and phrase changes.

16. Before choosing a replacement score, audition its beginning, middle and intended ending against the buyer and pictures. Reject a vocal-like out-point or a timbre that suggests the wrong action. Map audible attacks to frame-accurate cuts and title arrivals, then verify them by listening; waveform analysis alone does not approve the music. Every effect must correspond to a visible action. Remove unrelated foley, even when quiet.
17. Before using a deliberate camera move as a transition, verify that the next shot completes its direction. Resolve landscape media in portrait as a considered layout, and let the ending copy and score complete the opening promise.
18. Treat a client flag that a person's performance looks awkward as rejection of that time range and performance, not merely a request to shorten the same pose. Re-screen other ranges for a genuinely different natural action; keep the rejected frames out. Check that the first selected frames already show motion when a shot is meant to open mid-action. Give a client-named principal person a meaningful response beat without inventing their current role. When extending a film across business types, make each scene answer the same central buyer question with a concrete, visible action; label stock as illustrative and do not imply it was filmed locally.
19. For drone-heavy footage, screen the full moving candidate and sample densely around the selected span. Reject intermittent blur, wobbles and turns onto empty ground even when the midpoint still looks strong. Review the rendered cut, not just the source, after grading, graphics and retiming. When client feedback rejects the edit's overall form, replace that form; do not preserve a photo-card or generic title layout merely because the content is genuine. Record rejected ranges and why they failed in the source ledger.
20. Diagnose the cause of a bad frame before applying a frame-rate effect. Frame blending averages adjacent frames and optical flow estimates motion for retiming; neither restores sharp detail when several source frames are already blurred, torn or pointed away from the subject. Compare a short original/processed test at full size. Interpolate only between clean neighboring frames, inspect straight edges and moving objects for warping/ghosting, and retain a clean-cut fallback. For a genuine slow-to-fast ramp, map output frames monotonically to the clean source range and save the curve beside the media. See [Adobe's interpolation guidance](https://helpx.adobe.com/ca/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/change-clip-speed/apply-time-interpolation-methods-to-adjust-clip-speed.html).
21. Treat client-specific people exclusions as a hard preselection gate. Read the current client feedback and exclusion ledger before screening, block every flagged source from the timeline, covers, cutdowns and reusable selects, and run a final human-visible frame check before delivery. Preserve restricted camera masters in the archive unless the owner explicitly requests deletion; record the restriction beside the source so future editors cannot select it accidentally.

## Technical handoff

- Prefer a frame-accurate shot manifest with source paths, in points, speed, crop, text, and role. Keep footage and caption layers separate in an editable Premiere sequence. Render a review MP4 as well.
- For processed stabilization, ramps, and transitions, preserve source in/out, crop, filter settings, and original paths so an editor can recreate or retune the effect in Premiere. A baked clip is editable in the sequence but its effect parameters are not native controls.
- Use the client's proven logo animation as picture. For Nolan's current workflow, mute its baked audio and carry the chosen music and purposeful effects through a deliberate final resolve. Verify the exact exported ending is audibly present, not merely that the timeline clip was extended; Daisy's protected native D4 displayed a music clip through 30 s but rendered a near-silent last five seconds. Verify that a horizontal logo remains legible in a vertical crop; letterbox or fit it when cropping would cut the mark.
- Verify duration, frame rate, dimensions, title safe area, shot changes, music and brand sound, contact details, media links, and export playback. A working XML is not evidence that a saved Premiere project is online.
- Check constant frame rate and per-clip duration after an external speed ramp. A file can contain the requested frame count but still carry a shortened last frame or nonconstant average frame rate; normalize the time base before export and compare both drafts at identical duration.
- Treat rights, prior use, product identity, and client approval as release gates. Mark unknowns clearly; do not silently replace them with plausible assumptions.
- Version rendered media filenames on each Premiere revision. Premiere can lock imported media on Windows; overwriting open source files may fail or leave a stale preview. Import each final revision into a clean project, save it, and verify opener, middle proof, and end card are online.
- For personal OneDrive media beyond connector download limits, a separately authorized read-only rclone remote can stage original files. Verify account/drive identity and hashes. Never package OAuth tokens or use one person's token as the staff installation method. See [handoff workflow](references/onedrive-premiere-workflow.md).
- After each client edit, add verified reusable items to that client's small asset library: current logo and brand evidence, native editable graphic styles, a footage selects ledger with exact moments and rejected adjacent frames, and music/effects with source and licence. Keep camera masters and exports in their normal project/archive folders. Record working versus approved status and test fonts/media on a second workstation before calling the kit staff-ready.

When an edit reveals a generalizable mistake or better choice, update the relevant case study or workflow reference with the evidence and decision. Do not universalize one client's style.

## Required client-feedback retrieval and pre-send audio review

Before selecting a client or starting a new timeline, read the latest `CGPH TO DO`, `VIDEO APPROVALS`, `CG External`, and relevant client WhatsApp messages. Treat `CGPH TO DO` as the live assignment queue, `VIDEO APPROVALS` as the latest internal review authority, and `CG External` as evidence of work already in progress or delivered. Recheck `VIDEO APPROVALS` while an edit is active and immediately before delivery. Record new assignments, ownership, people exclusions, and revision feedback in the private production queue and client kit. Do not start or deliver an edit that duplicates another editor's active lane.

For every new edit/revision, read the latest private client-kit baseline and feedback before the story gate. Use `python scripts/audio_review.py context --client-kit <kit> --output <new-private-folder>` and read `READ-BEFORE-EDIT.md`; carry the relevant decisions into the existing story brief. Preserve preferred work when applying amendments: explicitly list the owner's **keep** elements (for example, an approved music cue, pace or motion language) alongside **change** elements, and do not discard the former because a story or source audit failed. Daisy November's owner-liked music and buoyant edit are a creative baseline even though its old product-range story was withdrawn. The owner may choose any suitable local editor for a protected revision; verify the actual export and an editable handover appropriate to that editor. Record evidence of actual changes, not a claim of model retraining.

Before any owner-review video send, follow [Gemini audio review and feedback gate](references/audio-review-gate.md). Extract the exact final Adobe export, run local audio checks, obtain the authorised Gemini critique and resolve findings. Run `audio_review.py check` immediately before attaching the reviewed file. Changed exports/new feedback invalidate old reviews. Missing perception, quota or upload access leaves this step pending; never quietly skip it or substitute LUFS for listening. Its pass permits owner-review handoff only, not client/publication approval. Use existing Premiere tools; no new plugin/API billing or client-data upload authority is created by this skill.
