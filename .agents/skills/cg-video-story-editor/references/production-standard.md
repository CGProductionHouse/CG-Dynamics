# Nolan production standard — every edit and revision

Owner feedback consolidated 28 October 2026. Read this **before selecting footage**, then record evidence against it in the existing private story/source/release record. Applies on MSI and office PC, in any suitable editor. It does not grant uploads, purchases, sends or installation permissions. A checklist does not certify what nobody watched or heard.

## Authority and scope

1. Check the owning issue/PR, current assignment and latest authorised review messages. Record the shared-library commit and current client-feedback checkpoint. Reconcile office changes before editing common files; never take over another lane's timeline. Refresh the library before a new assignment and after a shared-rule update, not halfway through an active render.
2. Maintain **keep / change / reject** decisions with the exact revision and evidence. Latest explicit owner correction wins; keep earlier notes as superseded history. An approved music cue or successful intro survives an unrelated amendment. Generic rules do not replace client fonts, colours, people requirements or exclusions.
3. Complete the existing [story gate](story-preproduction-gate.md): audience, why this month in this South African location/season, one useful message, intended feeling, visible proof and realistic CTA. Research current claims using appropriate sources. Draft strategy, political trend or calendar event is not approval or a reason by itself. Aim for about 30 meaningful seconds when that is the brief; never pad a short idea.

## Source and freshness gate

4. Search the authorised client library, including older folders. Check actual public source-month **and subsequent** Facebook/Instagram videos through the intended release; watch whole posts, including late montages. Record URLs/dates and exact proposed source ranges. Filenames, shoot dates, a partial export archive, adjacent takes, crops and music changes cannot establish unused picture.
5. Older posted shoots may supply **genuinely different moments for a new story job**. Compare old versus new purpose and recognisable action. Never lift a published beat/story as-is; do not blanket-ban a whole shoot or ask for new capture by default. Mark unknown prior use honestly, never reassure the owner that it is unused without evidence. An unresolved material overlap must be resolved or the select replaced before delivery.
6. Watch complete originals and the chosen span in motion. Log useful setup/onset/payoff/exit and rejected adjacent ranges. Exclude fumbles, awkward poses, cue resets, unwanted people and a camera drifting away; shortening the same rejected performance is not a replacement. Keep actual contact and consequence in sport/tool/action shots after retiming. Never infer a landed blow or knockout from reaction or camera shake.
7. Give each shot a job. Real client people/place/process must establish client identity; illustrative stock cannot become their premises, customer, staff, crop activity or result. Balance stock against real client proof. Do not present historical shelf items, prices or offers as current stock. Match season, wardrobe, local activity and wording; a barely moving soil/detail shot needs an important overlay/voice job or removal.
8. Classify each selected audio range as **dialogue / natural action or ambience / muted B-roll**. Visible direct-to-camera speech requires intelligible, source-aligned words or another shot. Natural laughter, working or listening may work as B-roll; crew direction, prompts and false starts do not. Mute irrelevant camera speech deliberately; do not leave it quietly under music. Inspect both boundaries and the final mix.

## Picture and typography gate

9. Verify per-file orientation, then stabilise ordinary camera selects by default. Tune against the original so intentional pans remain natural. Record a rough-shot exception only for deliberate sporty/action energy or a motivated transition. Check the entire delivered crop for warp, floating edges, lost hands/product and upside-down frames. See the repository stabilisation card for source-specific settings, not a universal preset.
10. **Correct, match, then style colour on every film.** Read [exposure and colour matching](exposure-colour-matching.md). Normalize per-shot exposure/white balance, match adjacent shots, then apply a restrained coherent look appropriate to the brand and scene. Check skin, products, whites and brand colours. A blanket LUT or lowering an overexposed image does not recover clipped detail. Preserve originals and correction settings. Titles/logo graphics must retain approved colours.
11. Lock the approved brand font/weight/substitute, palette, graphic language and logo source from evidence. No invented font, palette, black outline or look change for convenience. Preserve approved colour accents and shapes where useful. **Default on-screen marketing captions have no terminal full stops**; retain necessary punctuation in verbatim quotations, contact details or other exact text and record any client-specific override. This does not prohibit sentence punctuation in the proposed social-post caption.
12. The hook must show a meaningful action, promise or visual question immediately. Develop visual energy through the **whole** film: varied shot scales, purposeful motion, legible staggered/revealed type, depth or panels when the source supports them. Do not spend all craft on the intro and leave a static body. Hold one readable message across fast shots where needed; do not replace paragraphs every beat. A stronger effect must survive comparison with a clean cut; no mandatory effect quota or same preset on every client.
13. Use coherent professionally designed/licensed assets from approved libraries (including Adobe/Canva when accessible) rather than fake branded icons. Keep vectors/licences, separate labels and proper reading holds. Test orbit/carousel hierarchy and safe areas in motion, not just the settled frame. Preserve clip identity and action; a mock device is not proof of a filmed physical-screen transition.
14. Ground cutout edges at a frame boundary or believable object. Check all four layer boundaries, matte edges, occlusion, layer order, transparency and perspective throughout the moving shot **and export**, including the last pose. A left/bottom-anchored layer can still crop an arm at an internal right edge; expand permitted original coverage or use a genuine occluder/reject, never fabricate a limb. A floating cropped person, duplicate head or text incorrectly in front fails. If no clean matte/plate exists, use another treatment; do not call a synthetic proof natural masking mastery.

## Rhythm and sound gate

15. Choose story and approved music together before locking timing. Screen exact cue IDs against posted/rejected use. Do not reuse a rejected song or the recent prior film's cue merely from another in-point. For amendments preserve music the owner explicitly likes. Licence and catalogue mood tags do not prove creative fit; assess the actual selected phrase and ending.
16. **Music-led cuts land on audible beats or deliberately skipped beats across the full film.** Make a frame-based beat sheet; a detector is a candidate marker, not listening evidence. Trim/reposition the selected action or thoughtfully edit the score/SFX to get the cut without a weak tail. In a deliberately SFX-led opening, dialogue beat or action-led passage, document the alternative audible cue and reason. This is not permission for unexplained drift behind the pulse. Recheck after every timing/music revision.
17. Add intentional sound layers tied to actual actions, reveals and transitions. Keep voice, clean action/room sound, music and licensed effects on separable tracks/stems; preserve source in/out, cue onset/peak/tail, gain, fades and timing. No unrelated foley, whoosh on every cut, harsh flickering clicks or score that overwhelms voice. Check sync again after retiming or restoring visible speech. Use density and pressure/release where the story earns them.
18. Use the **official approved logo animation**, with its meaningful construction and full mark intact. Its embedded sound is muted in Nolan's workflow; continue music and purposeful separate effects over the picture to a deliberate ending. Check the exact export's final five seconds audibly and technically for silence, truncation or an inappropriate vocal/sting. A timeline clip extending to the end is not proof of rendered sound.
19. Review the entire exact export at normal speed with actual sound, then phone-size readability/framing; use legitimate independent review where available. Review source B-roll for crew chatter, isolated effects, full mix, voice clarity, stereo/mono behaviour, transitions and outro. Meter peaks/loudness, fades and silence separately. Never invent a hearing verdict from playback completion, waveforms, ASR, a failed model or a contact sheet. Record the real scope/listener/device; the owner is not the routine practice-audition service. The [audio gate](audio-review-gate.md) never authorises private/client uploads.

## Handoff and learning gate

20. Follow the [cover audit](reel-cover-and-instagram-audit.md): exact approved Canva artwork when selected, otherwise a relevant strong photo-led composition based on the client's actual cover collection/grid. Keep full logo/title/subject readable at grid size in full portrait, square, 3:4 and 4:5 crops. No padded landscape inset or fake client proof. Correct exposure on cover photographs too. Local crop tests are not an Instagram posting-preview claim.
21. Follow the [release workflow](release-and-learning-loop.md): verified canonical client short code/sequence, established descriptive stem, unique protected revision, reviewed export hash and correct attachment name; no invented identity or overwritten final. Proposed caption must fit the new purpose, current offer, contacts and evidence.
22. Internal default: exact **CG Nolan Approvals** recipient, video document with **only the proposed post caption in the same message**, then separate caption-free HD cover image. Internal notes/questions go in a separate message signed from Nolan. A current explicit instruction for directly playable HD video overrides the document default for that assignment; verify the sent media is actually playable/HD, not just renamed. Record exact destination, message types, Sent/Delivered and any platform limitation. Do not resend unchanged media. Internal send, reaction, owner approval and client/public approval are distinct states.
23. Keep protected source/recipe and separable editable assets. A flat MP4 or script is not a native editable staff project. Claim native or cross-device handover only after witnessed open, media relink, editable text/audio, save/reopen and native export on the target setup. Remain tool-agnostic; CapCut is an optional handover format, not mandatory for Nolan films. No rebuilding the rendering engine.
24. Acknowledge substantive amendments in the authorised group when assigned and control is unconflicted. Record exact feedback, preserve keep elements, make a protected revision and verify it end to end. After delivery reconcile the assigned queue/ownership and continue authorised next work or skill development. Do not use recurring reminders, repetitive tiny notes or a failed method as a substitute for sustained work; do not recreate cancelled monitors. A desktop conflict ends UI interaction, not useful local work.

## Evidence record before internal handoff

Keep this in the existing private release record, not a second task database. For each row use `pass`, `pending`, `fail` or justified `not_applicable`, with **what was actually inspected, exact revision/hash, evidence path and limitation**. A failed or pending material quality/rights/source gate means continue work or report the genuine blocker; do not label the candidate ready. Native/cross-device checks may remain unclaimed when outside the requested deliverable, but that limitation must be explicit.

| Required row | Minimum useful evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Authority / feedback | Owning issue, library commit, current feedback checkpoint, keep/change/reject |
| Research / story | Current guide, audience, local month/season, goal, three hooks, proof and CTA |
| Prior use / rights / claims | Public URL/date/scope, source ranges, old/new story jobs, licence/permissions, current offer |
| Selection / action | Useful in/out, contact/payoff, rejected neighbours, people exclusions, real client versus stock |
| Orientation / stabilisation | Exact span/crop/settings, moving original/corrected review, justified rough exceptions |
| Exposure / colour | Per-shot correction, neighbour comparison, final look and cover checks; see colour reference |
| Brand / typography / effects | Verified kit, no unwanted outlines/full stops, readable whole-film motion/safe areas, grounded masks |
| Rhythm | Actual cue and beat/cut frames throughout; documented alternative audible cue where applicable |
| Source sound / music / SFX | Classification, muted crew, cue licences/history, sync, stems, logo source muted |
| Exact-export picture / sound / phone | What was watched/heard, who/device/scope, tail, mono, measured checks and honest gaps |
| Cover / caption / filename | Current cover audit/crops, exact proposed caption, naming authority and reviewed hash |
| Editability / relink | Native/cross-device evidence if claimed; baked/source-only limits otherwise |
| Delivery / learning | Exact recipient/type/status or unsent, supersession, approval state, next owned work, reusable lesson |

Skill sync is access to instructions, not proof they were followed. The next agent must supply project evidence for these rows; copying the checklist or stating “all passed” is insufficient.
