# Story preproduction gate

**Established-client rule:** first load the saved exact-client library and current keep/change/reject decisions using `scripts/load_client_library.py`. Reuse the approved brand assets/reference films already located there. Do not repeat a general brand-guideline/Canva hunt for an ordinary new video. The work below concerns the new story, new instructions, actual selected-source history and current mutable facts. Refresh a brand asset only for a specifically changed/missing version or an explicit new direction; an unrelated amendment does not reset an established creative standard.

Complete this gate for **every** new client video. A client name, monthly slot, archive folder, and attractive footage are inputs; they are not yet a story. Do not start assembling a timeline until the one-page brief below has a defensible answer for each question. If a key fact is missing, record the gap and gather the source or revise the concept.

Read the [production standard](production-standard.md) for the cross-client owner rules and keep/change/reject checkpoint. Older posted shoots may supply genuinely different moments for a new story job; the prohibition is lifting a previously published beat/story as-is, not a blanket exclusion of a whole shoot. Treat material uncertainty honestly and resolve it before declaring a select fresh.

## Evidence before concept

1. Resolve the exact client and month. Read the current client guide, monthly strategy, package, content guideline, recent client corrections, current logo/brand kit, and latest published covers and poster designs (including Canva when that is where the client keeps them). Keep draft strategies distinct from approved instructions.
2. List every post and video available this year for that exact client. Mark **published**, export-only, planned, or unknown. **For each month whose raw footage you propose to reuse, check the actual Facebook/Instagram posts from that month through the planned release date before staging the edit.** A June shoot can appear in a July or later post. Watch the relevant published videos in full, including opening product flashes and late montages. Log public URL/date, opener, person addressed, promise, story turn, proof, CTA, visual and sound choices, and footage already used. A source folder's month is a reason to begin the public-post check, not a freshness signal. Do not treat a file in an export folder as published without publication evidence; equally, a missing file in an incomplete export archive does not prove it was never posted.
3. Retrieve comparable post results where available: reach, plays, 3-second views or retention, average watch, completion, saves, comments, shares, clicks, and qualified enquiries. Record the platform, observation period, and post ID. If only rankings or reactions are visible, say so. Do not infer that a creative choice caused a metric or invent a benchmark. Distinguish organic from paid and comparable from unrelated posts.
4. Check audience language and service scope against client evidence and the actual adviser/product. Use one primary audience per film. Note what action is realistic after viewing.
5. Sample current videos from that client's industry and a few adjacent high-quality editors. Record the date, source, opening mechanism, proof, pacing, sound structure and outcome evidence if available. Separate an enduring craft principle from a short-lived format; never infer performance from views alone or copy a competitor's claims, footage or soundtrack.

## One-page brief required before editing

CG's current requirement is roughly **30 seconds**: the connected production contract checks27–33seconds before rendering and against the actual export. Plan enough distinct, meaningful coverage; a self-authored compact-cut reason does not waive the owner's requirement. A new owner-authorised format needs a deliberate contract revision. Duration must develop the benefit, place or payoff rather than extend static titles or repeat sources. For revision feedback that the purpose is unclear, rewrite the viewer promise and CTA before making another effects pass. Use the [production execution path](production-execution.md), not descriptive notes alone.

Write these in plain language, with source links or local paths:

- **Why this post, why now?** State the actual month/business reason. A calendar date is not itself a reason; avoid fabricated urgency.
- **Season and place check.** If weather, clothing, crop growth, tourism, daylight or an event season is visible, verify what is normal for the client's location and publishing month using a relevant primary source. Separate typical calendar windows from a current-year forecast. Do not call mature harvest footage a November planting scene, show winter clothing in a summer message, or infer that every region shares one planting date. Record a source-backed reason for each seasonal visual and wording choice.
- **Who exactly?** One viewer, current situation, what they already believe, what they worry about, and what would feel useful rather than promotional.
- **One job and one measurable action.** Examples: prompt a qualified enquiry with a specific question, help viewers recognise a product distinction, or build trust in a real team. Do not blur awareness, enquiries and sales into one goal.
- **Single sentence message.** What should the viewer understand or do after the video? Remove any phrase that could belong to ten other clients.
- **Three hook candidates.** For each, storyboard the first frame, sound, exact first three seconds, and the reason the target viewer would stop. Reject hooks that only create noise, ask an abstract question, or postpone the subject. For sports, use [the observed sports-opening study](sports-opening-construction.md): first image → physical anticipation/change → graphic/sound choreography → payoff/exit. Three slogans with hypothetical effects are not three developed openings. Existing client style and actual source coverage must support the proposed scene.
- **Arc.** Define opening tension, development, proof, emotional or practical payoff, and CTA. Each beat must change the viewer's understanding. A list of examples is not an arc.
- **Comparison and hypothesis.** Name the closest relevant prior client video/post and its observed result. Specify what this video changes and which metric would show improvement. When comparable metrics are unavailable, mark the performance hypothesis unproven.
- **Freshness decision.** Name one current industry technique worth testing and why it suits this buyer and brand, plus one popular treatment rejected because it weakens clarity, credibility or product visibility. Record a clean-cut fallback so trend research does not become an effects requirement.
- **Source plan.** Assign every proposed shot a distinct visual role and `visual_cluster` (same action/person/setting/viewpoint). Log original file, in/out, exact source-month published-post URL/date, prior *story purpose*, genuinely different new purpose, rights, claims supported, original audio classification, and 9:16 suitability. Mark incomplete social-history checks **unverified** and hold that source. A different filename or crop does not make repeated coverage or an already-posted message new. For retail, old footage of a specific item cannot prove current shelf availability. Distinct screened moments from one older source can serve genuinely different new jobs; do not blanket-ban the shoot. The validator blocks overlapping repeated moments and the same cluster with the same story job. A return shot still needs actual narrative justification and moving review.
- **Scene intent sheet.** For each shot, write `what physically changes on screen | what the viewer should infer | why that matters now | title/graphic job | natural or licensed sound job | next action`. If a shot has little motion, hold it only when an important readable graphic or voice point genuinely needs that stable background; otherwise shorten or replace it. The same rule applies to text and sound: every element must support the intended inference or lead to the CTA. Compare the actual moving cut against the sheet before owner delivery.
- **Feasibility gaps.** Say which specific live footage, adviser participation, stock license, copy approval, or contact verification is still needed. Do not fill a narrative gap with vaguely related archive shots.

## Decision gate

Before building, read the brief as the target viewer and answer: “Why would I stop in the first three seconds? What do I learn? Why this client? What should I do next?” If any answer needs the editor to explain it, rewrite the opening or concept. Screen all selected moving footage and compare nearby shots at phone size for perceived repetition. A timeline must not be called a review draft if it fails this gate.

**Failure proof (26 October 2026):** TBS June-folder footage was called fresh after comparing only other local exports, then Amonique linked a published reel and the owner reported the same story/footage was used. The exact publication month was not independently verified. Daisy September product footage was called fresh after a partial September reel check, but the public reel showed a recognisable product/display from the proposed opening; a separate older store-discovery reel already had the same broad visit premise. Both packages were withdrawn. The published-post history through release and the *meaning* of its footage must be recorded before starting a replacement. Passing a file hash or pHash screen against the wrong months cannot waive this gate.

For a machine-checked handoff, save the brief as JSON with the field names in `scripts/preflight_story.py` and [production execution](production-execution.md), then run `python scripts/preflight_story.py story-plan.json` before rendering. Production requires the frame plan, scene jobs, music/anchors and bound visual applications as well as audience/goals/distinct hooks. The release entry point consumes this same plan and its actual render receipt. Neither command determines whether two different files show the same moment or whether the hook works emotionally; moving/story review remains mandatory.

After publication, compare observed retention and action with the hypothesis. Record what the evidence supports and what remains unknown. Update the case study and source ledger, not a universal style rule, when the lesson is client-specific.

## Cheap prior-use check before staging the edit

Emoya October showed why sampling only a presenter section fails: the September Reel hid most shoot B-roll in a late montage. When a public client Reel can be downloaded through an ordinary public media extractor, retain a local reference copy and inspect its full timeline before selecting raw files. Do not export browser cookies or bypass authentication. A 2 fps overview can locate montage sections; compare those sections more densely and record actual source matches. Perceptual hashes help flag matches through grading/compression, but camera similarity and low-detail backgrounds can cause false positives; validate flagged moments visually. Preserve a rejected-source list and the exact post ID in the client kit. This makes the next edit faster than discovering overlap after rendering.

`python scripts/screen_prior_use.py POST.mp4 OUTPUT.json RAW1.mp4 RAW2.mp4 --rotate-ccw` provides a local fingerprint screen (NumPy, SciPy and FFmpeg). Omit rotation for normal landscape/rotation-correct sources. It samples the reference at 25 fps and originals at 6 fps by default; adjust to the actual reference cadence and inspect short flashes more densely. The report is a shortlist for visual review, not proof that an unflagged clip has never been published. The helper was tested on a known posted Emoya macro (distance 2 bits) and an unused low-angle swing detail (16 bits).
