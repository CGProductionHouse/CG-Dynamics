# Reel cover and client Instagram audit

Every final social video ships with a separate portrait cover image alongside the MP4. **First check whether the client already has a suitable approved cover in Canva. If the owner selects one, export that exact page and use the exported file. Do not redesign it or substitute an agent-made variation.** A new cover is needed only when no suitable approved one exists or the owner requests a new design. A cover is a thumbnail, not a condensed poster. Select the image for the client's product or service and the film's actual promise. A visually pleasant stock lifestyle moment can still be the wrong cover when it hides the product.

## Before design

1. Confirm the exact client Instagram account from client records or the user. Do not substitute a national or similarly named account for a branch account.
2. Inspect the **cover-photo collection** in Canva and the recent visible Reels grid. Poster designs are not the cover reference. Look for an already approved client cover that fits the current film. Record the Canva design/page or exported file, profile URL, check date and inspected range. State the actual scope inspected; do not claim an exhaustive history scan from a partial grid.
3. If the owner chooses an existing Canva cover, treat its exact export as the artwork authority. Locate the export, verify the file visually and by dimensions, preserve an unchanged source copy, and put that file under the final naming convention. Check the full portrait and the Instagram posting preview; do not recreate its layout from screenshots. Skip the new-design steps below.
4. For a new design, write a one-sentence cover brief: **what must a stranger identify at grid size, and why does that image belong to this client?** List the product/service, visual priority, approved source, and rejected near-matches. If the film sells a worktop, the stonework must be the dominant subject; a generic person at a kitchen is insufficient. Prefer the client's own proof when available. Do not use illustrative stock as a client project image. Check that likeness and source permissions cover a still used as artwork.
5. For a new design, choose a different visual idea from recent covers, not merely a different frame of the same room, pose, product angle or background. Identify the closest previous cover by URL, Canva page or saved image and explain the visible difference. If the archive cannot be fully checked, say what was checked.
6. Verify the current logo, palette and fonts from the client's brand library and recent approved work. A platform export or stale archive is not automatically current.

## Build and crop check

1. For a new cover, compose at 1080 × 1920 or an exact 2× export such as 2160 × 3840. Treat the centered 4:5 region as the designed post area. Keep every word, logo and story-critical feature inside it. Let the surrounding top/bottom image extend the photo to full Reel height. The Instagram grid and feed may preview different crops, and the app may change its treatment. Preserve an owner-exported approved Canva cover at its original resolution.
2. Preview the full portrait image plus central 3:4, 4:5 and square crops. The product/service subject and full logo must remain recognizable at *actual small grid size*, not just be technically inside the crop. No word, logo or story-critical object may be clipped. Reposition photo and text, then repeat the checks. Compare the square/3:4 thumbnail beside three recent client covers to catch a weak or repetitive visual before delivery.
3. A reproducible local photo-led cover can be rendered with `python scripts/make_reel_cover.py path/to/client-cover-config.json`. It places the exact photo, a short optional headline and a small supplied logo; it rejects graphics outside the 4:5 post area. Run the script with `--strict` for a final cover: the config must document client relevance, original/stock source identity, a specific prior-cover comparison, and visual approval at grid size. These assertions force an editorial review; the renderer cannot judge image meaning. Adapt the placement to the actual image and client. Keep the source frame, configuration, font/license and logo path with the project.
4. Name the image with the final video's stem plus `_COVER.png` or `_COVER.jpg`. Put it beside the final MP4 in the correct client final export folder. Add the image to the client's reusable kit only if it is useful as a template. Avoid automatically repeating a published cover; an explicit owner choice to use an existing design takes precedence.
5. Recheck the actual Instagram posting preview before publication. A local crop simulation cannot guarantee the current app layout.

Record the audit in the project before delivery. For repeat videos, compare against all covers added since the last audit. Do not automatically repeat a person, stock scene, headline arrangement or background color merely because it performed well once. Reject any cover when a phone-size grid preview fails either test: **the viewer can identify the client's offering** and **the composition is visibly distinct from the nearest recent cover**.
