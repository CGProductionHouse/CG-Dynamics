## Technical details retained from the attempt

- For advisory work, begin with a credible trigger in the viewer's life, then name the question the adviser can help them think through. Avoid vague comfort claims, guarantees, or unsupported coverage promises.
- Show the client's real people doing the relevant work. Check whether the footage depicts staff or customers; do not silently change that identity in the script.
- Watch candidates in motion and check the portrait crop. These camera files were stored sideways; correcting the orientation and testing the full shot avoided a misleading still-frame selection.
- Check typography against every moving background at phone size. The first gold key-shot title was too pale; the higher-contrast revision was easier to read.
- Sound level matters even for a no-voice-over reel. A quiet first mix was lifted and measured at about -20.2 LUFS integrated, with a true peak below -4 dBTP. Listen again on a phone before release.
- Save both a review export and an online Premiere project. Keep picture, title images, score, and effects on separate tracks. In this draft, title artwork and picture treatments are rendered media, so their fine parameters live in the builder and manifest; an editor can replace or re-time them in Premiere.
- Treat historical staff likeness rights, prior use, current branch contact details, and client approval as release gates. Archive availability alone does not resolve them.
