# CG video rhythm and sound playbook

Use this with a verified brief, approved source media and a human editor. It is a method for making deliberate choices quickly, not a preset for every client.

## Before cutting

1. Watch two or three relevant CG final exports **with sound**. Log the opening shot lengths, music or sound cues, the first clear product moment, changes between wide and close shots, and how the final call to action enters. Separate observed craft from assumptions about public posting or approval.
2. Screen original footage in motion and in the intended aspect ratio. For each select, record source, time range, visible action, camera direction, crop, identity, rights, prior use, narrative purpose and whether the sound is usable. A contact sheet finds candidates; it cannot certify a moving crop or continuity.
3. Write the viewer's story in one sentence: *whose moment → what changes → what proves the client can deliver → what the viewer should do*. Pick music for that arc, with usable accents and a section that leaves room for the payoff. Confirm its licence for the intended channels.
4. Audition several licensed music candidates against the first three seconds, the turning point and the outro. Log what was heard at all three positions, including any vocal or vocal-like sound, and why each rejected track failed. An instrumental catalogue label is a shortlist signal, not proof of a clean final edit. Record the exact source in-point and the planned fade end; a song can change character near its end.

## Choose the opening's sound architecture

Decide before cutting whether the opening is led by a song's attacks or by an independent sound design cue. In a song-led opening, cut selected actions to audible accents and preserve the music phrase. In an SFX-led rush, keep the song silent during the rush, build a riser across the short shots, place a few brief whooshes at chosen cuts, resolve with one impact and its decay, then start the song with the story. Do not start a song at frame zero and duck it to manufacture a pause. The two structures need different timelines and audio maps.

## Build a rhythmic opening

1. In Premiere, listen to the chosen track and mark **audible** attacks or phrase changes on the timeline (M). Mark a strong beat, then subdivisions only when the footage earns faster cuts. Adobe documents timeline and source [markers](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/organize-media/apply-labeling/add-a-marker-to-a-clip.html) and demonstrates trimming visuals to a music waveform in its [music-led editing lesson](https://www.adobe.com/learn/premiere-pro/web/add-music-to-travel-videos).
2. Choose a short sequence of distinct, readable actions: tool, human hand, material, result, place. Put the action or frame change on the marker. Do not fill a slot with a weak shot merely to satisfy a beat grid. A single strong shot can hold for two beats.
3. Reserve a larger sonic accent for the turn into the story. Fast shots should create a visual sentence, then give the buyer a moment to understand the benefit. Check for harsh flicker, illegible text, repeated shots and abrupt exposure changes at phone size.
   Deliberately test a **release** after the rush: hold one meaningful human or product action without new copy and let the final effect decay. For an SFX-led rush, start the song after that hold. The exact length follows the gesture and sound design, not a fixed preset. A pause works only if the image rewards attention. See the [cinematic rush and release masterclass example](cinematic-rush-and-release-example.md) for an exercise based on a CG editor's supplied reference.
4. Put SFX on their **own tracks**. A flash may use a brief transient and a tiny decay; a whoosh can start ahead of the next picture and resolve after it begins. Match the type and size of the sound to the visible event. Do not fill every cut with the same whoosh or drown the source action.
   Align an effect's *audible peak*, not merely its file start, to the cut or action. Inspect the waveform and trim or time-shift its lead-in. Keep a clean decay before the song begins; check the actual rendered stem for accidental early placement.
5. Beat markers are edit candidates. Review the actual moving cut and shift it by a few frames when the hand, tool or camera movement lands better. An algorithm or optional BeatEdit marker pass can locate candidate beats; the editor chooses which ones advance the story.

## Carry the viewer through the body

- Replace claims that merely name a condition (for example, a room being "used") with observable verbs. Tie each word to its own shot: a hand explores material, someone cooks, people gather, or a moment lingers. Check that the verb adds meaning rather than redundantly labeling the picture. Keep the copy short enough to register at the actual shot length.
- For a hero word, choose one visual anchor and animate its arrival around the gesture. A small rise or masked reveal can be more legible than a whole sentence flying in. Keep title art separate from picture, test contrast against the moving crop, and inspect early/middle/late frames; a still at the fully revealed word hides poor entry timing. If a person should appear in front of a title, verify the matte across the whole shot before using a behind-the-person reveal.
- If a horizontal premises sign must read in full, inspect the whole source and the final portrait crop. A tight 9:16 extraction may show only the first word and draw attention to irrelevant features. A short, intentionally framed wide insert or a verified pan can preserve the complete name; check legibility at phone size and keep the building's less flattering details out of focus.
- If the wide insert leaves empty portrait space, make the aspect-ratio decision visible: combine distinct, related wide shots into a full-height set of panels, with each panel advancing the story. Check the complete sign at the first, middle and last frame, and avoid repeating the same unattractive part of the building across panels.
- Screen the final frames of a camera move for its intended transition. A downward whip earns its place only when the next shot picks up compatible movement. Otherwise cut before the move, or slow an earlier steady portion using actual source frames. Check that the slower clip never reaches the unwanted motion and that the cut still lands on the music or action.
- Alternate visual scale with purpose: a person using the space, a product surface or craft close-up, then room or result. Show both the detail and its consequence. Keep the client product visible in the portrait crop.
- When a client flags an interaction as staged or awkward, remove the shot and look for genuine work in progress. Review neighboring shots too: a replacement should change the visual cluster, not repeat the same people, pose or table from a new file. To broaden a business story, choose distinct working actions from relevant sectors and use each to answer the opening question with a specific, hypothetical change. Avoid an industry-name montage that loses the single viewer goal. Record stock as illustrative, not as a client or local business.
- Use the movement already in the sources. A pan or hand movement can end into a shot with compatible screen direction, shape or action. Prefer a cut on motion or a [match cut](https://www.adobe.com/uk/creativecloud/video/discover/match-cut.html) where it connects ideas; use a generated transition when a simple cut cannot carry the change.
- Mark key phrase or accent changes across the whole film and revisit cuts near them. Some cuts should ride the beat; others should wait for a gesture or meaning. Constantly fast cutting flattens the emotional payoff.
- Make a frame-based beat sheet before the final cut: cue source in-point, measured or tapped pulse, the actual audible attacks near each planned picture cut, title arrivals and any intentional off-beat gesture. If a new track replaces the old one, rebuild those positions and the title reveals. A BPM estimate or transient detector only proposes markers; check them against the music by ear. Do not claim a beat-matched edit solely because clip durations are multiples of a calculated beat.
- Lay the incoming action or ambience a few frames **before** its picture when anticipation helps; let the outgoing sound continue beyond its picture when it makes the next shot feel connected. Adobe calls these [J and L cuts](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/trim-clips/perform-j-cuts-and-l-cuts.html). Fade the source sounds against each other or room tone; an [audio crossfade](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-audio-effects/apply-audio-transitions/audio-crossfade-transitions.html) helps prevent a seam, but listen for doubled or phasey sound.
- Keep music, dialogue, effects, genuine camera sound, ambience and brand sound identifiable on separate tracks. Do an intentional final listen on speakers and headphones, and check whether the hook still works muted.
- Use an action-to-sound test for every effect: identify the visible action it supports, its audible start and peak, and whether it still belongs after a picture change. Remove a cue if it sounds like a different action (for example, writing under a shop scene), even if its level is low. A single continuous score with one motivated opening cue can be more immersive than a layer of unrelated stock foley. Listen to the isolated effects stem, then the full mix.
- For an SFX-led opening, verify the exported song stem is silent until the planned entry and continuous afterward. Verify a riser grows toward the reveal, the impact lands on the turn, and tails fade naturally. Do not sign off from RMS measurements alone; listen to the export.
- If a compressed riser sounds chopped or granular, test a cue whose native tail fits the opening, or use an unsped portion of a longer cue. Reserve whooshes for selected changes rather than stacking one on every flash. Start the main music on the first meaningful title or picture gesture, with enough attack to feel intentional; choose the song for the buyer and material, not merely because its metadata says "cinematic."
- A closing line should answer the opening verbs or promise in new words, then give the viewer a clear next action. Test the last picture and logo card as one phrase. Let the score carry a gentle final fade when an extra brand sound calls attention to itself; verify that the final seconds do not feel abruptly empty.
- Check the *first and last audible time* of every exported stem against the picture timeline. Some filter and container timestamp combinations can shift a delayed camera-sound or brand-sound stem back to frame zero even when its delay expression looks correct. A valid file and correct total duration do not prove synchronization; re-render from sample-positioned audio when timestamps misbehave.

## Reusable sound asset catalog

Use the [CG sound design catalog](sfx-library.md) to shortlist risers, whooshes and impacts. It records source links and use cases, not a bulk archive of stock files. License and download an Envato item for each specific client project when chosen, keep the project license with the edit, and do not redistribute the raw asset as a staff sample pack. The current Envato Workspace is a discovery surface. Check the actual account's team seats and sharing before claiming that all staff can open it. Adobe Creative Cloud Libraries support shared media assets in Premiere, but confirm storage quota and rights before moving any licensed files there.

## Fast, honest tool use

| Tool | Appropriate job | Editor remains responsible for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Premiere Search / Media Intelligence | Find plausible visual and transcript ranges in authorised originals | Correct shot, rights, identity, crop and story role |
| Premiere markers, Time Remapping and Essential Sound | Set rhythm, reshape real motion and organise audio | Musical feel, ramp quality and mix |
| BeatEdit 2, if licensed on that device | Candidate beat markers and rough arrangement of *preselected* shots | Every selected cut, motion continuity and narrative |
| Transcript / Paper Edit for speech | Editable dialogue assembly where language support is reliable | Quote accuracy, pauses, subtitles and performance |
| FFmpeg contact sheets or review exports | Rapid screening and objective technical checks | Full moving and listening review |

The deployment and licensing boundary for optional tools belongs to [CGPH AI video-editing implementation issue #856](https://github.com/CGProductionHouse/CG-Dynamics/issues/856). Do not claim an optional plugin is installed or licensed until the workstation confirms it.

## Review card for each revision

| Check | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Hook | First 2 seconds: exact frames and sound that earn attention; first buyer-relevant promise |
| Source truth | Each genuine, stock or archival shot's identity, rights and intended claim; duplicate source and visual-cluster check |
| Rhythm | Music marker times, shots aligned, shots deliberately held, reason for exceptions |
| Motion | Entry and exit action, screen direction, any speed or stabilization artifact |
| Sound | SFX source/licence, separate stems, prelap and tail lengths, fade, music balance |
| Phone review | Full portrait crop, text safe area, rapid flash comfort, contact legibility |
| Technical | Frames, frame rate, resolution, decode, black gaps, true peak, online Premiere media |
| Learning | One observed weakness → edit change → review evidence → reusable rule or client-specific note |

Treat platform advice as structure, not a guarantee of performance. [TikTok's Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/creative-codes) recommends hook/body/close, dynamic editing and purposeful sound. [Meta's Reels guidance](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads) stresses vertical framing, quality audio and safe-zone messages. Neither can replace an edit review against the specific buyer, footage and brand.
