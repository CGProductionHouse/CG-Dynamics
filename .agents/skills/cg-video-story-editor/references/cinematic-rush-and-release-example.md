# Masterclass example: cinematic rush and release

Reference: [Yu-Yen's short editing breakdown on TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@yuyenwng/video/7631341066553167126), supplied by the CG editor. The video displays moving footage above an annotated edit timeline. Its visible notes call out short cuts at the opening and end, keyframe zooms across clips, and multiple audio events, including risers, whooshes and source-like mechanical/water sounds. The editor's feedback identifies the key emotional feature: after the quick opening, a pause lets the viewer take in the image. Treat these as observations and feedback, not as a licensed asset pack or measured recipe for every edit.

## What to learn

1. **Write a visual sentence.** Short shots should progress through distinct actions or views. If two adjacent flashes say the same thing, remove one.
2. **Build sound in layers.** Use a rising layer to create anticipation, brief accents at selected picture changes, and an impact or release at the turn. Use real source sound where it is truthful. Put each layer on its own track so an editor can rebalance it.
3. **Resolve the rush.** After the peak, hold one meaningful gesture, face, product detail or result. Let the effect decay and briefly thin the music. Introduce the first line of copy only after the viewer can read that image.
4. **Return with purpose.** Bring music and pacing back under the next story idea. Do not run the entire film at opening speed.
5. **Animate from the footage.** A small keyframe zoom can sharpen an existing movement; cuts should follow hands, tools or camera direction. Reject a zoom that invents motion or hides the subject in a vertical crop.

## Practice exercise

Make two openings from the same five to seven verified shots and the same licensed music. In A, cut straight into the story. In B, add a short held image and a controlled sound release after the rush. Watch both at phone size, once muted and once on headphones. Log which version makes the first product or human moment clearer, where attention dips, and whether the sound transition feels earned. Record the selected version, source times, beat markers, hold duration and audio stem levels in the edit manifest. The hold duration comes from the chosen gesture and phrase, not this example.

## Rights and transfer

Study the reference's structure and timing. Do not extract its soundtrack, effects or footage into a client deliverable without a confirmed commercial licence. Recreate an original design from cleared library sounds, authorised production audio or new recordings. A user liking the reference is creative direction, not a transfer of its media rights.
