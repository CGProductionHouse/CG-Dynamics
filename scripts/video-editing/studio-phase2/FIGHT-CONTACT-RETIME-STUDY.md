This tests a useful distinction from the owner's sport references: hold more *real source frames* around a genuine visible contact, while keeping the lead-in and exit energetic. It does not simulate a Fight Night blow. The trainer and bag remain visible, and the person is not represented as a Staffy fighter. The page reports 59.94 fps; `ffprobe` confirmed the file's actual rate. No stock original is committed.

Source frames 50–109 cover one photographed bag strike and recovery. A 12 fps source contact screen shows the glove meeting the bag around frame 75. Both outputs have **30 frames at 24000/1001 fps, 1.25125 s**, with the same source endpoints. The control samples a nearly uniform two source frames per output frame. The alternate uses source-frame anchors: output 0→source 50, 8→70, 12→74, 18→80, 29→109. This gives frames 70–80 eleven consecutive source frames through the visible contact, compared with roughly every other source frame in the control. The lead-in/out accelerate to keep total duration fixed. Both maps are strictly increasing; no optical-flow synthesis, repeated contact frame, freeze or post blur is used. The script checks the source hash and emits the exact maps.

The contact sheet shows more distinct glove/bag positions around the strike in the alternate while preserving the same opening and recovery poses. This establishes temporal selection and a visible contact gate, **not that the retime feels better at normal speed**. Its exit may feel hurried, and no physical-phone playback or human normal-speed judgement has occurred. The footage has no source audio, so it cannot prove impact sound, beat sync, a music pocket, or a credible ring hit. Do not add a stock punch SFX to claim that a bag shot is a landed opponent strike.
