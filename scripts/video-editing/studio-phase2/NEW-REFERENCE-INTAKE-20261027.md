## What changes in the edit process

Before choosing an effect, name the shot's job and the viewer's new information. Plan one source-supported visual mechanism per sequence: an anchored subject freeze, an expanding screen, a motivated comparison, a real filmed screen reveal, a brief shutter at an actual action, or a clean unadorned cut. Repeating the same text reveal, zoom and generic B-roll across the whole film is not a variety plan. Keep a control cut in the revision package and be willing to retain it when the effect weakens source clarity.

Sound must correspond to a physical or visual event: on-screen tap, object contact, camera move, reveal or music phrase. Retain source sound where it communicates reality. Add licensed or original effects as removable stems; measure cue alignment, peak, mono and export. A `0 dB`-safe decode and waveform do not tell us whether a sound is appropriate. The existing local audio models failed silence/tone controls, so they cannot sign off the effect. An independent editor must hear a client mix at full speed on headphones and a phone before delivery.

The [rapid-shutter proof](skills/rapid-shutter/SKILL.md) and [window-grow proof](skills/window-grow/SKILL.md) use original synthetic media. A later [real-stock split-screen source test](skills/source-led-split-screen/SKILL.md) was rendered privately and **rejected** because cramped halves and unrelated shoots weakened the story. The [real-footage freeze-frame A/B](skills/grounded-freeze-frame/SKILL.md) was also rejected after full-size inspection exposed a severed arm and matte contamination despite correct subject/background motion measurements. These are editing experiments, not any of the owner's client videos, and do not qualify a technique as mastered.
