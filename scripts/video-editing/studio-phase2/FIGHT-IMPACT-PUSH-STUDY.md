# Source-anchored impact push: equal-timing A/B

**25 October 2026 — rejected for this practice shot.** [Plain](proof-fight-impact-push-plain.mp4), [6% contact push](proof-fight-impact-push-contact-push.mp4), [side-by-side](proof-fight-impact-push-ab.mp4), [six-point contact sheet](proof-fight-impact-push-contact.png), [270×480 contact still](proof-fight-impact-push-phone.png), [reproduction script](render_fight_impact_push.py).

This tests a short digital camera push as a possible emphasis for the [59.94 fps bag-contact retime](FIGHT-CONTACT-RETIME-STUDY.md), with identical source-frame timing across A/B. The source remains [Pexels #7986157](https://www.pexels.com/video/7986157/) under its [free licence](https://www.pexels.com/license/), local ignored SHA-256 `56d9d9b01954509236a5b81bec172f99d513afad9f2e880c33837b2cb06bf803`. It is a training bag, not a Staffy opponent hit. Both versions use the exact contact-slow map over source frames 50–109; output is silent because the stock file has no audio stream.

The alternate scales around `(625,510)` in the 720×1280 frame, from 1.00 at output frame 9 to 1.06 at frame 13 (source frame 75, glove/bag contact), back to 1.00 at frame 21. It uses real source frames, no shake, flash, synthetic motion blur, separate cutout or exposed canvas. The focal anchor is close to the right-side bag, keeping its edge and the glove visible. The proof script has a constant-picture timing control and hash-checks the source.

**Observed decision:** the contact sheet and 270×480 comparison retain the glove/bag meeting, but the 6% enlargement adds little readable information at phone scale. It trims more of the boxer's body and makes the fixed tripod shot look digitally pushed. For this already clear training shot, prefer the plain retimed picture. This is a rejection of this *source/effect pairing*, not a universal ban on camera pushes. A different event angle with spare space around a fast, small contact might justify one, but it needs a plain-cut comparison and actual normal-speed/phone review.

All three H.264 exports fully decoded: two 720×1280 A/B variants and one 1440×1280 side-by-side, each 30 frames at 24000/1001 fps (1.25125 s). Full-speed motion and sound were not human reviewed, and no event applicability is claimed.

**Event gate:** log real contact and source fps, preserve original camera sound, then test an optional short push only where the glove/opponent remain legible and the shot has crop margin. Protect frame edges and inspect several frames before and after the hit. A louder effect or faster zoom cannot rescue unclear contact.
