## Authority before a new cut

1. Read the active job, current client kit, current approval/revision messages and staff ownership. Nolan's `production-queue/2026-10-23-video-queue.md` is a continuity source, but its time-stamped state must be checked against current feedback. Keep the paused Nolan production automation paused.
2. Identify the *actual posted* client videos and the separately delivered or rejected revisions. **For every source-month folder proposed, open that month's public Facebook/Instagram posts and subsequent posts through the planned release date before declaring any source fresh.** A clip shot in June may have been published in July or later. Inspect the full posted videos, including late montages, with their manifests/source ledgers. Compare proposed shots by visible action, people, room, wardrobe, camera setup and stock identity, not filename alone. Record a public URL/date and the prior *story job* for each recognisable source cluster. A missing match in an incomplete local export archive, a new filename, a new crop or an unposted adjacent camera take is not proof of newness. If public history or visual comparison remains incomplete, say **unverified** and hold the proposed shot. Preserve an exclusion list in the private client media workspace; do not commit client footage or staff likeness sheets.
   **Owner correction, 26 October:** Older footage may be repurposed even when its shoot or a different moment from it has appeared in a post. Do not blanket-ban an outfit, room, or entire source folder because of a prior post. The new film must have a materially new purpose and feel fresh to a viewer; a crop, speed change, new text or soundtrack alone does not achieve that. Compare candidate source timecodes against the published edit, record the old and new story jobs, and reject a copied beat/story as-is. If exact picture or publication history is uncertain, label it uncertain and investigate before representing it as fresh. Do not repeatedly ask for new footage merely because an older setup was posted. Recheck the whole export and cover for repetition before sending.
3. Write the buyer, why **this month and this local season**, one message, useful proof, intended feeling/action, three distinct hooks, and a credible CTA before assembling. Compare the proposed purpose and sentence-level message with published client work, not just picture files; repurposed B-roll must do a demonstrably different job in the new story. A draft strategy does not become an approved script. For retail, treat old product footage as historical: neither its picture nor copy may imply current shelf stock, a recurring service, price, promotion or availability without a current check. A season/event is a reason only when the filmed scene and business action actually support it. Stock illustrates a point; it does not impersonate the client, a real adviser or a documented customer outcome.
4. Screen each complete moving source and then its proposed crop at normal speed. Log `source | visual cluster | shot job | exact in/out | setup/onset/payoff/release | adjacent rejected range and reason | portrait crop | source audio | rights/identity | prior use`. Examine frames around both edit boundaries. Retain only the useful moment. Reject the range where someone misses a cue, fumbles, poses awkwardly, looks away, resets, or the action becomes irrelevant. Do not hide an awkward performance with a shorter hold on the same pose. An apparently attractive still or technical decode is not moving-picture approval.
5. Check the selected shots in their actual sequence. Each must advance the story, make a meaningful visual or sound connection, and remain legible on a phone. Do not repeat a source, a recognisable visual cluster or a story formula to fill 30 seconds. Effects, typography and stock motion need a specific editorial purpose and a clean-cut comparison. Ground foreground cutouts at a believable frame or object edge. **27 October visual-variety gate:** map the beginning, middle and ending by shot job, camera scale/movement, foreground depth and typography treatment. Inspect every long static stretch and repeated reveal/zoom; replace repetition with a *source-supported* camera action, frame relationship or clean cut. The [seven new references](NEW-REFERENCE-INTAKE-20261027.md) supply possible mechanisms, not mandatory effects. A short shutter, growing frame, split view or burn must answer a specific scene need and survive a plain-cut A/B; do not sprinkle them across the film for novelty.
6. Select music *after* the story and moving cut exist. Compare the candidate with posted/rejected cue IDs, listen to the actual phrase and final export, and record why it suits this client. Classify every source audio range as dialogue that viewers need to hear, purposeful natural action, or silent B-roll. Mute crew direction, prompts, false starts and unrelated off-camera conversation under B-roll; do not leave camera audio open just because it measured below the music. Direct-to-camera speech needs its intelligible words or another picture. For each added effect, log the exact visible cause and frame of its onset/peak/release. Compare a dry cut with a removable noise or tonal cue at matched level, and check its pitch against the *actual* music where a tonal cue is proposed. Do not use a generic whoosh on every transition. Metering, silence detection, waveform alignment and decode prove technical properties only; they do not certify musical fit, absence of distracting speech or creative sound.
7. Confirm the current brand kit, approved fonts, graphics, colour treatment, logo animation and required portrait adaptation. Use professionally designed/imported graphics only where the treatment is coherent and genuinely serves the explanation. The PSG V2 static icon board is a rejected example despite its licensed icons. Check caption punctuation/style against current client feedback.
8. Review the complete exported film at normal speed, with actual sound, at phone scale; check titles throughout their motion, contact/gesture payoffs, source reuse, logo resolve, cover and caption. Save a versioned editable source with media online. Record which checks were witnessed, which were measured and which remain unverified. Do not deliver or call a render owner-review ready while a blocking creative, source, sound or rights gate is open.
9. Before sending an MP4, resolve the exact client's configured production identity against `docs/onedrive-naming-authority.md`, the existing client folder/export stem and CG Dynamics. Check the **actual attachment filename** in the send preview. Preserve `YYYY_MM_<CLIENT_SHORT_CODE>_VIDEO_<XX>` and the established descriptive stem; give protected revisions unique suffixes. Do not invent a code or sequence, overwrite an existing final, or send a private experiment basename as a forwardable client file. Verify any copy by hash before handoff.
