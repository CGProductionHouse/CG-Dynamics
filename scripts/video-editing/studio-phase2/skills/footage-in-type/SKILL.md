# Footage inside type — rendered, not mastered

**Lifecycle:** owner reference visually studied → original local proof rendered → **human motion/audio review pending** → client use untested → mastered **NO**.

The owner's [Solid Coast reference](https://www.tiktok.com/@solid.coast.editing/video/7558410119273549079) visibly uses a text-track alpha matte to reveal cyclist footage inside `INSIDE`, then keyframed type scale/position to push into full-frame footage. Its sound was not reviewed. The later [Miracamp walk-through-text tutorial](https://www.tiktok.com/@miracampschool/video/7663481654098349344) is a distinct subject-in-front-of-text composition; do not conflate its foreground mask with this text aperture.

Use a short, legible word with a large opaque stroke and a shot whose action reads through the glyphs. Keep the word centred and inside mobile safe areas long enough to read. In the push, aim at a filled letter stroke rather than an open counter (`O` zoom produced a black hole in the first attempt). Transition to the full moving shot without a visible flash, unwanted black gap or abrupt edge. Sound should support the reveal, but the owner's reference audio has not yet been heard or matched.

This lane's [4.5 s original proof](../../proof-reference-matte.mp4) uses a framewise Space Grotesk Bold alpha mask generated by `../../render_reference_matte.py` and FFmpeg `alphamerge`/`overlay`, with source hashes/licences in `../../project.json`. [Contact frames](../../proof-reference-contact.png) and a full decode confirm the matte and full-frame result. Pillow 12.2, FFmpeg 8.1 and the already available licensed local Mixkit assets were used; no new video engine or plugin is required. The mask is generated from editable source text in the script, but the final MP4 itself is not a native editor text layer. For a staff-editable handover, recreate the same alpha-matte idea in the chosen editor and test save/reopen/export there.

The first composite left a floating lower boundary on the keyed performer. The corrected proof pushes that source boundary below the canvas. For any isolated person/object, inspect every frame where its alpha edge enters or exits: terminate against the frame edge or a real occluder, with plausible scale and contact. A clean key alone does not make a cutout physically grounded.

Known limits: the proof uses green-screen stock, a simple end dissolve and one fictional word. It does not prove PSG source rights, a natural-background mask, a reference-matched camera push, reusable editor-native text, phone readability or approved sound design. Promote only after a suitable real CG shot, editable handover where needed, perceptual motion/audio review and explicit creative approval.

A later [270×480 opening A/B](../../MATTE-READABILITY-STUDY.md) changed only the scene fill under the same moving `MOVE` mask. Brighter blue slightly separates the word from black but does not fix its small opening scale or make the fragmented person inside narrow glyphs clearly readable. Do not use fill brightness as a substitute for choosing a shot and word shape that work together. This proxy is not a physical-phone or full-speed review.

## Source and scale follow-up — 27 October

`render_matte_scale_ab.py` checked the older `MOVE` treatment at its original 264-pixel and a larger 396-pixel opening size, with the same hash-verified Mixkit #28306 dancer source and identical moving picture. Both one-second silent candidates decoded, and the 270×480 five-phase contact was inspected. The larger word uses more of the phone frame, but the person still appears mostly as a face fragment in `O` and isolated skin in other glyphs. **Reject a size-only revision as a fix**; typography scale cannot make the underlying picture narratively readable.

`render_workshop_ab.py` then tested a different, source-motivated pair: `CUT` over the exact same portrait Mixkit [#39691 carpenter-saw](https://mixkit.co/free-stock-video/carpenter-slicing-a-wooden-board-in-a-close-up-shot-39691/) shot, SHA-256 `186bc1eb994695af1d7c2312d3f8ea6aac0a4e23767d24727471066e7cceb618`, under its Stock Video Free License. The plain title shows the glove, blade and wood immediately; the candidate holds moving picture **inside** a legible bold `CUT` for 25 frames, then pushes through the opaque `T` stroke and reveals the same continuing shot. Both 540×960, 24 fps, 2.000-second mute exports fully decoded. The first aperture render shifted the opening word left because the affine transform was not the identity at scale one; it was rejected and corrected before inspection. Five-phase phone contacts now show a complete word at the start and no hollow-letter black hole at the zoom midpoint. Original stock and renders are private outside Git.

This establishes an actionable source/word choice and a mechanically continuous, frame-anchored aperture. **It does not prove this is a better hook.** The plain control immediately communicates the saw action, while the aperture hides most of that action for its first second. Use the treatment only if anticipation before the reveal actually serves the story; retain the plain opening when the viewer needs immediate proof. The stock worker's `TDM` shirt is visible after reveal and must not imply a CG client's premises. No source sound exists, no cue/music was added or heard, and native editable typography/physical-phone/full-speed audience judgement remain open.
