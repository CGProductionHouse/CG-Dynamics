# Fight trailer sequencing — integrated practice, event proof pending

**Stage:** [10.010 s equal-picture stock audio A/B](../../FIGHT-INTEGRATED-STOCK-STUDY.md) rendered and technically verified; human sound/phone review, event material and staff handover pending. Mastered: **no**.

Start with an impact log from the full event source: original clip/timecode, whether glove-to-target contact is visible, immediate reaction, opponent and location, source camera audio, rights and any outcome that needs confirmation. Do not count the same strike twice, turn an obscured blow into a knockout, or let a sound effect assert contact absent from picture. Select the strongest distinct *readable* events before choosing music or transitions.

Map a 30-second trailer as a cold hit, distinct second impact, escalating exchanges, a short pressure pocket that leads somewhere, a strongest verified peak, and an approved end card. Treat this as a beat map that can change with the footage. Use plain cuts first. Keep enough frames to show each strike's setup, contact and consequence; retain two-fighter geography when a tight portrait crop would erase the opponent. A square or blurred-band rescue may be usable briefly but is a visible compromise. Reject repetitive inserts that only fill duration.

Build sound from original ring/camera audio. Add separately licensed music and at most useful, individually placed effects for observed contact. Keep a music-only or natural-sound control with identical picture, and compare at similar playback level. A measured duck or aligned waveform is only a mechanical result until a human hears the finished sequence at normal speed. Inspect full-speed picture/sound and a physical phone view for hit clarity, music fit, SFX artificiality, crop, title overlays and a clean finish.

Preserve source asset links, clip ranges, audio layers, edit settings and export receipt so staff can make revisions. Do not label a rendered MP4 an editable project. The stock study establishes that the existing FFmpeg/Python pieces can form one short sequence; it does **not** validate the requested event trailer or native editing handover.
