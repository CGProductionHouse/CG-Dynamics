# Kinetic caption finishing

**Status, 26 October 2026:** Five owner-shared Solid Coast Editing TikToks were visually sampled in the browser with audio muted. A protected Daisy A/B tested staggered rise and local gradient. Fully synthetic steady-title controls now compare a masked shine and a restrained opacity accent. A natural foreground-over-type source was screened and rejected. Full-speed audible review and native staff reopen have not passed. This is a source-grounded construction and selection card, **not a mastered effect pack**.

Choose the buyer sentence and real picture action first. Assign each title one job: interrupt with a concrete hook, clarify a choice, identify the business, or resolve the CTA. Compare a clean title with the treatment over the **same** shot, wording, duration and music. Retain only the version that reads better at normal speed and phone size. Use the client's approved font/colour and editable text layers; a technically successful raster export alone is not a staff handover.

| [Shine effect](https://www.tiktok.com/@solid.coast.editing/video/7584052900964388118) | A narrow diagonal mask crosses a graphic/logo, exposing a bright sweep. | Test one short branded resolve if its highlight has a clear destination. Inspect every matte frame, hair/hand edges and the entire word. An object-mask button is not proof that the mask tracks a particular Daisy source. |

For a client film, record `shot/timecode | sentence/job | clean control | effect variant | font/colour | keyframes/mask | sound event | full-speed phone verdict | editable reopen verdict`. Keep rejected treatments and reasons. A 3D or perspective title requires a believable photographed plane and source motion; use the existing [scene-perspective-type card](../scene-perspective-type/SKILL.md) rather than forcing depth onto a flat product frame. Anchor cutout edges at the frame or a real object. Stop the treatment before it masks the offer, human expression or CTA.

## Protected Daisy A/B, 26 October

[`render_caption_ab.py`](render_caption_ab.py) read the untouched local `199A5420.MP4` flower-window take into two **silent, private technical proofs**, 4.000 s/96 frames at 540×960/24 fps. Both use the existing Montserrat Bold and Daisy yellow/warm white. The control reveals both words together; the variant staggers their vertical arrival and adds a feathered gradient only behind the wording. Both MP4s fully decoded. The 0.56 s A/B and eight sampled variant frames show the second phrase arriving separately and the gradient improving separation from the changing pink flower. The variant SHA-256 is `A1553C3753D228C43539417E35924F6D34B908BA3741EA6349CE73B469CABF20`; media and contact images remain in the private Daisy `qa/caption-ab` folder, outside Git.

**Decision:** keep the stagger/gradient operation available, but reject this particular flower shot as the Daisy film's hook: it is largely decorative, includes bright window reflections, has no visible person or shopper action, and its posted-use history is not cleared. The placeholder Afrikaans wording is unapproved copy. These silent, half-resolution files prove neither musical sync nor whole-film energy, client readiness, native editable titles, object masks, 3D perspective, shine, or a phone/headphone viewing verdict. Do not turn this test into the November film merely because the effect renders.

## Synthetic masked-shine A/B, 26 October

[`render_shine_ab.py`](render_shine_ab.py) creates a fictional ECHOFORM title, a [steady control](proof-shine-steady.mp4), a [single-sweep variant](proof-shine-sweep.mp4) and a [five-frame phone-size contact](proof-shine-contact.png). Both are 3.000 s, 540 × 960 at 24 fps / 72 frames, silent H.264; both fully decode. The diagonal highlight moves across the lettering during frames 14–35 (0.92 s), clipped to the exact glyph alpha mask. The background, border and small tagline do not receive the sweep. No stock, client identity, downloaded logo, plugin or paid asset is used. SHA-256: steady `B6179394B6309E008A84460A1CE18EE5BA3094BFE406D53C85AFEB11F97CCC44`; sweep `B8CABE5D9B2E42E54B89DC47EF453B9F9DD027A82F984B33D503BEBED3AC2233`.

The first near-white base title made the white highlight practically invisible, so it was rejected and changed to a darker teal base before these proofs. At 270 × 480 the short sweep is visible in the contact without a whole-frame flare. This establishes a **masked visual mechanism**, not that shine improves a particular client logo, that its timing fits music, or that it is editable in Premiere/CapCut. Before client use, test one motivated branded resolve against the steady control at normal speed with sound and on a phone, preserve the official logo exactly, and prove a staff-revisable native title/mask if editability is required. Repeating this on every caption would dilute the effect.

## Natural foreground source rejection, 26 October

The [Mixkit #1252 three-frame source screen](../../NATURAL-OBJECT-MASK-20261026.md) tested a real left-to-right walking person over fictional `MOVE` type. The body reaches the lower frame edge, but the foreground covers too much of the word and the GrabCut mask includes a lower-right background wedge. The source/word placement is rejected before a moving render. This is the key editorial check for the Object Mask tutorial: a tracked outline alone does not justify an unreadable title or a pasted background edge.

## Restrained opacity accent A/B, 26 October

[`render_flicker_ab.py`](render_flicker_ab.py) reuses the exact fictional ECHOFORM stage and glyph mask from the shine proof. A [steady entrance](proof-flicker-steady.mp4) and [single restrained accent](proof-flicker-accent.mp4) have the same 3.000 s / 72-frame, 540 × 960 / 24 fps silent H.264 output and both fully decode. The [phone-size 12-frame contact](proof-flicker-contact.png) compares frames 11–16 for both versions. The control appears at frame 12 and holds; the variant appears at 75%, reaches full opacity, dips once to 75%, then remains at 100% for the remaining 57 frames. At the first accent frame, 7,488 changed pixels were inside the glyphs and **zero** outside the glyphs in the pre-encode raster comparison. SHA-256: steady `AC2FD755E1F2D44CFF88257AB63ADB65748AA11F8387BF86A1ED05CBDE4CDF2E`; accent `EF163298FBA9CC2E6D40023AC80ED2D297D4167AFC6CAC12C1FC12F4E48AA7C4`.

An initial 25%-opacity entrance was rejected as too faint at 270 × 480, then changed to the 75% reference-sized accent. The current accent is readable at sampled phone-size frames but adds little to a static fictional title; it is **not a default client caption animation**. A real scene must justify a single emphasis against a steady reveal, with full-speed sound/phone review and client-safe typography. No perceptual flicker comfort, beat timing, native opacity keyframes or staff editability is proven by the silent raster render.
