**Reference:** [Dani's Rapid Shutter tutorial](https://www.tiktok.com/@justdodani/video/7445684422860377366) visually shows staggered short clips and a 90-degree directional-blur control. The displayed frames do not establish a universal clip length or prove that the effect suits a client. [Multiply Sound's comparison](https://www.tiktok.com/@multiply.sound/video/7597296358311103758) motivates a separate tonal-versus-noise sound experiment; its actual sound was not heard.

**Use:** Only if outgoing/incoming subjects, direction, scale and story relation remain intelligible. Find the plain cut first, align the action peak to the chosen music event, then add brief alternate glimpses on a protected duplicate. Retain the existing grade and title legibility. Make any directional blur short enough to settle before the next shot's action. Flashing unrelated shots is a failure, however exciting the timeline looks.

**Original proof:** `py render_synthetic_ab.py proof` makes [plain cut with noise](proof/plain-noise.mp4), [shutter with the **same noise**](proof/shutter-noise.mp4), [shutter with tonal rise](proof/shutter-tonal.mp4), individual silent picture versions and cue WAVs. The first two isolate the picture change; the latter two isolate the sound change. All are 540×960, 30 fps, four seconds, generated from code. The [nine-frame contact sheet](proof/cut-contact.png) compares frames 55–64 around the cut. The first render made the circular subject teleport across the frame; it was **rejected and corrected** by starting room two at the outgoing x-position. The corrected render keeps direction and position consistent at the cut. Selected incoming frames receive a narrow vertical blur, and the shutter pattern settles after frame 62. This tests mechanics only; the design is deliberately schematic.

**Measured sound control:** The two original mono cues share the same A drone and 2.000-second accent. Only the 1.72–2.00-second rise changes: deterministic noise versus a rising 220–440 Hz harmonic. WAV peaks both measure −15.0 dBFS; whole-file RMS −34.24 dBFS noise and −34.16 dBFS tonal. Similar overall RMS does not mean equal perceived loudness or musical fit. The proof's synthetic tones are not a reusable licensed sound library or a chosen client music bed.

**Isolation check:** FFmpeg's packet SHA-256 for the shutter video stream is identical across the shutter-noise and shutter-tonal outputs (`2d5dc67f…d1a65b0`). Its noise audio stream is identical across plain-noise and shutter-noise (`214e05ef…98aa4f42`). This proves the sound A/B changes only audio and the picture A/B changes only picture; the first two-output draft confounded both and was corrected before acceptance.

**Verification:** All three muxed MP4s fully decode with H.264 video and AAC mono sound; `ffprobe` reports 4.000 seconds and 30 fps. The contact sheet was inspected at original size; its first, incorrect subject-position render was discarded. No normal-speed perceptual playback, sound listening, physical phone check, viewer-comfort check or native editor reopen has been done. **Do not apply the seven-frame alternation blindly to client footage; it may be too intense.** For a real edit, compare a shorter one- or two-glimpse version with the plain cut, inspect every frame for accidental occlusion/flicker and ask an independent editor to hear the full sequence. Maintain source sound and removable SFX stems. No plugin or purchase is required for the practice proof.

### Forward-only correction

The protected third variant keeps the same two-frame vertical blur but orders the footage monotonically: outgoing pose at frame 22, blurred first destination pose at 23, then the **next** destination source frame at 24. It never previews destination → returns to source → jumps to destination. `render_dancer_ab.py` now saves `dancer-forward-shutter.mp4`, a three-row full-width contact and an eight-frame 180×320 portrait crop contact alongside the unchanged plain and rejected return variants. The new 1280×720, 24 fps, 2.000 s mute candidate fully decodes. The source-index sequence and both contacts verify the visible back-and-forth error is removed. This is a successful **temporal ordering correction**, not proof the effect improves a film.

Portrait inspection found a new source limit: a fixed centred 9:16 crop clips the dancer's reaching hand at the destination pose. Shifting the crop left to include the hand cuts the outgoing head/shoulder at frames 20–22. Neither static crop carries the whole action; do not present this landscape take as a ready vertical example or hide the loss with a decorative zoom. The horizontal technique can be tested further against its plain cut with a genuine music beat, normal-speed comfort and an editable native timeline. For vertical client use, capture or select action with sufficient safe framing before applying the shutter. The source is silent, and no effect sound was fitted or heard in this real-footage trial.
