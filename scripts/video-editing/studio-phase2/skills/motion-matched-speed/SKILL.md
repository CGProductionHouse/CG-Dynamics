# Motion-matched speed — one-shot timing rendered; cross-shot match pending

**Lifecycle:** owner's [car ramp](https://www.tiktok.com/@miracampschool/video/7540770684838956310), [time-remapped building](https://www.tiktok.com/@travisdennymedia/video/7629402531680013581), [three-frame zoom](https://www.tiktok.com/@miracampschool/video/7504014296779590935) and [keyframed zoom](https://www.tiktok.com/@ihsangunyasar/video/7504667931322666258) visually sampled → one-shot [speed timing A/B](../../SPEED-RAMP-STUDY.md) rendered → cross-shot match and full-speed review pending → mastered **NO**. The tutorials' displayed 200% scale and 200-degree shutter examples are not default CG settings.

Mark a recognizable movement in the outgoing shot and a matching movement in the incoming shot: direction, screen position, scale and velocity. Cut at the overlap in *action*, then test the plain cut. If the action warrants acceleration, keep a readable lead-in and landing before adding a speed curve; leave enough actual source frames to avoid freezes or smeared interpolation. Try a keyed zoom only when it strengthens the focal handoff. Inspect every boundary frame for exposed canvas, subject jumps, stutter and motion blur that erases the product or face. The fastest three-frame pattern is an optional comparison, not a target duration.

**Practice gate:** acquire two or more cleared shots with matching action and known source fps/shutter. Save a plain-cut control and one ramp/zoom revision at delivery fps. Record the source in/out points, retime curve, focal-point coordinates and any blur. Review normal-speed picture with sound at phone size; verify the intended action remains intelligible. Prefer native editor controls for staff handover when they reproduce the approved result; FFmpeg can render a standalone study. Existing cards for [short zoom cuts](../short-zoom-cuts/SKILL.md) and [masked zoom](../masked-zoom-transition/SKILL.md) cover the narrower mechanics.

The first [source screen](../../MOTION-SOURCE-SCREEN.md) **rejected** a same-person wide/close coffee pair: the cup, gaze and expression jump across plausible cut frames. No ramp was rendered. Shared subject and location alone are insufficient; acquire an actual matching gesture before promoting this card.

The separate [59.94 fps dancer A/B](../../SPEED-RAMP-STUDY.md) proves deterministic frame remapping on one continuous shot, with the same start/end frames, no repeated source frames and a full-decode comparison. It does **not** test a cut between shots, audio sync, stutter perception or native editability. Do not infer cross-shot skill from a one-shot timing proof.

The later [59.94 fps bag-contact A/B](../../FIGHT-CONTACT-RETIME-STUDY.md) is closer to an impact: eleven consecutive real source frames cover the observed glove-to-bag moment while a constant-speed control samples roughly every other frame. It tests retime mapping and visible contact preservation only. The source has no audio; a bag strike is not an opponent hit. Keep normal-speed, phone, source-sound and real event gates open.
