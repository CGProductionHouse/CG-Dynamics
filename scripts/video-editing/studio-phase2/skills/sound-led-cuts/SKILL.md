The [mono fold-down check](../../FIGHT-MONO-COMPATIBILITY-STUDY.md) verifies that this one candidate's measured attack remains present through a standard left/right average. It does not approve its timbre or test a full layered mix. Recheck mono after adding music, room and camera audio; never infer whole-mix compatibility from one isolated effect.

The [public landing original-sound study](../../FIGHT-SOURCE-AUDIO-LEVEL-STUDY.md) demonstrates a different failure: its actual camera track has over-range peaks, and a nominal −3 dB adjustment still exceeds 0 dBTP after AAC encode. Measure the delivered file, retain the untouched source, and treat any corrective gain as source-specific. For the event sequence follow the [source-first sound playbook](../../FIGHT-SOUND-PLAYBOOK.md) and use only event-verified contact.

The [slowed-picture, same-picture audio A/B](../../FIGHT-RETIME-AUDIO-SYNC-STUDY.md) shows why preserving an original camera stem is only half the task: the source's strongest landing-region window stayed at 1.81 s while the slowed board was still airborne. A separately retimed working stem moved that window near the grounded frame at 2.36 s. This is mathematical sync evidence on a public skate clip, not a fight SFX recipe or a heard timbre approval. Keep the untouched source, recorded time map and working audio on distinct tracks.

For a non-impact ambience splice, the [public camera-audio continuity A/B](../../FIGHT-SOURCE-CUT-CONTINUITY-STUDY.md) demonstrates a 10 ms overlap that reduced one hard-cut sample jump without touching the remainder of the working track. It is a possible click repair, not a default duration. Do not fade through genuine contact or imply unrelated source takes are one continuous event; the resulting sound remains unheard.

Use this when a story has a visible action or reveal that can motivate sound. Mark the *picture event* first: contact, door opening, gesture, gaze change or scene reveal. Listen to the clean source audio before adding effects; a real location sound may be stronger than a library hit. Build the cut without sound design, then try one licensed pre-event cue and one event sound. Let a riser end at the cut or action, and place the impact on the visual contact frame. A whoosh belongs to a believable movement; do not add one to every cut. Compare the dry and designed cuts at matched loudness so louder does not masquerade as better.

Keep dialogue intelligible and preserve room tone across short gaps. Trim SFX tails or overlap them intentionally; inspect for clicks at edit points. Check the music, dialogue and effects together at normal speed through speakers and headphones, including the first and last second. Loudness measurements, limiter readings and waveform alignment are diagnostic aids, not listening approval. In the editable handover, keep source sound, music, riser and impact on separate named tracks; for a standalone render, keep a source manifest and per-layer timing/gain values.

**Practice gate:** with cleared footage and individually licensed audio, save a 5–10 s dry cut and a sound-led revision. Record cut frame, cue onset, impact frame, audio source/licence, per-layer gain, exported loudness/true peak, and a human listening note describing whether the event feels more specific and less cluttered. Reject an effect that hides dialogue, creates a false action, has an unlicensed sample or depends on an unheard TikTok sound. The existing Mixkit music/whoosh proof supplies technical AAC mixing evidence only; it has **not** received the required audible creative review.

The first [5.42 s coffee study](../../SOUND-CUT-STUDY.md) uses cleared, silent Mixkit video and the same music in both versions. The added Mixkit `Sip of water` cue is technically synchronized to the late close-up, but inspection of the moving-source samples shows the woman mouthing or blowing toward the mug; a sip is not established. **Reject that cue.** This is a useful editorial failure, not a sound-design success. No natural production sound or human listening approval was available. The protected picture stream is identical in both MP4s, proving only that the audio revision left the visual cut intact.
