# Scene-perspective title: real-grass control

This is a bounded original A/B for the [scene-perspective-type skill](skills/scene-perspective-type/SKILL.md), not a Fight Night or client edit. It uses [Mixkit #2029, a static-camera golfer shot](https://mixkit.co/free-stock-video/young-boy-golfing-2029/) under the [Mixkit Stock Video Free License](https://mixkit.co/license/modal/videoFree/). The ignored local 1280×720 source is actually 60000/1001 fps and has SHA-256 `45c834f4cc2909ad30584cfc192077b1250290c8fd7a1d93fdbc4ad0367c9e2a`. The source page describes the shot as static; this test does not imply moving-camera tracking. No original stock file is committed.

`python render_scene_perspective_type.py` takes 48 frames from the same source interval, starting at about 6.3 seconds, and renders a [flat title control](proof-scene-perspective-flat.mp4) and [ground-plane candidate](proof-scene-perspective-ground.mp4) at 24000/1001 fps (2.002 s). [Same-frame A/B](proof-scene-perspective-ab.png) shows the change. The script uses the existing Space Grotesk font, Pillow 12.2, OpenCV 5.0.0, NumPy and FFmpeg 8.1; it adds no application plugin or production dependency. The candidate projects `FOCUS` through a four-corner homography onto the visible grass, keeps it away from the golfer's shoes and flagpole, and blends the grass texture through the letters. The golfer and ball remain the original moving footage.

Both MP4s fully decoded. At a 480×270 image check, the word remains readable and the foreshortened version is more closely aligned with the green than the flat control. The very light lettering still looks more like a graphic placed *over* grass than paint actually on it; no shadow/grass interaction or foreground crossing proves physical integration. The camera is static, so this does **not** validate a track on a moving plane, natural occlusion, portrait delivery, audible timing, native editable title or a complete tutorial effect. It was assessed from sampled frames and decode, not watched at full speed on a physical phone. Keep the card at **rendered / human review pending**, not mastered.

For a future production version, first choose a title whose meaning and placement serve the real shot. Then obtain a moving-camera or moving-plane source with visible anchors, compare a plain flat title against a genuinely tracked plane, inspect every foreground crossing and phone frame, and save/reopen the staff-editable source in the chosen editor. Do not add perspective merely because the technique exists.

**Moving-shot source challenge (25 October):** [Mixkit boxing #40969](https://mixkit.co/free-stock-video/boxer-is-knocked-out-when-getting-hit-in-a-ring-40969/) has a moving camera and visible ring mat, but [three portrait-crop frames](proof-perspective-ring-source-rejected.jpg) at source 4.5, 5.0 and 5.5 s show the existing `SAMURA` floor sponsor mark taking most of the apparent type plane while a fighter's glove/arm enters from the right. The source is hash-checked in the existing fight study, under the [Mixkit Free Stock Video License](https://mixkit.co/license/modal/videoFree/). In the 9:16 crop there is no stable, uncluttered patch large enough for meaningful type without covering the sponsor or action. **Reject this source for a tracked typography render.** A technically accurate homography would not make the placement editorially justified. No overlay or masking was attempted, and this is not a moving-plane tracking proof.
