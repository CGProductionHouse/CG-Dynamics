# Film transport across two distinct locations: bounded source test

**29 October 2026, Nolan Studio PC.** This stock-only study extends the existing [film-strip transport construction](skills/bounded-burn/SKILL.md) to an actual two-shot cut. It is a generic city-to-coast chapter change, not a claim that the two locations share a route, traveler, event or client. There is no soundtrack or captured physical film burn.

## Source screen and rights

The outgoing [Mixkit #40739 city bridge at sunset](https://mixkit.co/free-stock-video/bridge-of-cars-and-people-in-the-city-at-sunset-40739/) and incoming [Mixkit #44366 beach aerial at sunset](https://mixkit.co/free-stock-video/aerial-tour-over-a-beach-at-sunset-44366/) are both labelled Stock Video Free License for commercial or personal use on their exact item pages; the [publisher's free-video license](https://mixkit.co/license/modal/videoFree/) permits modification and commercial/social-media use. A first beach candidate, [#2165](https://mixkit.co/free-stock-video/the-beach-at-sunset-in-the-background-2165/), was screened and rejected for its stronger blue/magenta colour jump against the warm city shot. The retained pair still differs in exposure and camera motion. The city file is 720 × 1280, 195 frames, 24000/1001 fps, SHA-256 `E6181E650B7ED27C0FFDEF4849987586879673A02A260AE2BB0FDB9DEF368365`; the beach file is 1280 × 720, 494 frames, same fps, SHA-256 `232810A388B834CCE5F93E954F2C9E02CA86196A1104447B96591E5C4B664A99`. These downloaded 720p tiers were inspected as bytes before framing. They and all outputs stay private/off Git.

## Plain control, transport and protected revision

The private source-specific script `work/research-continue/film-context/render_ab.py` **reuses** the existing card's `strip()` geometry and 31-position shutter integration. It maps city original frames 64–108 to output 0–44 and beach original frames 240–284 to output 45–89. Beach is cropped to a fixed 405 × 720 portrait window at source x337, then resized; city is already portrait. The plain control makes the actual location cut at output frame 45. The strip replaces only output frames 42–47 with held outgoing/incoming picture moving one 1000-pixel film pitch. The clean transport has no leak. R01 adds the original-strength procedural leak (0.72); protected R02 changes only the active leak strength to 0.32. All four are 90-frame silent H.264 MP4s at 540 × 960 and 24000/1001 fps.

I decoded **all 90 frames of all four MP4s**; size/fps match and no frame has mean luma below 8. The first 42 decoded frames match the plain control pixel-for-pixel in every variant. R01 and R02 also share that exact protected opening; all six intended active frames differ after the leak-strength revision. Their identical input tail does **not** decode pixel-identically (0/42 exact tail frames), even with a GOP boundary at output48; encoder rate-control state carries forward. Do not claim exact output-tail protection. The source maps, full interval contact sheet and full-size decoded R02 frames 44/47 were inspected. They show a continuous smeared strip crossing from bridge to sea with a restrained warm gap tint in R02; no repeated discrete head copies were observed in those sampled frames. The transition makes source detail unreadable for six frames. Adjacent mean absolute pixel difference around the plain cut peaks at 64.11 at frame45; the transport spreads changes of about 43–62 across frames42–48. That measures temporal redistribution, not better rhythm or comfort.

**Gate remains open.** The city-to-coast jump is legible as a generic chapter shift, but the plain cut is clearer and the effect's six-frame smear may be too conspicuous for a short social edit. Sampled stills and motion diagnostics cannot establish full-speed/physical-phone quality or heard sound. This is not a physical burn, licensed overlay, exact owner-reference reconstruction, client story, or native separable-layer handover. Keep the plain cut as the default until a genuinely useful shot pair, scene motive, phone playback, sound and editable-layer test justify this treatment.
