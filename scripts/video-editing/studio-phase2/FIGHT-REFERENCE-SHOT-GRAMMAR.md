## Visual operations to practise on real footage

1. **Hero hit selection:** inspect the whole source at full speed, log strike/contact/reaction timecodes, then protect the clear contact frames. Picture-first. The [stock contact triage](FIGHT-CONTACT-TRIAGE-STUDY.md) demonstrates why an attractive obscured shot fails.
2. **Detail-to-space reveal:** choose a source macro and a real arena/walkout view. Try a plain scale cut first; if a short zoom/focus release improves orientation, compare it with the [silent graphic resolve](FIGHT-GRAPHIC-RESOLVE-STUDY.md). Its look is a practice candidate, not a proven client treatment.
3. **Motion bridge:** identify the frame where a source glove, rope, pan or nearby person actually crosses the view. Match exit/entry direction and scale. A partial sweep may motivate a cut; it cannot conceal different bouts as a continuous blow.
   The later [same-take #40974 source screen](FIGHT-GLOVE-HANDOFF-STUDY.md) found that a blue glove passes near lens but leaves the opponent visible, so it cannot hide a skipped body-pose interval within that take either.
4. **Source-led speed:** mark available source fps and exact contact frames before changing speed. Fast lead-in, brief readable real-time contact, and a reaction may work; no additional freeze on already slowed 24 fps stock (see [rejected hold](FIGHT-SCENE3-CONTACT-HOLD-STUDY.md)).
5. **Controlled contrast:** grade one source shot against its untouched control at phone size. The [cool-shadow preset test](FIGHT-GRADE-STUDY.md) darkened the subject and was rejected. Retain face/glove separation and consistent skin, ring and brand colours before stylisation.
   Likewise, a [6% source-anchored impact push](FIGHT-IMPACT-PUSH-STUDY.md) added little to an already clear bag strike at phone size and was rejected for that shot. Do not add zoom to every contact by default.
6. **Geography reset:** after a macro or whip, show both fighters or a stable ring orientation before asking the viewer to read another strike. The [portrait crop test](FIGHT-VERTICAL-FRAMING-STUDY.md) rejected a fill that cut away one opponent.

The [geography-reset source screen](FIGHT-GEOGRAPHY-RESET-SOURCE-SCREEN.md) also rejects a proposed cut from a tight portrait stock fight to a landscape wide: it cannot establish chronological continuity and fails the portrait two-fighter composition gate. An editor should not rescue that source mismatch with more effects.

These are source-selection and cut decisions first, effects second. Avoid a montage of close-up gloves, generic flashes, artificial knockouts, repeated punches or masks with floating edges. The 30-second film needs a distinct shot progression and a reason for each effect. The two TikToks are inspiration, not sound licences or evidence that our existing stock footage matches them.

## Sound pass after the picture selects

The visual map above does **not** establish the references' music, mix, SFX or beat timing. Once the event footage arrives, preserve each selected clip's original audio and audition it at normal speed. Keep a dry picture/source-sound cut before music. Then make a separate licensed-music candidate and add only individually justified cues: pre-impact breath/footwork or crowd, original glove contact, optional supporting low-frequency hit, and brief ambience return. A pressure drop must lead to a visibly verified strike. Compare dry and designed versions at matched playback level on headphones, speakers and a physical phone; record *what was heard*, not just LUFS or waveform numbers. The owner's prior music rejection remains authoritative; no track has been selected here.
