# Existing stage flare versus added exposure pulse

**Decision: keep the natural cut; reject the added pulse on this source.** The [side-by-side A/B](proof-flare-cut-comparison.mp4), [plain cut](proof-flare-cut-control.mp4), [rejected pulse](proof-flare-cut-rejected-pulse.mp4) and [eight-frame contact](proof-flare-cut-contact.png) compare one stage drummer-to-singer cut. This is a stock-only visual technique study, not a finished music video or a client edit.

The sources are Mixkit [drummer's hands #472](https://mixkit.co/free-stock-video/drummers-hands-using-wooden-drumsticks-472/) (local SHA-256 `022555a6a6b108a57e54ca7dc4df48c0ab78ddf7e1d0facd5ccb84121c8f6f1f`) and [female singer #474](https://mixkit.co/free-stock-video/a-female-singer-on-the-stage-474/) (SHA-256 `924ed8a9dbf8b62a9d5f5603359718c8dbc3f40bf3e1ec829c0b08d6034cfcf6`), both under the [Mixkit Stock Video Free License](https://mixkit.co/license/modal/videoFree/). The downloaded clips are 1280×720 at 60000/1001 fps and have no audio stream. Stock originals remain ignored by Git.

The drummer section selects every other source frame from **272–360**, then the singer from **450–538**. This yields 45 output frames per shot at 30000/1001 fps. The outgoing picture already has a magenta light flare; the incoming picture already has a strong cyan light flare. The candidate multiplies RGB exposure around the cut: output frames 43/44/45/46 have gains 1.04/1.10/1.10/1.04. All other picture frames use the exact same source index in both variants. Both separate MP4s and the labelled comparison fully decode; the latter is 2560×720, 90 frames, 3.003 s, H.264, SHA-256 `3d95593867716e2cf966aad052e76a5e8a3ff52c512fbd7f9a670cedee79f612`.

The added pulse pushes more of the already bright singer image toward white. In the pre-encode frames at the cut's first singer frame, the fraction of pixels with grayscale luma ≥235 rises from **26.3%** in the control to **45.1%** with the pulse. The contact sheet shows weaker face detail in the candidate. This is a source-specific reason to reject the extra exposure: the real flare already motivates the cut. A single added pulse is not a reusable default transition, and a tutorial's 200-degree shutter-angle value is not validated here. Neither soundtrack fit nor normal-speed/phone perception was assessed; the source clips are silent.

Reproduce with `python render_flare_cut.py` after placing the two exact licensed stock files in ignored `media/`. The renderer checks source hashes and writes intermediates to ignored `output/flare-cut/`. It reuses FFmpeg and the already-installed Python imaging libraries; no plugin, native editor session, external client-media upload, or AI credit was used. Keep the plain cut unless a later soundtrack and full-speed review provides a genuine reason for a different treatment.
