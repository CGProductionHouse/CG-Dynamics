## The later event sound pass

1. Preserve all source camera tracks and identify which selected strike belongs to which microphone/timecode. Audition at normal speed before choosing a stock effect. If location sound is usable, use it as the impact anchor. If not, document why.
2. Make a dry timeline with clear picture and source audio. Separately place candidate ambience, licensed effect and music on named tracks. Keep alternate cues isolated so staff can remove a fake-sounding layer.
3. Compare dry, source-plus-one-cue and layered versions with identical picture and matched playback level. A supporting low end or air movement must fit the visible action; no SFX for a miss, obscured contact or invented knockout. The owner-rejected song cannot become the default bed.
4. Audition the complete 30 seconds at normal speed on headphones, speakers and a physical phone. Judge impact timbre, intelligibility of original venue sound, breathing room before peaks, musical build and end button. Technical LUFS/true peak and full decode come **after** a listened creative decision.
5. Preserve each audio asset URL, licence, exact in/out, gain, processing and export stem. Ask a human reviewer to compare final apparent impacts against their original camera sound and image.
