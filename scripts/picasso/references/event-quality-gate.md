# Event photography quality gate

Use this for a fast-moving event with mixed light, bursts and documentary moments. It supplements the runner; its measurements are review hints, not decisions.

1. **Turn examples into shoot-wide standards.** Save the reviewer’s exact marked frames and requested changes in the private job folder. Trace each marked gallery frame to its camera source. Record the underlying defect (for example blurred subject, blown bright skin or whites, unsuitable colour, weak moment) and check for that defect across every previously selected frame and adjacent original bursts. The marked examples are evidence, never an exhaustive removal list. Exclude specifically rejected frames from the next review set unless the reviewer explicitly reverses that call. Keep source files and a reversible decision log. For every new round of feedback, append a dated, source-linked standard to the private job record, mark which shoots and image classes it applies to, and recheck all affected selected frames and unshipped exports. A changed standard clears earlier editorial acceptance until affected images are reviewed again. Carry recurring CG preferences into future shoots, while allowing a reviewer to specify a narrower exception for a particular scene; do not apply one colour grade mechanically to every lighting condition.
2. **Build the story from original bursts.** Group by capture time, bout and viewpoint. Pick decisive actions, reactions, walk-ins, ring-card/venue details and winners so each fight and the whole night are represented. Compare adjacent frames at full size; a global blur score can be high because ropes, signs or lights are sharp while the boxer is blurred. Check the actual main subject’s face/eyes or action plane. Where the decisive moment is technically imperfect, retain it as REVIEW rather than silently losing documentary coverage.
3. **Edit from the original once.** Use scene-specific adjustments. Inspect RGB histogram, bright skin and garments, facial luminance, cast and noise. Preserve stage atmosphere and the original JPEG’s genuine highlights. If a face or garment is already channel-clipped, try a sharper adjacent frame with detail; darkening a white patch cannot restore it. Do not use one formal/daylight look across low-light fight frames, or map whole-frame statistics from a WhatsApp reference to another photo. Decide colour versus monochrome for each frame based on lighting and storytelling, not a single cast threshold.
4. **Check at two sizes.** At gallery size, judge moment, composition, consistent skin and coherent colour/B&W pacing. At 100% on subject detail, reject obvious focus misses, motion smear, halos, crunchy sharpening and plastic texture. Sharpening can improve apparent edge clarity only when genuine subject detail exists; it cannot repair a blurred capture. Review especially any unusually bright face, white headgear, shirt or ring floor.
5. **Calibrate before scaling.** Select 25 varied frames across daylight, walk-in, blue/red mixed light, dark ring action, portraits and mono candidates. Include exact camera frames matching staff-approved examples where available. Treat the staff-approved look and subject clarity as the minimum bar. Show before/after and critical 100% subject crops privately, not after-only thumbnails. Record human accept/revise/reject per frame and the chosen settings. Only after acceptance, apply the approved approach to a larger batch. Make a complete selection ledger: one entry per previously selected image, with source, set, subject focus, exposure/clipping, colour/skin, story value, decision and reason. Inspect every selected image, revisit nearby original frames for a stronger replacement, and reconcile ledger counts before preparing a new gallery. Do not turn a metric alone into a rejection. Technical hash/dimension checks remain necessary but never replace this editorial gate. `scripts/event_quality_review.py` can build private same-crop original/edited sheets and a CSV of review prompts from a custom event manifest. Its detected face may be the wrong person, and its sharpness and colour-difference numbers are not release decisions.
6. **Publish only reviewed work.** Require a recorded accepted 25-frame calibration and an explicit final KEEP/REVIEW/EXCLUDE decision for every intended image before staging or browser upload. Do not count rendered files, technical verification, or contact-sheet screening as KEEP. Confirm set count and visitor view, then send one signed PICASSO link to the named approvals group. If reviewers reject the set overall, unpublish it promptly and acknowledge withdrawal so they do not keep spending time on a known-bad draft. No automatic client delivery. The 26 Oct Staffy review violated this gate: Amonique found a blurred walkout frame (`card1:0053`) and said the overall editing looked like originals; the collection was hidden. Carry both defect classes into the whole-shoot audit, without presuming the marked image is the only blur.
