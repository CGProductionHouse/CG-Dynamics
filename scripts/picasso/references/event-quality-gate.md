# Event photography quality gate

## Fast path for the next shoot

Use the already licensed Lightroom Classic seat on a verified Windows host. Start with a private copy of the approved JPEG shoot and a separate catalog. Assisted Culling and burst adjacency narrow choices; a human checks story coverage and important subjects at 100%. Choose 25 varied frames across actual lighting groups. For each group, match an exact approved frame in Reference View, inspect clipping and skin under the scene's light, and approve a representative edit before synchronizing only intended Basic and People-mask settings to similar frames. Inspect mask overlays and several exports from each group before expanding the batch. Keep blue/red stage light, daylight, walk-ins and controlled monochrome as distinct groups. Use Photoshop or free Affinity for difficult local temporary blemishes or fabric cleanup only when the installed seat is available; retain layered working files and run the metadata-safe export check. The per-image native-size/editorial gate catches defects before Pixieset.

The crop review CSV records source and output measurements for the **same source-coordinate crop**, sampled at up to 512 pixels: median and 99th-percentile luminance, percentage with any channel near white, percentage with all channels near white, median saturation, and median LAB blue/yellow axis. Sort these columns to find candidates for native-size inspection. A detected crop may contain a spectator, referee, fixture or glove; a reviewer-selected subject box improves relevance but still requires visual judgment. A high near-white percentage does not prove blown skin, and a low one does not prove acceptable exposure. The gallery-wide audit's `rgb_change` compares an export to its **camera source**, never to a staff-approved look. Keep crop reports and manual box JSON in the private job, never GitHub. The 195-frame full crop pass took 431 seconds on the MSI; after a small amendment, use `--keys` to inspect changed frames and the hash-bound delivery audit's `--reuse-review` to revalidate the whole manifest without recomputing unchanged global measurements.

The available Adobe batch-edit/portrait connector skill stages local files in Creative Cloud for processing. That transfer is outside PICASSO's current private-local-photo authority, so do not route CG client JPEGs through it. ArtCraft PhotoCraft remains an early-alpha candidate; the official free LightCraft portable test was fast but did not reach the staff colour reference. Neither is the default editor. digiKam/darktable remain optional no-cost fallbacks when a verified host lacks a working Lightroom seat; avoid adding another editor until an actual gap is shown. No extra subscription, credit, hosted service, or public photo folder is needed for the next local proof.

Use this for a fast-moving event with mixed light, bursts and documentary moments. It supplements the runner; its measurements are review hints, not decisions.

1. **Turn examples into shoot-wide standards.** Save the reviewer’s exact marked frames and requested changes in the private job folder. Trace each marked gallery frame to its camera source. Record the underlying defect (for example blurred subject, blown bright skin or whites, unsuitable colour, weak moment) and check for that defect across every previously selected frame and adjacent original bursts. The marked examples are evidence, never an exhaustive removal list. Exclude specifically rejected frames from the next review set unless the reviewer explicitly reverses that call. Keep source files and a reversible decision log. For every new round of feedback, append a dated, source-linked standard to the private job record, mark which shoots and image classes it applies to, and recheck all affected selected frames and unshipped exports. A changed standard clears earlier editorial acceptance until affected images are reviewed again. Carry recurring CG preferences into future shoots, while allowing a reviewer to specify a narrower exception for a particular scene; do not apply one colour grade mechanically to every lighting condition.
2. **Build the story from original bursts.** Group by capture time, bout and viewpoint. Pick decisive actions, reactions, walk-ins, ring-card/venue details and winners so each fight and the whole night are represented. Compare adjacent frames at full size; a global blur score can be high because ropes, signs or lights are sharp while the boxer is blurred. Check the actual main subject’s face/eyes or action plane. Where the decisive moment is technically imperfect, retain it as REVIEW rather than silently losing documentary coverage.
3. **Edit from the original once.** Use scene-specific adjustments. Inspect RGB histogram, bright skin and garments, facial luminance, cast and noise. Preserve stage atmosphere and the original JPEG’s genuine highlights. If a face or garment is already channel-clipped, try a sharper adjacent frame with detail; darkening a white patch cannot restore it. Do not use one formal/daylight look across low-light fight frames, or map whole-frame statistics from a WhatsApp reference to another photo. When the exact staff-posted frame exists, compare skin, neutral lights, coloured gloves and dark background together. A global white-balance shift that improves skin but yellows a neutral ring light is not an approved match; use restrained selective work or mark the frame for a human editor. A compressed WhatsApp reference gives an editorial direction, not full-resolution focus proof or exact export RGB targets. Decide colour versus monochrome for each frame based on lighting and storytelling, not a single cast threshold. **Owner rule, 27 October:** When blue or magenta stage light makes skin look bad and a restrained colour correction would be slow or unnatural, try a contrast-controlled black-and-white finish. Keep strong colour frames in colour. Monochrome is a treatment choice, never a cure for missed focus, clipped detail or a weak moment; inspect subjects at native size before including it in a gallery.
4. **Check at two sizes.** At gallery size, judge moment, composition, consistent skin and coherent colour/B&W pacing. At 100% on subject detail, reject obvious focus misses, motion smear, halos, crunchy sharpening and plastic texture. Sharpening can improve apparent edge clarity only when genuine subject detail exists; it cannot repair a blurred capture. Review especially any unusually bright face, white headgear, shirt or ring floor.
5. **Calibrate before scaling.** Select 25 varied frames across daylight, walk-in, blue/red mixed light, dark ring action, portraits and mono candidates. Include exact camera frames matching staff-approved examples where available. Treat the staff-approved look and subject clarity as the minimum bar. Show before/after full scenes and critical 100% subject crops privately, not after-only thumbnails. Record human accept/revise/reject per frame and the chosen settings. Only after acceptance, apply the approved approach to a larger batch. Make a complete selection ledger: one entry per previously selected image, with source, set, subject focus, exposure/clipping, colour/skin, story value, decision and reason. Inspect every selected image, revisit nearby original frames for a stronger replacement, and reconcile ledger counts before preparing a new gallery. Do not turn a metric alone into a rejection. Technical hash/dimension checks remain necessary but never replace this editorial gate. `scripts/picasso/event_quality_review.py` (or this skill's `scripts/event_quality_review.py`) builds private full-scene and same-crop original/edited sheets and a CSV of review prompts from an event or immutable gallery manifest. Its detected face may be the wrong person, and its sharpness and colour-difference numbers are not release decisions.
6. **Publish only reviewed work.** Require a recorded accepted 25-frame calibration and an explicit final KEEP/REVIEW/EXCLUDE decision for every intended image before staging or browser upload. Do not count rendered files, technical verification, or contact-sheet screening as KEEP. Confirm set count and visitor view, then send one signed PICASSO link to the named approvals group. If reviewers reject the set overall, unpublish it promptly and acknowledge withdrawal so they do not keep spending time on a known-bad draft. No automatic client delivery. The 26 Oct Staffy review violated this gate: Amonique found a blurred walkout frame (`card1:0053`) and said the overall editing looked like originals; the collection was hidden. Carry both defect classes into the whole-shoot audit, without presuming the marked image is the only blur.

## Reference-matched fight-night colour check

For each lighting group, place the exact camera JPEG beside the staff-posted version of that same frame. At equal *display* size, review the main subjects' skin and facial brightness, neutral ring lights, glove colours and background blacks together. Inspect the camera file separately at 100% for focus; a compressed social-media post cannot certify native detail. Keep the posted version as a visual direction rather than a numerical RGB target. If the selected camera frame is soft, search its adjacent burst before trying a sharper-looking edit.

Make a reversible, scene-specific candidate from the camera original. Global warmth that turns a white fixture yellow fails the neutral check, even if skin looks closer. A colour-range skin mask must be viewed as a separate mask: reject it if it spills onto blue gloves, the referee, white fixtures or unrelated objects, or if it misses a subject under different light. Inspect the edited result at gallery size and native size. Record a pass or failure for each of the four visual anchors above and for both subjects' detail where applicable. A candidate that improves one anchor but leaves the overall staff look short stays REVIEW; never infer approval from pixel statistics, a generated mask, or an export integrity check.

Do not promote one frame's mask, curve, white balance or grade to a bout preset until several frames under the same light pass all checks. When stage lighting changes, split the group and recalibrate. Any remaining difficult face, blemish or mixed-light correction goes to a human editor in Affinity or an available licensed Adobe seat; retain the layered working file and export a separate metadata-sanitized JPEG. Preserve original dimensions and avoid encoding an already exported JPEG again.

## Audit the assembled gallery before upload

For a flat event upload with a manifest containing `items` (`key`, `source`, `file`, `group`, `route`), run `python <delivery_consistency_audit.py path> --manifest <private manifest.json> --images <private upload folder> --out <private audit folder>`. The script is bundled in this skill's `scripts/` folder and tracked at `scripts/picasso/` in the repository. This read-only check verifies count, names, dimensions, RGB/sRGB/EXIF and builds a CSV and contact sheets grouped by fight. The CSV highlights unusually different brightness within a group, near-white pixels, almost imperceptible source-to-export changes and missing editing provenance. These are **review prompts**, not automatic rejections or a substitute for inspecting every selected subject at 100%. A plain `colour-checked` route is insufficient: record the actual edit source, settings or preset, and any monochrome/manual exception in the private manifest.

For a one-photo gallery revision, add `--reuse-review <previous immutable revision/audit/review.csv> --no-sheets`. The audit still hashes every camera source and output, remeasures changed files, and recomputes all flags; it reuses prior measurements only where the key, filename and both hashes match. Do not use a CSV from a different shoot or manually altered measurement source. On Staffy revision r5, this reused 194 unchanged rows and decoded one changed JPEG in about three seconds; the earlier full 195-image audit took several minutes. Build fresh contact sheets when a full gallery visual pass is needed, and never treat cached measurements as prior photographic acceptance.

For native-detail review of an immutable flat gallery revision, run `python <event_quality_review.py path> --manifest <private revision/manifest.json> --out <private review folder>`. It verifies camera and output hashes and makes original/edited full-scene and same-crop sheets for each selected photo, grouped by fight. It also accepts the older event manifest format. A detected face may be a referee, spectator or background person; inspect the orange boxes in the full scene before interpreting the crop or its focus probe. For a **focused** rerun with a known main subject, put native source pixel coordinates in a private JSON mapping such as `{"card1:0070": [x1,y1,x2,y2]}` and pass `--keys card1:0070 --subject-boxes <private.json>`. The box is validated against the camera image and marked as reviewer-selected. This corrects the review crop only; it does not edit or approve the photo. Never reject a fight frame solely from an automatically selected bystander crop.

Complete a **copy** of `review.csv` with one row per intended upload: `subject_focus`, `grade_vs_reference` and `scene_consistency` must each be `PASS`; `editorial_decision` must be `KEEP`, with reviewer and reason. Exclude/rework any failed item from a new upload folder and regenerate the audit. Record the accepted 25-frame calibration separately as JSON with `status`, `reviewer`, `accepted_at`, at least 25 exact `sample_keys` covering varied groups, and `sample_sha256` mapping each accepted key to the reviewed output hash. Run the tool's `--check-release --review <completed CSV> --acceptance <record.json>` with the same manifest and upload folder. It verifies the reviewed file and source hashes, row set and acceptance sample; it never publishes. If a calibrated sample is re-exported, its old acceptance fails. A pass records an accountable decision, not proof that the photography is good. Keep the review records private. Do not regenerate the audit over a completed review CSV.

The 27 October Staffy 195-image emergency gallery was sent before this explicit gate. Its original selection ledger still marked images `REVIEW`, and the staging manifest collapsed several editing routes to `colour-checked`. Treat it as **awaiting photographic approval**, not as a reference look for future bulk jobs. Apply any new feedback class across the whole gallery and neighboring bursts; only amend the same Pixieset collection after the revised files pass the gate and the reviewers have the new revision link.

When replacing individual photos in an existing Pixieset review collection, use its visible **Replace photo** action so the collection link and photo slot stay stable. Keep a separate local revision that exactly mirrors the intended collection: record each replaced filename, revised JPEG path, specific edit route and reason in a private JSON `replacements` array, then run `python scripts/picasso/assemble_gallery_revision.py --base-manifest <manifest> --base-images <images> --replacements <private.json> --out <new-private-revision>`. The tool refuses an existing destination, verifies unchanged JPEG and camera-original hashes, checks replacement dimensions, sRGB ICC and absent EXIF, and writes a new manifest with updated output hashes. Run `delivery_consistency_audit.py` on that revision. A Pixieset success toast proves the upload action, not that the whole gallery meets the photographic gate; inspect the replaced thumbnail and visitor gallery, then seek reviewer approval. Never use a local snapshot as proof of a remote replacement that was not observed.

Before a replacement when another editor may be working in the gallery, search the exact Pixieset filename and use its owner **Download** control to obtain the current full-size JPEG. Hash that download and compare it with the last confirmed local gallery revision. If the bytes differ, pause that slot and inspect the newer work; never overwrite it from an old snapshot. On 28 October, the live downloads of Fight 11 `199A4902` and `199A4940` matched the prior r5 hashes exactly, so those slots had not changed. A separate r6 private revision staged warmer Lightroom candidates for those two files, passed 195/195 source and output hash checks, and reused 193 unchanged metric rows. The company Chrome profile `cgproductionhouse.com` first could not open the Pixieset upload picker. After the owner enabled the ChatGPT browser extension's **Allow access to file URLs** setting, both candidates were uploaded through the visible **Replace photo** action in the existing collection. Pixieset processing cleared, the thumbnails changed, and fresh owner downloads of both photo slots matched the exact r6 JPEG SHA-256 hashes. The photo IDs and gallery link stayed the same. This proves the two replacements, not a photographic pass for the rest of the gallery. If the picker fails on a future host, use the documented Chrome extension file-access troubleshooting route and obtain any required permission; do not claim a local revision is live before the remote download matches.

Keep comparison thumbnails, crop previews and editing scratch files **outside** the revision's `images/` directory; the assembler deliberately rejects an image folder whose filenames differ from its manifest. On the 27 October Staffy revision, a Fight 05 frame with strongly magenta skin worked better in controlled monochrome, while a Fight 04 result portrait was made too yellow by the earlier global stage grade and looked more natural when re-exported from its camera original. Compare original, current gallery export and candidate at equal size before each decision. Returning to the camera look can be the correct correction; record that route explicitly and strip source EXIF on the export. After a Pixieset replacement, refresh and search the exact filename to verify the new thumbnail persists, then create the new immutable local revision. Neither a file chooser event nor a changed local manifest alone proves the remote photo changed.

**Wait for Pixieset processing.** The file chooser returning does not mean an upload has completed. On a Staffy red-lit group portrait, refreshing while its thumbnail still showed the old colour version left the old file in place. Retry through the visible Replace photo action, watch the selected tile's **Processing** state finish, and verify the new black-and-white thumbnail before writing the next revision. If the tile still shows the old pixels, leave the manifest unchanged. A red cast across all faces can also warrant monochrome, provided the full scene and each important face pass native-size detail review.

On a later Fight 03 replacement, the first attempt again failed when the editor was refreshed before its upload completed. The retry used a private JPEG copy with the **same filename as the Pixieset slot** and observed `Photo replaced successfully`, then `Processing`, then a rendered thumbnail. Keep this sequence; the Pixieset Info pane retained the original upload time and file size after replacement and therefore cannot prove the new bytes. Record the upload as UI-confirmed with byte-level remote verification unavailable, and preserve the local revision's exact replacement hash. This frame's original JPEG has bright skin highlights that cannot be recovered; its new neutral stage grade improves the excessive orange cast without claiming to fix clipping.

Lightroom Classic remains the preferred licensed batch editor when its seat is available. Adobe documents [copying selected Develop settings across multiple photos](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/process-and-develop-photos/develop-module-options.html) and [recomputing AI subject/people masks on each target photo during Copy, Sync and Auto Sync](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/process-and-develop-photos/masking.html). This is a supported route, not proof that an unreviewed batch is photographically accepted.

## Lightroom Classic route verified on the MSI

Use a separate local catalog and **Add** approved camera JPEG copies so the catalog holds non-destructive edits while the originals stay in place. Use Assisted Culling to triage burst focus and eyes, then inspect every intended subject and documentary moment; the 27 October Staffy 25-frame test changed from 25 SELECT at subject-focus threshold 50 to 17 SELECT / 8 REJECT at 80, demonstrating that its flags are threshold-dependent. Put an exact approved frame in **Reference View** alongside its camera source. Calibrate Basic tone/white balance and native **People → Facial Skin + Body Skin** masks on a representative frame; inspect the overlay for missed skin and spill onto clothes, gloves, lights, the referee or background. For same-light frames, Copy Settings with only the intended Basic fields and Masking checked; Lightroom recalculates the AI mask for the new people. One cross-scene transfer recomputed the mask correctly but was reset because correct segmentation does not mean the grade suits different lighting. Never paste a single ring grade over daylight, mixed light and walk-ins. At export, explicitly choose the private destination, JPEG quality at least 90, sRGB, no resize, minimum metadata and no extra output sharpening unless a full-size test supports it. Verify the rendered file's dimensions, ICC, EXIF and visual match. All such exports remain REVIEW until the 25-frame human gate passes.

For fast frame-level comparison in Lightroom Classic, use **Before/After Left/Right**: Adobe documents synchronized zoom and pan, so inspect the same face or white headgear at 100% in both versions. In Develop, the histogram's shadow/highlight indicators show clipping overlays; hold **Alt** while moving Whites or Blacks to see individual RGB-channel clipping. Treat these displays as inspection aids. A stage fixture already clipped in the source JPEG cannot be recovered by lowering Whites. Do not turn Auto Sync on for a mixed-light event selection; Adobe says it applies each slider move to all selected photos.

For approved same-light sequences, Adobe documents **Synchronize Settings** and **Auto Sync** in Reference View; select only the intended adjustments and confirm that Lightroom re-renders People masks on each frame. **Match Total Exposures** is based on aperture, shutter speed and ISO metadata, so treat its result as a candidate under rapidly changing stage lights, not a visual-match guarantee. Lightroom Classic's Adaptive Color profile guidance is for **RAW**, so do not make it the JPEG batch recipe. Use Lightroom Classic's non-generative **Heal** or **Clone** for individual temporary blemishes where suitable, with Generative AI off and a 100% texture check; broader skin/fabric work needs a supervised editor. Automation saves repeated UI work after a look is accepted. It cannot determine whether a blurred subject, clipped skin, ugly colour cast or weak moment should be delivered.

Official Lightroom Classic references: [Assisted Culling](https://helpx.adobe.com/uk/lightroom-classic/desktop/organize-photos-in-lightroom-classic/assisted-culling.html), [Reference View](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/process-and-develop-photos/develop-module-tools.html), [copy and paste settings](https://helpx.adobe.com/uk/lightroom-classic/desktop/help/copy-paste-settings.html), and [batch AI masks](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/process-and-develop-photos/masking.html). These document supported capabilities; the Staffy colour and subject-detail results still require actual frame-level review. digiKam's [similarity view](https://docs.digikam.org/en/left_sidebar/similarity_view.html) can group near-duplicates without deciding documentary value. darktable's [Windows binaries and CLI](https://docs.darktable.org/usermanual/development/en/special-topics/program-invocation/invocation-per-os/) are a no-cost fallback if licensed Lightroom is unavailable on a verified host, not another editor to install by default.
