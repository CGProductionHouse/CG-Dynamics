## 5. Patient-facing vs referrer-facing mode

### Patient-facing

Use plain language first. Explain what a condition, symptom or specialist role means without diagnosing the reader.

Useful patient copy:
- reduces confusion;
- explains why specialist assessment may matter;
- gives a calm next step;
- makes room/contact logistics easier;
- separates general information from personal medical advice.

Do not overwhelm patients with abbreviations, procedural detail or worst-case scenarios.

### Referrer/doctor-facing

More technical language is acceptable when the audience is clinical.

Use exact verified clinical terminology and only verified referral/process details. Never invent:
- referral thresholds;
- work-up requirements;
- imaging requirements;
- urgency pathways;
- hospital/procedure availability.
