## Reusable decision method

1. Define the target metric before selecting the route. Earned operating revenue, invoicing, receipts, MRR, annualised exit rate, gross merchandise value, profit and valuation answer different questions. Keep VAT, refunds, financing, intercompany activity and genuine pass-through amounts appropriately separated. The accountant determines recognition for actual contracts.
2. Audit current assets and reachability. Code, staff, equipment, client relationships and expertise are options, not proof of paid demand or spare capacity. Product readiness must be established through the actual authorised workflow, not inferred from repository presence.
3. Compare opportunities broadly, then choose a primary path and a meaningful fallback. Evaluate time to customer cash, contribution, buyer access, recurrence, working capital, founder effort, support, regulatory exposure and repeatability. Sunk development effort is not a reason to launch every product.
4. Build the revenue bridge without double counting. Only the incremental portion of an upgrade adds to an already-retained base. Existing projects cannot be counted again as new projects. A multi-entity internal transfer does not increase consolidated third-party revenue.
5. Reverse-engineer sales and capacity. Make the proposed win rate, meeting conversion, attendance and qualified-account flow editable. Model churn, partial first-month earnings, full onboarding effort, delivery labour, other direct costs, acquisition, shared overhead and cash timing. Unknown costs stay unknown, not zero.
6. Demand paid evidence before broad product investment. Interviews, interest, letters of intent, signed scopes, payments and renewals have different evidential strength. A narrowly scoped, honestly delivered service can validate a workflow before it is automated.
7. Test transferability. Count founder rescue time, contractor effort, support and rework. Revenue per employee is misleading when contractor and founder dependence are concealed. Build the repeated expensive bottleneck, not a broad feature wishlist.
8. Protect cash and permissions. Tax reserves, customer obligations, suppliers and committed delivery are not discretionary capital. Live campaigns, purchases, provider credentials, messages, financial transactions and production writes retain their existing approval gates.
9. Use founder cases critically. Identify disclosure type, timeline, capital, team, distribution and failure points. Unknown CAC and margins remain unknown. Acquisition value and payment volume are not operating revenue. A purposive case collection is not a success-probability estimate.
10. Convert evidence into adaptive execution. Review cash, retention, accepted delivery and qualified pipeline before extending the build queue. Calendar dates do not override failed commercial, safety or capacity gates.

These are proposed management practices and reasoning distinctions, not empirical claims of guaranteed uplift or universal benchmark thresholds.

## Reference calculation requirements

Use one declared currency and VAT basis per calculation. Reject missing, malformed and non-finite inputs. Do not coerce missing values to zero. Use explicit bounded fractions and valid dates. Label expected fractional customer counts as scenario expectations. Distinguish closing MRR/ARR from revenue earned over the period. Do not distribute and retain the same after-tax cash twice. Tax tables must carry their jurisdiction, effective tax year, source and professional-review limitation.

For manual state: keep storage opt-in and warn about shared devices; sanitise imports and render text safely; require a past completed month and source reference before a manually marked closed result can appear as a target achievement. A checkbox does not independently authenticate the source or authorise execution.

## Evidence and freshness

Official tax/regulatory sources outrank influencer or generic marketing claims, but explanatory guidance, administrative implementation and enacted law are not interchangeable. Recheck at the actual decision date. Supplier price lists are asking prices, not proof of realised demand for a different scope. Research access coverage must distinguish abstracts, selected sections, indexed text and complete pages.
