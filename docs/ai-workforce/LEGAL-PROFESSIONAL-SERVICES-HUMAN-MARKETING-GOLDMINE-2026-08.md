## Human truth of the category

People rarely look for legal help because life is going well.

They are often:

- anxious;
- embarrassed;
- angry;
- confused;
- under time pressure;
- worried about cost;
- worried they will not be understood;
- unsure whether their problem is serious enough;
- afraid of making the situation worse;
- trying to protect family, business, property, money or reputation.

The strongest marketing does not begin with the firm saying how excellent it is. It begins by showing that the firm understands the moment the client is in.

## What legal-service buyers actually value

### Source LPS-H1 — Solicitors Regulation Authority consumer segmentation research

Publisher: Solicitors Regulation Authority
Canonical page: https://www.sra.org.uk/sra/research-publications/consumer-segmentation-research/
Source type: regulator-led consumer research
Rights state: official_reference

Verified findings:

- approachability matters;
- emotional support matters;
- responsiveness matters;
- transparency about process and cost matters;
- the way the service is delivered often matters more than demographics;
- willingness to answer basic questions in plain language builds trust;
- face-to-face or video contact can be important early in the relationship;
- common barriers include perceived cost, time, energy and difficulty choosing a provider.

Marketing lesson:

Legal marketing should make the firm feel easier to approach before it tries to make the firm look impressive.

Useful messaging angles:

- “You do not need to know the legal words before you call.”
- “We explain the next step before asking you to take it.”
- “You will know who is handling your matter.”
- “Clear answers. No disappearing.”
- “Start with the part you understand. We will help with the rest.”

Avoid:

- “Excellence. Integrity. Results.”
- “Your trusted legal partner.”
- “Passion meets purpose.”
- stock imagery of handshakes, scales and gavels without a human story.

### Source LPS-H2 — SRA consumer comparison research

Publisher: Solicitors Regulation Authority
Canonical page: https://www.sra.org.uk/sra/research-publications/consumer-understanding-dct/
Source type: regulator-led consumer decision research
Rights state: official_reference

Verified findings:

- recommendations from friends and family are a major first step;
- previous-use loyalty matters;
- online research is a major discovery route;
- local-area search matters;
- family-law consumers often inspect the website of a recommended firm;
- many consumers compare more than one provider;
- reviews and pricing information influence evaluation, but price is not the only factor.

Marketing lesson:

Referral marketing and digital marketing are not separate. The recommendation gets the firm onto the shortlist; the website, reviews, people and response speed determine whether the referral converts.

Required client journey:

```text
Problem happens
→ ask someone trusted / search online
→ shortlist firms
→ inspect website, reviews and people
→ judge clarity, fit and responsiveness
→ make contact
→ decide whether the first interaction feels safe and competent
```

## South African trust and credibility signals

### Source LPS-H3 — Legal Practice Council practitioner search and conduct framework

Publisher: Legal Practice Council
Canonical sources:

- https://lpc.org.za/
- https://lpc.org.za/legal-practitioners/code-of-conduct/
- https://lpc.org.za/members-of-the-public/search-practitioners/

Source type: South African statutory regulator
Rights state: official_reference

Marketing relevance:

- practitioner status should be represented truthfully;
- registration and practising status can be checked;
- publicity must remain professional and not misleading;
- professional titles, specialisation and outcomes must not be exaggerated;
- a marketing claim must not create a false expectation or imply a guaranteed legal result.

Practical website and content use:

- explain who is admitted and in what role;
- distinguish attorney, advocate, candidate legal practitioner and support staff;
- show practice areas without implying expertise that cannot be supported;
- link credentials and professional standing only where verified;
- use plain-language explanations of what the firm can and cannot help with.

## Campaign and creative research

### Source LPS-H4 — Deneys Reitz brand campaign

Source: Brand Republica campaign record
Canonical page: https://www.brandrepublica.com/deneys-reitz-attorneys
Source type: South African law-firm brand campaign
Rights state: case_study_reference

Reported outcome:

- the campaign helped align employees and business units around a common position;
- the firm was reported as topping the South African table and winning best law firm in Africa.

Transferable human lesson:

Professional-service branding works better when the internal behaviour supports the external promise. A law firm cannot advertise clarity while its own people communicate in a cold, fragmented way.

CG application:

Before a brand campaign, interview partners, associates, reception and recent clients separately. Look for the behaviour everyone repeats without prompting. That is more valuable than asking leadership for three brand values.

### Source LPS-H5 — Travis Schultz & Partners “Proven Expertise” campaign

Source: Raw Lens Media case study
Canonical page: https://www.rawlensmedia.com/case-studies/travis-schultz-partners/
Source type: cinematic legal brand campaign
Rights state: case_study_reference

Verified campaign structure:

- flagship cinematic brand story;
- people-first values combined with expertise;
- designed for cinema, digital, social and outdoor use;
- created as a flexible campaign asset system rather than one isolated video.

Transferable lesson:

Do not make the lawyer the hero. Make the client’s difficult moment the centre, and show the legal team as calm, present and capable.

Commercial structure:

1. A normal day is interrupted by a serious problem.
2. The person tries to keep functioning while carrying the problem.
3. Small human details show pressure rather than melodrama.
4. The firm enters as a steady presence, not a saviour.
5. The close gives one simple next step.

### Source LPS-H6 — TopDog Law radio campaign and performance style

Source: Washington Post profile of voice actor Terrell “Lucky” John
Canonical page: https://www.washingtonpost.com/style/2026/03/03/topdog-law-ads-voice/
Source type: human creative-practice profile
Rights state: journalistic_reference

Verified creative pattern:

- highly memorable radio performance;
- dramatic delivery, humour, music and mini-stories;
- spontaneous script writing rooted in nightlife and car-dealership advertising;
- polarising but culturally recognisable;
- the firm credited the creative approach with opening attention among audiences ignored by mainstream legal marketing.

Transferable lesson:

Legal advertising does not have to sound solemn to feel serious. Distinctive voice, rhythm and culturally familiar storytelling can make a dry category memorable.

Limitation:

- memorability is not proof of case quality or return on ad spend;
- this style is not transferable to every practice area;
- South African adaptation must fit language, community and professional rules.

## Human commercial and video script structures

### 1. “The call you keep putting off”

Best for:

- family law;
- debt and disputes;
- labour issues;
- estates;
- personal claims.

Structure:

- Person opens the contact page, closes it.
- Repeats this over several ordinary moments.
- The problem remains in the background of home or work life.
- Voiceover: “You do not need the perfect explanation. You just need somewhere to start.”
- Real staff member answers simply and calmly.
- CTA: “Tell us what happened. We’ll help you understand the next step.”

Why it works:

It recognises avoidance and anxiety instead of pretending the client is ready and confident.

### 2. “Legal words translated”

Best for short-form video.

Structure:

- One intimidating legal phrase appears.
- Attorney gives the plain-language meaning in one sentence.
- Then explains why it matters in a real-life situation.
- Ends with one warning about when to get advice.

Rule:

No lecture. One concept per video.

### 3. “What happens after you contact us?”

Best for website and retargeting.

Structure:

- Show the actual first-call or enquiry process.
- Explain who responds.
- Explain what information is needed.
- Explain what happens if the firm cannot help.
- Explain when fees are discussed.

Human benefit:

Reduces the fear of entering an unknown process.

### 4. “The small business problem before it becomes a legal crisis”

Best for commercial, labour, contract and compliance firms.

Structure:

- A founder notices a small unresolved issue.
- The issue appears in several meetings and messages.
- It grows through delay.
- The lawyer is shown helping the owner clarify options early.
- Close: “The best legal problem is often the one dealt with before it becomes urgent.”

### 5. “One question people are embarrassed to ask”

Best for family, criminal, estates and debt-related services.

Structure:

- Attorney states the question exactly as a client might say it.
- Answers without judgement.
- Gives a clear next step.

This should feel like a private conversation, not a performance.

## Photography direction

Avoid:

- fake boardroom handshakes;
- gavels;
- scales of justice;
- crossed arms in a corridor;
- generic skyline shots;
- everyone staring sternly into camera.

Shoot instead:

- a receptionist answering calmly;
- an attorney explaining something across a table;
- real working notes and preparation without exposing data;
- client-perspective framing entering the office;
- video-call consultations;
- quiet detail moments before a meeting;
- team collaboration around a matter;
- local context around court, office and community;
- approachable portraits in actual working spaces;
- different generations and roles in the firm.

## Paid advertising strategy

### Search campaigns

Segment by exact problem and practice area.

Do not send all legal searches to the homepage.

Campaign groups:

- urgent/high-intent matters;
- family and personal matters;
- labour disputes;
- business and contract matters;
- property and conveyancing;
- wills and estates;
- claims and litigation;
- branded search;
- location-specific search.

Negative-keyword themes:

- jobs;
- salary;
- courses;
- degree;
- internship;
- free templates;
- university;
- “become a lawyer”;
- unrelated legal definitions;
- practice areas the firm does not serve.

### Meta and social ads

Best use cases:

- educational problem recognition;
- retargeting website visitors;
- promoting seminars and business workshops;
- estate-planning campaigns;
- employer/employee educational content;
- property and conveyancing education;
- local brand trust;
- staff-led video.

Avoid cold ads that make dramatic promises about winning cases.

### Lead quality fields

- matter type;
- location;
- urgency;
- key date or deadline;
- opposing party conflict check where lawful;
- whether documents exist;
- whether another practitioner is already involved;
- preferred contact method;
- language preference;
- how they found the firm.

Do not collect detailed confidential facts in a public ad form when a secure follow-up is more appropriate.

## Reputation and review strategy

Reviews should help prospective clients understand the experience, not just produce a five-star number.

Useful review prompts:

- Was the process explained clearly?
- Did the team respond when expected?
- Did you know who was handling your matter?
- Did you feel treated with respect?
- Were fees and next steps explained?

Do not ask clients to describe confidential facts publicly.

Do not pressure clients to review during a vulnerable moment.

## Anti-sludge standard

Reject:

- “Navigating complexity with confidence.”
- “Your trusted legal partner.”
- “Where expertise meets excellence.”
- “Passion meets purpose.”
- “Tailored solutions for your unique needs.”
- “Committed to delivering exceptional outcomes.”
- unexplained claims of being leading, premier or best.

Replace with:

- the real problem;
- the real fear;
- the first useful answer;
- the real process;
- the real person;
- the real next step.

## Review-state Skill Cards

### LEGAL-HUMAN-CONTENT-BRIEF

Trigger: Any legal social, video or campaign brief.

1. Name the exact client problem.
2. Capture the client’s real words.
3. Identify the emotion without exploiting it.
4. Choose one useful answer.
5. Show one human proof point.
6. Remove all generic legal phrases.
7. Verify professional and confidentiality boundaries.
8. End with one realistic next step.

### LEGAL-REFERRAL-TO-WEBSITE-CONVERSION

Trigger: Referral-heavy law firm.

1. Identify top referral sources.
2. Identify what referred prospects search before contacting.
3. Audit website proof, people, process, reviews and contact speed.
4. Build practice-area pages for referral validation.
5. Track referral source and final matter quality.
6. Improve the weak point between recommendation and enquiry.

### LEGAL-SEARCH-INTENT-CAMPAIGN

Trigger: Google Ads or SEO campaign.

1. Separate practice areas and problem language.
2. Map high-intent and informational searches.
3. Build exact landing pages.
4. Add negative keywords.
5. Track qualified consultation, not only form submission.
6. Review search terms weekly.
7. Feed client-language learning back into content.

### LEGAL-TRUST-VIDEO-SYSTEM

Trigger: Brand film or recurring short-form plan.

1. Choose a real client moment.
2. Show the human stakes.
3. Show the team’s behaviour, not self-praise.
4. Explain the process simply.
5. Capture modular cuts for social, website and ads.
6. Verify confidentiality and claims.
7. Measure enquiries, watch quality and assisted conversion.
