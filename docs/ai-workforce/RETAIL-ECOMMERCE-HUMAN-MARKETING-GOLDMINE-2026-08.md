Last updated: 2026-08-06

## Purpose

This pack helps CG Production House market retail and ecommerce clients using real customer behaviour, proven campaign structures and human product storytelling. It complements the platform-focused `RETAIL-ECOMMERCE-MARKETING-SOURCE-PACK-2026-08.md`.

The goal is not to produce generic catalogue posts. The goal is to help people notice, understand, trust, choose and buy the right product.

## Core buyer truths

Retail customers usually ask:

- Is this product right for me?
- Is the price fair?
- Can I trust the seller and the product?
- Is it available now?
- What will delivery cost and how long will it take?
- What happens if it does not fit, work or meet expectations?
- Is this offer real or manufactured urgency?
- Can someone show me how it works?

Strong retail marketing reduces uncertainty. It shows the product clearly, proves the benefit, explains the offer honestly and removes buying friction.

## South African and evidence-led campaign references

### R1 — TFG Precision Marketing

Source: WARC / MMA Smarties South Africa
URL: https://www.warc.com/en/article/tfg%3A-precision-marketing-13ff57daa8044107b5b263931a59d19d

Reported result:

- 98% increase in voucher redemptions;
- 30% uplift in sales;
- R1.8 billion in net sales, as reported in the case study.

Why it matters:

TFG used customer data to build 78 distinct segments rather than sending one generic promotion to everyone. The transferable lesson is not “use AI”. It is: understand different customers, product interests, timing and purchase signals, then make the offer relevant.

Evidence limitation:

These are case-study-reported results. The exact attribution method and incrementality should be reviewed before treating all reported net sales as caused solely by the campaign.

### R2 — Makro full-funnel video

Source: Think with Google
URL: https://business.google.com/en-all/think/search-and-video/makro-full-funnel-marketing-lead-generation/

Why it matters:

Makro used one creator-led video concept to support both product awareness and conversion. The creative did not separate “brand” and “sales” into unrelated campaigns. It demonstrated a real product in an entertaining format and connected attention to a shoppable outcome.

Transferable lesson:

- use a credible human to demonstrate the product;
- make the product central to the story;
- build creative variants for awareness, consideration and purchase;
- connect video exposure to product-page and sales measurement.

### R3 — Geewiz dynamic shopping-feed campaign

Source: 365 Digital
URL: https://www.365digital.africa/geewiz-criteo-ecommerce-roas-case-study

Reported result:

- R713,600 revenue from R28,650 media spend;
- 312 sales in May 2024;
- 2,491% reported ROAS.

Why it matters:

The campaign used dynamic product-feed advertising to match users with products they had shown interest in. The result shows the value of accurate product data, relevant retargeting and strong catalogue structure.

Evidence limitation:

This is an agency-reported case. Revenue attribution, margin and incrementality are not independently verified.

### R4 — Block & Chisel product-feed and signal refinement

Source: Tomorrow Labs
URL: https://tomorrowlabs.co.za/case-study/block-chisel/

Reported result:

- Google Ads spend reportedly decreased by 79.55%;
- ROAS increased from 18.28 to 38.64;
- monthly attributed revenue increased from R193,687.05 to R690,574.61.

Why it matters:

Better product-feed inputs and clearer campaign signals improved performance. Retail advertising is often limited by inaccurate titles, weak images, poor categorisation, missing identifiers and bad conversion data before it is limited by creative volume.

Evidence limitation:

Agency-reported results. Gross revenue is not profit and the comparison period must be checked before wider use.

### R5 — Friedman & Cohen ecommerce growth

Source: Cheymaxim
URL: https://cheymaxim.co.za/projects/friedman-cohen

Reported result:

- 90% increase in ROAS;
- 43% reduction in cost per conversion over the reported comparison period.

Why it matters:

The work combined ecommerce UX, technical SEO and paid media. It supports a key CG rule: do not treat ads as separate from product-page and checkout quality.

Evidence limitation:

Agency-reported; the underlying revenue, margin and attribution model are not publicly detailed.

### R6 — Coca-Cola Beatcan

Source: WARC
URL: https://www.warc.com/en/campaigns/coca-cola%3A-beatcan-612b49db75fc4136b9b49178bb3d5a5c

Reported result:

- 17% increase in sales;
- 77% increase in Brand Love, as reported by WARC.

Why it matters:

The packaging became an interactive cultural object. Product, music, participation, social content and in-store presence worked together. The lesson for retail is that packaging and physical products can become content platforms rather than passive objects.

### R7 — SPAR A Christmas #LikeNoOther

Source: WARC
URL: https://www.warc.com/en/article/spar%3A-a-christmas-%23likenoother-afd989ea97e74619a0ca663fe779d0c4

Reported result:

- R2 million in groceries donated;
- 28.9 million social impressions;
- 209 million reported total social reach;
- campaign trended four times on X.

Why it matters:

The campaign connected retail activity to a real community action. This worked because the social contribution was specific and visible, not a vague purpose statement.

Evidence limitation:

Reach and engagement do not prove sales impact. Use this case for brand participation and community storytelling, not as direct-response proof.

### R8 — Takealot personal shoppers

Source: Reuters
URL: https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/south-africas-takealot-bets-townships-fend-off-global-rivals-2024-12-16/

Verified operational marketing insight:

Takealot recruited personal shoppers to help customers in townships and rural areas who faced technology, trust or access barriers. This shows that ecommerce growth in South Africa is not only a website problem. Human assistance, local trust, payment support and logistics can be part of the customer-acquisition model.

### R9 — Checkers Sixty60 and on-demand category expansion

Source: Reuters
URL: https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/shoprites-checkers-extends-on-demand-delivery-general-merchandise-2024-05-17/

Checkers expanded the Sixty60 convenience promise beyond groceries into general merchandise. The marketing lesson is that a strong service proposition can stretch into adjacent categories when the customer already trusts the speed and fulfilment experience.

## Human content system

### 1. Product demonstration

Show the product doing the job.

Structure:

1. recognisable customer problem;
2. product introduced naturally;
3. real demonstration;
4. one or two meaningful benefits;
5. limitation or correct-use note where relevant;
6. price, availability or next action.

### 2. Selection help

Useful formats:

- which size is right;
- which model is right;
- three products for three budgets;
- beginner versus advanced option;
- what changes between variants;
- who should not buy the expensive version.

### 3. Objection handling

Answer real customer questions:

- Is it worth the price?
- How long will it last?
- Does it fit true to size?
- Is it difficult to use?
- What is included?
- Does it work for my situation?
- Can I return it?
- Why is this cheaper or more expensive?

### 4. Customer-language content

Gather language from:

- reviews;
- customer-service questions;
- search queries;
- product comments;
- store staff;
- sales calls;
- return reasons;
- abandoned-cart feedback;
- interviews with repeat customers.

Do not rewrite every phrase into polished corporate English. Keep the meaning and emotional texture of how customers speak.

### 5. Staff and founder expertise

Use real people to explain:

- how to choose;
- what customers commonly get wrong;
- what is genuinely worth spending more on;
- product care;
- quality differences;
- how the product is sourced, made or selected.

### 6. Product-in-life storytelling

Show the product in the moment where it matters:

- school morning;
- payday shop;
- wedding gift;
- first flat;
- weekend braai;
- gym bag;
- home-office setup;
- baby arrival;
- road trip;
- winter preparation;
- last-minute replacement.

## Commercial and short-form script structures

### Script A — Problem, demonstration, proof

- Hook: the exact customer frustration.
- Show: demonstrate the product solving it.
- Proof: specification, comparison, customer quote or visible result.
- Action: shop, visit, compare or ask for help.

### Script B — Honest product comparison

- “These look similar, but they are not for the same person.”
- Explain the meaningful difference.
- Match each option to a use case.
- State who should buy which one.

### Script C — Staff pick

- Introduce a real staff member.
- Name the product they repeatedly recommend.
- Explain the customer problem it suits.
- Demonstrate one feature.
- Mention one limitation to increase trust.

### Script D — Promotion without shouting

- State the real occasion or need.
- Show the product or bundle.
- Explain the saving clearly.
- State dates, eligibility and exclusions.
- Show how to redeem.

### Script E — Customer story

- What were they trying to solve or buy for?
- What worried them?
- Why did they choose this product?
- What happened after purchase?
- Keep the result specific and avoid implying universality.

## Paid media directions

### Google Search and Shopping

Build around:

- exact product and category intent;
- brand plus product;
- use case;
- problem-to-product searches;
- comparison searches;
- local availability;
- price and promotion searches;
- high-intent seasonal terms.

Requirements:

- accurate product feed;
- current stock and pricing;
- strong product titles and images;
- relevant landing pages;
- purchase and refund tracking;
- margin awareness where available;
- separate brand and non-brand reporting.

### Meta and TikTok

Strong uses:

- demonstrations;
- creator trials;
- staff recommendations;
- customer stories;
- new-product discovery;
- bundles;
- retargeting;
- seasonal inspiration;
- store and event activation.

Do not depend on generic product-on-white-background posts as the main creative system.

### YouTube

Use for:

- longer comparisons;
- buying guides;
- category education;
- product setup;
- creator reviews;
- seasonal planning;
- retargeting audiences.

## SEO and product-discovery themes

- product category + location;
- product + use case;
- product + size or specification;
- product comparison;
- best product for a specific need;
- product care and maintenance;
- compatibility questions;
- gift ideas by person, occasion and budget;
- local stock availability;
- delivery and returns;
- troubleshooting;
- product review and demonstration.

Product pages should include original, useful information rather than copied manufacturer descriptions only.

## Merchandising and website conversion

Check:

- first image quality;
- product title clarity;
- price and sale-price accuracy;
- availability;
- variant selection;
- delivery estimate;
- return terms;
- dimensions and specifications;
- use-case explanation;
- reviews;
- comparison options;
- mobile usability;
- checkout steps;
- payment methods;
- trust and contact information.

A creative campaign should not launch when price, stock, delivery or checkout information is unreliable.

## Promotions and giveaways

Useful promotional mechanics:

- product bundles built around a real task or occasion;
- buy-and-get offers with clear eligibility;
- loyalty rewards;
- first-purchase incentives;
- replenishment reminders;
- event-only offers;
- trade-in or upgrade events where operationally supported;
- useful samples;
- demonstration days;
- limited seasonal ranges with real stock evidence.

Useful giveaways:

- a complete solution or experience, not an unrelated expensive prize;
- customer-selected product bundle;
- store credit tied to the category;
- consultation or personal shopping experience;
- creator or expert workshop;
- product trial kit;
- community shopping support with transparent rules.

Avoid engagement bait that attracts people who will never buy from or care about the brand.

## Physical and omnichannel activations

- product testing stations;
- styling or fitting days;
- expert demonstration days;
- repair and care clinics;
- live comparison stations;
- creator shopping challenges;
- community pop-ups;
- seasonal preview evenings;
- click-and-collect content;
- QR-led product education;
- customer-voted product selections;
- local delivery demonstrations.

Every activation should create reusable photos, video, customer questions and product insights.

## Retention and repeat purchase

Build from actual purchase cycles:

- replenishment reminders;
- care instructions;
- accessories and compatible products;
- next-stage products;
- loyalty rewards;
- early access;
- useful post-purchase education;
- review requests after enough use time;
- reactivation based on category and last purchase, not indiscriminate discounting.

## Anti-sludge rules

Reject or rewrite:

- “shop the latest trends” with no specific point of view;
- “quality meets affordability”;
- “something for everyone”;
- “upgrade your lifestyle”;
- “retail therapy awaits”;
- “unbeatable prices” without evidence;
- “must-have” for ordinary products;
- fake countdowns and false scarcity;
- generic lifestyle imagery that hides the product;
- invented reviews;
- “best seller” without current sales evidence;
- “selling fast” without live stock evidence.

Prefer specific human lines such as:

- “This is the one we recommend when you need…”
- “The cheaper version is enough for…”
- “Before you buy, check this measurement.”
- “Here is what changes between the two.”
- “This bundle covers the full job, so you do not discover the missing part halfway through.”

## Measurement model

Report separately:

- reach and video attention;
- product-page sessions;
- search demand;
- product views;
- add-to-cart;
- checkout starts;
- purchases;
- tracked revenue;
- refunds and cancellations;
- new versus returning customers where reliable;
- average order value;
- category and product performance;
- cost per purchase;
- contribution margin where available;
- ROAS with attribution limitations;
- store visits or voucher redemptions where verified;
- repeat-purchase rate;
- review volume and themes;
- stock and fulfilment limitations.

Do not call clicks, carts or checkout starts sales.

- carts;

- next test;
- no unsupported causal claim.
