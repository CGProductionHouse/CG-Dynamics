## Purpose

Expand the building-materials and home-improvement marketing library with practical, human-centred intelligence for:

- solar and backup-energy solutions;

- insulation and thermal comfort;

This pack exists to help CG Production House create better campaigns, ads, websites, SEO, content, activations and lead-generation systems for clients in these categories.

It does not exist to produce generic environmental messaging.

## Core human truth

Most people do not buy “sustainability” as an abstract ideal.

They buy outcomes such as:

- lower electricity bills;
- fewer outages and less disruption;
- a cooler or warmer home;
- lower water use;
- less maintenance;
- greater comfort;
- better resale value;
- fewer repairs;
- healthier indoor air;
- pride in making a practical long-term decision;
- a building that keeps working when conditions get tougher.

Marketing should lead with the lived benefit, then support it with verified environmental value.

Avoid vague language such as:

- “building a greener tomorrow”;
- “sustainability meets innovation”;
- “eco-conscious living redefined”;
- “powering the future”;
- “better for the planet” without proof.

## South African market evidence

### Green Building Council South Africa

The GBCSA’s 2025 cost-and-trends report is based on empirical costing data from 199 Green Star certified office buildings and is positioned as a business-case resource for green building in South Africa.

Marketing lesson:

- do not assume customers will accept a green premium without explanation;
- show actual cost, payback, comfort, resilience and long-term value;
- use credible third-party certification and project evidence rather than decorative green claims.

The GBCSA also frames green buildings around energy, water, materials, waste, indoor environmental quality, transport and socio-economic benefit.

Practical content ideas:

- “What makes a building genuinely green?” series;
- myth-versus-fact videos about cost;
- project case studies that show the design decision, cost implication and lived outcome;
- before-and-after energy or water use where verified;
- architect, contractor and owner interviews about what changed in practice.

### Green-certified property performance

The 2025 MSCI Green Annual Property Index, reported by GBCSA in June 2026, found that green-certified Prime- and A-grade offices outperformed comparable non-certified offices over a ten-year period.

Marketing lesson:

- sustainability can be positioned as asset quality and long-term value, not only environmental virtue;
- this is strongest for commercial property, developers, landlords and investors;
- never generalise office-index results to every home or project.

## Proven marketing case studies

### Wetility — TikTok solar subscription campaign

Reported results:

- 17,102 qualified leads;
- 98.9 million impressions;
- 616,000 clicks;
- R175.42 cost per lead;
- 115,000 followers gained.

Human insight:

A complicated solar-subscription offer became understandable when explained in everyday language with familiar local visuals and UGC-style creative.

Transferable lesson:

- explain the offer before selling the technology;
- make savings and convenience clear in the first seconds;
- use real homes and recognisable South African situations;
- test multiple human presenters and hooks;
- separate education content from conversion content, but let both support one another.

Example short-form structure:

1. “Your electricity bill is still climbing, but buying a full solar system is not your only option.”
2. Show the current frustration.
3. Explain the subscription simply.
4. State what is included and what is not.
5. Show a local installation and real homeowner.
6. CTA: calculate or check eligibility.

### oneSolar — higher-quality Google Ads leads

Reported results:

- 2,000% increase in qualified leads;
- 53.6% improvement in marketing-qualified-lead conversion rate;
- 41% reduction in cost per click.

Human insight:

As load-shedding urgency changed, customer motivation shifted toward savings, independence and long-term energy planning.

Transferable lesson:

- update messaging when the market’s emotional trigger changes;
- do not keep advertising the crisis after customer motivation has moved on;
- segment residential, commercial and agricultural intent;
- qualify by location, property type, current bill, roof/site conditions and decision stage;
- measure qualified opportunities, not raw form fills.

### GoSolr — neighbourhood visibility campaign

Campaign format:

- branded delivery bikes in selected neighbourhoods;
- high-visibility outdoor creative;
- flyers delivered directly into homes.

Transferable lesson:

- pair digital targeting with physical presence in the same suburb;
- use QR codes or unique landing pages by area;
- retarget people in those neighbourhoods online;
- make the brand feel visible and established before asking for a high-value enquiry.

### Jewson — Making Better Homes Awards

Campaign purpose:

- raise awareness of products that make homes more sustainable, safe, comfortable and energy efficient;
- celebrate tradespeople already doing the work well.

Human insight:

Trade audiences respond better when sustainability is attached to recognition, workmanship and pride rather than a lecture.

Transferable lesson:

- celebrate contractors, installers and customers;
- create awards, project spotlights and local competitions;
- let real work become the marketing asset;
- build a content pipeline from entries, judging, site visits and winner stories.

## Audience motivations

### Homeowners

Primary motivations:

- reduce monthly costs;
- keep essential systems running;
- improve comfort;
- avoid buying the wrong system;
- increase property appeal;
- prevent future retrofit expense;
- understand what is genuinely worth paying for.

Content that connects:

- “What will this save me?”
- “What still works during an outage?”
- “What size system do I actually need?”
- “What makes one insulation product different from another?”
- “What does a bad installation look like?”
- “What should I ask before signing?”

### Contractors and installers

Primary motivations:

- reliable product availability;
- fewer callbacks;
- easy installation;
- technical support;
- reputation;
- proof that the product performs;
- repeat business;
- margins and project efficiency.

Content that connects:

- field demonstrations;
- installer tips;
- side-by-side product tests;
- failure analysis;
- project time saved;
- contractor interviews;
- honest lessons from difficult installations.

### Architects and specifiers

Primary motivations:

- credible technical data;
- compliant systems;
- performance evidence;
- design integration;
- material quality;
- long-term building value;
- reduced operational burden.

Content that connects:

- specification guides;
- CPD-style educational events;
- project case studies;
- material detail videos;
- performance comparisons;
- post-occupancy outcomes;
- interviews with design and technical professionals.

### Developers and commercial property owners

Primary motivations:

- asset performance;
- tenant demand;
- operating cost;

- long-term return.

Content that connects:

- verified project numbers;
- certification journeys;
- tenant and owner interviews;
- retrofit case studies;
- investment and lifecycle-cost explanations;
- measured energy, water and occupancy outcomes.

## Messaging angles that feel human

### Better than abstract “green” messaging

- “A home that stays comfortable without fighting the weather all day.”
- “Spend less on power, not more time thinking about power.”
- “The system that was sized for your house, not copied from someone else’s quote.”
- “Built to reduce callbacks, not create them.”
- “Lower running costs without making the building feel compromised.”
- “Use less water without turning daily life into a sacrifice.”
- “Materials that last longer, so the project does not have to be done twice.”

### Proof requirements

Any claim involving savings, efficiency, carbon, water reduction, durability, payback or performance must identify:

- exact product or system;
- test or measurement basis;
- property/project conditions;
- comparison baseline;
- time period;
- exclusions;
- source;
- reviewer.

### Longer video and commercial ideas

#### The bill-on-the-fridge story

Opening:

A homeowner stands at the fridge looking at another electricity bill.

Human tension:

They are tired of reacting every month but do not trust exaggerated solar promises.

Story:

A consultant arrives, asks practical questions, checks the property and explains the options plainly.

Proof:

Show system design, installation and real monitoring.

Closing:

“Not the biggest system. The right system.”

#### The room nobody uses

Opening:

A family avoids one room because it is unbearable in summer and freezing in winter.

Story:

The solution is not another appliance but better insulation, shading or glazing.

Proof:

Show surface temperature, comfort and use before and after.

Closing:

“Comfort should be built in.”

#### The contractor callback

Opening:

A contractor’s phone rings again about the same failed material.

Story:

He changes to a better-supported system and explains what reliability means to his reputation.

Closing:

“Your material carries your name long after you leave site.”

## Greenwashing guardrails

Never use:

- “100% eco-friendly”;
- “zero impact”;
- “carbon neutral” without scope and evidence;
- “sustainable” as an unsupported adjective;
- “saves up to” without a defensible basis;
- “pays for itself” without assumptions and timeframe;
- “off grid” where the system is not genuinely designed for it;
- “maintenance free” unless literally supported.

Every sustainability claim must be specific enough to test.

## Review-state skill cards

### SUSTAINABILITY-HUMAN-BENEFIT-BRIEF

Trigger: Any green-building, solar, energy or water campaign.

Steps:

1. Identify the real human problem.
2. Define the practical benefit.
3. Identify the proof available.
4. State assumptions and limitations.
5. Choose the most relatable human story.
6. Build the CTA around a useful next step.
7. Remove all vague green language.

### GREEN-CLAIM-PROOF-CHECK

Trigger: Any efficiency, saving, carbon, water or durability claim.

Steps:

1. Capture the exact claim.
2. Identify product/system scope.
3. Record baseline and comparison.
4. Record source and date.
5. State conditions and exclusions.
6. Reject or revise unsupported language.
7. Store reviewer and expiry date.

### SOLAR-LEAD-QUALITY-CHECK

Capture:

- location;
- property type;
- ownership or decision authority;
- monthly electricity use or bill range;
- roof/site suitability where known;
- backup versus saving objective;
- budget or finance preference;
- decision timeframe;
- residential, commercial or agricultural use;
- whether another quote already exists.

## Final rule

Sustainability marketing must make people feel that the business understands their home, project, comfort, cost and risk.

The environmental benefit matters, but the communication must begin with a life people recognise and a problem they genuinely want solved.
