# AI Workforce — Education and Training Industry Library

Last updated: 2026-07-26
Status: Actual researched industry pack; all production knowledge remains review-gated.
Territory: South Africa.

## Purpose

Create a trustworthy industry layer for:

- private schools and education centres;
- tutoring and supplemental education;
- higher education institutions;
- private colleges;
- occupational and skills-development providers;
- short-course and professional-development providers;
- online learning businesses;
- workplace learning, learnerships and apprenticeships;
- student recruitment and enrolment campaigns.

This pack supports marketing strategy, social content, paid media, websites, lead handling, enrolment reporting and reputation management. It does not decide accreditation, recognition, admission, funding or employment outcomes.

## Industry structure

The AI must separate these journeys:

1. School or learner support
   - parent/learner discovery;
   - curriculum or subject fit;
   - assessment;
   - enrolment;
   - attendance;
   - academic support;
   - progression.

2. Higher education
   - programme research;
   - entry requirements;
   - application;
   - admission decision;
   - registration;
   - study progression;
   - completion;
   - qualification award.

3. Occupational education and training
   - provider and programme verification;
   - workplace/learner eligibility;
   - application;
   - enrolment;
   - theory/practical/workplace components;
   - assessment;
   - certification.

4. Short courses and non-NQF learning
   - learning objective;
   - duration and delivery;
   - attendance;
   - assessment where applicable;
   - certificate of attendance/completion;
   - no automatic qualification status.

5. Employer-funded learning
   - skills need;
   - provider and programme fit;
   - quotation/proposal;
   - learner nomination;
   - enrolment;
   - delivery and attendance;
   - assessment;
   - evidence and reporting.

An enquiry, application, admission, registration, attendance, completion and qualification award are different outcomes.

## Source register

### Source E1 — South African Qualifications Authority

Publisher: SAQA
Canonical sources:

- https://saqa.org.za/Services/Registration-Of-Qualifications/
- https://saqa.org.za/frequently-asked-questions/
- https://saqa.org.za/verification-services/

Source type: statutory NQF authority
Rights treatment: official_reference; qualification records may be referenced with attribution
Freshness: qualification status and registration dates must be checked live

Verified findings:

- SAQA registers qualifications and part-qualifications on the National Qualifications Framework after recommendation through the quality-council system.
- SAQA maintains searchable records for registered, expired and replaced qualifications and unit standards.
- The National Learners’ Records Database contains education and training achievement records supplied to SAQA.
- SAQA verification confirms recorded South African qualifications; it does not verify every short course or foreign qualification through the same service.
- A qualification can have registration, enrolment and achievement end dates.

Marketing implications:

- “SAQA registered” must refer to the exact qualification or part-qualification, not merely the provider.
- Qualification ID, title, NQF level, credits, registration status and end dates should be retained.
- Expired or replaced qualifications must not be marketed as newly available without transition evidence.
- An NQF level alone does not make two qualifications equivalent.

Prohibited overclaims:

- “We are SAQA accredited.”
- “Every certificate we issue is nationally recognised.”
- “NQF level 4 means matric.”
- “SAQA guarantees employment.”

### Source E2 — Department of Higher Education and Training provider registration

Publisher: DHET
Canonical source:

- https://www.dhet.gov.za/

Relevant official registers:

- Register of Private Colleges
- Register of Private Higher Education Institutions
- lists of public universities, TVET colleges and other PSET institutions

Source type: national education department and provider-registration authority
Freshness: current register must be checked

Verified findings:

- Private higher-education institutions and private colleges operate within formal registration frameworks.
- DHET publishes official provider registers and lists.
- Provider registration and programme accreditation are related but distinct facts.

Marketing implications:

- A provider should state the exact registered legal/institutional identity and registration status.
- “Registered college” does not prove every course offered is accredited or NQF registered.
- A trading name must not obscure the registered provider responsible for delivery.

### Source E3 — Council on Higher Education / HEQC programme accreditation

Publisher: Council on Higher Education
Canonical sources:

- https://che.ac.za/index.php/focus-areas/programme-accreditation
- https://che.ac.za/faqs/post-school-education/what-accreditation-and-why-should-programmes-be-accredited

Source type: higher-education quality council
Freshness: programme status and accreditation period must be verified

Verified findings:

- The Higher Education Quality Committee evaluates higher-education programmes against accreditation criteria.
- Only HEQC-accredited programmes may be offered as higher-education programmes by institutions within scope.
- Private providers also require DHET registration.
- Programme accreditation evaluates minimum requirements relating to programme inputs, processes, outputs and impact.
- Institutional audit and programme accreditation are not the same process.

Marketing implications:

- Accreditation must be attached to the exact programme and approved site/mode where applicable.
- Institutional registration cannot be substituted for programme accreditation.
- “Accredited institution” must not imply that every future or proposed programme is already accredited.
- Accreditation can be time-bound or conditional and must retain current status.

### Source E4 — SAQA quality-council separation

Publisher: SAQA
Canonical source:

- https://saqa.org.za/frequently-asked-questions/

Verified findings:

- Umalusi is responsible for quality assurance in general and further education and training within its mandate.
- QCTO is responsible for occupational qualifications and related provider accreditation within its mandate.
- CHE/HEQC is responsible for higher-education programme accreditation.
- Training providers may need both registration and accreditation through the appropriate bodies.

Knowledge rule:

- The AI must first identify the qualification sub-framework and programme type before naming the relevant authority.
- Umalusi, QCTO, CHE, SAQA and DHET roles must not be collapsed into one generic “accreditation body”.

### Source E5 — Umalusi

Publisher: Umalusi
Canonical source family:

- https://www.umalusi.org.za/

Source type: statutory quality council for general and further education and training qualifications within scope
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: verify provider and assessment-body status

Research use:

- Confirm accreditation/quality-assurance status for schools, colleges, assessment bodies or programmes within Umalusi’s mandate.
- Verify exact qualification, provider role and assessment arrangement.

Marketing implications:

- A school, centre, college and assessment body may have different roles.
- “Umalusi accredited” must not be used without exact current evidence.
- Exam preparation or tutoring does not make a business an accredited assessment body.

### Source E6 — Quality Council for Trades and Occupations

Publisher: QCTO
Canonical source:

- https://www.qcto.org.za/

Source type: statutory occupational-qualifications quality council
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: provider, programme and occupational-qualification status must be verified

Research use:

- Occupational qualifications can include knowledge, practical and workplace components.
- Provider accreditation, assessment-centre roles and workplace arrangements may differ.

Marketing implications:

- A learnership, apprenticeship, skills programme, occupational qualification and short course are not interchangeable.
- “Workplace-ready” and employment claims require evidence.
- Completion of training does not automatically prove occupational competence or certification.

### Source E7 — SAQA verification limitations

Publisher: SAQA
Canonical source:

- https://saqa.org.za/verification-services/

Verified findings:

- SAQA verification letters are limited to recorded South African qualifications in scope.
- Short courses, foreign qualifications and professional designations can require different verification routes.

Marketing implications:

- A certificate of attendance should not be described as a verified qualification.
- Foreign-qualification evaluation is not the same as South African qualification verification.
- A professional designation is not automatically an academic qualification.

### Source E8 — Consumer Protection Act

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical source:

- https://www.gov.za/documents/consumer-protection-act

Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal-risk flag

Education-marketing implications:

- Fees, deposits, payment plans, cancellation terms, duration and inclusions should be clear.
- “Guaranteed pass”, “guaranteed job”, “100% placement”, “internationally recognised” and salary claims require strong current evidence.
- Course names, certificate type and qualification status must not mislead.
- Images and testimonials should not imply outcomes that are not representative or verified.

### Source E9 — POPIA and learner information

Publisher: Information Regulator
Canonical source family:

- https://inforegulator.org.za/

Source type: privacy regulator
Rights treatment: official_reference

Marketing and operations implications:

- Applications can contain identity, contact, academic, financial, disability and minor-child information.
- Children’s information and special personal information require heightened controls.
- Student data collected for admission or learning does not create unlimited marketing permission.
- Results, attendance, disciplinary records and support needs must not be exposed in marketing or broad dashboards.

### Source E10 — Qualification records are public reference material

Publisher: SAQA qualification register
Canonical source family:

- https://regqs.saqa.org.za/

Verified finding:

- Registered qualification and part-qualification records are public reference material, with SAQA attribution requirements.

Rights treatment:

- Store qualification metadata and lawful excerpts with attribution.
- Do not present provider-created course notes or textbooks as public-domain material merely because the related qualification is public.

- Completion is not necessarily competence, pass or qualification award.
- A certificate generated by a provider is not automatically an NQF qualification.

## Candidate review-gated Skill Cards

### ESC-01 — Provider and programme status are separate

DHET registration does not replace programme accreditation.

### ESC-02 — SAQA registers qualifications, not providers

“SAQA accredited provider” is generally an unsafe description.

### ESC-03 — Quality council depends on programme type

Umalusi, QCTO and CHE have different mandates.

### ESC-04 — Enrolment funnel stages remain separate

Enquiry, application, admission, registration and completion cannot be merged.

### ESC-05 — Short-course certificates are not automatically qualifications

Certificate of attendance/completion does not prove NQF status.

### ESC-06 — Employment and pass guarantees are high risk

Outcome claims require exact evidence and conditions.

### ESC-07 — Qualification status is time-sensitive

Registration, enrolment and achievement end dates must be retained.

### ESC-08 — Learner data requires strict privacy

Academic, financial, disability and minor information must not leak into marketing.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- No provider or programme called accredited without exact current evidence.
- No SAQA role misrepresented.
- No guaranteed employment, salary, pass or admission outcome.
- No application treated as enrolment.
- No short-course certificate presented as a qualification without proof.
- No learner data promoted into universal knowledge.
- No child/student story used without lawful authority.
- No unreviewed knowledge in production answers.
