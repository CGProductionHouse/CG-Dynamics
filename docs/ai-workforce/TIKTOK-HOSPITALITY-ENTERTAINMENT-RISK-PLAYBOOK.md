# AI Workforce — TikTok Hospitality and Entertainment Risk Playbook

Last updated: 2026-07-26
Status: Actual researched source pack; all production knowledge remains review-gated.
Territory: South Africa, with TikTok global platform rules and local alcohol-marketing context.

## Purpose

Create a practical TikTok compliance and publishing layer for restaurants, bars, pubs, nightlife, sports-viewing venues, entertainment events, staff-led venue content and food-and-beverage brands.

This pack is designed for recurring situations where venue content is removed, muted, restricted from the For You feed, or where an account receives repeated violations or a ban.

It does not diagnose a specific account without the exact TikTok notice, post, caption, sound, account type and appeal history.

## Important distinction

TikTok can take different actions that are often described casually as a "violation":

- remove the post;
- make it ineligible for the For You feed;
- age-restrict it;
- mute or remove its sound;
- restrict a feature;
- issue an account warning or strike;
- ban an account after repeated or severe violations.

The AI and staff must preserve the exact action and the exact policy reason shown in TikTok. A muted video is not automatically a Community Guidelines removal, and poor reach is not proof of a formal violation.

## Official source register

### Source T1 — TikTok Community Guidelines, current framework

Publisher: TikTok
Canonical page: https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines
Source type: official platform policy
Current version observed: released 14 August 2025, effective 13 September 2025
Freshness: high; verify before every major policy review
Rights treatment: official_reference / metadata_and_link_only

Verified findings:

- TikTok applies its Community Guidelines to all users and content.
- Regulated goods and commercial activities are treated separately from ordinary entertainment content.
- Commercial content promoting a product, brand or business must use TikTok's content disclosure setting.
- Repeated violations, a severe single violation or an account dedicated to prohibited activity can result in account-level enforcement.

Safe candidate knowledge:

- A venue account should be treated as commercial content even when a post feels informal or staff-led.
- The correct content-disclosure workflow must be part of publishing QA.
- A prior enforcement history can increase account risk even when later posts look less severe.

### Source T2 — Alcohol and regulated goods

Publisher: TikTok
Canonical policy family: Community Guidelines → Regulated Goods, Services and Commercial Activities
Source type: official platform policy
Freshness: high

Verified findings:

- TikTok does not allow the unauthorised trade or marketing of alcohol and other regulated goods.
- Trade includes sale, purchase, exchange, giveaway, redirection, contact information and directing people to transact on or off platform.
- Adults using excessive amounts of alcohol may be age-restricted and made ineligible for the For You feed.
- Young people may not be shown possessing or using alcohol.
- Paid alcohol advertising may have limited exceptions only with TikTok's explicit permission, age targeting and legal compliance.

High-risk venue patterns:

- direct alcohol sales wording;
- directing viewers to order alcohol through a link, number, DM or physical location without an allowed context;
- giveaways or competitions where alcohol is the prize;
- drinking challenges, downing, chugging, waterfalls or speed-consumption content;
- glorified intoxication;
- pressure to drink;
- staff or customers who may appear underage holding alcohol;
- captions that frame alcohol as the route to confidence, popularity, sexual success or performance;
- repeated account identity centred primarily on alcohol promotion rather than the broader venue experience.

Important limitation:

Showing an adult venue, bottle, glass, bar counter or responsible social setting is not automatically the same as prohibited alcohol trade. The complete video, caption, text overlays, sound, hashtags, account identity, age cues, CTA and prior enforcement history matter.

### Source T3 — For You feed eligibility

Publisher: TikTok
Canonical policy family: For You feed eligibility standards
Source type: official platform policy

Verified findings:

- Some content may remain on TikTok but be ineligible for the For You feed.
- Excessive alcohol consumption by adults is one example of content that can be restricted from recommendation.
- Undisclosed marketing content may be made ineligible.
- Reproduced, visibly watermarked or low-quality content may be ineligible even when it is not removed.

Knowledge rule:

Do not collapse `removed`, `age_restricted`, `FYF_ineligible`, `sound_muted` and `low_distribution` into one status.

### Source T4 — Unoriginal content and intellectual property

Publisher: TikTok
Canonical policy family: Integrity and Authenticity / Unoriginal Content
Source type: official platform policy

Verified findings:

- Content that violates copyright, trademark or other intellectual-property rights is not allowed.
- Reproduced content without meaningful new creative edits, visible third-party watermarks or overlaid third-party logos may be ineligible for the For You feed.
- Low-quality content can also be ineligible.

Hospitality implications:

- Do not upload an Instagram Reel download carrying an Instagram watermark.
- Do not use broadcast sport footage, television screens, music videos or third-party performance footage unless the venue has the necessary rights and the post is permitted.
- A reaction or venue recap does not automatically create rights to the underlying footage or music.
- Staff-led original footage is generally a safer creative base than reposted media.

### Source T5 — Commercial use of music

Publisher: TikTok Support / TikTok for Business
Canonical sources:

- https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/commercial-use-of-music-on-tiktok
- https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/commercial-music-library
- https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-to-use-the-commercial-music-library

Source type: official platform music guidance
Freshness: high

Verified findings:

- TikTok recommends that content promoting a brand, product or service use the Commercial Music Library because those tracks are pre-cleared for commercial use.
- TikTok says licences for music outside the Commercial Music Library generally do not cover commercial use in brand-promoting content.
- Business Accounts are directed to Commercial Sounds.
- Commercial Music Library availability depends on region, placement and track-specific conditions.
- If a business uses original sound or music outside the Commercial Music Library, it must hold the necessary rights and confirm that through the music-usage process where applicable.
- TikTok may later mute or remove content if it loses rights to a Commercial Sound.

Critical correction about the one-minute observation:

Current official sources reviewed do not establish a universal rule that every organic TikTok video over 60 seconds must be muted. A video may be muted because:

- the selected sound is licensed only for a shorter usable duration;
- the sound is not cleared for commercial use;
- the track is unavailable in the account's region or placement;
- the uploaded edit contains copyrighted music embedded outside TikTok;
- TikTok later loses or changes rights to the sound;
- an ad or Promote workflow has a separate maximum duration.

TikTok's current ad-format policy states that certain video ads must be between 5 and 60 seconds. That ad specification must not be presented as a blanket organic-post rule.

Publishing rule:

Before posting a venue video longer than the selected commercial track:

1. Check the track's available duration in the Commercial Music Library.
2. Check region and allowed placement.
3. Do not assume that adding the sound inside TikTok clears embedded copyrighted music already present in the exported file.
4. Export a clean version with dialogue and venue-owned/original ambience where possible.
5. Keep proof of the selected sound, account type and publication settings.

### Source T6 — Promote and paid usage

Publisher: TikTok for Business
Canonical source: https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-tiktok-advertising-policies-for-promote
Source type: official advertising guidance

Verified findings:

- Promote content must comply with Community Guidelines and advertising policies.
- Promote requires original sound or sound cleared for commercial purposes.
- A post that is acceptable organically is not automatically eligible for Promote.
- Alcohol-related promotion can face stricter advertising restrictions than ordinary organic venue storytelling.

### Source T7 — Current ad technical duration

Publisher: TikTok for Business
Canonical source: https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-ad-format-and-functionality
Source type: official advertising specification

Verified finding:

- For the specified ad formats and territories, including South Africa, ad video duration is listed as a minimum of 5 seconds and maximum of 60 seconds.

Limitation:

This is an advertising-format rule. It must not be converted into a universal rule for all organic posts.

## Why an apparently alcohol-free video may still be removed

Possible evidence-backed categories to investigate:

1. Commercial disclosure
   - The post promotes a venue but the disclosure setting was not used.

2. Music or copyright
   - General-library music used on a business post;
   - copyrighted music embedded in the export;
   - television, match or event footage audible or visible;
   - visible third-party watermark.

3. Regulated-goods context
   - the caption, CTA, profile, hashtags or previous account activity frames the post as alcohol trade even when no drink appears in the specific clip.

4. Dangerous activity
   - drinking games, falls, rough physical activity, unsafe stunts or challenges can trigger separate safety rules.

5. Harassment, humiliation or sexualised content
   - staff banter can be interpreted differently without local context.

6. Minors or age ambiguity
   - people who look young in a nightlife/alcohol environment increase risk.

7. Spam or deceptive behaviour
   - repeated reposting after removal, identical uploads, engagement bait or attempts to evade enforcement.

8. Unoriginal or low-quality content
   - recycled Reels, third-party footage, visible watermark or poor-quality exports.

9. Automated classification error
   - legitimate content may be misclassified. The exact notice and appeal outcome must be retained.

The AI must never claim the cause without the TikTok notice and the full post package.

Every TikTok enforcement incident should retain:

- client_id;
- TikTok account handle and account type;
- post ID and original file hash;
- posted timestamp;
- video duration;
- caption, hashtags and on-screen text;
- sound title, sound ID and source;
- Commercial Music Library status;
- sound usable duration;
- region and placement;
- content-disclosure setting;
- alcohol visible yes/no/uncertain;
- alcohol consumption yes/no/uncertain;
- apparent minor present yes/no/uncertain;
- CTA and destination;
- third-party footage or watermark;
- exact TikTok notice wording;
- enforcement type;
- policy category;
- appeal submitted and timestamp;
- appeal evidence;
- appeal result;
- repost attempted yes/no;

- final learning status.

Never store only `community guidelines violation` as the reason.

## Pre-publication TikTok QA for venues

### Account and disclosure

- Confirm Personal, Creator or Business Account type.
- Confirm whether the post promotes the venue, event, product or offer.
- Use the required commercial-content disclosure.

### Alcohol

- No minors or age-ambiguous people consuming or holding alcohol.
- No drinking challenge, speed drinking or intoxication glorification.
- No alcohol giveaway or direct trade CTA without exact policy approval.
- Keep venue experience broader than alcohol-only promotion.
- Use clear adult/responsible context where alcohol is material.

### Music

- Use a Commercial Music Library track for commercial content unless independently licensed.
- Confirm the region, placement and available sound duration.
- Remove copyrighted background music captured on location where rights are unclear.
- Keep a clean master with original dialogue/ambience.

### Video and rights

- Original footage preferred.
- No Instagram watermark.
- No unauthorised broadcast sport, television, music video or performer footage.
- Confirm consent for identifiable customers and staff where required.

### Creative and caption

- Make the entertainment story clear in the first seconds.
- Avoid ambiguous captions that resemble direct alcohol sale or pressure.
- Avoid repeated identical uploads after enforcement.
- Do not use unrelated hashtags to manipulate discovery.

## Safer hospitality content directions

Lower-risk themes when executed honestly:

- food preparation and service craft;
- staff personalities and workplace humour that does not humiliate;
- venue atmosphere without excessive drinking;
- live-event setup and behind-the-scenes work;
- customer experience with permission;
- menu education;
- community and sports atmosphere without unauthorised match footage;
- non-alcoholic options;
- responsible adult social occasions;
- venue challenges that do not pressure alcohol consumption or create dangerous acts.

Higher-risk themes requiring exact review:

- shots, downing or chugging;
- drinking games;
- alcohol-focused competitions;
- extreme intoxication humour;
- alcohol price or purchase CTAs;
- young-looking participants;
- broadcast rugby or music footage;
- sexually suggestive nightlife content;
- aggressive pranks;
- reposting a removed post with small edits.

## Content length guidance

There is no universal evidence in the official sources reviewed that organic venue videos must stay below one minute to avoid muting.

Use duration according to the creative job:

- short discovery clip: one clear idea;
- staff personality video: enough setup for the joke but no dead introduction;
- event recap: fast sequence with original/licensed sound;
- explainer or story: longer only when the narrative earns it;
- paid ad: follow the exact current ad-format duration, which may cap at 60 seconds.

The AI should recommend a duration range as a creative hypothesis, not as a permanent algorithm rule.

## Account-ban response protocol

1. Preserve every notice, email, screenshot and post file.
2. Do not mass-delete evidence before review.
3. Do not repeatedly create replacement accounts to evade enforcement.
4. Map each incident to an exact policy category.
5. Appeal factual misclassification with concise evidence.
6. Remove or revise genuinely non-compliant patterns.
7. Audit account type, disclosure, music rights, alcohol CTAs and prior reposts.
8. Maintain a clean publishing period focused on clearly compliant original content.
9. Record appeal outcomes and do not assume silence means approval.

## Candidate review-gated Skill Cards

### TIK-HOSP-01 — Preserve exact enforcement state

Removal, FYF ineligibility, age restriction, sound muting and account action are different events and require different remedies.

### TIK-HOSP-02 — Venue content is commercial

Informal staff-led content can still promote the business and should follow commercial disclosure and music rules.

### TIK-HOSP-03 — Alcohol trade is broader than showing a drink

Captions, CTAs, contact details, giveaways and off-platform redirection may create regulated-goods risk even when the clip itself is not alcohol-focused.

### TIK-HOSP-04 — Excessive drinking is recommendation risk

Adult excessive alcohol consumption can be age-restricted and excluded from the For You feed; minors may not be shown using or possessing alcohol.

### TIK-HOSP-05 — Music clearance is account- and use-specific

A sound visible in TikTok's general library is not automatically licensed for a business's commercial post.

### TIK-HOSP-06 — One minute is not a universal organic mute rule

Check sound duration, music rights, account type, placement and whether the workflow is organic, Promote or Ads before attributing a mute to video length.

### TIK-HOSP-07 — Original venue footage reduces several risks

Original footage without third-party watermarks, embedded copyrighted music or broadcast material is a safer publishing base.

### TIK-HOSP-08 — Reposting after removal can worsen enforcement

Repeated violations or attempts to circumvent enforcement can contribute to account-level action.

## Exact-client deep audit later

When a specific venue audit is authorised, collect:

- profile handle and account type;
- account status page;
- every violation notice;
- removed/muted post files;
- captions and hashtags;
- sound IDs and source;
- appeal history;
- dates of warnings and bans;
- examples of posts that remained live;
- examples of posts removed without visible alcohol.

Then compare compliant versus enforced posts without assuming the visible alcohol is the only variable.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- No unsupported claim about why TikTok removed a specific post.
- No universal algorithm hack.
- No blanket claim that videos over 60 seconds are muted.
- No evasion of account enforcement.
- No alcohol marketing directed at minors.
- No drinking challenge recommendation.
- No copyrighted music or broadcast footage treated as cleared merely because it appeared in the app.
- No normal agent use before human review.
