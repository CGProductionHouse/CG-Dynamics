# AI Workforce — Social-Native and Short-Form Storytelling Source Pack

Last updated: 2026-07-26
Status: Research complete for review-gated use.
Purpose: Give CG staff and AI agents practical, source-backed guidance for TikTok, Instagram Reels, Facebook Reels, YouTube Shorts and LinkedIn video.

## Core operating principle

Short-form is not simply a long video cut shorter.

A strong short-form piece should align:

```text
Audience tension
→ immediate reason to continue
→ one clear idea
→ visible proof or payoff
→ recognisable brand cue
→ native interaction or next step
```

The purpose is not to chase views in isolation. The purpose is to create attention that can be attributed to the correct brand, communicate something useful or memorable, and move the viewer toward a relevant next action.

## Source hierarchy

1. Current official platform documentation and business guidance.
2. Platform analytics definitions.
3. Controlled or disclosed-method research.
4. Peer-reviewed attention, memory and persuasion research already stored in the Advertising Evidence Library.
5. Verified CG client performance observations, kept client-specific.

No platform claim should be treated as permanent. Each platform rule or metric requires a review date.

# 1. Platform source findings

## 1.1 YouTube Shorts format and duration

Official source:
- YouTube Help — Upload YouTube Shorts.
- YouTube Help — Understand three-minute YouTube Shorts.
- https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12779649
- https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877

Current finding:
- Square or vertical uploads of up to three minutes can be categorised as Shorts.
- Videos uploaded after 15 October 2024 are subject to the current three-minute Shorts classification rules.
- A Short over one minute with an active Content ID claim can be blocked globally.
- Music from the Shorts Audio Library may have track-specific usage limits such as 30, 60 or 90 seconds.

Safe operational rule:

```text
For Shorts longer than one minute:
- use original audio;
- use music with exact rights evidence;
- or use YouTube Audio Library tracks that do not create a Content ID claim.
```

Limitation:
- YouTube may change classification, music or monetisation rules.
- A permitted duration does not mean a longer Short will perform better.

## 1.2 YouTube Shorts views and engaged views

Official source:
- YouTube Help — Get started creating YouTube Shorts.
- https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070

Current finding:
- Since 31 March 2025, Shorts views count starts and replays without a minimum watch-time threshold.
- The earlier metric remains available as Engaged views, showing viewers who chose to continue watching.

Reporting rule:
- Do not compare post-March-2025 Shorts views directly with older Shorts views without a methodology note.
- Do not treat a view as evidence that the message was understood.
- Prefer Engaged views, average percentage viewed, retention shape, comments, shares, profile actions and downstream business outcomes when evaluating quality.

## 1.3 YouTube recommendation and discovery

Official source:
- YouTube Help — Search and discovery tips for Shorts.
- https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225

Current finding:
- YouTube says it does not favour one particular Shorts format.
- Discovery depends on viewer personalisation, performance, topic interest, competition and seasonality.
- No minimum posting cadence is required for a Short to perform.

Safe claim:
- Consistent quality and audience relevance matter.

Blocked claim:
- “Posting daily guarantees reach.”

## 1.4 YouTube originality

Official source:
- YouTube Help — Tips on how ads work for Shorts.
- https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12929256

Current finding:
- Unedited film or TV clips, reuploads of other creators and compilations without original contribution may be ineligible for Shorts revenue sharing.

CG rule:
- Use original client footage or a clearly transformative, rights-cleared treatment.
- Do not publish downloaded TikToks or Instagram videos with another platform watermark as a default cross-posting workflow.

## 1.5 Meta Reels creative construction

Official source:
- Meta for Business — Instagram and Facebook Reels ads.
- https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads

Current finding:
- Meta recommends vertical 9:16 creative, quality audio and keeping key messages inside the safe zone.
- Meta supports placement-specific creative and A/B testing.

CG rule:
- Frame and edit for 9:16 first.
- Keep essential faces, products, subtitles and CTA text away from interface overlays.
- Do not rely on auto-cropping a horizontal master as the primary Reels strategy.

Limitation:
- Meta’s published performance figures are platform marketing claims and should not be presented as guaranteed CG outcomes.

## 1.6 LinkedIn video role

Official source:
- LinkedIn Marketing Solutions — Unlock the Power of LinkedIn Video Ads to Build Your B2B Brand, 3 March 2026.
- https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/linkedin-ads/unlock-the-power-of-linkedin-video-ads-to-build-your-brand

Current finding:
- LinkedIn positions video as a way to help B2B decision-makers understand complex purchases and build brand memory.

CG application:
- LinkedIn short-form should usually provide professional utility, expertise, process visibility, proof, point of view or a clear buying insight.
- A trend copied from TikTok may be unsuitable if it removes professional relevance or brand credibility.

# 2. Short-form strategic models

## 2.1 One-video-one-job rule

Each short-form asset must have one primary job:

- attract a new audience;
- explain one idea;
- demonstrate one proof point;
- create recognition;
- answer one objection;
- announce one offer;
- drive one action;
- deepen community participation.

A video may have secondary effects, but the brief must name one primary job.

## 2.2 Audience-state model

Classify the viewer before writing:

```text
Unaware
Problem aware
Solution aware
Brand aware
Ready to act
Existing customer or community member
```

Examples:

- Unaware: relatable tension, entertainment, surprising observation.
- Problem aware: demonstrate the problem or cost of ignoring it.
- Solution aware: explain the method or category choice.
- Brand aware: proof, people, process, comparison, offer.
- Ready to act: price, availability, booking, deadline, CTA.
- Existing customer: participation, recognition, loyalty, behind-the-scenes access.

## 2.3 Short-form story spine

A default adaptable structure:

```text
0.0–1.5 s: recognisable tension, action or outcome
1.5–4 s: context and brand connection
4–12 s: proof, process, contrast or escalation
12–20 s: payoff or useful conclusion
Final beat: natural next action
```

This is not a rigid time law. It is a planning scaffold.

## 2.4 Open-loop discipline

An open loop should create a real unanswered question that the video resolves.

Good:
- Show the failed result, then explain the cause.
- Show the finished dish, then reveal the process.
- State a specific misconception, then demonstrate the correction.

Bad:
- “You will not believe this.”
- “Wait until the end.”
- Withholding ordinary information without a meaningful payoff.

Rule:
- Curiosity must be earned by the content.

## 2.5 Proof-first structure

Useful for high-trust industries:

```text
Claim
→ visible evidence
→ explanation
→ limitation or condition
→ action
```

Examples of proof:
- real process;
- real product use;
- instrument reading;
- before and after with conditions disclosed;
- customer quote with consent;
- registration or certification with current evidence;
- actual venue attendance;
- verified project stage;
- real dashboard result with correct scope.

## 2.6 Character-led structure

Useful for hospitality, sport, retail and service brands:

```text
Recognisable person
→ recurring role or tension
→ authentic reaction
→ brand-world payoff
```

The recurring person can become a brand asset only after audience familiarity and uniqueness are demonstrated.

## 2.7 Transformation structure

```text
Before state
→ critical action
→ after state
→ what caused the change
```

Boundary:
- Do not imply causation when the change has multiple causes.
- Health, financial, professional, performance and safety transformations require stricter evidence.

## 2.8 Demonstration structure

```text
Problem condition
→ product or process in use
→ visible result
→ application limits
```

A demonstration must represent normal or clearly stated test conditions.

## 2.9 Social proof structure

```text
Specific customer situation
→ action taken
→ verified outcome
→ who it may be relevant for
```

Do not use testimonials as universal efficacy proof.

## 2.10 Event and community structure

```text
Identity
→ stakes
→ participation
→ recognition
→ next fixture or event
```

Useful devices:
- captain or team introductions;
- table names;
- rankings;
- predictions;
- challenge outcomes;
- supporter reactions;
- recurring rituals;
- milestone recognition.

# 3. Hook library by function

Hooks are categories, not copy templates that should be repeated mechanically.

## 3.1 Outcome hook

Start with the result.

- finished product;
- final score;
- dramatic visual change;
- customer reaction;
- completed installation;
- visible queue or crowd.

## 3.2 Problem hook

Show a costly, frustrating or recognisable problem.

- failed part;
- common mistake;
- slow process;
- wasted spend;
- poor fit;
- confusing rule;
- customer pain point.

## 3.3 Contradiction hook

Challenge a relevant assumption.

- “More views did not produce more bookings.”
- “The cheapest option cost more over the full project.”

The contradiction must be supportable.

## 3.4 Demonstration hook

Begin with action rather than introduction.

- tool entering frame;
- food preparation;
- tyre test;
- packaging line;
- match reaction;
- transformation in progress.

## 3.5 Human reaction hook

Use a genuine reaction tied to the brand world.

Avoid staged fake surprise presented as spontaneous.

## 3.6 Specificity hook

Specific detail can create credibility.

- exact model;
- exact venue area;
- exact process step;
- exact problem condition;
- exact deadline or quantity where verified.

## 3.7 Identity hook

Speak to a real group or role.

- first-time home renovators;
- procurement managers;
- parents comparing programmes;
- rugby supporters;
- restaurant regulars;
- local business owners.

Do not create discriminatory or exploitative targeting.

## 3.8 Question hook

Use a question the intended audience genuinely asks.

Avoid generic engagement bait.

# 4. Native creative language

## 4.1 Visual immediacy

The first frame should communicate at least one of:

- who;
- what;
- where;
- problem;
- outcome;
- movement;
- emotion.

Avoid opening with:
- a long logo animation;
- an empty establishing shot;
- a presenter greeting with no context;
- multiple seconds of setup.

## 4.2 Human scale

Short-form often benefits from:
- faces;
- hands;
- eye line;
- movement;
- first-person or close observational framing;
- real environments;
- recognisable staff.

This does not mean every video must be handheld or informal.

## 4.3 Text and captions

Use captions because:
- audio may be off;
- accents, noise and mobile speakers can reduce clarity;
- captions improve access.

Caption rules:
- preserve meaning rather than dumping raw automated text;
- correct names, prices and technical terms;
- keep lines readable;
- maintain safe-zone clearance;
- use contrast that remains legible over moving footage;
- identify speakers where needed;
- do not cover the key visual proof.

## 4.4 Audio

Audio can provide:
- speech clarity;
- rhythm;
- emotion;
- location authenticity;
- recurring brand recognition.

Music does not rescue a weak idea.

Every track requires rights resolution through `MUSIC-COPYRIGHT-PLATFORM-RIGHTS-SYSTEM.md`.

## 4.5 Editing rhythm

Edit according to information and emotion, not a fixed cut-every-second rule.

Increase pace when:
- showing process steps;
- building energy;
- compressing repeated action.

Hold longer when:
- proof needs inspection;
- emotion needs space;
- a technical explanation requires comprehension;
- the reaction is the payoff.

## 4.6 Pattern interruption

Useful pattern changes:
- angle change;
- scale change;
- movement;
- sound change;
- text reveal;
- new speaker;
- object entering frame;
- before/after contrast.

Do not insert random effects that weaken comprehension or brand tone.

# 5. Platform adaptation

## 5.1 TikTok

Best suited to:
- participation;
- personality;
- observational humour;
- trends with brand relevance;
- authentic process;
- reactions;
- creator-like explanation;
- community rituals.

Mandatory checks:
- business-account music rights;
- commercial disclosure;
- regulated-goods risk;
- minors and age ambiguity;
- dangerous acts;
- reused content;
- watermark;
- account enforcement history.

Do not assume:
- TikTok requires low production quality.
- Every trend suits every brand.
- A popular sound is commercially usable.

## 5.2 Instagram Reels

Best suited to:
- brand world;
- visual proof;
- products and environments;
- lifestyle context;
- staff personality;
- collaborations;
- service demonstrations;
- polished but native edits.

Use:
- 9:16;
- safe-zone text;
- quality audio;
- placement-specific versions where needed.

## 5.3 Facebook Reels

Potential strengths:
- local reach;
- community sharing;
- practical demonstrations;
- events;
- offers;
- older audience segments;
- cross-posted Meta-native content.

Do not assume Instagram and Facebook audiences will respond identically.

## 5.4 YouTube Shorts

Potential strengths:
- discovery;
- searchable expertise;
- demonstrations;
- repeatable series;
- connection to long-form channel content;
- durable educational clips.

Mandatory checks:
- view-definition date;
- Engaged views;
- copyright and Content ID;
- over-one-minute music risk;
- originality.

## 5.5 LinkedIn video

Potential strengths:
- expertise;
- buying-group education;
- professional point of view;
- case-study evidence;
- process and capability;
- recruitment and culture;
- founder or specialist commentary.

Avoid:
- copying consumer trends without relevance;
- unsupported corporate claims;
- jargon-heavy scripts with no human tension.

# 6. Short-form series systems

A series is more useful than isolated ideas when it creates a repeatable expectation.

## 6.1 Expert answer series

Each episode answers one real buyer question.

Record:
- question source;
- target audience;
- verified answer;
- limitation;
- CTA.

## 6.2 Process proof series

Show one stage per episode.

Examples:
- inspection;
- preparation;
- installation;
- quality control;
- packaging;
- service follow-up.

## 6.3 People and role series

Show what real staff do and why it matters.

Avoid fake “day in the life” scenes presented as documentary truth.

## 6.4 Myth and misconception series

Use only where the correction is sourced.

Structure:

```text
Misconception
→ why it seems plausible
→ correct distinction
→ practical action
```

## 6.5 Community progression series

Useful for sport, events and membership brands.

Track:
- standings;
- milestones;
- challenges;
- recognitions;
- next event.

## 6.6 Product application series

One product, one use case, one proof point.

Never invent compatibility.

## 6.7 Offer explanation series

Explain:
- who it is for;
- what is included;
- conditions;
- next step.

Do not use artificial scarcity.

# 8. Retention diagnosis

Do not diagnose from one metric alone.

## 8.1 Early drop

Possible causes:
- unclear first frame;
- slow introduction;
- wrong audience;
- misleading hook;
- weak audio;
- text unreadable;
- subject not recognisable.

## 8.2 Mid-video drop

Possible causes:
- repeated information;
- no escalation;
- proof arrives too late;
- explanation becomes generic;
- editing rhythm does not match information.

## 8.3 End drop before CTA

Possible causes:
- payoff already delivered;
- CTA feels bolted on;
- CTA asks too much;
- destination does not match intent.

## 8.4 Strong retention but weak business action

Possible causes:
- entertaining but weak brand attribution;
- no relevant offer;
- audience too broad;
- CTA mismatch;
- poor landing page;
- wrong campaign objective;
- content attracting non-buyers.

## 8.5 High views but low Engaged views

Interpretation:
- starts or replays may be high while fewer viewers choose to continue.

This is especially relevant to YouTube’s post-March-2025 Shorts view methodology.

# 9. Measurement hierarchy

## Attention

- starts or views;
- Engaged views where available;
- hold rate;
- average watch time;
- average percentage viewed;
- retention curve.

## Response

- comments;
- shares;
- saves;
- profile visits;
- follows;
- remixes;
- direct messages.

## Intent

- link clicks;
- landing-page visits;
- menu or product views;
- booking starts;
- lead-form starts;
- WhatsApp starts;
- search lift where validly measured.

## Outcome

- completed lead;
- qualified lead;
- booking;
- paid order;
- checked-in attendee;
- repeat purchase;
- verified revenue.

Rules:
- A view is not a lead.
- A message is not a booking.
- A click is not a sale.
- A viral post is not automatically commercially successful.
- An organic correlation is not proof of incremental lift.

# 10. Creative testing

Test one meaningful question at a time.

Examples:
- outcome-first versus problem-first opening;
- staff-led versus product-led proof;
- direct explanation versus demonstration;
- offer CTA versus education CTA;
- polished studio versus real-location execution.

Record:

```text
hypothesis
controlled_variable
constant_elements
audience
placement
spend_or_distribution_window
primary_metric
downstream_metric
minimum_evidence_rule
result
limitations
next_action
```

Invalid test patterns:
- different audience, budget, offer, landing page and creative all changed;
- declaring a winner from one high-view outlier;
- comparing organic reach from unrelated dates without context;
- choosing CTR while ignoring lead quality.

# 14. Research limitations

- Official platform guidance often mixes product education with platform marketing.
- Platform studies may not generalise to South African SME audiences.
- Organic distribution changes over time.
- A format that works for one client may fail for another because brand, audience, offer and account history differ.
- Short-form performance is affected by topic interest, competition, seasonality, account state, rights and distribution.
- No universal hook, duration, posting frequency or editing speed guarantees success.
