## Purpose

Create a trustworthy marketing and operating knowledge layer for:

- guarding services;
- armed response;
- alarm monitoring;
- CCTV and surveillance;
- access control;
- event security and crowd management;
- executive protection;
- investigations and loss prevention;
- fire-detection and related security service providers;
- security training providers;
- physical-risk consulting;
- cyber-risk and incident-response services where the provider is legitimately qualified.

This pack supports marketing strategy, lead handling, website intelligence, content planning, paid media, reporting and quick Assistant answers. It does not replace legal advice, operational security advice, emergency response, firearm licensing, law-enforcement functions, professional risk assessment or incident-command decisions.

## Industry structure

The AI must separate these service journeys:

1. Guarding
   - site survey;
   - risk profile;
   - staffing model;
   - shift pattern;
   - scope and post orders;
   - mobilisation;
   - incident and service reporting.

2. Armed response and alarm monitoring
   - premises eligibility;
   - alarm installation and commissioning;
   - monitoring activation;
   - signal received;
   - verification;
   - dispatch;
   - arrival;
   - handover or escalation.

3. CCTV and surveillance
   - lawful purpose;
   - site assessment;
   - camera placement;
   - field of view;
   - recording and retention;
   - access control;
   - incident retrieval;
   - disclosure and deletion.

4. Access control
   - credential issuance;
   - identity validation;
   - visitor handling;
   - entry/exit events;
   - exceptions;
   - revocation;
   - audit trail.

5. Event security
   - event risk categorisation;
   - venue capacity;
   - safety plan;
   - crowd-flow design;
   - access and ticket controls;
   - emergency coordination;
   - post-event reporting.

6. Security training
   - training-provider verification;
   - course scope;
   - learner registration;
   - attendance;
   - assessment;
   - completion;
   - PSiRA or other required registration steps.

7. Cyber-risk services
   - readiness assessment;
   - vulnerability or control review;
   - incident triage;
   - containment;
   - evidence preservation;
   - recovery;
   - post-incident improvement.

These are not equivalent outcomes. A lead, quote, signed contract, alarm signal, dispatch, attendance, incident, arrest, recovery and conviction are separate states.

## Source register

### Source S1 — Private Security Industry Regulation Act 56 of 2001

Publishers: South African Government / PSiRA
Official PSiRA legislation page: https://www.psira.co.za/index.php/legislation
Government/SAPS references: https://www.saps.gov.za/resource_centre/acts/acts.php
Source type: legislation and regulator source
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: current Act, regulations, exemptions and amendments must be checked

Verified findings:

- South Africa regulates the occupation and business of rendering security services.
- PSiRA's objective is to regulate the private security industry and exercise effective control over the occupation in the public and national interest.
- Security businesses and individual security officers have separate registration requirements.
- Registration status can be verified through PSiRA's online and mobile services.

Safe candidate knowledge:

- A business registration claim must be verified against the exact legal entity.
- An individual's registration does not prove the employing business is registered.
- A business registration does not prove every deployed officer is currently registered, trained or suitable for a particular role.
- Registration does not prove service quality, response time, firearm authority or absence of complaints.

Prohibited overclaims:

- "PSiRA approved security system" unless PSiRA has approved that exact matter and the wording is accurate.
- "Government approved" as a loose substitute for registration.
- "All our guards are compliant" without current personnel evidence.

### Source S2 — PSiRA Code of Conduct for Security Service Providers

Publisher: PSiRA
Official source family:

- https://www.psira.co.za/index.php/legislation
- https://www.psira.co.za/dmdocuments/Code%20of%20Conduct/2014_Code_of_Conduct.pdf

Source type: enforceable industry conduct code
Rights treatment: official_reference; retain current version and review date

Verified findings:

- PSiRA determines and enforces minimum standards of occupational conduct.
- The Code addresses duties, improper conduct and obligations across categories of security service providers.
- Marketing claims cannot be separated from how the provider actually deploys, supervises and manages services.

Marketing implications:

- Do not glorify unlawful force, intimidation, retaliation or vigilantism.
- Do not imply powers equivalent to SAPS.
- Do not disclose operational weaknesses, client layouts, control-room procedures or response routes for promotional value.
- Testimonials and case studies must not reveal protected incident details or create false universal promises.

### Source S4 — Firearms Control Act 60 of 2000

Publishers: SAPS / South African Government
Official sources:

- https://www.saps.gov.za/services/flash/firearms/faq_applying_for_new_licence.php
- https://www.saps.gov.za/resource_centre/acts/acts.php

Source type: legislation and official licensing guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference; high-risk legal and safety flag

Verified findings:

- A firearm requires the appropriate licence, permit or authorisation.
- Competency and prescribed training are separate prerequisites.
- A person requires a licence for each firearm possessed.
- Firearm authority is not created by employment in security.

Marketing implications:

- "Armed response" must not imply every responder, vehicle or service call is armed unless that is operationally and legally accurate.
- Do not show unsafe handling, casual brandishing, intimidation or unverified weapon use.
- Do not reveal serial numbers, storage methods, armoury details or exploitable procedures.
- Do not claim "fully licensed firearms" without current documentary evidence tied to the relevant business and personnel context.

### Source S5 — Safety at Sports and Recreational Events Act 2 of 2010

Publisher: South African Government / Department of Sport, Arts and Culture source family
Official text family: https://www.gov.za/documents/safety-sports-and-recreational-events-act
Supporting government source: https://www.dsac.gov.za/node/443
Source type: legislation and official event-safety guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference

Verified findings:

- Event safety depends on risk categorisation, safety planning, venue and capacity controls, crowd management and coordination.
- Event organisers, venue owners, safety officers, security providers and public authorities can have different responsibilities.
- Security headcount alone does not prove a safe event.

Marketing implications:

- Do not sell "guaranteed safe event" packages.
- A successful past event does not prove the next event is low risk.
- Attendance estimates, ticketing controls, crowd-flow design, emergency access and alcohol context must remain separate evidence fields.

### Source S6 — Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013

Publishers: South African Government / Information Regulator
Official sources:

- https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act
- https://inforegulator.org.za/guidance-notes/
- https://www.gov.za/documents/notices/protection-personal-information-act-regulations-english-afrikaans-06-mar-2026

Source type: privacy legislation and regulator guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference; privacy-risk flag

Verified findings:

- CCTV images, access logs, number plates, biometrics, voice recordings and incident files can contain personal information.
- Security does not create unlimited authority to collect, retain, combine or disclose personal information.
- Purpose, lawfulness, proportionality, access control, security safeguards, retention and data-subject rights matter.
- Certain high-risk processing can require prior authorisation.

CCTV and access-control rules:

- Record the lawful purpose before deployment.
- Avoid collecting areas unrelated to the security purpose.
- Limit access to authorised users.
- Retain footage only for a justified period or preserved incident purpose.
- Keep disclosure and export logs.
- Treat facial recognition and biometric matching as high risk.
- Do not use security footage as casual social content.
- Blur uninvolved people, plates, access credentials and sensitive areas where publication is lawfully authorised.

### Source S7 — Regulation of Interception of Communications Act 70 of 2002

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical source: https://www.gov.za/documents/regulation-interception-communications-and-provision-communication-related-information--13
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal-risk flag

Verified implication:

- Audio recording, call interception and communication monitoring raise separate legal issues from ordinary silent CCTV.
- A camera with a microphone should not be treated as legally identical to a camera without audio.
- Marketing should not promise covert monitoring, call recording or interception capabilities without a lawful-use review.

### Source S8 — Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020

Publishers: South African Government / Department of Justice
Official sources:

- https://www.gov.za/document?field_gcisdoc_doctype=542&field_gcisdoc_subjects=All&search_query=Cybercrimes
- https://www.justice.gov.za/legislation/acts/acts_full.html

Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference

Verified findings:

- Unlawful access, interception, interference and misuse of data or systems can create criminal exposure.
- Cybersecurity service providers require explicit authority and scope before testing or accessing systems.

Marketing implications:

- Do not market unauthorised access as "ethical hacking" merely because the intent is promotional or educational.
- Do not publish exploit details, credentials, access tokens or incident evidence.
- "We hacked this client to prove security" is unsafe without documented prior authority and disclosure approval.
- Incident response, vulnerability testing and managed security are different services and lead journeys.

### Source S9 — Critical Infrastructure Protection Act 8 of 2019

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical source: https://www.gov.za/documents/acts/critical-infrastructure-protection-act-8-2019-english-isixhosa-28-nov-2019
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; national-security risk flag

Verified finding:

- Critical infrastructure has a formal legal context and cannot be used as a casual marketing label for any important client site.

Marketing implications:

- Do not call a site "critical infrastructure" without verified status or accurate non-statutory wording.
- Do not expose layouts, vulnerabilities, guard rotations, access methods or response dependencies.
- Case studies involving sensitive infrastructure require heightened review and client authority.

### Source S10 — SAPS crime statistics

Publisher: South African Police Service
Official source family: https://www.saps.gov.za/services/crimestats.php
Source type: official administrative crime statistics
Rights treatment: official_reference; retain period, geography, offence category and reporting basis
Freshness: periodic

Safe use:

- Crime statistics may describe reported crime patterns for a defined period and geography.
- They may support context and prevention education.

Limitations:

- Reported crime is not the same as all crime occurring.
- National or provincial figures do not prove one suburb, premises or client is unsafe.
- A category trend does not prove a specific security product will prevent the offence.
- Do not use statistics to manufacture panic or imply guaranteed victimisation.

### Source S11 — Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical source: https://www.gov.za/documents/consumer-protection-act
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference

Security-service implications:

- Pricing, contract period, installation, monitoring, cancellation, call-out charges and exclusions should be clear.
- "Free installation", "free response", "24/7", "unlimited", "guaranteed response" and "zero risk" require exact support and material conditions.
- Equipment ownership, rental, maintenance, batteries, connectivity, false alarms and service areas must not be hidden.
- A promotional response-time figure must preserve its exact measurement basis and limitations.

## Response-time truth model

Never publish a response-time claim without:

- service type;
- geography;
- measurement start event;
- measurement end event;
- date range;
- sample size;
- median or percentile;
- exclusions;
- data source;
- whether cancelled and false alarms were included;
- whether the figure covers all calls or selected priority incidents.

"Average response time" is unsafe when it hides long-tail outcomes or selected zones. Prefer median and percentile reporting where the operational data supports it.

## High-risk content patterns

Require legal, operational and client review before publication:

- live incident footage;
- identifiable suspects or victims;
- arrest or guilt claims;
- firearm handling;
- tactical procedures;
- control rooms and camera maps;
- access credentials;
- guard rosters;
- client vulnerabilities;
- alarm-panel codes;
- number plates and faces;
- body-camera or audio recordings;
- "caught in the act" claims without verified context;
- cyber exploit demonstrations;
- graphic injury or death;
- retaliation, intimidation or vigilante messaging.

## Fear-based advertising boundary

Acceptable:

- verified statistics with period and geography;
- practical prevention steps;
- clear product limitations;
- calm explanation of response processes;
- safety and preparedness education.

Unacceptable or review-required:

- implying the viewer will be attacked unless they buy;
- fabricated crime alerts;
- staged incidents presented as real;
- using victims without authority;
- graphic shock content to drive leads;
- claiming a product makes crime impossible;
- portraying security officers as police or soldiers where that is inaccurate;
- publishing unsupported "crime hotspot" labels.

## Candidate review-gated Skill Cards

### SSC-01 — Verify business and personnel separately

PSiRA business registration and individual officer registration are distinct claims and require current evidence.

### SSC-02 — Security providers are not SAPS

Private security functions, powers and communications must not be presented as equivalent to police authority.

### SSC-03 — Armed employment does not create firearm authority

Firearm competency, licensing, authorisation, business controls and role deployment must remain separate.

### SSC-04 — CCTV is personal-information processing

Footage, plates, biometrics, access logs and audio require lawful purpose, proportionality, safeguards and retention controls.

### SSC-05 — Audio is not the same as silent CCTV

Recording or intercepting communications can create additional legal risk.

### SSC-06 — Event safety is a system

Guard numbers alone do not prove an event is safe; categorisation, planning, capacity, crowd flow and coordination matter.

### SSC-07 — Response-time claims require a measurement definition

Never publish an average without start/end events, period, geography, sample and exclusions.

### SSC-08 — A signal is not an incident

Alarm, verification, dispatch, arrival, incident and outcome must remain separate states.

### SSC-09 — Crime statistics are context, not a threat script

Use exact periods and geography; do not manufacture panic or promise prevention.

### SSC-10 — Cyber testing requires authority

Good intent does not authorise system access, interception or exploitation.

### SSC-11 — Security content must not weaken security

Marketing should never expose client layouts, procedures, credentials, rosters or exploitable response information.

### SSC-12 — Prevention is not directly observed

Do not calculate or advertise crimes prevented without a valid methodology.

## Quick Assistant answer pattern

For a question such as:

"Can we say this company guarantees a seven-minute response?"

Return:

```text
Status: Evidence required / likely unsafe as written

Check:
- exact service and zone;
- response start and end event;
- measured period and sample;
- median and percentile results;
- exclusions and false alarms;
- contract wording and operational approval.

Safer wording:
- describe the response process;
- publish a properly defined historical metric only when verified;
- do not guarantee an outcome affected by distance, traffic, availability, incident conditions or public-authority response.
```

For a question such as:

"Can we post this CCTV clip of a theft?"

Return:

```text
Status: High-risk review required

Check:
- lawful authority to publish;
- active police or legal matter;
- victim, suspect and bystander privacy;
- faces, number plates, audio and access information;
- client approval;
- whether publication creates defamation, safety or operational risk.

Default:
Do not publish until authorised, appropriately redacted and operationally cleared.
```
