## Executive direction

Hino Oranje Bloemfontein should not be marketed like a passenger-vehicle dealership with larger vehicles.

A truck buyer is usually buying productive capacity, uptime and a transport solution. The commercial question is not only `which truck do I like?` but:

- will the truck fit the actual job;
- what payload/body/application is required;
- what will downtime cost;
- what will fuel, servicing and maintenance cost;
- can parts be supplied quickly;
- can the dealer support the truck after sale;
- can finance and replacement cycles be planned;
- what does the truck earn or enable for the business.

The strongest local marketing territory is therefore:

> **The Bloemfontein Hino team that helps Free State businesses choose, run and support the right truck for the job.**

The commercial brand pillars should be:

1. correct truck/application matching;
2. uptime and aftersales support;
3. total cost of ownership rather than sticker price alone;
4. local dealer accountability;
5. Hino national product, warranty, service and parts depth.

- lost revenue;
- overtime;
- replacement vehicle costs;
- customer penalties;
- idle staff;
- delayed construction/farm work;

## Content architecture

## 1. `Built for the job`

Show one real application at a time.

Examples:

- refrigerated delivery;
- dropside building-supply truck;
- agricultural body;
- tipper;
- courier/urban box body;
- fleet freight application.

Structure:

- business problem;
- truck/application;
- why this configuration suits the work;
- CTA to discuss requirements.

Use only current approved specs.

## 2. `Ask before you buy`

Educational series around truck-buying questions.

Examples:

- payload vs body weight;
- route type;
- kilometres per month;
- stop/start vs long-haul use;
- body-builder timing;
- service planning;
- finance considerations;
- replacement cycle.

This can create qualified leads because the content teaches customers how to describe their actual need.

## 3. `Total Support in Bloem`

Localise Hino's national support platform.

Content subjects:

- service booking process;
- preventative maintenance;
- parts counter;
- technician/workshop process;
- genuine parts;
- service-plan explanation;
- warranty basics;
- roadside/support process only if current local process is verified.

## 4. `Meet the truck people`

Truck buyers often buy from relationships.

Feature real:

- sales consultants;
- service advisors;
- technicians;
- parts team;
- finance contact where approved.

Better prompts:

- `What do customers get wrong when choosing a truck?`
- `Which detail saves operators headaches later?`
- `What should a fleet manager tell us before we quote?`

Avoid generic staff biographies.

## 5. `Operator stories`

Real local customers using their Hino.

Strong story structure:

1. What does the business do?
2. What job does the truck perform?
3. Why was this configuration selected?
4. What matters most to the operator?
5. What role does Hino Oranje support play?

Use real customer consent and verified claims.

## 6. `Truck breakdown`

Not breakdown as failure — break down the truck/application visually.

Show:

- cab;
- safety features;
- body;
- access;
- driver environment;
- service points;
- application equipment.

Translate feature into operator value.

## 7. `Used stock now`

Used content should be inventory-first:

- exact unit;
- year;
- mileage;
- body/application;
- transmission/engine only from listing;
- price if current and approved;
- finance/trade-in CTA;
- last verified date.

Sold units must be removed quickly.

## 8. `Workshop truth`

Trust-building aftersales content.

Examples:

- what a service actually checks;
- why skipping intervals costs more later;
- wear-item education;
- genuine-parts reasoning;
- pre-trip/fleet inspection.

Keep technical claims approved by Hino/dealer staff.
