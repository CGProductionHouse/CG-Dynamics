## 7. Human creative standard — #248 is mandatory

Correct facts are the floor, not the finished marketing.

Future Piek work must feel like a sharp person understood the exact asset, branch, audience and occasion and cared about the line.

### Captions

- Start from what is actually in the image/video and what the artwork already says.
- **Add; do not repeat.** Add context, personality, a useful detail, observation, tension, reason to act, local relevance or a second layer.
- Vary rhythm, sentence length, opening structure and CTA.
- Prefer a specific observation or point of view over generic brand filler.
- Do not optimise the copy into bland SEO language.
- Historical captions establish minimum structure/footer conventions only; they are **not** the future creative-quality ceiling.
- One strong sentence can be enough when the creative is simple, but it still needs a point.
- UK English remains the default.

Avoid generic filler such as:

- `elevate your experience`;
- `discover the difference`;
- `where quality meets...`;
- `experience excellence`;
- `your trusted partner`;
- generic `something for everyone`;
- unsupported superiority;
- any interchangeable line that could sit under ten unrelated businesses.

### Dynamic hashtags / SEO

Default maximum: **5 hashtags** unless an explicit platform/client brief overrides it.

At generation time choose the strongest set for the exact:

- content mode/entity;
- topic/product/service;
- platform;
- location/category when relevant;
- current search/trend context where reliable evidence exists.

Stored historical hashtag banks are seed/reference material only. Never paste the same bank mechanically. Never call a hashtag `trending` without current evidence. Hashtags must not create false branch/partner/service/location claims.

Use natural searchable wording in the caption itself as well; discoverability is not only hashtags.

For practical service/product posters:

- **title:** clearly frames the actual service/product/occasion;
- **subtitle:** adds one supporting customer-facing detail;
- title and subtitle must not repeat each other.

Match the visible asset literally enough to be trustworthy.

Durable examples of the rule:

- windscreen image -> mention the windscreen clean rather than vague `care`;
- staff assisting a vehicle -> describe the actual assistance;
- convenience product image -> name the useful customer benefit/product;
- rusk image -> call it a **rusk**, not bread;
- Get Together alcohol/food imagery -> treat it as restaurant/hospitality content and verify current product/menu availability where needed.
