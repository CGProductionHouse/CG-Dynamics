## 5. Human marketing DNA — cross-account

The most durable lesson from repeated Project corrections is that Piek content must sound like a person who understands the exact service, product, branch and occasion.

### Use

- natural everyday wording;
- one clear customer-facing idea per line;
- specific service/product detail;
- practical reasons to stop, visit, eat or use the service;
- local/community relevance when it is real;
- friendly confidence without performance or hype;
- UK English;
- concise copy when the message is simple.

### Do not use

- generic corporate filler;
- influencer-style exaggeration;
- forced slang or humour;
- empty aspiration;
- copy that could fit any fuel station or restaurant;
- unverified superiority claims;
- generic clichés such as `elevate your experience`, `discover the difference`, `where quality meets...`, `best in town`, `we are the best`;
- wording that describes an image inaccurately;
- cleverness that makes the line awkward or unclear.

The approved feel is warm, dependable, clean and human rather than loud or over-written.

### Formula rule

Avoid mechanical `from X to Y` copy when a direct sentence is stronger.

Do not force wordplay that stops sounding like something a real customer or staff member would say.

## 7. Poster and text-on-post rules

### Preferred service-poster structure

For practical service posts:

- **title:** clearly identifies or frames the actual service/product shown;
- **subtitle:** adds one supporting customer-facing detail.

Title and subtitle must not repeat the same thought.

Examples of the human rule, not slogans to reuse:

- windscreen image -> talk about the windscreen clean, not vague `care`;
- staff assisting at a car -> talk about the actual assistance;
- convenience/Quickshop image -> talk about what the customer can get or why it makes the stop easier;
- coffee + rusk image -> call the product a **rusk**, not bread;
- Get Together wine/liqueur image -> treat it as restaurant/hospitality content and verify the current offering.

### Voting/nomination posters

Recurring Engen Northridge/Best of Bloemfontein work established this style:

- fun and interactive;
- conversational;
- clearly tell people what to do;
- include the closing date where relevant;
- keep the line fresh across a series;
- do not keep recycling `make it count`, `bring the win/title home` or `last chance`.

How-to-vote copy can sound conversational, e.g. a natural “If you didn’t know yet, here’s how...” style rather than formal instructions.

### Heritage/community posts

Prefer real South African people/tradition/community language over stock `Today we celebrate...` corporate openings.
