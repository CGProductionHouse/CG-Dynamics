## Strategic diagnosis

BloemVascular should not market like a retail clinic.

The strongest strategic position is:

> The trusted vascular referral and patient-education centre for central South Africa, combining subspecialist expertise, evidence-based care and clear guidance through complex vascular conditions.

The practice has three different growth systems that should not be mixed:

1. **Referrer confidence** — GPs, physicians, podiatrists, renal teams, diabetic-care teams, wound-care providers, cardiologists and hospital specialists need to know when and how to refer.
2. **Patient understanding** — patients and families need calm, accurate explanations of symptoms, urgency, treatment pathways and appointment logistics.
3. **Professional authority** — educational leadership, academic work, multidisciplinary collaboration and regional service breadth should strengthen trust without claiming superiority.

## Buyer and referral journeys

### Patient-led journey

1. Patient notices a symptom or receives a diagnosis.
2. Searches the condition, symptom or specialist type.
3. Looks for urgency guidance, location, credentials and treatment options.
4. Reviews the practice website, hospital profile and Google listing.
5. Calls, uses WhatsApp or submits the website form.
6. Reception determines the correct room, surgeon, urgency and documents.
7. Consultation occurs.
8. Investigations, conservative management, intervention or multidisciplinary referral follows.
9. Follow-up and long-term management continue.

### Clinician-led referral journey

1. Referrer identifies vascular concern.
2. Searches or recalls BloemVascular.
3. Confirms scope, surgeon, hospital and referral requirements.
4. Sends referral letter and relevant imaging or labs.
5. Practice triages urgency.
6. Patient is booked and receives logistics.
7. Referrer receives feedback where clinically and legally appropriate.
8. Shared care or follow-up continues.

### Emergency boundary

The website and social channels must clearly distinguish:

- general information;
- routine appointment requests;
- urgent clinical referral;
- medical emergency.

Social DMs and WhatsApp are not emergency channels. Content must direct suspected acute limb ischaemia, stroke symptoms, ruptured aneurysm symptoms, severe bleeding or major acute swelling/breathlessness to emergency services without attempting diagnosis online.

## Reputation and review strategy

Medical review marketing is sensitive.

Recommended approach:

- prioritise accurate listings and professional information;
- monitor Google and directory reviews;
- respond without confirming patient status or clinical details;
- never debate treatment publicly;
- move complaints to a private, governed process;
- obtain legal/HPCSA guidance before actively soliciting or republishing testimonials;
- never use a patient story, image or case without explicit written consent and clinical approval;
- anonymisation alone may not be sufficient if a patient can be recognised.

## Data, privacy and AI boundaries

- Appointment forms collect health-related information and must be POPIA-governed.
- Limit free-text fields to what reception genuinely needs.
- Publish privacy and retention information.
- Do not send sensitive medical details through unsecured marketing systems.
- Do not place patient health data into general ad-platform audiences.
- Do not use AI to diagnose, triage emergencies or recommend treatment.
- AI-generated medical content requires named clinical review.
- CG Dynamics may suggest content and analyse marketing performance, but cannot replace clinical judgement.
- Comments and DMs containing symptoms must receive a safe escalation response, not personalised medical advice.
