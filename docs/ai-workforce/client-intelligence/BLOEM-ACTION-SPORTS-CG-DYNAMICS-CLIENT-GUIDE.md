# Bloem Action Sports — CG Dynamics Client Guide

CG Dynamics is the permanent client source of truth. This document is the current ChatGPT working guide derived from that intelligence. When durable client information changes, update CG Dynamics first and then refresh this guide.

Canonical client name: **Bloem Action Sports**

Use this guide for everyday ChatGPT and staff work. It is not a research report and it does not replace the permanent Dynamics intelligence.

## 1. Voice and human marketing rules

Bloem Action Sports content should sound like people who know the venue, the games and the local audience.

Keep the tone:

- **Confident** — experienced without boasting.
- **Clean** — clear and straight to the point.
- **Local & Friendly** — familiar to Bloem people without forcing slang.
- **Energetic yet Professional** — reflects the pace of indoor sport without hype.
- **Human** — sounds like the team behind the counter or people on the court, not a scripted agency.

Default to English for general content. Use Afrikaans when the brief asks for it or when a short local phrase genuinely fits. Natural local wording such as “maak ’n span” or “ons sien julle op die baan” is fine when appropriate. Do not force bilingual copy.

Never use em dashes in captions or social copy.

Avoid generic AI/corporate language such as:

- “elevate your experience”
- “discover the difference”
- “where quality meets…”
- “take your game to the next level”
- “feel the energy”
- “where passion comes alive”
- “something for everyone”
- “unforgettable fun”
- “where champions are made”
- “make every moment count”
- vague teaser copy such as “Something exciting is coming”

If the wording could be pasted onto almost any sport or venue, it is probably too generic.

Humour is welcome when it comes from real indoor-sport behaviour, player reactions, team rituals, umpire moments, bench reactions or familiar match frustrations. Do not force jokes, chase memes, use influencer slang or embarrass players/teams.

## 3. Poster and text-on-post rules

For normal sport artwork, the preferred structure is:

**SHORT TITLE**  
Short supporting subtitle.

The subtitle must add meaning instead of repeating the title.

Write to the person actually playing the sport. Use the pressure, timing, movement, read or decision they recognise from a real match.

Better direction:

**BREAK THE HOCKEY PRESS**  
Move the ball before the gap closes.

Avoid vague titles/subtitles such as “Stay in Control”, “Start the Play”, “Own the Moment” or generic motivation with the sport name added afterwards.

Keep poster wording short enough to work visually.

When separate creatives exist for cricket, netball, hockey or soccer, write sport-specific copy for each rather than forcing one umbrella line across all sports.

Do not add labels such as `FS Mix`, `Super League` or a division name unless they genuinely apply to that artwork/event.

## 4. Sport-specific copy guidance

### Hockey

Use real indoor-hockey moments where accurate:

- press;
- passing lane;
- first pass;
- turnover;
- gap closing;
- quick release;
- off-ball movement;
- defensive pressure;
- shot opportunity.

### Netball

Use actual netball play:

- movement into space;
- feed timing;
- circle entry;
- intercept pressure;
- defensive read;
- quick release;
- reset;
- shooting opportunity;
- court positioning.

Unless the content is specifically about youth development or coaching, avoid generic “building foundations”, “developing the future” or “young talent” framing.

### Cricket

Use actual indoor-cricket moments:

- batting partnership;
- running pressure;
- third-ball pressure where accurate;
- bowling spell;
- catching;
- fielding reaction;
- scoring zones;
- overs;
- defending a total;
- chasing a target.

Avoid generic “hit harder”, “play stronger” and “give it your all” filler.

### Soccer

Action Soccer is currently active at Bloem Action Sports.

Use real five-a-side situations:

- pressure on the ball;
- passing option;
- quick combination;
- space opening or closing;
- defensive recovery;
- finishing chance;
- movement off the ball;
- transition after a turnover.

Do not recycle wording from another sport and simply replace the sport name.

## 5. Content, reels and video guidance

Recurring useful content includes:

- new season and team registration;
- winter/summer league campaigns;
- fixtures, results and standings;
- finals;
- team/player highlights;
- real match action;
- birthday and corporate/team-building content;
- venue/facility features;
- umpire recruitment/qualification when a current programme exists;
- matchday and spectator moments;
- event-day information.

Finals can be promoted as a bigger venue occasion, but food stalls, bar specials, prizes, entertainment and similar extras are **event-specific**. Confirm them each time.

For parties and team building, focus on practical organiser benefits such as indoor/weather-safe activity, organised play, group participation and currently available venue features. Avoid generic “create unforgettable memories” wording.

For reels, prioritise real footage:

- catches, goals, shots, saves;
- team celebrations;
- umpire calls/reactions;
- quick court sequences;
- behind-the-scenes moments;
- spectator reactions.

Keep editing clean and natural. Avoid gimmicky filters, excessive effects and processing that makes players or the venue look artificial.

Recent CG-produced content is not humour-heavy by default. Humour is an option, not a compulsory format.

## 6. Image generation, editing and visual direction

Real players, real court energy and recognisable venue/sport details are preferred over generic stock-looking or obviously AI-generated imagery.

When a CG Production House team member asks for one visual change, make that change only.

Do not:

- redesign unrelated parts of the image;
- move elements that were not mentioned;
- invent extra objects;
- alter branding unnecessarily;
- change logo wording;
- replace or reposition text unless requested;
- make real players look artificial;
- add wrong sport equipment or court elements.

Preserve the original subject, branding, composition and real-sport feel unless a broader redesign is explicitly requested.

For generated visuals, keep anatomy, sport equipment, player positions and court context believable. Do not invent client logos, contact details, promotions or wording that were not supplied.

Useful established correction pattern:

- remove the net only;
- make the image clearer without changing the scene;
- reduce the AI look;
- fix one letter only.
