## Identity and scope confidence

This client is the Bloemfontein police rugby club, also publicly referenced as **BFN Police**, **BFN Polisie** and **Bloemfontein Police**.

Verified public evidence:

- Free State Rugby Union records list **BFN Police I / BFN Polisie I** as Rowan Cup winners in the Mangaung Metro League and First League B in 2019;
- public match archives record Bloemfontein Police first-team participation at Bobbies Park in national Gold Cup competition in 2016;
- Bobbies Park is publicly associated with rugby fields, a clubhouse/bar environment, parking and community sport use in southern Bloemfontein;
- public venue listings place Bobbies Park around Hanna Road / Fichardt Park, Bloemfontein;
- the club therefore has a real competitive rugby identity, local history and a viable match-day/community base.

- current sponsor list, sponsorship rights and commercial commitments;

## Strategic diagnosis

BFN Polisie Rugbyklub should not be marketed as a generic sports club.

Its strongest brand territory is:

> hard, honest Bloemfontein club rugby built on discipline, brotherhood, local pride and a real pathway for players who still want meaningful competitive rugby.

The club has four connected growth jobs:

1. recruit and retain players;
2. build match-day attendance and community energy;
3. attract sponsors through clear commercial value;
4. turn fixtures, personalities and club history into a consistent local media product.

The biggest likely weakness is fragmented communication. Amateur clubs often post isolated scorecards, team lists and sponsor graphics without building an ongoing story. That creates activity without brand growth.

BFN Polisie needs one connected season narrative.

## Audience system

### Current and prospective players

Segments:

- former school players not entering university rugby;
- university or college players seeking more game time;
- working adults wanting competitive rugby;
- police, security, emergency-service and public-service personnel where relevant;
- experienced club players moving to Bloemfontein;
- players returning after injury, work or family breaks;
- younger players seeking senior-club exposure;
- specialist positions the club actively needs.

They ask:

- What level does the club play?
- Will I actually get an opportunity?
- Who are the coaches?
- How serious are training and selection?
- Is the culture welcoming or cliquey?
- What are the fees and time commitments?
- Is there medical and conditioning support?
- Can this club help me improve or be noticed?

They want:

- food, drink and atmosphere;
- family-friendly match days;

## Competitive landscape

### Shimlas / UFS rugby

Strengths:

- elite university identity;
- Varsity Cup and high-performance visibility;
- strong facilities, coaching and player pathways;
- major student audience and institutional media support.

BFN Polisie opportunity:

- do not imitate university rugby;
- own the pathway for working players, overlooked talent and players who want serious rugby outside the university structure;
- build a grittier, more accessible local identity.

### CUT Ixias and university structures

Strengths:

- student recruitment;
- institutional facilities;
- structured competitions;
- access to younger athletes.

BFN Polisie opportunity:

- offer continuity after study;
- create game-time opportunities;
- form ethical player-development relationships rather than presenting the club as an enemy of university programmes.

### Old Greys / Collegians / Ramblers and other Bloemfontein clubs

Typical strengths:

- established local recognition;
- alumni networks;
- social-club traditions;
- venue familiarity;
- existing player pipelines.

BFN Polisie opportunity:

- sharpen its identity around discipline, effort, belonging and competitive opportunity;
- communicate consistently enough that prospective players know exactly how to join;
- make Bobbies Park feel like a recognisable home ground.

### Schools and academies

Schools are not direct commercial competitors but they compete for attention and dominate local rugby storytelling.

Opportunity:

- build a transition route from school and academy rugby into adult club rugby;
- host position clinics, preseason days and school-leaver information sessions;
- avoid approaching minors without appropriate permissions and safeguarding.

### Other entertainment choices

The club also competes with:

- televised professional rugby at home or in pubs;
- Cheetahs fixtures;
- padel, golf, gym and social sport;
- weekend events and nightlife;
- family commitments.

A match day must therefore offer more than 80 minutes of rugby.

## Positioning system

### Core position

**Real Bloem club rugby. Hard work, local pride and a place to belong.**

### Supporting pillars

1. **Competitive rugby** — real fixtures, selection standards and ambition.
2. **Opportunity** — a visible route for players who still want meaningful rugby.
3. **Brotherhood** — honest team culture, responsibility and belonging.
4. **Bobbies Park** — a recognisable home ground with local character.
5. **Community** — supporters, families, alumni and local businesses around one club.
6. **History** — past achievements used as proof, not nostalgia without relevance.

### Language to avoid

Avoid generic claims such as:

- more than a club;
- one big family;
- where legends are made;
- blood, sweat and tears;
- the best club in Bloem;
- champions, unless tied to a specific verified competition and year.

Replace these with specific evidence:

- training attendance;
- player journeys;
- selection milestones;
- verified results;
- coach insight;
- club records;
- sponsor activations;
- community participation.

## Player recruitment engine

### Always-on recruitment page

Create one permanent page or mobile landing page containing:

- exact teams and competition level;
- age and eligibility;
- training venue and times;
- season dates;
- fees and kit requirements;
- named coaches and contact person;
- registration form;
- WhatsApp action;
- trial or preseason process;
- what new players should bring;
- code of conduct and commitment expectations.

### Recruitment campaigns

#### “Still got rugby in you?”

Audience: former school, university and club players.

Creative:

- old boots coming out of a cupboard;
- first night back at training;
- a player explaining why he returned;
- clear trial date and no vague motivational ending.

#### “Your position is needed”

Run only where the coaching staff confirms a real need.

Examples:

- front-row recruitment;
- halfback depth;
- outside-back pace;
- kickers;
- team managers, physios or analysts.

Never create fake scarcity.

#### “From work to training”

Show players finishing real workdays and arriving at the club.

This can own a territory university teams cannot: disciplined adult club rugby around real life.

### Recruitment funnel

Track:

1. enquiry;
2. attended first training;
3. completed registration;
4. eligible for selection;
5. selected;
6. retained after 30 days;
7. retained through season.

Follower growth is not a player-recruitment result.

## Match-day product

### Information standard

Every home match post should clearly state:

- opponent;
- date;
- venue;
- first kickoff and other team kickoff times;
- gate or entry information;
- food/bar availability where confirmed;
- parking and entrance details;
- family information;
- weather update close to match day;
- sponsor recognition;
- result-update channel.

### Match-day rhythm

#### 5–7 days before

- fixture announcement;
- opponent context;
- reason the game matters;
- ticket or entry details.

#### 2–3 days before

- player or coach story;
- sponsor activation;
- venue and supporter information.

#### Day before

- verified team announcement;
- short captain or coach message;
- weather and final logistics.

#### Match day

- arrival and warm-up;
- score updates;
- real crowd and sideline moments;
- halftime score;
- final score;
- player-of-the-match context;
- sponsor visibility.

#### Within 24 hours

- accurate result;
- key moments;
- photographs with names;
- honest reflection;
- next fixture.

## Content system

### Performance content

- one training objective explained by a coach;
- set-piece sessions;
- conditioning standards;
- player comeback stories;
- position-specific insight;
- match review with one lesson;
- preseason testing;
- selection and debut moments.

Do not publish tactical detail that disadvantages the team.

### Personality content

- player profiles that go beyond nicknames;
- work, family and rugby balance;
- “who is most likely to…” formats used sparingly;
- kit-room and travel moments;
- team traditions;
- honest rookie and veteran conversations;
- coaches as people, not only authority figures.

### Entertainment content

- controlled teammate challenges;
- crossbar and kicking contests;
- old photo recreations;
- supporters choosing player-of-the-match candidates;
- derby-week banter that stays respectful;
- rapid match-day reactions;
- club-history quizzes.

### Community content

- youth clinics;
- local-business visits;
- sponsor staff days;
- blood drives or community initiatives where genuine;
- alumni reunions;
- family and supporter stories;
- volunteer recognition.

### History content

The 2019 Rowan Cup and other verified achievements should become a structured archive series:

- exact competition and year;
- team photograph;
- named players and coaches where confirmed;
- short memories from participants;
- connection to the present team.

Never turn uncertain memories into factual records without verification.

Follow a player from work to training without overproducing it.

One clear, non-sensitive point:

- what standard is expected;

### “Old Bobbie, new Bobbie”

Pair an alumnus with a current player to connect history and future.

## Reputation and conduct

A rugby club brand can be damaged quickly by:

- player misconduct;
- alcohol-related incidents;
- disrespectful derby content;
- discriminatory language;
- unapproved use of minors’ images;
- false injury or selection information;
- public attacks on referees or unions;
- sponsor conflicts;
- unsafe training or event claims.

Required controls:

- social-media code;
- match-day media owner;
- image and consent process;
- crisis escalation contact;
- no posting of medical details without permission;
- no naming of disciplinary matters before authorised disclosure;
- brand and sponsor approval rules;
- comment moderation standards.

## Final strategic position

BFN Polisie Rugbyklub should become the most recognisable **working-person’s competitive rugby club brand in Bloemfontein**: serious enough for ambitious players, human enough for families and supporters, and organised enough for sponsors to trust.
