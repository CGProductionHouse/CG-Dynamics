## Human creative standard for Bloem Vascular

### Voice

Bloem Vascular should sound:
- clinically confident;
- calm and reassuring;
- clear and informative;
- respectful and empathetic;
- precise rather than vague;
- patient-centred;
- professionally credible without self-congratulation.

The tone should reduce confusion and anxiety without minimising the seriousness of vascular disease.

Do not sound:
- sales-driven;
- casual about diagnosis or risk;
- dramatic or fear-based;
- emotionally detached;
- like a generic hospital brochure;
- like a wellness influencer;
- like a retail clinic advertising a quick fix.

### Patient-facing vs referrer-facing mode

**Patient-facing copy**
- use plain language first;
- explain what a term means and why assessment may matter;
- reassure without promising an outcome;
- make the next step clear;
- distinguish general information from individual diagnosis/treatment;
- avoid dense abbreviations and specialist jargon unless immediately explained.

**Referrer/doctor-facing copy**
- may use more precise clinical terminology;
- can focus on referral thresholds, pathways, imaging/documentation or multidisciplinary care only when exact clinical information has been verified;
- should remain concise, factual and useful;
- never invent referral criteria or urgency pathways.

### Captions: add, do not repeat

Read the supplied image, poster or video first. Do not restate the headline, on-screen copy or spoken dialogue.

Instead add one useful second layer, such as:
- a plain-language explanation;
- why the topic is relevant to vascular assessment;
- what a vascular surgeon considers in general terms;
- a practical appointment/location detail;
- a distinction between two commonly confused concepts;
- a calm next step;
- a referrer-specific clinical context when the audience is professional.

### Caption length and rhythm

No fixed Bloem Vascular sentence count or option count is proven from the accessible Project history.

Working rule:
- keep simple captions concise but meaningful;
- educational posters should not be reduced to empty one-liners;
- explain enough to make the content useful;
- focus each caption on one clear idea rather than a long list of claims;
- vary openings and sentence rhythm;
- use the full approved contact footer every time.

Working rule:

### Emojis

## Content formats that suit Bloem Vascular

Durable formats from the existing intelligence and Project history include:

### What does a vascular surgeon do?
Explain specialist scope in plain language and differentiate vascular care from adjacent specialties without disparaging them.

### What is vascular surgery?
Explain arterial/venous disease, open surgery and endovascular treatment at category level. Do not imply every condition needs surgery.

### What is a vascular hybrid theatre?
Use only current verified theatre/facility facts. Explain the concept and role of imaging/procedural capability without promising outcomes or inventing equipment.

### Condition education
One condition or symptom topic at a time. Explain the issue, when assessment can matter and what type of specialist evaluates it without diagnosing the audience.

### Patient pathway / appointment clarity
Useful topics include:
- which room to contact;
- room directions;
- what the first consultation generally involves when confirmed;
- how to prepare when the exact checklist is approved;
- medical-aid/admin reminders without coverage guarantees.

### Referrer education
Use professional language for verified referral pathways, clinical updates and collaboration. Do not create referral thresholds or work-up requirements from general knowledge alone.

### Practice and specialist authority
Use factual, freshly verified qualifications, conference/academic activity and team information. Authority should come from precise evidence rather than prestige adjectives.
