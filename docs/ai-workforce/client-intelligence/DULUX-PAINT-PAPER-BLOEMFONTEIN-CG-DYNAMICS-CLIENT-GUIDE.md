# Dulux Paint & Paper Bloemfontein - CG Dynamics Client Guide

CG Dynamics is the permanent client source of truth. This document is the current ChatGPT working guide derived from that intelligence. When durable client information changes, update CG Dynamics first and then refresh this guide.

## Scope

Work only on **Dulux Paint & Paper Bloemfontein**.

Use this guide for everyday client work. Do not treat it as a research report or replace the permanent CG Dynamics record with it.

## Human marketing rule

Dulux marketing must feel human, specific, useful and locally relevant.

Write like real people in a Bloemfontein paint store, not a corporate brand deck or generic AI copy.

Strong content usually names a real:
- colour;
- room;
- surface;
- paint problem;
- product or product category;
- preparation step;
- staff member;
- in-store process;
- project decision.

If the wording could be pasted onto another paint brand unchanged, rewrite it.

Avoid empty phrases such as:
- “elevate your space”;
- “discover the difference”;
- “where quality meets…”;
- vague luxury language;
- generic inspiration;
- unsupported “best” claims;
- repetitive “you can trust us” wording;
- robotic or influencer-style hype.

Premium/refined language is acceptable only when tied to a real finish, product, expertise point, room result or supported Dulux quality claim.

## Caption rules

- Keep captions natural, specific and concise.
- A short one-liner is often enough when the artwork already carries the detail.
- Captions must add something to the artwork instead of repeating it word-for-word.
- Add one useful detail, context point or practical CTA.
- Default to natural English.
- Do not mix Afrikaans and English unless specifically requested.
- If Afrikaans is requested, use natural everyday spoken Afrikaans.
- Do not use em dashes in client-facing copy unless specifically requested.
- Use a maximum of **5 focused, relevant hashtags**.
- Rotate hashtags by topic rather than mechanically repeating the same set.

## Poster and text-on-post rules

Prefer:
- short headings;
- one clear idea;
- wording tied directly to the visual;
- specific product, colour, room, surface or service language;
- subtitles that add information rather than repeat the heading.

Avoid:
- generic inspirational headlines;
- headline and subtitle saying the same thing;
- awkward wording added only to sound clever or premium;
- long copy when one strong line works.

For voting or award content, give people a real reason to support the store, especially expertise, product/range depth or service. Do not keep recycling “bring the win home”, “bring the title home” or similar wording. Old award names, voting instructions and closing dates are not permanent facts.

## Colour-copy guidance

Describe colour by **what it does to the room**, not only with adjectives.

Do not stop at “beautiful”, “timeless”, “calming”, “stylish” or “perfect”.

Explain whether the shade:
- softens the room;
- warms it up;
- cools it down;
- adds contrast;
- makes another finish stand out;
- quietens the space without making it feel flat;
- holds its own in bright light;
- works better as an accent than across every wall.

Historically approved thinking: **“Sage Green softens the room.”** Use the principle, not the exact wording as a template.

Do not imply that a colour shown on screen is an exact real-world paint match. Encourage physical swatches/testers and checking colour in real lighting when accuracy matters.

## Product and technical guardrails

Useful product content should connect:
1. the surface or project;
2. the problem or required result;
3. the relevant product/category or preparation step.

Useful recurring areas include:
- interior walls;
- exterior/weather applications;
- primers and preparation;
- cupboards/melamine where currently supported;
- tile or specialist surfaces where current Dulux documentation supports the application;
- finishes;
- tinting and mixing;
- colour comparisons;
- paint ranges;
- wallpaper combinations where the local offering is currently confirmed;
- current promotions.

Never invent or infer:
- local stock;
- prices;
- sizes;
- technical performance;
- coverage;
- durability;
- washability;
- UV or moisture suitability;
- substrate compatibility;
- drying/application claims;
- warranties.

Use current official Dulux product information or technical documentation for technical claims.

A national Dulux range does not prove that the Bloemfontein store currently stocks that exact product or size.

## Content, Reels and video

Dulux short-form content usually works best when:
- it is about 20 to 30 seconds;
- the hook lands in the first 2 to 3 seconds;
- something happens visually from the start;
- the store, staff, product, colour or process is central;
- it can be understood without unnecessary explanation.

Voice-over is optional. No-VO concepts must still make complete sense through visuals and on-screen text.

Prioritise:
- real staff;
- tinting and mixing;
- colour reveals;
- mixing-machine/process footage;
- ASMR details;
- real swatches;
- finish comparisons;
- colour comparisons;
- actual paint problems;
- surface preparation;
- product education;
- transformations;
- before/process/after;
- quick staff advice;
- practical project checklists;
- current promotions;
- occasional award/voting content while genuinely live.

Humour is welcome when it naturally comes from staff, colour names, customer dilemmas, painting frustrations or store processes. It is episodic, not the default voice. Do not force memes or trends.

Do not simply rename old concepts such as paint-shake tests, colour-name humour or ASMR mixing. Find a fresh mechanic.

## Staff and Irene

Real staff are a strong part of the brand.

Historical CG work positioned **Irene** as an approachable in-store advice personality. Use her where relevant only after confirming her current role and availability.

Staff content should answer a real customer question or demonstrate expertise, not merely show someone posing with a tin.

Never invent staff names, roles or availability.

## Visual direction

Prioritise:
- real store imagery;
- real staff;
- colour walls and physical swatches;
- paint being poured, mixed or tinted;
- close-ups of texture and finish;
- readable product labels and colour codes;
- actual rooms and surfaces;
- before/process/after;
- hands-on action and movement.

Avoid:
- generic lifestyle stock imagery when real store/product material is available;
- repetitive static tin-on-counter shots;
- visuals that imply a digital shade is an exact paint match;
- too much text fighting with colour/product visuals.

## Image generation and editing

When generating or editing client visuals:
- preserve real Dulux products, packaging, labels, logos and brand marks unless the user explicitly asks to replace or redesign them;
- preserve identifiable real people and their key appearance unless the requested edit specifically changes them;
- preserve the original composition, crop, product placement and important store context unless a broader redesign is requested;
- change only what the user asked to change;
- do not invent new product claims, prices, promotions, labels, signage, staff details or store information inside an image;
- do not alter product colours or paint shades in a way that could imply an inaccurate real-world match unless the task specifically requires a conceptual colour visual;
- for clean-up work, remove distractions or improve lighting/finish without unnecessarily redesigning the scene;
- for commercial retouching, keep products and people recognisable and believable.

## Current social/content lessons

Recent accessible CG content reinforces:
- short, readable copy often performs the job better than long polished wording;
- captions should add to artwork, not duplicate it;
- colour content should explain the effect in the room;
- expertise is stronger proof than generic popularity;
- premium/refined wording must be supported by something real;
- voting, awards, app CTAs, promotions and special campaign treatments are temporary, not permanent feed rules;
- the content mix should stay varied across product, colour, staff, process, education, transformation, promotion and occasional campaign content;
- humour should support the content, not dominate it;
- five focused hashtags are the ceiling.

A published post is supporting evidence, not automatically a permanent rule. Explicit client/CG corrections and repeated approvals take priority.

## Calls to action

Good CTAs reduce uncertainty or tell the customer what to do next:
- bring a colour code;
- bring a room photo or inspiration reference;
- compare physical swatches in store;
- ask which product belongs on a surface;
- ask which primer or finish makes sense;
- phone for current stock or pricing;
- visit the store to see the colour/finish in person;
- vote when a current campaign is genuinely live.

Do not use empty CTAs such as “discover the difference” or “elevate your space”.

App/download CTAs are campaign-specific and must be rechecked before reuse.

## Repeated corrections and mistakes to avoid

Do not:
- write generic corporate paint copy;
- solve generic wording by adding more adjectives;
- describe colour only with vague adjectives;
- repeat artwork in the caption;
- use more than 5 hashtags;
- mix Afrikaans and English unless requested;
- reuse old sale prices;
- advertise national ranges as confirmed local stock;
- promise perfect colour matching from every source;
- guarantee digital colour accuracy;
- invent contact details, social handles, delivery areas or trade terms;
- assume a historical staff role is still current;
- treat one published execution as a permanent rule;
- recycle overused voting phrases;
- let unrelated funeral/grief wording from the old Tone of Voice PDF influence Dulux work.

## Unresolved facts: never guess

The following are not currently safe to publish without fresh confirmation:
- store email;
- current WhatsApp number;
- first-party local Facebook and Instagram handles/URLs;
- first-party store opening hours;
- Sunday/public-holiday hours;
- delivery availability, area and terms;
- current wallpaper brands/range;
- contractor/trade-account terms;
- full current local stock list;
- Irene’s current formal title and availability;
- any historical surface-specific product/service claim that has not been rechecked.

## Bottom line

Make Dulux content useful enough that a Bloemfontein customer can picture the colour, understand the product decision or recognise the person/process that will help them in store.
