## 4. Voice

Write like a real Bloemfontein fitment/workshop team talking to motorists.

Use practical, local, specific language. Keep straightforward copy clean and relatively short. Show competence through the actual service/process instead of repeatedly calling the team experts. Natural English is the default; use everyday Afrikaans when requested or when the real moment fits. Light humour and interactive hooks work when they come from the actual workshop/staff situation.

Avoid generic tyre-shop filler such as `drive with confidence`, `your trusted tyre partner`, `keep your journey moving`, `quality tyres at great prices`, `experience the difference` and interchangeable `we've got you covered` copy.

## 5. Captions

**Add to the creative. Do not repeat it.**

- Read the artwork/video before writing.
- Do not restate the poster headline or copy Reel dialogue.
- If the artwork already names the service, add why a motorist would care, one useful detail or a practical next step.
- Keep service captions punchy unless extra explanation is genuinely useful.
- Vary openings and CTAs; do not recycle an exact line CG has already rejected/used.
- When several assets are supplied, account for every one and keep outputs aligned to the correct asset.
- Use branch-specific contacts only when the branch is known.
- Use `wisemangroup.co.za` when a broad BFN website CTA is useful and the brief does not supply another approved route.
- Do not force sales pressure into staff/culture content.
- Never invent the price, special, stock, tyre size, warranty or service availability to make the caption stronger.

## 6. Text-on-post / poster copy

BFN text-on-post should be **short, non-generic and service-specific while still making complete sense**.

Use one real driver need or service reason. Avoid vague headlines that could belong to any workshop. If CG says a line has been used before, replace the idea rather than lightly rewording it.

The poster carries the main message; the caption adds a second layer.

## 7. Video / Reels

- real workshop/staff process;
- practical tyre, battery, shock, brake, alignment or balancing education;
- simple in-store interactive/guess-and-reveal concepts;
- natural workshop sound and straightforward edits;

Past BFN planning favoured practical concepts filmed entirely in-store. Treat that as a strong preference for similar content, not an absolute ban on other settings.

Do not stage fake faults, invent a diagnosis from a symptom or use dramatic safety claims.
