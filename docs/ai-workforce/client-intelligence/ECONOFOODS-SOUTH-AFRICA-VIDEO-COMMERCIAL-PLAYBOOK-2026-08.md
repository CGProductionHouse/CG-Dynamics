### The product-essential comedy test

For a funny idea, ask:

## 7. Language, rhythm, emojis and captions

### Language

- Default to natural **English** unless the brief/asset asks for another language.
- Natural South African expressions or a short Afrikaans/local phrase may work when they genuinely fit the speaker/audience.
- Do not force bilingual copy or slang to manufacture personality.
- Spoken dialogue must sound like something the employee would actually say on camera.

### Sentence rhythm

For scripts and short captions:
- one clear idea at a time;
- short, conversational lines;
- interruptible dialogue;
- varied openings and sentence lengths;
- avoid polished corporate paragraphs.

### Emojis

Recent public social content is often emoji-rich, but there is no verified rule requiring emojis in every CG caption.
- Use them when they support the platform/creative.
- Omit them when the asset is cleaner without them.
- Do not use emojis to compensate for generic wording.

### Caption rule: add, do not repeat

When a video/poster already says the product, offer, dialogue or CTA, the caption should add a second layer, for example:
- the relatable customer moment behind it;
- one useful product/use-case detail;
- a quick reason the offer matters;
- a sharp observation;
- branch/validity context where current and necessary;
- a simple next action.

Do not transcribe the video or restate artwork line-for-line.

### Generic copy to reject by default

Avoid interchangeable lines such as:
- `quality and affordability go hand in hand`;
- `something for everyone`;
- `shop smarter`;
- `great value every day`;
- `your one-stop shop`;
- `discover the difference`;
- `experience excellence`;
- `elevate your experience`;
- `where quality meets...`.

If the wording could sit under ten other supermarket brands unchanged, rewrite it.

## 8. What makes an Econofoods video worth shooting

A strong idea should answer these before scripting:

1. **Commercial job:** What must this video do?
2. **Real anchor:** Which product, offer, meal, customer truth, staff truth or store moment makes the idea uniquely relevant?
3. **Fast hook:** What is understood in the first two seconds?
4. **Proof/reveal:** What will the viewer actually see that earns the claim/joke?
5. **Memory/action:** What should remain after the punchline?
6. **Feasibility:** Can the idea be shot with the actual staff, store/office and product constraints in the brief?

A clever concept that cannot be executed cleanly with the available employee/location is not a useful CG concept.

## 11. Project-specific production lessons

Accessible Econofoods Project history adds practical lessons beyond the August playbook.

### In-store staff constraints

Recent work has needed concepts that can function with **employees rather than actors/CG staff**, sometimes with very limited employee availability.

Therefore:
- write the role for a real employee first;
- keep dialogue easy to perform;
- build energy through camera movement, product handoffs, B-roll, cuts and text rather than requiring exaggerated acting;
- do not assume customers/buyers/extras are available unless the brief says so;
- if only one employee is available, make one person enough.

Do not make `one employee only` a permanent company rule; it is a recurring production constraint that must be checked per shoot.

### Central-office employee content

A presenter can remain at a desk/one position when the concept uses:
- relevant B-roll;
- product inserts;
- on-screen text;
- changing backgrounds/angles;
- staff/process footage;
- strong edit rhythm.

Office content should still connect to how employees support/grow the business, not default to generic meeting/laptop montages.

### Ready-made meal/product FAQ lessons

Project work around ready-made meals showed that simple FAQ formats can be useful when the product facts come from the supplied packaging/brief.

Do not turn launch-specific flavours, heating instructions, storage instructions or expiry wording into permanent range facts. Re-read the current packaging/brief each time.

### Rebrand / new-look continuity

Project work has also used a `new look, same familiar product` reassurance angle.

Treat this as a **campaign mechanism**, not a permanent claim. Never say the taste/product is unchanged unless that exact current rebrand brief/client evidence confirms it.

### Avoid repetitive skits

A prior `dilemma/choice`-style idea was rejected as too similar to earlier work. Durable lesson: when the user asks for more ideas, change the **mechanism**, not only the product or wording.
