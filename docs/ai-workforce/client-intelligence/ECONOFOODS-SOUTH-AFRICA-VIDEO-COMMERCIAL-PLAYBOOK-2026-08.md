# Econofoods South Africa — Video-First Commercial Playbook

Last updated: 2026-08-07
Status: Current-source client intelligence. Video-first scope only. Keep isolated to Econofoods South Africa. Apply `CLIENT-MEMORY-FRESHNESS-AND-VERIFICATION-PROTOCOL.md` before operational use.

## Why this pack exists

CG Production House currently works with Econofoods primarily through video production rather than full-service marketing. This pack therefore does **not** try to turn Econofoods into a generic marketing account. It exists to make every video idea, script, shoot and edit commercially sharper while protecting the brand's cheeky, energetic personality.

The objective is to help CG answer four questions before every shoot:

1. What commercial job must this video do?
2. What product, price, craving, convenience or customer truth gives the joke a reason to exist?
3. What is the fastest entertaining hook that fits Econofoods rather than generic supermarket content?
4. What should the viewer remember or do after watching?

Humour is not the strategy. Humour is the delivery mechanism.

## Customer language and market reality

Independent South African customer discussion provides useful directional language, but must not be treated as statistically representative research.

Recurring themes found in current public discussions:

- Econofoods is associated with bulk frozen foods and strong prices;
- consumers specifically mention chicken, bacon, fish, frozen vegetables, cheese and freezer products;
- shoppers compare Econofoods favourably against major supermarkets on selected categories;
- price pressure is a major reason customers seek Econofoods out;
- convenience and breadth are also positively mentioned;
- some consumers report inconsistent product preference or quality across particular items, so blanket `best quality` claims should be avoided unless product-specific evidence supports them.

Commercial implication:

**Value is already believed. Video does not need to scream `cheap` every time. It can dramatise how far the shopper's money, freezer or meal plan goes.**

## Brand personality — operating definition for CG

User-confirmed current direction: Econofoods is a **cheeky brand**.

For CG, cheeky should mean:

- playful;
- quick;
- self-aware;
- slightly mischievous;
- comfortable with everyday South African humour;
- not too polished to be human;
- confident enough to make the product the punchline;
- commercially clear underneath the joke.

Cheeky should **not** become:

- random skits unrelated to shopping;
- forced Gen-Z slang;
- insulting customers;
- mocking affordability or financial pressure;
- fake viral trends with no product relevance;
- childish acting;
- sexually suggestive humour around ordinary food products unless explicitly approved;
- copy that sounds like every social-media intern trying to be funny.

## The Econofoods comedy rule

Every funny video should pass this test:

> Remove the product or offer. Does the joke still work exactly the same?

If yes, the idea is too generic.

A strong Econofoods concept makes the product, price, freezer, bulk pack, store, meal or customer behaviour essential to the joke.

## Primary commercial video jobs

Every video should be tagged with one dominant commercial job before scripting.

### 1. Craving creation

Goal: make a food item feel immediately desirable.

Best for:

- chips;
- ribs;
- chicken;
- seafood;
- bacon;
- desserts;
- snackable products;
- braai products.

Creative direction:

- macro food visuals;
- sound-led cooking;
- reaction comedy;
- `you said you were not hungry` scenarios;
- reveal timing;
- indulgent close-ups;
- product enters before the logo.

### 2. Value dramatization

Goal: show what the shopper gets, not only the number on the poster.

Better than a static price readout:

- trolley comparison;
- freezer-fill challenge;
- `what R___ gets you`;
- family meal stack;
- side-by-side pack-size reveal;
- restaurant or braai output from one purchase;
- `month-end maths` comedy.

### 3. Product discovery

Goal: expose surprising or overlooked categories.

Hooks:

- `Wait, Econofoods sells this?`
- `Things I did not know were hiding in this freezer.`
- staff picks;
- weirdly specific cravings;
- `three things under one roof`;
- category roulette.

### 4. Store traffic

Goal: create a reason to visit now.

Best inputs:

- new specials;
- new store;
- seasonal stock;
- opening weekends;
- regional promotion;
- event activation;
- limited-time availability.

### 5. Basket expansion

Goal: make customers add complementary items.

Video structures:

- `You came for chicken. Here is how that went.`;
- one meal, full basket;
- starter + main + side + dessert;
- braai basket;
- school-lunch basket;
- freezer emergency kit;
- lazy-Sunday basket.

### 6. Foodservice / reseller demand

Goal: remind restaurants, caterers, takeaways and grocery resellers that Econofoods is not only consumer retail.

Creative direction:

- prep-volume visual proof;
- bulk boxes moving through a kitchen;
- `feeding 50 people` framing;
- cost-control scenarios;
- behind-the-scenes delivery / stock preparation;
- fast service rather than lifestyle fluff.

### 7. Brand affection

Goal: make Econofoods feel like a brand people enjoy following, not merely a price list.

This is where the cheeky personality can run hardest, but the content should still originate from real shopper/staff/product behaviour.

## Recommended recurring video franchises

These should be treated as repeatable formats rather than one-off ideas.

## A. `Econo Maths`

Premise:

Take a familiar grocery problem and solve it visually with value.

Examples:

- `Payday was three days ago. Relax.`
- `One bag. Five lunches.`
- `What the family thinks R500 buys vs what Econofoods says.`
- `The freezer before month-end / after Econofoods.`

Tone:

Dry, fast, clever. Avoid humiliating people for budget constraints.

Commercial use:

Excellent for promotional bursts and price-led paid ads.

## B. `Freezer Problems`

Premise:

The universal behaviour around a full or empty freezer becomes the joke.

Examples:

- standing with the door open for inspiration;
- hiding the good snacks from the family;
- the person who says `there's no food` while the freezer is packed;
- trying to fit one more bulk pack;
- discovering the product you forgot you bought.

Commercial use:

Brand affection + product discovery + bulk-buying association.

## C. `Want ___ with that?`

Build on the existing `Want chips with that?` campaign lineage rather than abandoning it.

Format:

A familiar food order or scenario escalates into an unexpectedly generous Econofoods add-on/reveal.

Why it works:

- ownable repetition;
- easy episodic structure;
- product-led punchline;
- can move across chips, sauces, sides, cheese, desserts, seafood, bacon and more.

Guardrail:

Do not repeat the exact same beat until it becomes predictable. The recurring title can stay while the visual mechanism changes.

## D. `Staff Shouldn't Say This`

Premise:

Staff member gives an overly candid but useful product opinion.

Examples:

- `This one never survives until Friday in my house.`
- `Buy two. I'm saving you a second trip.`
- `Don't ask me where aisle three is. Ask me where the good chips are.`

Commercial use:

Humanises the store, creates recommendation authority and gives staff personality.

Need:

Use real staff who are comfortable on camera. Never script fake testimonials as if spontaneous.

## E. `The Econo Test`

Premise:

Test a real commercial question on camera.

Examples:

- how many portions from one pack;
- how many air-fryer baskets;
- how much freezer space required;
- blind taste comparison where legally and reputationally appropriate;
- `can this feed four?`;
- cook-time test using official instructions.

Commercial use:

High credibility because the video proves something useful.

## F. `POV: You Came In For One Thing`

Premise:

Classic retail basket creep, but product-specific.

Opening:

`POV: you came in for chicken.`

Cut:

Basket/trolley now contains dessert, chips, cheese, bacon, seafood, vegetables.

End:

Simple price/value or Econofoods beat.

## G. `Meal Rescue`

Premise:

Everyday cooking emergency solved with freezer convenience.

Scenarios:

- forgotten school lunch;
- unplanned visitors;
- rugby crowd arriving;
- `what's for dinner?` at 17:45;
- load-shedding-adjacent convenience only where product preparation safely supports it;
- quick air-fryer meal.

Commercial use:

Convenience plus product education.

## H. `Bulk Behaviour`

Premise:

Make the irrational joy of buying a massive value pack the joke.

Examples:

- carrying the pack like a trophy;
- family member guarding freezer territory;
- someone calculating unit value way too seriously;
- `small pack people vs bulk pack people`.

## Commercial video structures that suit Econofoods

## Structure 1 — Joke first, sell second

0–2 sec: instantly recognisable customer situation.

2–7 sec: escalation.

7–15 sec: product/offer becomes the punchline.

15–20 sec: clear pack/price/store/date where applicable.

Best for organic-first content that can later be boosted.

## Structure 2 — Product first, cheek second

0–2 sec: irresistible food/product visual.

2–5 sec: surprising line or reaction.

5–12 sec: value/product proof.

12–20 sec: offer and CTA.

Best for paid conversion creative.

## Structure 3 — Fake-serious commercial

Open like an overproduced luxury advertisement.

Use dramatic lighting, slow motion and overly serious voiceover.

Twist: the subject is something everyday such as chips, bacon or a bulk box.

Why it fits:

The contrast is inherently cheeky while still making the product look excellent.

## Structure 4 — Customer truth

Start with a real behaviour rather than a scripted joke:

- freezer door browsing;
- month-end calculator;
- hiding snacks;
- feeding unexpected guests;
- shopping while hungry;
- pretending a bulk pack is `for the family`.

Then exaggerate only slightly.

This should become a major source of Econofoods concepts because recognisable behaviour is more shareable than arbitrary comedy.

## Structure 5 — Challenge / proof

Ask one simple question and answer it visually.

Examples:

- `Can one box feed the whole office?`
- `How many portions are actually in here?`
- `R___ braai challenge.`

High value for paid and organic because the narrative is clear without sound.

## Video length architecture

CG's historical Econofoods package is already strongly suited to short commercial video.

Recommended default masters:

- 15–25 sec for the main social cut;
- 6–10 sec hook cut for paid retargeting / Stories;
- 25–40 sec only where the concept genuinely needs a setup;
- longer event / campaign edits remain separate deliverables.

Do not stretch a 12-second idea to 30 seconds merely because the package says 20–30 seconds.

## First two seconds — non-negotiable

Avoid:

- logo intros;
- exterior store establishing shot;
- staff member walking into frame;
- `Hi guys`;
- `Did you know...`;
- generic trolley pushing;
- slow product pan with no conflict.

Prefer:

- surprising product visual;
- direct line;
- visible price/value shock;
- food action;
- recognisable shopper problem;
- unexpected reaction;
- punchline setup already in progress.

## Scriptwriting standard

Scripts should sound spoken, not written.

Econofoods dialogue should usually be:

- one idea per line;
- short;
- interruptible;
- conversational;
- locally natural;
- less `brand voice paragraph`, more human reaction.

### Bad

`At Econofoods, we believe that quality and affordability should always go hand in hand.`

### Better

`This whole box? Ja. Check the price again.`

### Bad

`Visit your nearest Econofoods store for incredible savings.`

### Better

`You've got freezer space. Use it.`

The second examples are directional style examples, not approved client copy.

## Editing language

Recommended Econofoods edit feel:

- fast cold opens;
- hard cuts over excessive transitions;
- strong food sound;
- reaction timing;
- punch-in crops where comedic;
- on-screen text only where it improves comprehension;
- prices and offer conditions remain extremely legible;
- meme language only when it still feels current at posting time;
- music supports pace but does not carry the concept;
- food should look appetising even in comedy-led videos.

Avoid turning every clip into frantic TikTok editing. Brand recognition needs repeatable rhythm.

## Visual world

Econofoods has permission to be colourful and energetic.

CG should use:

- freezer doors;
- product stacks;
- trolleys;
- bulk packs;
- loading/stock movement;
- checkout moments;
- real staff;
- home/kitchen scenarios where the product story requires it;
- controlled tabletop food beauty;
- exaggerated premium lighting for parody commercials.

The store itself should feel active and abundant, not cheap or chaotic.

## Product beauty still matters

A cheeky brand can accidentally make the food look secondary.

Every food-led video should capture at least one strong commercial beauty moment:

- steam;
- crisp texture;
- cheese pull where genuine;
- sauce pour;
- frying sound;
- grill marks;
- frozen-to-cooked transformation;
- clean pack reveal;
- serving moment.

The laugh earns attention. The beauty shot creates appetite.

## Price and promotion videos

Price cannot be a tiny end-frame afterthought when value is the commercial job.

Recommended hierarchy:

1. Hook.
2. Product.
3. Pack size / quantity.
4. Price.
5. Dates / participating-store qualifications where required.
6. CTA.

Never use outdated offer footage as evergreen content without removing mutable price/date overlays.

## Regional versus national creative

Econofoods publishes region-specific specials and has a multi-region store network.

Each video must be classified as:

- national evergreen;
- national promotion;
- region-specific promotion;
- store-opening/local activation;
- foodservice/B2B;
- event/brand culture.

Do not create a beautiful national-looking edit around a deal that applies to only one region without an obvious location treatment.

## Store-opening videos

CG has previous store-opening production experience with Econofoods.

The strongest store-opening formula is not a standard ribbon-cut montage.

Build three phases:

### Before

- local tease;
- build / stocking transformation;
- `your freezer is about to have options`;
- staff faces;
- location clue reveal.

### Opening day

- doors opening;
- first trolley;
- first customer reactions;
- strongest product/value visuals;
- crowd energy;
- location anchor.

### After

- `what people actually bought`;
- staff favourites;
- local customer comments;
- overlooked sections;
- next-week offer.

## Event content

CG has also covered Econofoods conferences, Food Fiesta and sports/event work.

For these jobs, capture footage in three buckets:

1. **event proof** — scale, turnout, energy;
2. **human brand** — staff, culture, interaction;
3. **future commercial assets** — clean product/staff/B-roll that can be reused later.

Do not edit all event footage into one sentimental highlight reel and lose the future campaign value.

## Foodservice / B2B video opportunity

Official Econofoods sources confirm restaurant, grocery-store, reseller and foodservice servicing.

This deserves a different visual language from consumer comedy.

Best concepts:

- cost-per-portion stories;
- volume prep;
- kitchen speed;
- freezer stock confidence;
- restaurant operator testimonial where genuine;
- delivery and picking;
- `what a takeaway needs for Friday night`;
- product consistency and pack format.

Tone can remain human and cheeky, but credibility outranks jokes.

## Competitor context

Econofoods competes for wallet share against several different categories, not one clean competitor set:

- major supermarket chains such as Checkers, Pick n Pay and Spar;
- Food Lover's Market on specials and food value;
- independent butcheries on meat quality and trust;
- discount/value retailers and specialist frozen-food stores;
- local wholesalers and cash-and-carry operators;
- Best Before-style discount shopping for budget-driven customers, though the product model differs.

Public customer discussion suggests shoppers often split baskets between stores rather than choosing one retailer exclusively.

Video implication:

Do not try to prove `Econofoods replaces your whole grocery shop` unless the actual range and campaign support that claim.

A more believable strategy is to own specific high-value missions:

- freezer fill;
- bulk proteins;
- quick meal stock;
- event/party food;
- month-end value;
- foodservice volume.

## What not to copy from mainstream supermarket content

Avoid defaulting to:

- presenter walks aisle listing specials;
- price cards flying onto screen one by one;
- generic recipe tutorial every month;
- trolley montage;
- staff dancing because a trend exists;
- `top five products` with no premise;
- endless voiceover saying `save, save, save`;
- fake family breakfast scenes;
- over-polished corporate brand film for routine monthly content.

Econofoods should feel quicker, cheekier and more product-obsessed.

## Shoot-day architecture for a four-video monthly package

When CG is delivering four core short videos, do not conceive four unrelated clips.

Recommended monthly mix:

### Video 1 — Hero commercial

One strong concept with the most production value.

Purpose:

Brand memorability + product or promotion.

### Video 2 — Value / offer proof

Clear commercial conversion job.

Purpose:

Traffic / sales.

### Video 3 — Human / cheeky franchise

Recurring format using staff or customer truth.

Purpose:

Brand affection and organic reach.

### Video 4 — Product discovery / food beauty

Show something people may not know, or make one item look irresistible.

Purpose:

Basket expansion / craving.

This gives the month balance instead of four price ads or four skits.

## Performance learning system for CG

Because CG only controls video production, performance learning should be attached to each video rather than pretending CG owns the full marketing funnel.

For every video store:

- concept/franchise;
- commercial job;
- featured product/category;
- region/store;
- promotion or evergreen;
- hook type;
- duration;
- people on camera;
- organic vs paid use;
- first-three-second retention where available;
- average watch time;
- completion rate;
- shares;
- saves;
- comments;
- clicks / offer response where available;
- sales or store-lift evidence only when supplied by Econofoods.

The aim is to learn patterns such as:

- Which humour type retains best?
- Which products create saves versus shares?
- Do staff-led videos outperform actor-led videos?
- Does price shown in the first five seconds improve paid conversion?
- Which recurring formats survive repetition?
- Which store-specific videos create local engagement?

Do not call a video commercially successful because it has views alone.

## Campaign territories worth developing

### `Freezer Rich`

Reframe wealth as having a freezer stocked with useful food.

Visual humour:

- opening freezer like a luxury safe;
- dramatic jewellery-style product lighting;
- someone proudly showing bulk packs.

Commercial fit:

Bulk/value, without using poverty humour.

### `Month-End Olympics`

Different shopping behaviours treated like sports disciplines.

Examples:

- trolley speed;
- calculator speed;
- freezer Tetris;
- bulk-pack carry.

Strong for recurring monthly-special periods.

### `The Good Stuff Is Frozen`

Challenge the idea that frozen equals boring.

Needs excellent food transformation visuals and careful product truth.

### `Econo Emergency Services`

Meal emergencies solved dramatically.

Examples:

- guests arrived;
- school lunch forgotten;
- braai doubled in size;
- lazy dinner.

Comedy comes from treating ordinary meal problems as high-stakes emergencies.

### `Serious Food. Unserious Ads.`

Not necessarily a public tagline. Useful internal creative principle.

Make food and value credible; let the advertising be playful.

## Guardrails

### Food claims

Do not claim:

- healthier;
- fresher;
- superior quality;
- locally sourced;
- halal;
- allergy-safe;
- sustainably sourced;
- specific nutritional benefits;

unless current product or Econofoods evidence supports the exact claim.

### Pricing

Every price-led video requires current verification of:

- product;
- pack size;
- price;
- dates;
- participating stores/regions;
- stock wording where required.

### Comparative advertising

Avoid direct superiority claims against named competitors unless substantiated and approved.

### Customer humour

Never make the customer look stupid for needing value. Joke about recognisable behaviour, not financial hardship.

### Staff culture

The `HO HOLA` culture is an official company concept. Use it as context for human content, but do not invent employee testimonials or internal claims.
