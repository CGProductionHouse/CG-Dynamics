## Honest diagnosis of the current public presence

## 1. The website communicates ambition but not enough commercial specificity

The current site repeatedly uses language such as:

- extraordinary;
- innovative;
- bespoke;
- captivating;
- cutting edge;
- beyond ordinary;
- results that speak;
- out of this world.

The visual world is distinctive, but the language often remains generic agency language. A serious buyer still needs answers to:

- Which businesses are the best fit?
- What exact problems does CG solve?
- What does a typical engagement include?
- What commercial outcomes has CG helped create?
- How is strategy different from ordinary social posting?
- How are content, paid media, website, SEO and follow-up connected?
- How quickly can CG start?
- What is the process from enquiry to launch?
- What proof exists by industry and service?

The core website gap is not lack of services. It is insufficiently visible proof, offer architecture and conversion logic.

## 2. The service architecture is too broad and undifferentiated

The public offer currently mixes:

- ongoing social management;
- one-off posters;
- high-end video;
- studio rental;
- websites;
- photography;
- design;
- animation;
- strategy;
- personal milestones.

This range is a strength operationally, but it makes the agency harder to understand commercially. Buyers should see a small number of clear solution systems, then supporting production services.

## 3. There is a mismatch between premium language and some low entry prices

Very low studio entry prices can attract useful local volume, but they may also weaken the premium agency signal if presented alongside “exclusive” and “high-end” positioning without clear separation.

The studio should have its own buyer journey and product logic. Agency retainers and strategic production should not appear equivalent to hourly room hire.

## 4. Proof is not organised around buyer decisions

The current gallery contains many visual assets, but a gallery is not automatically a case study.

A buyer needs proof structured as:

- client context;
- problem or opportunity;
- work delivered;
- creative reasoning;
- channels used;
- result or observed response;
- constraints and limitations;
- testimonial where approved.

CG already has a diverse client base and considerable real work. The public presence should make that experience legible.

## 5. The agency must prove performance without pretending attribution

CG Production House should move confidently toward performance-led marketing, but must not claim that likes, views or reach caused sales where sales tracking is missing.

Every client engagement should distinguish:

- content outputs;
- distribution;
- platform performance;
- enquiries;
- quotes or bookings;
- sales/revenue where connected;
- factors outside CG's control.

This honest reporting stance can become a competitive advantage.
