## Human creative rule

Start from the exact item, room, material detail or process shown.

A good C&L line should make the reader notice something real about the furniture or understand why it matters in a home/project.

If the sentence could sit under ten unrelated furniture brands unchanged, rewrite it.

Avoid default AI/interiors filler such as:

- “elevate your space”;
- “transform your home”;
- “timeless elegance”;
- “where style meets comfort”;
- “crafted for every lifestyle”;
- “where quality meets…”;
- “discover the difference”;
- vague “luxury” / “premium” statements with no real detail.

Premium/refined language is fine only when tied to something visible or verified.

## Caption rules

- Default to natural English.
- Do not force Afrikaans or language mixing.
- If Afrikaans is requested, keep it natural and conversational.
- Keep captions concise unless there is a real build story or useful buyer education.
- Normal default: about 1–4 useful sentences before contacts/hashtags.
- Add to the artwork/video instead of repeating its headline or dialogue.
- One clear point is stronger than a list of luxury adjectives.
- Do not turn every post into a sales pitch.
- When options are requested, make each angle genuinely different.

Useful C&L angle variation:

1. **Practical benefit** — what the piece does for the room/use.
2. **Feeling/use** — what living or gathering around the piece feels like, grounded in the visual.
3. **Craftsmanship** — a real detail in shaping, sanding, joinery, edge, finish or care.

Do not claim custom, made-to-order, available-now or ready-to-deliver status unless the exact current task confirms it.

## What captions should add

Add one useful layer such as:

- a visible workmanship detail;
- why the proportions suit the use;
- how the piece fits into the room;
- what process step the viewer is seeing;
- a useful question for a buyer;
- a direct commission/product action when the post is selling;
- a human observation about using the furniture every day.

## Text-on-post rules

Keep text-on-post short and focused on one idea.

Good directions:

- one exact product benefit;
- one visible material/craft detail;
- one room/use feeling;
- one commission/product CTA when relevant.

Avoid:

- generic luxury headings;
- a heading and subtitle that repeat each other;
- unsupported “best”, “premium-grade”, “heirloom” or lifetime wording;
- filling every post with the same words such as handcrafted/bespoke/timeless.

### Craft/process

### Lifestyle/home

Use lifestyle scenes to make the **real furniture** easier to imagine in use.

Keep the furniture as the hero. Show believable scale and real interaction with the piece.

### Commission/project story

When a real brief exists, tell the sequence:

customer need → material/design decision → process → finished piece → real delivery/installation/proof if available.

Never fake testimonials or project proof.

### Buyer education

Useful topics include:

- measuring a room before choosing a table;
- table proportions and circulation space;
- what to look at in edge/profile/base design;
- what information to send for a commission enquiry;
- finish/material comparisons only when exact current options are known.

## Image generation and editing

C&L image work must protect trust in the real furniture.

### Preserve the product

Unless redesign is explicitly requested, preserve:

- real proportions;
- tabletop size/thickness;
- edge/profile;
- grain/texture;
- finish tone;
- base/legs;
- joinery/construction details;
- branding/logos;
- important product placement and crop.

Do not make the table larger/smaller or structurally different just to fit a generated room.

### Physical realism

Avoid:

- furniture sitting on another table/surface unrealistically;
- impossible support geometry;
- wrong scale against hands/chairs/people;
- plastic-looking wood;
- duplicated product parts;
- décor/food blocking the furniture when the furniture is the subject.

A tight, believable product close-up is better than a wide fake-looking scene.

### Single-change edits

When the user asks for one change:

- make that change;
- leave unrelated parts alone;
- preserve already-approved objects, composition and product details;
- do not add/remove extra items without instruction.

### People

If a lifestyle scene includes people:

- make them naturally posed and anatomically believable;
- keep hands/skin/body proportions realistic;
- avoid defaulting to a visibly homogeneous cast when a broader South African lifestyle scene is intended;
- do not let people overpower the furniture unless requested.
