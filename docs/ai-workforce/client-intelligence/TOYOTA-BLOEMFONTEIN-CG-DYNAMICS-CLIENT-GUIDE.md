## 4. Voice

Toyota Bloemfontein should sound like the local team, not a national brochure or outside agency.

Use confident, professional and clean language. Keep it warm, local and specific. Use `we`, `our` and `us` for team/personality moments. Default to natural English; use natural everyday Afrikaans when requested or when the real moment suits it. Light humour works when the person, workshop or clip already makes it funny. Normally keep emojis to 1–2.

Avoid influencer hype, forced slang, forced jokes, generic dealership language, unsupported superlatives and empty “come see us” CTAs. Do not default to filler such as “elevate your journey”, “drive your dreams”, “where performance meets style”, “discover the difference”, “experience excellence” or “your perfect ride awaits”.

## 6. Poster / text-on-post

Keep artwork copy short, specific and readable.

Good directions:
- one real feature + why it matters;
- one service need + practical action;
- one parts need + enquiry route;
- one exact local offer with current terms;
- one real team/customer/community moment.

Do not overcrowd artwork or use generic lines that could belong to any dealership. Never imply a price, instalment, saving, stock position or finance term unless the current local source supplies it. The caption must add to the poster, not paraphrase it.

## 8. Vehicle content

Before publishing a model/offer claim, verify the exact local vehicle:

- model and variant;
- new/used/demo status;
- year if relevant;
- local availability;
- colour if the visual/claim depends on it;
- price;
- finance terms;
- warranty/service-plan inclusion;
- promotion dates/stock limits;
- correct enquiry route.

National Toyota availability is not local stock proof. Do not publish specs from memory. Verify the exact current model/variant source and then confirm the local unit.

For pre-owned vehicles, only state history, condition, certification, mileage, inspection or warranty facts that the exact listing supports. Do not assume every used vehicle is Toyota Certified/Automark.

## 9. Service and parts

Current local evidence supports service booking and parts enquiries.

Good content shows the real workshop/team/process, one practical maintenance point, the actual part when discussing parts, and what the customer should do next. Show competence instead of merely calling the team “experts”.

Never promise turnaround times, reuse old service hours, guarantee warranty outcomes or claim body-repair capability without current confirmation. Keep warranty, service plan and maintenance plan separate.

## 10. Team, handovers and community

Team content should feel internal. Use `we`, `our` and `us` naturally. Staff names/roles are mutable, so confirm them before publishing a named feature.

For customer handovers, use real customers with permission. Do not invent why they bought the vehicle or what the vehicle means to them.

Local golf, charity, family/padel and dealership-event content is valid community material. Each event/sponsorship is time-bound unless a current partnership is confirmed.

## 11. Images and editing

Prefer real dealership assets over stock imagery.

When editing a real vehicle or dealership image:

- preserve the vehicle’s exact shape and proportions;
- preserve badges, grille, lights, wheels and trim unless specifically asked to change them;
- preserve Toyota/CFAO branding and signage;
- do not add features/accessories the real vehicle does not have;
- do not turn the vehicle into a different trim/model during cleanup;
- keep real people recognisable and believable;
- do not fabricate customer handovers or staff;
- preserve the composition unless a redesign is requested;
- when one change is requested, change only that thing unless a broader redesign is explicitly requested.

Generated/concept imagery must never be presented as proof of local stock, a real customer/staff member, a real local event or a real vehicle configuration.
