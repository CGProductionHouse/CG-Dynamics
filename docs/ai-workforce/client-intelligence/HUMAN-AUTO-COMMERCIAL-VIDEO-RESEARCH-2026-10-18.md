## Commercial job and audience hypotheses

The film should earn a qualified next step: an exact-vehicle enquiry, a booked
test drive, a genuine viewing or a service booking. Views are a diagnostic,
not evidence a car sold. The following are creative hypotheses to validate with
the sales/service team, not researched demographic facts:

- A working buyer needs to see the vehicle solve a real task, not watch a badge montage.
- A household buyer needs to imagine an ordinary day using it, not a list of features.
- A used-car buyer needs specific evidence and answered questions, not broad reassurance.
- An owner needs to understand the booking/process next step and who will help.
- An unfamiliar-brand shopper needs a credible local introduction and a reason to experience it.

Choose one buyer problem and one action per film. Do not merge every brand,
branch, model and service into a single generic dealership video.

## Commercial concept treatments — options, not promised monthly deliverables

### 1. One day. More than one role. — Ford working/household crossover

**Commercial question:** Can this exact vehicle fit the buyer's work and home life?
Open on a match cut between a work bag and an ordinary household load, both
placed into the same real vehicle. Follow one consented protagonist through
two believable parts of their day, with the actual loading/cabin actions doing
the explaining. Reveal the exact model/derivative after the use-case hook.
Use a clear Human Auto branch end frame and a test-drive invitation.

**Proof to capture:** actual cargo fit, access and cabin use on the shoot unit.
No unsupported towing/payload, off-road, fuel-saving or safety claim. No staged
unsafe driving, exaggerated speed or implied customer testimonial. Proposed
45-second hero plus shorter cuts only if commissioned; not an added entitlement.

### 2. Bring your real-life checklist. — family SUV decision film

**Commercial question:** What should this household physically try on a test drive?
Use three practical moments, such as loading everyday items, settling into the
rear cabin and setting up the driver's position. Each shot answers a buyer's
question through an observable action rather than a specification caption.
The closing invitation is to bring the viewer's own checklist to the branch.

**Production difference:** intimate eye-level interior photography and clean
dialogue, rather than another orbiting beauty shot. Select the exact suitable
Ford or MG unit after availability and derivative checks; never imply a child
seat fits or that a safety feature exists without verification.

### 3. Before your next working day. — Ford Transit/business-use option

**Commercial question:** Is there a practical fit for this customer's actual work?
Follow a real consenting local business loading its ordinary equipment, making
a stop and getting on with the job. A salesperson supplies one verified answer
to that business's own vehicle question. The commercial payoff is a branch
conversation about fit, not a made-up calculation about profit or running costs.

This is conditional on an exact demo/unit, location and business participant.
Vehicle navigation on the site does not prove a local Transit is available.
Do not invent cargo dimensions, capacity or a customer endorsement.

### 4. See it properly. — Human Auto MG consideration film

**Commercial question:** What can a shopper learn from experiencing the car in person?
Begin with an unfamiliar-brand shopper's genuine question. Show a considered
sequence of the real cabin, controls, ordinary luggage use and a salesperson's
specific demonstration. Use restrained sound, material details and one local
human interaction; close on a viewing/test-drive action for MG Bloemfontein.

The premium feel comes from production craft, not calling every item luxury.
Only demonstrate features present on the filmed derivative. No comparative
price, superiority, ownership-cost or warranty assertion is required.

### 5. This exact car. Your actual questions. — pre-owned decision film

**Commercial question:** Is this listed unit worth the buyer's trip to view it?
Film one real current unit and answer three approved questions about that unit.
Show condition/detail evidence honestly, then a short presenter-led walkaround
and an exact viewing invitation. Produce separate takes for an evergreen
buyer-question opening and the unit-specific middle/end so a sold unit can be
withdrawn without misleading viewers with its old offer.

Do not claim an inspection standard, perfect condition, clean history, warranty,
finance approval or guaranteed stock unless the exact evidence is supplied.
Human Select branding/contact remains gated; the guide does not clear a guessed identity.

### 6. The people behind your next kilometre. — aftersales trust film

**Commercial question:** What actually happens after a customer makes a service enquiry?
Follow a real service advisor receiving the booking information, a technician
explaining one routine approved step and the advisor returning with the next
action. Make workshop sound and real process detail the visual proof. End on
the correct brand/branch booking route, not a sales enquiry number by default.

Film only the real agreed workflow. Keep job cards, registration plates,
customer details and screens out of frame or appropriately cleared. No remote
diagnosis, guaranteed completion time or invented service-plan inclusion.

## Production standard and useful deliverable design

Before scripting, lock the entity/branch, commercial question, actual unit,
verified product proof, protagonist/location permission and one CTA. Then:

1. Open with the buyer's situation or a distinctive action; establish Human Auto
   recognisably without spending the whole opening on a logo animation.
2. Capture a beginning, proof sequence and commercial payoff. Beauty shots serve
   that narrative; they do not replace it. Use real vehicle/ambient sound and
   controlled dialogue rather than relying entirely on a music bed.
3. Plan vertical-safe action and readable titles during framing. Design a mobile
   short cut and an appropriate hero framing; do not assume a centre crop is a
   separately art-directed film. Exact export/cutdown quantities remain scope-bound.
4. Keep the strongest factual proof visible, captions readable and the final
   action singular. Verify every badge/trim and all client-approved on-screen copy.
5. Use a dated release checklist for unit availability, contacts, offers and
   participant consent. Do not post through an expired promotion or sold stock.

## Testing, feedback and November-to-December learning

Start with one agreed treatment and two materially different openings from the
same shoot where scope permits. Hold the entity, vehicle, CTA, exposure window
and distribution conditions as comparable as practical. Record platform-specific
definitions for viewing/retention and clicks; never combine non-equivalent metrics.

Use a consistent campaign/creative label and the existing approved enquiry path.
Ask staff to record qualified enquiries, test-drive bookings, attendance and
where known the associated sales outcome. Separate measured facts from unknown
attribution. No new paid-media spend, CRM integration or tracking is authorised.

At the review, ask which question produced useful conversations, where viewers
left, whether the branch could fulfil enquiries and what staff heard repeatedly.
That evidence decides the next treatment or proof sequence; it is not a promise
of a percentage sales increase. November findings can inform December once they
exist. Do not invent future results to fill the next-month strategy.
