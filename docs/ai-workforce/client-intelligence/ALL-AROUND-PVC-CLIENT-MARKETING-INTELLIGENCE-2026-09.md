## 5. Human marketing position

The client should not sound like a generic "home transformation" brand. Its strongest content territory is **making material choices feel simpler and more practical**.

Customers are often trying to answer concrete questions:
- What material works for this ceiling, floor or wall?
- What does the finish actually look like?
- Which size/variant do I need?
- Is the product available now?
- How much maintenance will this add later?
- Who handles installation?
- What should I ask before I commit?

Marketing should reduce that uncertainty. Product education and finished-space inspiration can work together, but practical specificity should carry the message.

### Recommended positioning direction

All Around PVC helps people start with the right material conversation for the surface they want to change: ceiling, floor or wall.

This is a strategic direction, not a slogan that must appear verbatim.

## 6. Caption voice and human creative rules

### What feels right

- **English is the default** for current social copy unless the task specifically asks for Afrikaans or another language.
- Plain, natural, locally understandable wording.
- Human and practical rather than polished-to-death.
- Concrete product/use-case detail over broad lifestyle claims.
- Short to medium captions for normal posts; do not over-explain a simple product visual.
- A caption should sound like someone who understands renovation choices, not like a generic corporate brand.
- Useful education is welcome, but keep technical explanation in ordinary language.

### Caption must add, not repeat

If the artwork already says:
- the product name;
- stock status;
- sizes;
- a headline;
- a CTA;

the caption should add a different layer such as:
- why that matters for the project;
- one practical choice or use case;
- what to check before buying;
- a simple maintenance point;
- a human observation;
- the next useful action.

The user repeatedly rejected captions that simply echoed poster wording.

### Hook styles that suit the client

Use hooks built around:
- a familiar renovation problem;
- a decision customers actually make;
- a practical "before you buy/check this" detail;
- real timing/stock pressure only when current stock is freshly confirmed;
- a room/surface use case;
- a material comparison or finish question;
- an installation/process moment shown in the creative.

Examples of direction, not fixed templates:
- `Before the ceiling goes up, this is the part worth checking.`
- `A wall finish is easy to love in a sample. The real question is how it works in your room.`
- `Two ceiling lengths, one practical question: what suits the job you are planning?`

### Rejected / overused patterns

Avoid by default:
- `transform your space`;
- `elevate your home`;
- `quality meets style`;
- `upgrade your space`;
- `discover the difference`;
- `experience excellence`;
- `your trusted partner`;
- `solutions for every need`;
- decorative/fancy headings that say little;
- generic "great move" / "smart move" phrasing;
- repeating the exact words already visible on the poster;
- formulaic three-benefit sentences when one specific idea is stronger;
- empty luxury language that could belong to paint, furniture, property or tiles without changing the words.

A line is too generic if it could be pasted under an unrelated business with almost no change.

### Humour and personality

- Humour can be light and situational.
- Do not force jokes into product education.
- Relatable project frustration, cleaning/maintenance realities or renovation indecision can support wit.
- Do not use gimmicky slang just to sound human.

### Emoji use

No client-specific mandatory emoji convention is established. Use sparingly when it helps readability/platform tone; omit them when the creative is cleaner without them.
