## 8. Human creative and caption standard

The Project history repeatedly shows that technically correct legal copy is not enough.

### Captions

**Captions must add to the artwork/video, not repeat it.**

The strongest HMHI/Hill, McHardy & Herbst captions:

- approach the same subject from a second angle;
- explain why the legal topic matters in normal life;
- add one useful next step or practical point;
- sound like a person from the firm/agency wrote them;
- stay clear enough for a non-lawyer;
- do not overuse the firm name in every sentence;
- keep serious legal topics calm rather than alarmist;
- avoid copying the text-on-post into the caption;
- use a consultation CTA only when it suits the post.

### Text-on-post

Project corrections favour:

- specific, relatable headings over generic legal slogans;
- a clear title plus short supporting subtitle where appropriate;
- wording linked to the actual service/problem shown in the visual;
- short copy that still says something meaningful;
- varied sentence structures rather than every heading starting the same way;
- human phrasing for sensitive topics such as criminal charges, family matters and estates;
- multiple distinct options by default for creative copy tasks unless the user asks for one.

Avoid formulaic filler such as:

- `your trusted legal partner`;
- `protecting your rights` with no specific context;
- `legal solutions tailored to you`;
- `when it matters`;
- `starts here`;
- `with confidence` as a default close;
- excessive `legal guidance` phrasing;
- fear-based or aggressively confrontational hooks;
- influencer-style language, forced humour, slang or meme copy.
