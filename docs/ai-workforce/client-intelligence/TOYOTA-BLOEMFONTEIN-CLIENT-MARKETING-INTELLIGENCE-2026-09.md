## 6. Core audience and buyer thinking

### New-vehicle shoppers

Write around real questions:

- Which Toyota/variant fits the actual job or family need?
- What changes between trims?
- Is this vehicle at the Bloemfontein dealership now?
- What does the quoted price actually include?
- What are the finance assumptions?
- What are the service-plan/warranty terms for this exact deal?
- Can they trade in their current vehicle?
- What is the next practical step: enquire, visit, book a drive, request finance information or confirm stock?

Avoid generic “drive your dreams” copy. Show the reason the specific vehicle makes sense.

### Pre-owned shoppers

The current local dealer carries dynamic used stock, including non-Toyota makes. Useful content should focus on the **actual listed unit** and current evidence:

- year/model/variant;
- mileage;
- condition/history only if the listing supports it;
- actual features;
- current price;
- inspection/certification only when the exact vehicle qualifies;
- finance/trade-in only with current terms.

Never generalise a Toyota Certified/Automark listing template to every used vehicle.

### Service customers

Useful service content reduces uncertainty:

- what the customer should book;
- what happens when the vehicle arrives;
- who is working behind the scenes;
- service timing/hours when current;
- genuine Toyota process/parts only when supported;
- simple maintenance education;
- the difference between a service, repair, inspection, warranty claim or other work where relevant.

Humour works well in service content when the workshop/team moment creates the joke. It must not undermine competence or imply unsafe shortcuts.

### Parts customers

Useful parts content should show the real part/product, correct application and enquiry route. Do not claim local stock, fitment compatibility or “genuine” status without evidence for that item.

### Business/fleet buyers

Recent local social content supports fleet-vehicle/business messaging as a content direction. Keep it vehicle/application-led. Do not promise managed-fleet services, nationwide delivery, uptime guarantees or account terms unless the current local brief/source supports them.

## 7. Human marketing voice

Toyota Bloemfontein content should sound like the team at the dealership.

### Use

- confident, professional language;
- clean, short sentences;
- warm local wording;
- vehicle-specific detail;
- practical service/parts context;

- natural English;
- natural everyday Afrikaans when the brief asks for Afrikaans or the moment genuinely fits it;

- influencer-style hype;
- overdone emojis;
- forced jokes;
- slang that does not sound like the team;
- corporate dealership filler;
- national-brochure wording with no local relevance;

If a caption could be pasted onto a competing dealership without changing a word, it is too generic.
