## 4. Commercial and audience intelligence retained from the August pack

The August research remains strategically useful when treated as strategy, not as newly verified operational fact.

### Core commercial problem

Central Canvas does not merely sell fabric. The strongest customer problems are practical:
- cargo or equipment exposed to weather;
- damaged tarps creating downtime;
- covers/nets that do not suit the real application;
- repeat repair/replacement decisions;
- customers needing a local workshop/supplier that can explain what is required.

### Primary audience territories

1. **Trucking, logistics and fleet operators**  
   Care about load protection, downtime, repair/replacement decisions, repeatable fit, turnaround and dependable contact.

2. **Farmers and agricultural businesses**  
   Care about working-condition durability, seasonal transport, covers/netting for real farm use and repairs before busy periods. Do not infer a specific agricultural product unless confirmed.

3. **Industrial / construction / commercial buyers**  
   Care about fit-for-application, supplier reliability, procurement clarity, turnaround and proof.

4. **Home / outdoor customers**  
   Pool covers and roll-up lapa blinds are now current supported categories. Lead with the actual use case and visible product rather than generic lifestyle language.

The B2B manufacturing research remains the reusable foundation: technical and commercial buyers want risk reduction, exact application fit, useful proof, plain explanations and confidence about support. Do not repeat generic industrial research inside this client record.

## 5. Human marketing position

Central Canvas should feel like a real Bloemfontein workshop/manufacturer, not a generic corporate “solutions” brand and not an influencer page.

Strong territory:
- the actual truck, trailer, pool, lapa or cargo problem shown in the creative;
- workshop process and workmanship details;
- repair decisions and real damage;
- product fit and application;

- practical education customers can use;

Strategic direction:

This is a direction, not a slogan.

## 6. Caption voice and creative rules

### Core voice

- Confident, never arrogant.
- Clean and professional; clarity first.
- Local and grounded for South Africans.
- Natural, like real people behind the business.
- Warm without becoming overfamiliar.
- Write in the brand voice as **we/us**, not “they”.
- Short, active sentences are preferred for routine social.
- English is the default unless the task is in Afrikaans or a natural Afrikaans line improves the post.
- Afrikaans must sound like everyday spoken Afrikaans, not stiff/formal translation.
- No forced slang, fake enthusiasm or influencer-style hype.
- No em dashes in social copy.

### Captions must add, not repeat

If the artwork/video already says the product, headline, date, offer or CTA, the caption must add a second layer:
- what the customer should notice;
- why the application matters;
- a practical use/maintenance point;
- a process detail;
- a human workshop observation;
- a timely reason to enquire;
- a genuine customer benefit supported by evidence.

Do not simply rewrite the artwork into sentences.

### Durable user corrections from Project work

Central Canvas work has repeatedly rejected:
- generic wording;
- text that could belong to any business;
- repeated formulaic structures;
- overuse of the same nouns/verbs;
- “from ... to ...” constructions used as a default;
- headings/subtitles that say little;
- the word `finish` / `finished` as routine marketing filler;
- repeatedly calling products “covering” when a more exact product/action word exists;
- captions that repeat the post;
- cringy or overperformed humour.

Prefer:
- one clear product/job/customer point;
- specific headings such as the actual service when needed (`Truck Tarps & Repairs`);
- natural delivery/dispatch language when that is what the image shows;
- concrete workshop detail;
- customer relevance over decorative phrasing.

### Humour

Humour is allowed when it is natural to staff/workshop reality. It should feel like a business owner or team member making an observation, not a scripted influencer bit.

For short warehouse/service videos, prior Project direction favoured:
- staff talking to camera;
- real services/products;
- natural humour;
- an out-of-the-box premise without becoming cringe;
- enough time for the idea to land rather than an artificially short gag.

## 7. Product-specific content behaviour

### Truck tarps and repairs

This is a major content pillar. Good angles:
- real damage and what is being repaired;
- inspection before rain/route/work;
- workshop repair process;
- stitching, seams, eyelets, reinforcement or fit only when visibly/source-supported;
- keeping a working vehicle in service, without inventing turnaround;
- real truck application rather than isolated generic tarp imagery.

Do not promise a repair time, waterproofness, legal load compliance or exact material spec without current evidence.

### Pool covers

Use real pool/application context and current product evidence. Do not automatically claim:
- child-safety compliance;
- water-saving percentages;
- weight-bearing capacity;
- material ratings;
- automated systems;
- exact installation method.

### Cargo nets / anti-theft mesh

Show actual application and explain only supported benefits. Do not imply certification, theft prevention guarantees, legal compliance or load ratings unless current product documentation proves them.

### Roll-up lapa blinds

Use patio/lapa application, usability and workshop/fit detail where shown. Do not invent material, weather rating, transparency, installation capability or custom sizes.

### Printed tautliner / side tipper tarps

Use exact vehicle/trailer context and visible branding/application. Do not invent printing process, material spec, fleet contract, turnaround or national service area.

### Tarp accessories

Name the actual accessory when known. Do not turn the category into an invented list.

## 8. Poster / text-on-post rules

- Prefer a clear **title + short supporting subtitle** when that suits the design.
- Keep text specific to the actual image/product.
- A short title can be the real service/product rather than a forced creative metaphor.
- The subtitle should add a useful customer point, not restate the heading.
- Avoid generic words such as `quality`, `solution`, `built to last`, `we've got you covered` unless there is a concrete reason and the phrase is not being used as filler.
- Do not mechanically open with `From...`, `Create...`, `Use...`, `Set...` or similar template verbs.
- For delivery/dispatch images, say what is actually happening rather than forcing a product claim.
- Keep one message per asset. Put extra context in the caption.

## 9. Video / content-format guidance

Recurring formats that fit Central Canvas:
- real truck tarp repair walkthrough;
- one common damage point explained;
- “what we need to quote/assess this” using current process facts;
- cutting / stitching / workshop process;
- dispatch or delivery moment;
- before/after only when it is genuinely the same job;
- staff member explains one service to camera;
- real product application on a truck, pool, lapa or cargo load;
- seasonal rain-preparation content when operationally relevant;
- customer/job handover moments with permission;
- DIY education only where an actual current DIY product/kit has been confirmed.

Content should be functional and honest. No posed lifestyle scenes are needed to make the brand interesting.

## 10. Visual and image-generation/edit rules

### Preserve reality

For edits of supplied Central Canvas imagery:
- preserve the real truck/trailer/equipment and its angle unless the user asks for a structural change;
- preserve tarp/canvas/PVC shape, fit, seams, tension, proportions and visible hardware;
- preserve logo wording and brand geometry;
- do not alter vehicle branding, registration details, staff identity or product configuration unless explicitly requested;
- sharpen/clean/upscale without redesigning unrelated elements when the request is quality-only;
- if a colour change is requested, change the requested surface/product rather than drifting the whole scene;
- generated product visuals must remain physically plausible.

Prior Project image work repeatedly asked for:
- tarp-specific imagery rather than generic backgrounds;
- exact requested product colours;
- straighter/cleaner tarp presentation without changing the intended angle;
- realistic pool-cover and anti-theft-mesh visuals;
- black/green tarp backgrounds and product-focused compositions.

### Brand colour correction

- **green is an accent colour**;

This is a visual-system rule, not a restriction on the real colour of a photographed/generated tarp product.
