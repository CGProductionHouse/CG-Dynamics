- confident without arrogance;
- clean and professional;
- direct;
- reassuring;
- human;

## 12. Video/Reel/content guidance

### Strong recurring formats

1. **People behind the service**
   - response officers;
   - control-room/technical staff where security-sensitive detail is protected;
   - leadership/team moments;
   - community engagement.

2. **Service process**
   - alarm signal -> verified process -> dispatch/escalation, without exposing tactics;
   - guarding with backup;
   - CCTV/alarm/technical service;
   - access/perimeter;
   - SecureAgri planning.

3. **Community proof**
   - school/community presence;
   - recognised assistance/partnerships;
   - real activations;
   - staff showing up rather than generic CSR language.

4. **Human/light campaign content**
   - staff trend formats;
   - Best of Bloem;
   - playful case-file concepts;
   - witty physical activations.

5. **Incident/proof content**
   - only when facts are verified and publication is lawful/approved;
   - focus on response process and outcome without exposing private/sensitive detail.

### Hooks

Hooks should create a specific question or situation, not vague suspense.

Examples of strong direction:

- “Your alarm has triggered. What happens next?”
- “A guard at the gate is only one part of the plan.”
- “The camera is recording. But can it capture the detail you actually need?”

These are structures, not fixed scripts.

### Humour boundary

The KitKat “OFFICIAL CASE FILE” creative and employee wind-up-doll trend show that SecuriForce can be genuinely funny.

Use humour when:

- the campaign is clearly playful;
- the joke is harmless;
- the brand remains recognisable;
- the service is not trivialised.

Do not joke about:

- a real victim;
- a real active incident;
- weapons;
- violence;
- suspects/guilt;
- customer vulnerability.

## 13. Visual and image-editing rules

Project history is unusually clear here.

### Preserve by default

- real SecuriForce logos and branding;
- readable vehicle livery;
- uniforms;
- equipment;
- faces/recognisable people;
- vehicle make/shape/placement when not part of the requested edit;
- overall composition/crop;
- important text.

### Quality standard

- keep text/logo edges sharp;
- do not return a needlessly blurred image;
- do not crop out parts of the subject unless asked;
- preserve exact brand colours where supplied;
- maintain realism;
- leave faces unchanged unless the user explicitly asks for privacy blur or another facial edit.

### Change only what was requested

If the user asks to:

- remove branding, remove only the specified branding;
- replace a vehicle, do not redesign unrelated vehicles/people;
- make a guard appear to run/chase, preserve identifiable features and clothing/branding;
- blur a face, blur only the requested face(s);
- clean highlights, do not alter faces or branding as a side effect.

If a broader redesign is explicitly requested, the broader change is allowed.

### Incident/security visual safety

Do not publish or generate visuals that accidentally expose:

- alarm codes;
- access credentials;
- control-room screens with sensitive data;
- response routes;
- guard rosters;
- client layouts/vulnerabilities;
- firearm serials/storage;
- personal data;
- unredacted number plates/faces where publication is not authorised.

Staged campaign imagery must not be presented as a real incident.
