## Strategic reading

Avodah Manufacturers should not market itself as a generic welding shop or a catalogue of steel products.

Its strongest defendable space is:

> Practical livestock-handling systems designed around safer people, calmer animal flow, faster work and durable South African farm use.

The product is steel. The customer outcome is operational control.

Marketing should therefore move beyond “strong gate” language and repeatedly prove:

- why the geometry works;
- how animal flow improves;
- how handling becomes safer;
- how labour and time are reduced;
- how systems fit actual farm layouts;
- how the equipment performs under pressure;
- how custom design solves farm-specific problems;
- how local manufacturing supports repairs, extensions and long-term use.

## Human buying questions

Farmers are likely to ask:

- Will cattle move through it naturally?
- Can one or two people operate it safely?
- Will animals turn back or bunch up?
- How strong are the hinges, frames, welds and locking points?
- What steel thickness and pipe specification are used?
- Is it fixed, modular or mobile?
- Can it be customised to my kraal?
- Can Avodah inspect or design my layout?
- What happens if I need another gate or extension later?
- Is delivery available?
- Is installation included?
- What is the lead time?
- What maintenance is required?
- Can I see it working with real animals?
- How does it compare with cheaper local fabrication?

Marketing must answer these questions with visual proof, specifications and working demonstrations.

## Positioning system

### Core positioning

Avodah Manufacturers designs and builds durable livestock-handling systems that help farmers move animals more calmly, work more safely and get demanding farm jobs done faster.

### Supporting proof pillars

1. **Flow** — layouts and gates designed around how cattle and sheep move.
2. **Safety** — better control for handlers, workers and animals.
3. **Strength** — materials, welds, frames and locking points built for farm pressure.
4. **Practicality** — equipment that is simple to operate, maintain and extend.
5. **Custom fit** — solutions adapted to the farm, herd and workflow.
6. **Local support** — Bloemfontein manufacturing with direct human access.

### Language to avoid

Avoid unsupported phrases such as:

- best in South Africa;
- indestructible;
- zero-stress handling;
- guaranteed to save labour;
- maintenance-free;
- safest system available;
- lifetime equipment;
- any animal, any farm;
- custom engineered, unless actual engineering responsibility and sign-off are confirmed.

Use specific, defensible language instead.

## Short-form video concepts

### “Een hek. Een probleem opgelos.”

1. Show one recurring farm frustration.
2. Show the Avodah component.
3. Demonstrate it working.
4. Explain the design decision.
5. End with WhatsApp quote action.

### “Hoekom draai hulle hier om?”

1. Show cattle hesitating or turning.
2. Identify the visual or layout problem.
3. Show the corrected system.
4. Explain the change in simple Afrikaans.

### “Van skets tot kraal”

1. Farmer sends photos or sketch.
2. Avodah develops the layout.
3. Workshop fabrication.
4. Delivery/install.
5. Live animal test.

### “Die deel wat niemand sien nie”

1. Close-up of hinge, pin, brace or latch.
2. Explain why it matters under load.
3. Show real operation.
4. Tie it to safety and longevity.

### “Boer toets dit self”

1. Farmer operates the system.
2. No scripted praise required.
3. Ask what changed in the work process.
4. Capture exact practical feedback.
