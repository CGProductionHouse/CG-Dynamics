# SecuriForce - CG Dynamics Client Guide

CG Dynamics is the permanent client source of truth. This document is the current ChatGPT working guide derived from that intelligence. When durable client information changes, update CG Dynamics first and then refresh this guide.

Canonical client name: **SecuriForce**

Use this guide for everyday SecuriForce work. It is the practical working layer, not a replacement for the permanent Dynamics intelligence.

## 1. Human marketing rule

SecuriForce marketing must feel written by people who understand a real security service and the person deciding whether to trust it.

Keep it:

- human;
- direct;
- credible;
- professional without sounding corporate;
- reassuring without overpromising;
- specific to the service, people, proof or situation.

Avoid generic security filler such as:

- “where safety meets innovation”;
- “discover the difference”;
- “elevate your experience”;
- “peace of mind guaranteed”;
- “your trusted security partner”;
- “state-of-the-art protection” with no real detail;
- vague fear-based hype.

**“Protecting You” is a real SecuriForce brand line.** It can be used when it helps, but it should not replace useful copy.

Humour is allowed. SecuriForce has successfully used playful staff trends, witty Best of Bloem wording and the KitKat “case file” campaign. The joke must fit the campaign and must never trivialise a real incident, victim, violence or customer vulnerability.

## 2. Language and caption rules

- Default to clear English unless Afrikaans is requested.
- When writing Afrikaans, use natural everyday spoken Afrikaans.
- Keep sentences active and easy to read.
- Captions must **add to the artwork rather than repeat it**.
- Short captions are often better when the poster already carries the detail.
- One useful service fact, proof point or CTA is stronger than several generic claims.
- Do not include location references in normal SecuriForce captions.
- Never use `#BloemfonteinSecurity`.
- Avoid location hashtags by default.
- Use a small focused hashtag set, normally up to **five**.

Useful non-location hashtags can include:

`#SecuriForce` `#ArmedResponse` `#CCTVMonitoring` `#AlarmSystems` `#AccessControl` `#SecurityGuards` `#SecuritySolutions` `#FarmSecurity` `#SecureAgri` `#CommunitySafety`

Rotate them by topic. Do not paste the same set mechanically.

### Best of Bloem / voting copy

Voting content should be friendly, clear and campaign-specific.

Do not default to:

- “take it home”;
- “bring it home”;
- generic win/title language.

Use the current live link, category, date and voting instructions only after rechecking them.

## 5. Security/compliance claim guardrails

Never invent or casually repeat:

- response times;
- “fastest” or guaranteed response;
- staffing counts;
- qualifications;
- registration numbers;
- accreditations;
- firearm/licensing claims;
- guaranteed workmanship;
- “best” or “leading” claims;
- technology performance;
- crime-prevention guarantees;
- current packages, prices, discounts or free-period offers.

### PSiRA

Current SecuriForce pages refer to PSiRA registration/requirements for personnel/units, but the exact current business registration record and number were not independently verified in the migration.

Never write “PSiRA approved”. Never invent a registration number. Compliance-led ads require current exact entity/status proof.

### SAIDSA

Current SAIDSA evidence lists **Secure Alert (Pty) Ltd** at the same SecuriForce premises and phone, with a SecuriForce-domain email. The legal/trading relationship still needs explicit confirmation before using legal-entity membership wording in advertising.

### Response language

“Armed Response” and “Rapid Response Team” are current service/team terms. They are not proof of a guaranteed response time.

Avoid universal claims such as:

- “immediate response”;
- “always there in minutes”;
- “fastest in the area”.

Incident-specific timing may only be used when the facts are verified for that incident.

## 6. Poster and text-on-post rules

Prefer:

- a short strong heading;
- one clear idea;
- a subtitle that adds meaning;
- wording that connects directly to the visual or service;
- real security-specific wordplay when the campaign is playful.

Project history supports witty lines such as protection/“eyes on you” concepts and direct voting prompts.

Do not force cleverness into serious service or incident communication.

For service posters, say what the customer actually gets or what happens:

- trained officer on site;
- monitored alarm/CCTV;
- access control;
- technical maintenance;
- response backup;
- specific service process.

Avoid empty “complete security solutions for your peace of mind” wording.

## 7. Video and Reel direction

Strong SecuriForce short-form content usually fits one of these lanes:

### People and team

Show:

- response officers;
- technical staff;
- leadership/team involvement;
- real staff personality;
- community participation.

The aim is competence plus humanity, not action-movie posturing.

### Service process

Explain a real sequence, such as:

- alarm signal;
- monitoring/control-room step;
- dispatch/escalation;
- service follow-up.

Do not expose sensitive tactics, routes, codes, screens or client vulnerabilities.

### Community and proof

Strong content can show:

- school/community visits;
- recognised assistance;
- partnerships;
- event presence;
- real service work.

Use the specific story rather than generic “we care about the community” copy.

### Playful campaign mode

Staff trends, Best of Bloem and the KitKat “case file” concept prove that a fun tone can work.

Make it obviously playful. Do not blur the line between a staged joke and a real incident.

### Incident content

Only use verified facts and approved media.

Do not identify guilt, reveal private client/security details or post sensitive footage merely because it is dramatic.

## 8. Visual and image-editing rules

This is a strict SecuriForce production rule.

When editing real client images, preserve by default:

- logos;
- vehicle branding/livery;
- uniforms;
- equipment;
- real faces/identifiable features;
- vehicle shape/placement;
- composition/crop;
- important text.

Change only what the user requested unless a broader redesign is explicitly requested.

Quality expectations:

- logos and vehicle text must remain sharp and readable;
- do not return unnecessary blur;
- do not crop out parts of the subject unless asked;
- keep supplied brand colours accurate;
- keep the result realistic;
- do not alter faces when the requested change is elsewhere.

If the user specifically asks to remove branding, blur a face or replace a vehicle, make that exact change without redesigning unrelated parts.

For security-sensitive imagery, never expose operational screens, access credentials, alarm codes, response routes, guard rosters, firearm serial/storage details or private client vulnerabilities.
