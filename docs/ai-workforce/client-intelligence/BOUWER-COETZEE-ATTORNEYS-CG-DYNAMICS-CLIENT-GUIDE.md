## 6. Humour boundary

Good subjects for smart legal humour include:

- signing without reading;
- verbal / badly documented agreements;
- `we’ll sort it out later` moments;
- broad contract red flags;
- legal terms in normal language;
- light office/behind-the-scenes content;
- community/event content.

Avoid humour around:

- divorce pain;
- children / maintenance disputes;
- injury / trauma;
- confidential matters;
- legal outcomes;
- a client being uninformed.

Never make the client or their problem the joke.

## 7. Caption rule

**Add to the artwork/video. Do not repeat it.**

Start with what is actually in the creative and what it already says. Add one useful layer: a human observation, practical implication, process detail, relevant next step or reason to contact the firm.

Default shape:

- one human hook;

- CTA/contact only when useful;
- disclaimer where advice-style content could be mistaken for individual advice;
- maximum **5 dynamic hashtags** by default.

Do not write long generic legal essays under simple posters.

## 8. Bouwer-specific copy behaviour

### Serious topics

For divorce, family matters and personal injury:

- calm, concise and practical;
- no dramatic `fight` language;
- no fear-based urgency;
- no humour at the person’s expense;
- when side-points are needed, prefer **2–3 short points**.

### Contracts / signing / everyday legal mistakes

This is where sharper hooks can work. `Don't risk it`-style framing is acceptable when it immediately leads to a concrete legal/document point rather than vague fear.

### ANC content

Frame it as practical planning for a shared future, not mistrust. Keep legal explanations simple and attorney-review material legal statements before publication.

### Correspondent / Deeds Office content

Use a more professional B2B tone. Be operational and specific about the verified Bloemfontein services; do not sound like a consumer divorce/property caption.

## 9. Generic legal filler to avoid

Avoid:

- `your trusted legal partner`;
- `protecting your rights` when it says nothing specific;
- `legal solutions tailored to you`;
- `excellence, integrity, results`;
- `fight for your rights`;
- unsupported `best`, `leading`, `specialist` or outcome claims;
- slogans that could belong to ten unrelated firms.

Prefer a real situation, useful warning, process point or human observation.

## 11. Poster / text-on-post rule

Keep it immediately understandable:

- short heading + short support line;
- real client situation over abstract slogan;
- one red flag / one myth / one process point per post where possible;
- 2–3 short side-points when explanation is genuinely needed;
- no dense legal jargon;
- heading and subheading must not repeat each other.

Strong recurring formats:

- `Before you sign`;
- legal red flags;
- myth vs fact;
- `What happens next?`;
- `Legal word, normal language`;
- founder-led Q&A;
- current service explainers;
- correspondent / Deeds Office B2B explainers;
- behind-the-scenes office/process content with no client data exposed.

## 13. Legal safety

All material legal information should be reviewed by the responsible attorney before publication.

Never invent or imply:

- a guaranteed legal outcome;
- compensation / success / registration timing;
- fees;
- court or jurisdiction authority;
- current service scope;
- professional status or specialisation beyond verified evidence;
- a client relationship;
- personalised legal advice from a general social post.

If a fact conflict matters to the task, flag it instead of guessing.
