# Cape Lumber — CG Dynamics Client Guide

CG Dynamics is the permanent client source of truth. This document is the current ChatGPT working guide derived from that intelligence. When durable client information changes, update CG Dynamics first and then refresh this guide.

## 1. Client identity and positioning

- **Canonical CG client:** Cape Lumber
- **Public trading style:** Cape Lumber Marketing
- **Business:** timber and building-material supplier serving professional, commercial and project buyers.
- Cape Lumber is a **supplier, not a contractor**.

Never present Cape Lumber as providing carpentry, joinery contracting, installation, on-site fitting, deck/pergola construction, structural engineering, fireproofing or fire-retardant services. It may supply materials used for these applications, but it does not perform the installation/construction work.

Primary audience: builders, contractors, architects, developers/project teams, joiners, manufacturers, shopfitters/fabricators, independent tradespeople and professional project buyers. Serious project-focused homeowners are a secondary audience, but content must remain supply-led rather than DIY/tutorial-led.

## 2. Human marketing voice

Cape Lumber should sound like an experienced timber supplier who understands the material and the buying process.

Use:
- confident, knowledgeable wording;
- clean, practical language;
- specific products, grades, treatments, sizes, sourcing and delivery context;
- a professional but approachable Cape supplier voice;
- premium positioning through precision and competence, not luxury adjectives;
- concise copy when the message is simple.

Avoid:
- influencer-style hype;
- memes, slang or forced humour;
- empty corporate phrases such as “elevate your experience”, “discover the difference”, “where quality meets…”, “bringing your vision to life”, “strength meets style” or “solutions for every need”;
- vague “premium quality” claims when a real product fact would be stronger;
- generic copy that could belong to any building-material brand;
- over-promising.

Humour can be used occasionally, but it should be dry, trade-aware and based on real purchasing, dimensions, sourcing, quantities or delivery situations. It should never make technical requirements sound trivial.

## 3. Captions

Cape Lumber captions should add value beyond the artwork.

Rules:
- **Do not repeat the text already on the post.**
- Add a useful product detail, buyer reason, sourcing point, delivery context, specification note, quote prompt or availability context.
- Keep straightforward captions reasonably short.
- Make the copy commercial and practical rather than inspirational.
- Do not default to “DM us”.

Preferred CTAs:
- request a quote;
- send the material/timber list;
- send the specification;
- enquire about stock, treatment, machining, sourcing or delivery;
- call or email.

Recent approved CG-style lines such as **“WE DELIVER. YOU BUILD.”**, **“NEED IT? WE SOURCE IT.”** and **“Reliable supply keeps projects moving.”** demonstrate the preferred economy and supplier focus. They are examples, not permanent slogans, and should not be repeated mechanically.

## 4. Poster / text-on-post rules

Keep artwork copy short and product/service-led.

Preferred structure:
- one clear heading;
- one short support line.

The wording must match the visual:
- delivery visual → delivery message;
- laminated beam → beam supply, length, specification or sourcing;
- board/sheet material → that actual product and why it matters;
- stock/load → product, order scale, sourcing or supply context.

Do not turn a poster into a datasheet. Do not place a generic brand slogan on technical product photography.

## 5. Content ideas and recurring formats

### Product spotlights
Useful recurring categories include:
- structural/treated Pine;
- S5 structural Pine;
- H2/H3 treatment and Vacsol Azure;
- laminated Pine beams/posts and glulam;
- Saligna and imported Spruce laminated timber;
- Meranti and hardwoods;
- Garapa, Yellow Balau, Massaranduba and Jatoba when current;
- exterior/commercial plywood;
- Marine Ply BS1088;
- shutterply and Birch plywood;
- OSB/OSB3;
- fibre cement/Nutec;
- MgO board;
- gypsum/Rhino board;
- IsoBoard/confirmed insulation;
- mouldings, skirting, T&G products, handrails and custom/PAR profiles.

A product spotlight should ideally answer a buyer question: what it is, what grade/treatment applies, what size/length matters, whether it is stocked/sourced, what type of professional application commonly specifies it, or what the buyer should send for a quote.

### Sourcing / procurement
This is a strong Cape Lumber territory. Useful hooks include:
- send the timber list;
- need a specific species/profile/length;
- hard-to-find product;
- mixed project order;
- unusual specification.

Do not promise unlimited sourcing or guaranteed lead times.

### Delivery / bulk supply
Show commercial usefulness rather than merely saying “we deliver”. Good content includes:
- long or bulky material;
- project quantities;
- load preparation;
- destination/region;
- mixed orders;
- quote information.

### Technical education
Good builder/architect topics include:
- H2 vs H3;
- Vacsol Azure;
- S5 Pine;
- laminated beam lengths/dimensions when current;
- Pine vs Saligna vs Spruce;
- Marine vs exterior plywood;
- board/material comparisons;
- stocked vs sourced items.

Stay within supplier expertise; never replace structural/engineering specification.

### Behind the scenes
Use real operational proof when available:
- timber/board stock;
- loading and delivery prep;
- product arrivals;
- material selection/detail;
- long beams shown at scale;
- sourcing/mill/import relationships where permission exists;
- machining partner process where appropriate.

Do not invent warehouse capacity, machinery, factory capability or stock quantities.

## 6. Reels / video guidance

A proven Cape Lumber format is a short information video of roughly **20–30 seconds**, often without voice-over.

Good structure:
1. strong first-screen hook;
2. one clear product/service subject;
3. short on-screen facts;
4. real stock/material/delivery movement;
5. commercial CTA.

Do not overcrowd frames with technical paragraphs. Voice-over is allowed when the concept genuinely benefits from it.

Expert-led video with Denis or another confirmed representative can work well when available. Keep it direct, experienced, lightly scripted and focused on one buyer question, for example H2 vs H3, what to send for a timber quote, laminated beams, hardwood differences, board choices or unusual sourcing.

## 7. Image generation and editing rules

For Cape Lumber image work, preserve product truth.

When editing a supplied image:
- preserve the real product, visible branding, proportions and key composition;
- change **only what was requested** unless the brief explicitly asks for a broader redesign;
- do not casually replace logos, labels, timber profiles, product colours, dimensions, machinery, vehicles or identifiable stock with invented alternatives;
- do not make Cape Lumber look like the installer/contractor;
- do not add unsupported warehouse, workshop or manufacturing capability;
- keep enough visual context to understand the material/product being marketed.

When generating a new marketing visual:
- prioritise realistic timber/board/product detail;
- show beams, sheet materials, stock stacks, edges, grain, profiles, loading or delivery at believable scale;
- use people only when they add real supplier context;
- avoid generic lifestyle imagery that weakens the product/supply story;
- avoid creating technical labels/specifications that have not been supplied or verified.

If a product reference image is provided, treat it as the source of truth for its branding, visible form and material characteristics.
