# Automotive Dealership and Aftermarket — Human Marketing Goldmine

Last updated: 2026-08-06
Status: researched source pack; review-gated
Territory: South Africa first, with international campaign evidence where transferable

## Purpose

This pack exists to help CG Production House market automotive dealerships, workshops, service centres, tyre and fitment businesses, parts suppliers and related vehicle businesses better.

It is not a generic automotive encyclopedia. Every source and lesson must improve one or more of:

- social content;
- paid media;
- search and SEO;
- dealership and workshop lead generation;
- service and parts retention;
- commercial scripting;
- photography and video direction;
- giveaways and promotions;
- showroom, test-drive and workshop activations;
- trust, reputation and customer experience.

## Human truths that repeatedly drive automotive marketing

1. People do not only buy a vehicle. They buy confidence in the decision.
2. Buyers are often anxious about being misled, oversold, overcharged or trapped in finance they do not understand.
3. A car becomes part of a person's identity, routine, family life, work and freedom.
4. Service and repair customers care about honesty, clarity, speed, safety and not being embarrassed for not knowing technical language.
5. Dealerships become more relatable when real staff, real customers and real workplace culture are visible.
6. Technical features work best when translated into a human moment, problem or consequence.
7. Local relevance matters: road conditions, school runs, long-distance travel, farms, business use, fuel costs, weather, safety and community identity.

## Proven campaign and case-study research

### 1. Volkswagen — Laughing Horses

Source: WARC case study
Canonical page: https://www.warc.com/en/article/volkswagen%3A-laughing-horses-2f6c565edba74c4bb8abc2c3c8de3c31

Documented result:

- more than 40 million social views within the first weeks;
- approximately 600% increase in Google search volume for “trailer assist”.

Human insight:

A technical feature became memorable because the campaign used a funny, recognisable situation rather than explaining the feature like a brochure.

Transferable lesson:

- Start with the awkward or relatable problem.
- Let the feature resolve it.
- Make the human reaction more memorable than the specification.

Useful CG adaptations:

- reverse-camera content built around a real parking embarrassment;
- hill-start assist shown through a learner-driver or family moment;
- tyre grip shown through a Free State storm or gravel-road scenario;
- workshop diagnostics shown as “that sound you keep pretending not to hear”.

### 2. Bidvest McCarthy — dealership-native TikTok

Source: CBR Marketing case study
Canonical page: https://cbrmarketing.co.za/bidvest-mccarthy-tiktok-campaign-case-study/

Documented result:

- more than 2 million video views;
- 80,021 likes;
- 699 shares;
- more than 25,000 new followers;
- search impressions for “McCarthy” increased 54%;
- organic clicks increased 88%.

Human insight:

The content worked because 75 dealerships contributed real people, real floors and real vehicles instead of forcing polished television ads into TikTok.

Transferable lesson:

- Build a repeatable staff-content system.
- Let dealership personalities appear.
- Make the content native to the platform.
- Use paid media to amplify what already feels organic.

Useful CG adaptations:

- recurring salesperson formats;
- “which car fits this customer?” challenges;
- workshop myths answered by technicians;
- genuine handover reactions;
- dealership humour based on daily reality;
- local customer questions turned into episodes.

### 7. Dunlop Genuine Parts / Danny's Automotive

Source: Ads of the World
Canonical page: https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/from-breakdown-to-breakthrough-with-ai

Documented result:

- 13,227,793 impressions;
- 33,535 clicks.

Human insight:

The campaign used breakdown anxiety during holiday travel and a memorable contrast between the part drivers hate and the genuine part they should ask for.

Transferable lesson:

Seasonal anxiety can be useful when handled humanely:

- December road-trip readiness;
- winter battery concerns;
- tyre safety before rain;
- school-holiday travel checks;
- towing and farm-use preparation.

Avoid fearmongering. Show the risk, then provide a clear practical action.

## Commercial and video script structures

Do not copy protected scripts. Use the human mechanism.

### Structure A — The overlooked problem

1. Open on a familiar frustration.
2. Let the viewer recognise themselves.
3. Reveal the vehicle, service or part as the practical answer.
4. Show the emotional relief.
5. End with one clear action.

Example use:

- the parent who cannot fit everything into the current car;
- the driver ignoring a dashboard light;
- the business owner losing time to unreliable transport;
- the customer who is nervous about workshop pricing.

### Structure B — The car as part of life

1. Begin with a person or family routine.
2. Show the vehicle quietly supporting that life.
3. Build small emotional details.
4. Avoid overexplaining the product.
5. Close on the meaning of the journey, not a generic slogan.

Suitable for:

- family vehicles;
- bakkies;
- first cars;
- retirement or travel vehicles;
- long-owned customer vehicles;
- customer handovers.

### Structure C — The honest expert

1. Real technician or salesperson addresses one real question.
2. Explain it in plain language.
3. Show the part, symptom or test.
4. State what matters and what does not.
5. Invite the viewer to ask or book.

Suitable for:

- workshop trust;
- tyres;
- parts;
- vehicle comparisons;
- finance myths;
- maintenance education.

### Structure D — The recurring character series

1. Establish recognisable staff personalities.
2. Use one workplace situation per episode.
3. Keep dialogue natural and short.
4. Let product or service appear inside the story.
5. Build recurring jokes, rivalries and callbacks.

Suitable for TikTok, Reels and YouTube Shorts.

## Anti-sludge standard

Reject phrases such as:

- “driving your dreams forward”;
- “where excellence meets performance”;
- “your journey starts here”;
- “passion meets precision”;
- “more than just a car” unless the work proves what it means;
- “unmatched quality and service” without evidence.

Replace with:

- a real customer problem;
- a real use case;
- a specific proof point;
- a recognisable human moment;
- plain language;
- one believable promise.
