# AI Workforce — Financial, Insurance and Credit-Related Services Industry Library

Last updated: 2026-07-26
Status: Actual researched industry pack; all production knowledge remains review-gated.
Territory: South Africa.

## Purpose

Create a trustworthy industry layer for:

- financial advisers and authorised financial services providers;
- insurance and microinsurance;
- investment and retirement-related services;
- credit providers and credit intermediaries;
- debt counselling and credit rehabilitation;
- mortgage, vehicle and asset-finance journeys;
- payment, lending and financial-technology businesses;
- accounting-adjacent financial education and lead-generation activity;
- crypto-asset and other newly regulated financial-service contexts where applicable.

This pack supports marketing strategy, social content, lead generation, website intelligence, reporting and client communications. It does not provide financial, credit, insurance, legal, tax or investment advice.

## Industry structure

The AI must separate these journeys:

1. General financial education
   - educational information;
   - no personalised recommendation;
   - no suitability conclusion;
   - no promised return or outcome.

2. Financial advice
   - licensed provider and representative;
   - disclosure and mandate;
   - client circumstances and needs;
   - recommendation;
   - record of advice;
   - implementation and ongoing service.

3. Insurance
   - quote request;
   - underwriting information;
   - quotation;
   - acceptance;
   - premium and inception;
   - policy servicing;
   - claim notification;
   - assessment;
   - claim outcome.

4. Consumer credit
   - enquiry;
   - application;
   - consent and bureau checks;
   - affordability assessment;
   - approval or decline;
   - agreement and disclosure;
   - disbursement;
   - repayment and account servicing.

5. Debt counselling
   - enquiry;
   - assessment;
   - formal application;
   - over-indebtedness determination;
   - proposal or restructuring process;
   - ongoing payment and review.

6. Investment or savings product
   - educational interest;
   - advice or execution-only route;
   - product disclosure;
   - risk and fees;
   - contribution or investment;
   - ongoing reporting;
   - withdrawal, switch or maturity.

7. Financial lead generation
   - campaign lead;
   - consent and contact permission;
   - qualification;
   - referral to licensed provider;
   - advice or application handled by the authorised party.

A lead, quote, application, approval, policy inception, disbursement and retained client are not equivalent outcomes.

## Source register

### Source F1 — Financial Advisory and Intermediary Services Act 37 of 2002

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/financial-advisory-and-intermediary-services-act
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal and financial risk flag
Freshness: verify amendments and current conduct standards

Verified findings:

- The FAIS Act regulates the rendering of specified financial advisory and intermediary services to clients.
- Financial advice and intermediary services are regulated activities, not ordinary marketing labels.
- Authorisation, product category, representative status and scope matter.

Marketing implications:

- Do not call a business or individual a financial adviser, authorised FSP or representative without current evidence.
- Store the exact FSP number, licence categories, provider name and checked date where a licence claim is used.
- A lead-generation business must not present itself as the advice provider unless it is authorised for that activity.
- General educational content must not quietly become a personalised recommendation.

Prohibited overclaims:

- “FSCA approved investment.”
- “Government-backed adviser” without exact basis.
- “Guaranteed suitable for everyone.”
- “No risk.”
- “Guaranteed return.”

### Source F2 — FAIS General Code of Conduct

Publisher: South African Government / FSCA source family
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/financial-advisory-and-intermediary-services-act-general-code-conduct-authorised-financial
Source type: conduct code
Rights treatment: official_reference; current version must be verified

Verified principles:

- Providers and representatives must act honestly, fairly, with due skill, care and diligence, and in clients’ interests.
- Information must be factually correct, clear and not misleading.
- Relevant product, provider, remuneration, conflict and risk information may need disclosure.
- Advice suitability depends on client circumstances and needs.

Marketing implications:

- A social post may educate, but cannot substitute for suitability assessment or required disclosure.
- “Best product”, “lowest cost”, “safe”, “tax free”, “capital guaranteed” and comparative claims require exact evidence and conditions.
- Lead forms should clearly identify who will contact the person and whether the interaction is education, quotation, application or advice.

### Source F3 — FSCA mandate and authorised-provider search

Publisher: Financial Sector Conduct Authority
Canonical pages:

- https://www.fsca.co.za/
- https://www.fsca.co.za/FSB-Search/
- https://www.fsca.co.za/Regulated-Entities/

Source type: statutory conduct regulator and live licence verification
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: licence status is time-sensitive

Verified findings:

- The FSCA regulates and supervises market conduct in the South African financial sector.
- The FSCA provides searches and lists for authorised financial services providers and other regulated entities.
- Current authorisation must be verified rather than inferred from branding, historical posts or an old certificate.

Knowledge rule:

- Store licence-check date and source.
- “Authorised” does not mean every product, representative or activity is covered.
- FSCA regulation is not a promise that the customer will earn a return or have a claim paid.

### Source F4 — Treating Customers Fairly

Publisher: Financial Sector Conduct Authority
Canonical sources:

- https://www.fsca.co.za/Document-For-Consultation/
- https://www.fsca.co.za/Financial-Consumer/

Source type: conduct-supervision framework
Rights treatment: official_reference

Verified findings:

The Treating Customers Fairly framework expects fairness through the product lifecycle, including:

- customer confidence in the firm’s culture;
- products designed for identified customer groups;
- clear information before, during and after sale;
- suitable advice where advice is given;
- products and service performing as customers were led to expect;
- no unreasonable post-sale barriers to switching, claiming or complaining.

Marketing implications:

- Campaign design cannot be separated from servicing, complaints and claims outcomes.
- Conversion optimisation must not remove material disclosures or create dark patterns.
- A high lead volume with unsuitable targeting can be a conduct failure rather than success.

### Source F6 — Insurance Act 18 of 2017 and insurer regulation

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/insurance-act-18-2017-english-afrikaans-18-jan-2018-0000
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal and financial risk flag

Verified scope:

- The Insurance Act provides the prudential framework for insurers and insurance groups.
- Insurer, intermediary, binder, underwriting and advice roles are not interchangeable.
- The existence of an insurer licence does not prove a particular intermediary relationship or policy term.

Marketing implications:

- Identify the actual insurer, intermediary and product role where required.
- “Underwritten by”, “administered by”, “brokered by” and “insured with” must remain distinct.
- Claims, exclusions, waiting periods, excesses and underwriting conditions cannot be hidden behind a simple monthly-price ad.

### Source F7 — National Credit Act 34 of 2005

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/ss/documents/national-credit-act
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal and financial risk flag

Verified findings:

- The Act promotes a fair and non-discriminatory consumer-credit market.
- It regulates consumer information, credit marketing, credit providers, bureaux and debt counselling.
- It prohibits certain unfair credit-marketing practices and reckless credit granting.
- Responsible credit granting and over-indebtedness controls are central to the framework.

Marketing implications:

- “Pre-approved” must not be used loosely.
- “Guaranteed approval”, “no checks”, “blacklisted welcome”, “instant cash” and similar claims carry high risk.
- An application cannot be presented as approved before the actual assessment.
- Credit advertising must not conceal cost, repayment, eligibility or material terms.

### Source F8 — National Credit Regulator and provider registration

Publisher: National Credit Regulator

- https://www.ncr.org.za/documents/

Source type: statutory credit regulator

Verified applications:

### Source F9 — Affordability assessment and reckless-credit boundary

Publisher: NCR / National Credit Act regulations source family
Canonical source family: NCR affordability-assessment materials and National Credit Regulations
Source type: regulatory assessment framework
Rights treatment: official_reference

Verified principles:

- Credit approval requires assessment under the applicable legal and regulatory framework.
- Income, expenses, existing obligations and repayment ability matter.
- Marketing lead qualification cannot replace formal affordability assessment.

Marketing implications:

- A marketing score, form answer or estimated affordability result is not a lending decision.
- Do not promise approval based on salary alone.
- Do not encourage applicants to omit debts or alter information.

### Source F10 — Financial Intelligence Centre compliance framework

Publisher: Financial Intelligence Centre
Canonical pages:

- https://www.fic.gov.za/Compliance/
- https://www.fic.gov.za/about/
- https://www.fic.gov.za/faqs/what-is-an-accountable-institution/

Source type: statutory AML/CFT regulator and guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: guidance, schedules and directives must be checked

Verified findings:

- Accountable institutions listed in Schedule 1 must fulfil FIC Act obligations.
- Obligations can include registration, risk-based controls, customer due diligence, beneficial-ownership identification, sanctions screening, monitoring, record keeping and regulatory reporting.
- An institution’s risk management and compliance programme must reflect its actual risks, controls and services.

Marketing and lead implications:

- A lead is not automatically ready for onboarding.
- Identity, beneficial ownership, sanctions and risk checks may be required before a relationship or transaction proceeds.
- Marketing systems must not expose or reuse AML, sanctions or suspicious-activity data.
- Never tell a customer that a suspicious transaction report was filed.

### Source F11 — FIC reporting obligations

Publisher: Financial Intelligence Centre
Canonical pages:

- https://www.fic.gov.za/faqs/what-are-my-reporting-obligations/

Verified findings:

- Main regulatory reporting streams include cash-threshold, suspicious/unusual and terrorist-property reporting, with exact requirements depending on the institution and transaction.

## Advice versus information matrix

### General information

May include:

- definitions;
- broad product categories;
- educational explanations;
- general risks;
- regulator links;
- questions a consumer may ask a provider.

Must not include:

- personalised suitability conclusion;
- “you should buy” recommendation;
- exact allocation based on personal data;
- claim that the user qualifies or will be approved;
- promise of return, saving, payout or tax result.

### Regulated advice or intermediary activity

Requires exact review of:

- authorised entity;
- representative status;
- licence category;
- product category;
- disclosure;
- client information;
- suitability and needs;
- record-keeping obligations;
- conflicts and remuneration;
- implementation authority.

## Claim-risk register

High-risk phrases include:

- guaranteed approval;
- guaranteed return;
- no risk;
- best investment;
- lowest premium;
- instant payout;
- no credit checks;
- debt written off;
- blacklisting removed;
- tax-free without qualification;
- FSCA approved product;
- government approved investment;
- claim always paid;
- pre-approved where no actual approval exists;
- free where fees or conditions apply.

Every such phrase requires evidence, conditions and regulatory review—or must not be used.

## Candidate review-gated Skill Cards

### FSC-01 — Financial information is not automatically advice

Educational content must not become a personalised recommendation or suitability conclusion.

### FSC-02 — Licence claims require current verification

Store exact entity, registration or FSP number, category and checked date.

### FSC-03 — A financial lead is not a financial outcome

Lead, quote, application, approval, inception and retained relationship are separate states.

### FSC-04 — Credit marketing cannot promise approval

Formal affordability and credit assessment remain separate from campaign qualification.

### FSC-05 — Insurance roles must remain distinct

Insurer, intermediary, administrator, binder holder and adviser are not interchangeable.

### FSC-06 — Customer fairness applies through the lifecycle

Promotion, advice, service, claims, complaints and cancellation all affect conduct quality.

### FSC-07 — Financial data is high-risk personal information

Income, debt, policy, account, beneficiary and claim data require strict purpose and access controls.

### FSC-08 — AML onboarding is not marketing qualification

Customer due diligence, beneficial ownership and sanctions controls are compliance processes.

### FSC-09 — Disclaimers cannot cure a misleading headline

Material conditions must be prominent, consistent and understandable.

### FSC-10 — Revenue measures must preserve product economics

Premium, contribution, assets, credit amount, commission, fees and profit are different measures.

## Assistant quick-answer protocol

For a quick staff question such as:

> Can we say this client guarantees approval?

The Assistant should return:

1. Use status.
2. Exact industry and product context.
3. Current verified regulator/source.
4. Why the wording is risky.
5. A safer factual alternative.
6. Required disclosure or evidence.
7. Escalation flag if the answer depends on legal/compliance review.

Example:

```text
Use status: Not approved

Claim: “Guaranteed loan approval”
Reason: Credit approval requires the provider’s formal assessment and cannot be promised by marketing.
Safer wording: “Apply for an assessment” or “Check whether you may qualify,” subject to the provider’s approved wording.
Required: NCR/provider verification, product terms, eligibility, cost disclosure and landing-page consistency.
```
