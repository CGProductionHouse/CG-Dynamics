Territory: South Africa, with transferable international campaign principles where useful.

## Purpose

Help CG Production House market:

- architects and architectural practices;
- property developers and residential estates;
- estate agencies and property professionals;
- builders and construction companies;
- interior designers and related built-environment services;
- commercial, mixed-use and lifestyle developments.

This pack exists to improve positioning, content, video, photography, websites, SEO, paid media, launches, activations, lead quality, trust and sales support.

It excludes bookkeeping, project administration, procurement and general construction operations unless they materially affect marketing truth.

## Human reality of the category

People do not buy architecture, construction or property from a logo. They buy confidence that:

- the person understands how they want to live or work;
- the project will not become an expensive disaster;
- the design will feel right, not merely photograph well;
- the professional can explain complexity without making them feel stupid;
- the promised quality, programme and process are believable;
- the location, lifestyle and long-term value make sense;
- the team has completed real work and can prove it.

Strong marketing therefore combines aspiration with proof. Beautiful images create desire, but proof, process and human clarity create action.

## South African campaign and brand evidence

### 1. Just Property — More or Less

Source: SimonSAYS case study.

Reported outcomes:

- 4.2 million brand views, up 111%;
- 306,000 website visitors, up 6.7%;
- 491,000 sessions, up 12%;
- 4,069 leads, up 54%;
- listing enquiries up 94%;
- 1,150 new Google reviews;
- Meta conversations up 188%.

Human lesson:

The campaign did not rely on random listing posts. It gave the brand one coherent voice across radio, outdoor, digital, social and print, while still allowing local relevance.

Transferable principle:

A property brand should not behave like separate agents posting unrelated stock. One recognisable idea should connect awareness, listings, reviews, recruitment and local branches.

### 2. Just Property — #YourStorey

Source: WARC case study.

Reported outcomes:

- 22,209,667 online impressions;
- 402 guide downloads;
- 223 credit checks submitted.

Human insight:

First-time buyers were anxious and inexperienced. Vlogging, guidance and a buyer journey were more relatable than polished property boasting.

Transferable principle:

For anxious, high-consideration buyers, useful guidance is part of the campaign, not a side blog.

### 3. The Bolton, Rosebank

Source: Rainmaker Marketing project showcase.

Reported outcomes:

- R240 million in sales in 12 months;
- all 282 units sold in under 18 months;
- 72% of Rosebank transfers between March 2023 and March 2024.

Human lesson:

Strong product positioning, a clear urban-lifestyle story and a sustained campaign can make one development dominate a local category.

### 4. Umhlanga Arch

Source: Rainmaker Marketing project showcase.

Reported outcomes:

- 90% of units sold before public release;
- more than R1.85 million in earned editorial exposure;
- recognised for development marketing in South Africa and Africa.

Human lesson:

Immersive selling tools, strong pre-launch storytelling and scarcity before public release can turn a development launch into a cultural event.

Transferable principle:

For off-plan property, the campaign must make an unbuilt future feel tangible enough to trust.

### 5. Steyn City precision targeting

Source: Amorphous case study.

Reported outcome:

- 2.38% conversion rate after four weeks;
- the agency states the campaign paid for itself many times over.

Human lesson:

Mass reach is wasteful when only a small group can afford the product. High-value property marketing needs relevance, familiarity and repeated exposure to qualified audiences.

### 6. Avec La Terre

Source: Stratitude case study.

Reported outcomes:

- 13% inbound lead rate;
- 12 property sales before construction began.

Human lesson:

The campaign sold a specific relationship with nature, not vague luxury. The promise was carried consistently through identity, website, advertising and sales material.

### 7. South African development lead-generation evidence

Source: Property Professional report on amplifi campaigns.

Reported examples:

- Knysna listing: 36,352 views, 1,672 website visits and 11 leads at R113 per lead;
- Randburg development: R15,000 over four weeks generated 172 leads and 11 unit sales;
- Sedgefield high-end property: R3,000 generated 31 leads at R38 per lead.

Human lesson:

Property marketing should be measured through website visits, qualified leads and actual unit sales, not only impressions.

### 8. Development launch and sell-out systems

Source: Adprop Marketing first-party case-study portfolio.

Reported portfolio claims:

- R933 million-plus in development sales;
- 5,000-plus leads generated;
- seven sold-out developments;
- one development generated 3,000-plus leads in five months and 105 sales.

Use with caution:

These are agency-reported results. They are useful directional evidence, but should not become universal benchmarks without independent verification.

## Human audience groups

### Residential architecture client

Usually wants:

- a home that reflects how the family actually lives;
- certainty around process and fees;
- confidence that the architect will listen;
- help balancing beauty, budget and practicality;
- evidence of completed homes;
- guidance through approvals and construction.

Common fears:

- being pushed into the architect’s style;
- cost spirals;
- endless delays;
- not understanding drawings;
- a beautiful house that is unpleasant to live in.

### Property-development buyer

Usually wants:

- confidence in the developer and delivery;
- location and lifestyle clarity;
- believable value and rental/resale prospects;
- exact unit, price and availability information;
- visualisation of the finished place;
- finance and purchase-process guidance.

Common fears:

- buying off plan and receiving something different;
- hidden costs;
- poor build quality;
- delays;
- weak management after handover;
- buying into marketing hype.

### Commercial client

Usually wants:

- business fit;
- credibility with investors, tenants or customers;
- programme and cost clarity;
- operational efficiency;
- brand expression through space;
- evidence of comparable delivery.

### Construction client

Usually wants:

- reliability;
- clear quotation scope;
- realistic timelines;
- visible workmanship;
- communication;
- site discipline;
- accountability when problems arise.

## Positioning angles that feel human

Useful angles:

- “Here is how your daily life shaped the plan.”
- “What we changed after listening to the client.”
- “The expensive mistake this detail prevents.”
- “Why this room is placed here.”
- “What you cannot see in the final photograph.”
- “How the site changed the design.”
- “What R3 million buys here, honestly.”
- “Who this development is actually for.”
- “The trade-off we made and why.”
- “What happened between the render and the finished building.”

Weak angles to reject:

- “Where vision meets reality.”
- “Building dreams.”
- “Designing the future.”
- “Luxury redefined.”
- “Excellence in every detail.”
- “Your trusted partner.”
- “Spaces that inspire.”

These phrases say nothing unless supported by a real human story or specific proof.

## Commercial and short-form script structures

### Script 1 — The life problem

1. Open on a real frustration.
2. Show the existing spatial problem.
3. Let the client or professional explain it plainly.
4. Reveal the design response.
5. Show how daily life changes.
6. End with a relevant invitation.

Example structure:

“Every morning this family crossed three rooms just to get school bags, lunch and keys out the door. So the entrance became more than a pretty passage…”

### Script 2 — The honest decision

1. State the desired outcome.
2. Reveal the constraint.
3. Show two possible choices.
4. Explain the decision in human language.
5. Show the result.

### Script 3 — The site truth

1. Begin with the site condition: wind, slope, view, heat, street or privacy.
2. Show why a generic plan would fail.
3. Demonstrate the design move.
4. Show the lived benefit.

### Script 4 — Off-plan confidence

1. Introduce the person or family buying.
2. Show the decision they are trying to make.
3. Explain the location and unit in concrete terms.
4. Show render, plan, view and finish evidence.
5. Address one objection honestly.
6. Invite a site visit or sales conversation.

### Script 5 — Builder proof

1. Show a detail before it is covered.
2. Explain why it matters.
3. Show the team doing it properly.
4. Show the finished result.
5. Tie quality to fewer future problems.

## Photography direction

Capture:

- people using spaces naturally;
- transitions between inside and outside;
- detail plus context;
- morning and evening light;
- construction process before finishes conceal quality;
- materials in real weather;
- circulation and arrival;
- views from seated and standing positions;
- staff and clients interacting without staged handshakes;
- neighbourhood and landscape context.

Avoid:

- only empty wide-angle rooms;
- exaggerated lens distortion;
- every image at sunset;
- workers posed unnaturally with tools;
- generic hard-hat line-ups;
- renders presented as completed reality.

1. Awareness and lifestyle.
2. Development proof and credibility.
3. Unit-specific consideration.
4. High-intent lead generation.
5. Site-visit or launch-event conversion.

Lead qualification should capture:

- budget range;
- purchase timing;
- finance or cash status where appropriate;
- unit type;
- owner-occupier versus investor;
- preferred contact method;
- site-visit readiness.

## Measurement

Track separately:

- reach and qualified attention;
- project-page visits;
- floor-plan and brochure engagement;
- phone, WhatsApp and form enquiries;
- qualified leads;
- booked consultations;
- site visits;
- offers or reservations;
- sales or signed appointments where the client provides verified data;
- cost per qualified lead;
- lead-to-visit rate;
- visit-to-sale rate;
- sales by campaign and unit type;
- review growth;
- organic search visibility;
- referral contribution;
- stale inventory and unavailable-unit enquiries.

Do not call impressions property demand. Do not call a lead a sale. Do not call a reservation a transfer.

## Marketing-specific claim controls

Verify before publishing:

- prices;
- unit availability;
- completion dates;
- views;
- distances and travel times;
- rental-yield claims;
- capital-growth claims;
- awards;
- professional registrations;
- green-building certifications;
- “sold out” statements;
- “only unit left” scarcity;
- render versus completed-status labels.

Never use unverified claims such as:

- guaranteed return;
- guaranteed completion;
- best investment;
- no risk;
- maintenance free;
- fully sustainable;
- award winning without exact proof;
- walking distance where the real route contradicts it.

## Review-state Skill Cards

### BUILT-ENVIRONMENT-HUMAN-STORY-BRIEF

1. Identify the real person and decision.
2. Record the lived problem or aspiration.
3. Identify the site, budget or process constraint.
4. Capture the professional decision in plain language.
5. Gather visual proof.
6. End with a specific next step.
7. Remove generic design slogans.

### PROPERTY-DEVELOPMENT-LAUNCH-CAMPAIGN

1. Confirm exact inventory, pricing and release phases.
2. Define the buyer groups.
3. Build the location and lifestyle story.
4. Build developer and delivery proof.
5. Create unit-specific content.
6. Configure qualification and lead routing.
7. Plan preview, launch and site-visit activations.
8. Track lead, visit, reservation and sale separately.
9. Pause unavailable inventory immediately.

### ARCHITECTURE-PROJECT-CASE-STUDY

1. Capture the client brief.
2. Explain the site and constraints.
3. Show rejected or changed options where useful.
4. Explain key decisions without jargon.
5. Photograph lived use, not only empty form.
6. Record measurable or observed outcome honestly.
7. Include client perspective with permission.

### CONSTRUCTION-PROOF-CONTENT

1. Identify a hidden quality detail.
2. Film it before concealment.
3. Let the responsible person explain why it matters.
4. Connect the detail to durability, safety or maintenance.
5. Avoid unsupported superiority claims.
6. Archive by project and construction stage.
