# AI Workforce — Legal and Professional Services Industry Library

Last updated: 2026-07-26
Status: Actual researched industry pack; all production knowledge remains review-gated.
Territory: South Africa.

## Purpose

Build a trustworthy industry layer for attorneys, advocates, conveyancing/bond practices and related professional services.

This pack must help the AI support:

- marketing strategy;
- educational content;
- lead generation;
- client intake;
- website and reporting;
- brand/reputation;
- compliance-aware copy.

It must not provide legal advice, predict case outcomes or classify clients from their names.

## Industry structure

Separate at least:

- litigation and dispute resolution;
- commercial/corporate work;
- family and matrimonial work;
- estates, wills and trusts;
- property, conveyancing and bonds;
- labour/employment work;
- criminal defence;
- personal-injury/claims work;
- compliance and advisory work;
- notarial services;
- advocacy;
- professional services adjacent to law.

A firm may serve several segments. Marketing, lead qualification and confidentiality risks differ by segment.

## Source register

### Source L1 — Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/legal-practice-act
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal-risk flag only
Freshness: re-verify amendments and commencement notices

Verified purpose:

- Creates the Legal Practice Council and provincial structures.
- Regulates admission, enrolment and professional conduct.
- Establishes accountability mechanisms, an ombud framework and the Legal Practitioners' Fidelity Fund.
- Supports transformation and access to legal services.

Marketing implications:

- Practitioner status, title and enrolment claims must be accurate.
- Do not call someone an attorney, advocate, conveyancer or notary unless the exact professional status is verified.
- Do not imply LPC endorsement merely because a practitioner is regulated.
- Access-to-justice or transformation claims need evidence rather than generic virtue language.

### Source L3 — Financial Intelligence Centre: legal practitioners as accountable institutions

Publisher: Financial Intelligence Centre
Core sources:

- https://www.fic.gov.za/2025/07/08/drafting-a-risk-management-and-compliance-programme-2/
- https://www.fic.gov.za/Compliance/
- https://www.fic.gov.za/2023/11/02/advocates-must-register-with-the-fic/

Source type: official regulatory guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: current guidance must be re-verified periodically

Verified findings:

- Legal practitioners falling within the accountable-institution scope must develop, maintain and implement a risk management and compliance programme (RMCP).
- RMCPs address risk assessment, customer due diligence, record keeping, reporting, sanctions controls and staff training.
- A law firm limited to conveyancing can face a different risk profile from a civil-litigation practice.
- Client-level risk assessment affects the level of due diligence required.
- Suspicious and unusual activity reporting has confidentiality/tipping-off implications.

Marketing and intake implications:

- Do not promise frictionless onboarding where identity or beneficial-ownership checks may be required.
- "Confidential" does not mean a practitioner can ignore statutory reporting duties.
- Do not explain anti-money-laundering controls in a way that helps users evade them.
- Intake forms should collect only necessary information and protect sensitive records.
- Conveyancing and trust-fund content should include fraud-awareness without exposing internal controls.

### Source L4 — POPIA and privacy risk

Primary source family:

- Protection of Personal Information Act and Information Regulator guidance.

Industry relevance:

Legal leads often include highly sensitive facts, identity documents, financial information, health information, family disputes, criminal allegations and property records.

Safe candidate knowledge:

- Public lead forms should collect only information needed for first contact.
- Detailed legal facts and documents should move to secure channels and controlled access.
- Marketing consent is separate from the fact that a person requested legal help.
- Case studies require proper permission and de-identification; changing a name alone may be insufficient.

## Buyer/client journey

### Awareness and problem recognition

The person may not know the legal category of the problem. Search and content often start with a life event or business risk:

- divorce/separation;
- unpaid account or contract dispute;
- property sale/transfer;
- arrest/charge;
- deceased estate;
- workplace dispute;
- business formation/agreement;
- injury or claim;
- compliance concern.

Content should explain the issue and next step without diagnosing the case publicly.

### Initial enquiry

Useful first-intake fields:

- name and safe contact method;
- broad matter category;
- urgency/deadline;
- opposing party names for conflict checking;
- location/jurisdiction context;
- preferred consultation format;
- consent to contact;
- no detailed confidential narrative unless the channel is suitable.

A submitted enquiry does not itself create a confirmed attorney-client relationship. Wording must be reviewed by the firm.

### Qualification and conflict checking

Lead stages should include:

- new enquiry;
- conflict check pending;
- conflict identified;
- consultation proposed;
- consultation booked;
- consultation completed;
- mandate/engagement pending;
- instructed/retained;
- declined/not suitable;
- referred;
- no response;
- closed.

Do not expose internal conflict-check details to client dashboards.

### Engagement

The system must distinguish:

- enquiry;
- consultation;
- legal advice;
- accepted mandate;
- matter opened;
- invoice/payment;
- outcome.

Do not report enquiries as clients or legal matters as wins.

## Decision drivers

Common trust factors:

- verified professional status;
- relevant experience stated honestly;
- plain-language explanation;
- clear process and next step;
- responsiveness;
- confidentiality and secure handling;
- realistic expectations;
- transparent consultation/fee approach where approved;
- local knowledge/jurisdiction;
- professional reputation;
- empathy without sensationalism.

## Claims and content risk register

High-risk claims requiring evidence/review:

- "specialist" or "expert";
- "best" or "leading";
- guaranteed outcome;
- guaranteed bail, divorce, transfer, settlement or claim value;
- success rate;
- "no win, no fee";
- exact turnaround time;
- confidentiality described as absolute;
- LPC, court or government endorsement;
- comparative attacks on another practitioner;
- client testimonials revealing matter details;
- case-study results without context;
- fee or finance claims without terms.

Never publish:

- identifying facts from an active/private matter without authority;
- legal strategy or privileged material;
- accusations presented as proven fact;
- court outcomes without checking the official record and appeal status;
- AI-generated case citations presented as real.

## Content strategy architecture

Recommended pillars:

1. Plain-language legal education
2. Process and expectation setting
3. Prevention and document readiness
4. Professional team and verified credentials
5. Local/community access
6. Business/legal risk awareness
7. Property/conveyancing education where relevant
8. Estate and family planning
9. Frequently asked questions
10. Secure first-contact guidance

Strong formats:

- "What happens next" short videos;
- process timelines with limitations;
- document checklists;
- myth-versus-fact sourced to legislation;
- deadline/reminder content;
- role explanations: attorney vs advocate vs conveyancer vs notary;
- property-transfer terminology;
- business-contract risk education;
- anonymised pattern-based examples with review.

Avoid:

- fear-heavy "you will lose everything" hooks;
- courtroom entertainment that trivialises clients;
- definitive advice without facts;
- posting confidential messages/documents as social proof;
- making current affairs commentary sound like a client-specific opinion.

## Paid media and lead generation

Possible lead types:

- consultation request;
- call request;
- property-transfer enquiry;
- bond/conveyancing enquiry;
- will/estate enquiry;
- business-contract consultation;
- family-law consultation;
- labour consultation;
- claims screening;
- urgent legal assistance.

Report separately:

- submitted leads;
- contactable leads;
- conflict-cleared leads;
- consultations booked;
- consultations attended;
- mandates accepted;
- matters opened;
- verified fees/revenue where authorised.

Lead-quality factors:

- service fit;
- jurisdiction;
- urgency/deadline;
- conflict status;
- ability/eligibility to instruct;
- matter value/complexity only where appropriate;
- duplicate/spam;
- consent and safe contact.

Do not optimise only for cheapest lead. Sensitive or urgent matters may require higher-cost, lower-volume acquisition.

## Candidate Skill Cards

All enter `needs_review`.

### LEGAL-01 — Educate without diagnosing

Principle:
Explain general process and options, but do not give definitive case-specific advice without the required facts and professional engagement.

### LEGAL-02 — Protect confidentiality before content value

Principle:
Client confidentiality, privilege and sensitive personal information override the desire for testimonials, case studies or engaging content.

### LEGAL-03 — Verify status and credentials

Principle:
Professional titles, enrolment, specialist wording and practice-area experience must be verified before publication.

### LEGAL-04 — Separate lead stages

Principle:
An enquiry, conflict-cleared lead, consultation, mandate and matter outcome are distinct states and must be reported separately.

### LEGAL-05 — No outcome guarantees

Principle:
Legal advertising must not guarantee results, timelines or monetary outcomes that depend on facts, opponents, courts, regulators or other external decisions.

### LEGAL-06 — Secure intake by design

Principle:
Collect minimal first-contact information publicly and move detailed facts/documents into secure, access-controlled channels.

### LEGAL-07 — Compliance can shape onboarding

Principle:
Identity, beneficial-ownership and risk checks may be legally required; convenience claims must not imply that due diligence can be skipped.

### LEGAL-08 — Plain language builds access and trust

Principle:
Translate legal processes and terms accurately without removing important limitations or presenting a simplified explanation as complete advice.

### LEGAL-09 — Current source beats old commentary

Principle:
Use current legislation, official forms, regulator guidance and court rules for time-sensitive legal process content.

### LEGAL-10 — Client outcome evidence stays contextual

Principle:
A past result does not predict a future result. Any approved case-result content must retain matter context, limitations and permission.
