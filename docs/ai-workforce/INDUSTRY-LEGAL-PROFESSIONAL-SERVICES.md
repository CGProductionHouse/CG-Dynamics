# AI Workforce — Legal and Professional Services Industry Library

Last updated: 2026-07-26
Status: Actual researched industry pack; all production knowledge remains review-gated.
Territory: South Africa.

## Purpose

Create a trustworthy industry layer for attorneys, conveyancers, notaries and related regulated professional practices.

This pack supports:

- marketing strategy;
- social content;
- paid lead generation;
- website journeys;
- lead qualification;
- reputation and trust;
- client reporting;
- compliance-risk review.

It does not provide legal advice and does not classify any client from its name.

## Industry structure

The AI must separate at least these service journeys:

1. General legal advice and consultations
2. Litigation and dispute resolution
3. Conveyancing and property transfer
4. Bond registration and cancellation
5. Wills and deceased estates
6. Commercial contracts and company work
7. Labour and employment matters
8. Family and matrimonial matters
9. Debt, insolvency and collections
10. Notarial and authentication work
11. Trust and company services
12. Specialist regulated work

A legal lead is not a single category. Urgency, confidentiality, conflict checks, jurisdiction, matter type, documentation and ability to act differ materially.

## Source register

### Source L1 — Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/legal-practice-act
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal-risk flag only
Freshness: verify amendments and commencement notices

Verified findings:

- The Act establishes the Legal Practice Council and Provincial Councils.
- It regulates admission, enrolment and professional conduct of legal practitioners.
- It creates mechanisms including the Legal Services Ombud and the Legal Practitioners’ Fidelity Fund.
- Its objects include accountability, independence, integrity, standards and access to legal services.

Safe candidate knowledge:

- Claims that a person is an attorney, advocate, conveyancer or notary should be tied to real current enrolment and scope.
- Professional credentials are not decorative marketing labels.
- Marketing should explain service scope and process without implying guaranteed outcomes.

Prohibited overclaims:

- “Guaranteed win.”
- “The best lawyer in South Africa” without defensible substantiation.
- “Specialist” or “expert” where the firm cannot support the claim.
- Presenting a candidate legal practitioner as an admitted practitioner.

### Source L2 — Legal Practice Council mandate and enrolment

Publisher: Legal Practice Council
Canonical pages:

- https://lpc.org.za/
- https://lpc.org.za/about-us/mandate-of-lpc/
- https://lpc.org.za/legal-practitioners/enrolment-of-legal-practitioners/

Source type: statutory regulator
Rights treatment: official_reference / metadata_and_link_only
Freshness: current regulator pages

Verified findings:

- The LPC is the national statutory regulator for attorneys, advocates and candidate legal practitioners.
- A person may practise as a legal practitioner only if admitted and enrolled under the Act.
- The LPC sets norms and standards and regulates professional conduct.
- Complaints are handled through the relevant Provincial Council.

Marketing implications:

- Staff profiles must distinguish admitted practitioners, candidate legal practitioners and support staff.
- Office location, practising status and role should be current.
- Complaint and professional-contact information should never be invented.
- “Registered”, “admitted”, “enrolled”, “conveyancer” and “notary” require exact evidence.

### Source L3 — LPC Code of Conduct

Publisher: Legal Practice Council
Canonical page: https://lpc.org.za/legal-practitioners/code-of-conduct/
Source type: professional conduct code
Rights treatment: official_reference / metadata_and_link_only
Freshness: verify Gazette updates and replacement versions

Verified scope:

- The Code applies to attorneys, advocates, candidate legal practitioners and relevant juristic entities.
- It is the professional-conduct standard for the profession.

Safe marketing interpretation:

- Content must protect client confidentiality and professional independence.
- A marketing story should not expose identifiable matter details without proper authority.
- Testimonials, case studies and outcome claims require careful review.
- Legal information content should clearly distinguish general education from matter-specific advice.

Do not activate detailed advertising-rule claims until the exact current Code provisions are reviewed page by page.

### Source L4 — Financial Intelligence Centre: legal practitioners

Publisher: Financial Intelligence Centre
Canonical pages:

- https://www.fic.gov.za/accountable-institut/legal-practitioners/
- https://www.fic.gov.za/document/pcc-47a-guidance-on-the-interpretation-of-legal-practitioners/
- https://www.fic.gov.za/document/sector-risk-assessment-revised-assessment-of-the-legal-practitioners-sector/

Source type: official AML/CFT regulator
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: re-verify directives, PCCs and sector-risk assessments

Verified findings:

- Certain legal practitioners and legal-practice entities are accountable institutions under the FIC Act.
- Legal services can involve conveyancing, company and trust work, client funds and transactions that carry money-laundering risk.
- Applicable practices must conduct customer due diligence, keep records, report required matters and maintain a risk-management and compliance programme.

Marketing and lead implications:

- A website form may collect an enquiry, but it does not complete client acceptance or FICA checks.
- “Start your transfer instantly” must not imply that identity, conflict, authority, source-of-funds or mandate requirements disappear.
- Sensitive identity and financial documents must not be requested through insecure public channels merely for marketing convenience.
- Leads should be marked `pending_conflict_and_compliance_checks` where relevant rather than “accepted client”.

Prohibited overclaims:

- “No documents required.”
- “Anonymous property transfer.”
- “Instant approval” for a regulated legal engagement.

### Source L5 — FIC RMCP guidance

Publisher: Financial Intelligence Centre
Canonical page: https://www.fic.gov.za/2025/07/08/drafting-a-risk-management-and-compliance-programme-2/
Source type: official compliance guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference

Verified findings:

- Legal-practice RMCPs must address customer due diligence, record keeping, reporting, risk-based controls and staff training.
- Risk differs by practice area; a conveyancing practice and a civil-litigation practice do not necessarily have the same risk profile.
- Suspicious behaviour may require reporting even when a transaction is not completed.

Safe candidate knowledge:

- Marketing operations and intake workflows must not flatten all legal matters into one low-friction funnel.
- Risk-based onboarding is part of professional operations, not simply “admin friction”.

### Source L6 — Deeds Registries Act and electronic deeds modernisation

Publishers: South African Government, Presidency, Chief Registrar of Deeds
Canonical sources:

- https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201505/act47of1937.pdf
- https://www.thepresidency.gov.za/president-ramaphosa-signs-law-easier-and-uniform-deeds-registration-processes
- https://cit2.eservices.gov.za/EDRS/

Source type: legislation and official system information
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: verify implementation dates, regulations and system status

Verified findings:

- South Africa’s deeds-registration system is governed by legislation and official registry procedures.
- Recent reforms provide for modernisation and an electronic deeds registration and recordal system.
- Conveyancing remains a formal legal and registry process; a digital system does not make transfer immediate.

Marketing implications:

- Separate `instruction received`, `documents outstanding`, `signed`, `lodged`, `prep`, `registered` and `finalised` states.
- Do not promise a fixed registration date without matter-specific support.
- “Online” or “electronic” does not mean “instant”.
- Process education is valuable content: transfer duty, compliance documents, bond cancellation, guarantees, lodgement and registration are distinct stages.

### Source L7 — Title deeds and conveyancer role

Publisher: Western Cape Government
Canonical page: https://www.westerncape.gov.za/service/title-deeds-proof-property-ownership
Source type: official public-service guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference

Verified finding:

- A lawyer specialising in property transfers, a conveyancer, assists with transfer of title into the purchaser’s name.

Safe candidate knowledge:

- Conveyancing marketing should explain professional role, process and documentation rather than presenting the estate agent, bank, bond originator and conveyancer as interchangeable.

### Source L8 — SARS transfer duty

Publisher: South African Revenue Service
Canonical page: https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/transfer-duty/register-for-transfer-duty/
Source type: official tax-process guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: tax thresholds, rates and forms are time-sensitive

Marketing implications:

- Transfer duty, transfer costs, bond costs and purchase price are different amounts.
- Any calculator or example must show date, assumptions and exclusions.
- Do not hard-code tax thresholds or rates into evergreen content without a review date.
- A zero-transfer-duty result does not imply zero transfer costs.

### Source L9 — Master of the High Court: deceased estates

Publisher: Department of Justice and Constitutional Development
Canonical pages:

- https://www.justice.gov.za/master/
- https://www.justice.gov.za/master/deceased.html
- https://www.justice.gov.za/master/deceased-how.html
- https://www.justice.gov.za/master/wills.html

Source type: official public-service and process guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: re-verify directives and online system changes

Verified findings:

- The Master supervises administration of deceased estates.
- Estate reporting, original wills, appointment, advertisements, accounts and distribution are formal process steps.
- The department provides an online registration path, but original documents may still be required.
- A will and intestate succession lead to different administration contexts.

Marketing implications:

- Use compassionate, process-clear content rather than urgency manipulation.
- Do not promise estate-finalisation timelines without file-specific evidence.
- Do not call a will “valid” from a marketing intake form.
- Separate will drafting, estate reporting, executor appointment and estate administration.

### Source L10 — Administration of Estates Act 66 of 1965

Publisher: Department of Justice and Constitutional Development
Canonical source: https://www.justice.gov.za/legislation/acts/1965-066%20admin%20estates.pdf
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal-review required

Safe candidate knowledge:

- Deceased-estate administration is prescribed by law and supervised by the Master.
- Marketing content should describe general stages and direct people to professional advice, not give personalised distribution conclusions.

### Source L11 — POPIA and confidential lead handling

Publisher: South African Government / Information Regulator
Canonical legal page: https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act
Source type: legislation and privacy framework
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal-risk flag

Industry implications:

- Legal leads commonly contain sensitive personal, financial, family, employment, health or criminal-matter information.
- Public forms should collect only what is necessary for the stated intake purpose.
- Internal notes and documents must not appear in client-facing reports or another client’s context.
- Advertising audiences built from legal-matter data require heightened caution.
- A consultation request is not consent to publish or reuse matter details.

### Source L12 — Consumer Protection Act

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/consumer-protection-act
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; scope must be reviewed for each service context

Marketing implications:

- Fees, exclusions and deliverables should be communicated clearly.
- “Free consultation” must state material conditions.
- Legal advertising should avoid misleading outcome, speed or price claims.
- The CPA’s applicability can vary by transaction and service context; do not make blanket legal conclusions.

## Buyer and client needs

### First-time legal consumer

Common needs:

- plain-language explanation;
- whether the firm handles the matter;
- urgency and next step;
- likely process;
- documents required;
- fee basis;
- confidentiality;
- realistic expectations;
- named contact.

### Conveyancing client

Common needs:

- clarity on who appointed the conveyancer;
- transfer and bond stages;
- documents and signatures;
- transfer duty and costs;
- progress updates;
- expected dependencies;
- fraud-resistant payment instructions;
- registration confirmation.

### Business client

Common needs:

- scope and commercial objective;
- turnaround dependencies;
- decision-maker availability;
- document control;
- risk allocation;
- confidentiality;
- ongoing advisory relationship;
- billing predictability.

### Litigation or dispute client

Common needs:

- urgency triage;
- limitation or deadline awareness;
- document preservation;
- merits and risk discussion;
- cost and process expectations;
- no guaranteed outcome;
- clear communication frequency.

## Website intelligence

Recommended Website tab metrics:

- service-page sessions;
- contact-form starts/completions;
- click-to-call and click-to-WhatsApp;
- booked consultations;
- organic search queries by service area;
- location and directions interactions;
- top educational articles;
- source-to-qualified-enquiry rate;
- page-level form failure;
- consent and privacy-notice status;
- practitioner-profile traffic;
- existing-client portal usage where applicable.

Do not expose:

- matter descriptions;
- privileged or confidential notes;
- uploaded evidence;
- opposing-party information;
- trust-account information;
- another client’s lead or matter data.

## Paid media implications

Potential campaign groups:

- exact service-intent search;
- location/service search;
- conveyancing and bond education;
- wills/estate planning;
- business contracts/compliance;
- labour consultations;
- remarketing only where lawful and appropriate.

High-risk practices:

- targeting sensitive legal conditions too narrowly;
- using matter details for testimonials without authority;
- implying guaranteed recovery, acquittal, divorce result or registration date;
- using fear, shame or coercion;
- calling an enquiry a retained client;
- sending confidential details into ad-platform conversion labels.

## Reporting model

Report separately:

- enquiries;
- valid enquiries;
- consultation bookings;
- attended consultations;
- qualified matters;
- mandates issued;
- mandates accepted;
- referred/declined matters;
- response time;
- service-area mix;
- source and campaign;
- cost per valid enquiry;
- cost per consultation;
- cost per accepted mandate where verified.

Never report:

- “cases won” without exact verified and ethically approved context;
- gross settlement or transaction values as marketing revenue;
- confidential matter outcomes;
- submitted enquiries as accepted mandates.

## Candidate review-state Skill Cards

### LP-01 — Credentials require exact evidence

Principle:
Use only current, supportable professional titles and practising claims.

Source basis:
Legal Practice Act and LPC enrolment.

### LP-02 — No outcome guarantee

Principle:
Explain process, options and dependencies without guaranteeing a legal result.

Limitation:
Exact professional advertising rules require current Code review.

### LP-03 — Enquiry is not client acceptance

Principle:
A submitted form does not complete conflict, compliance, mandate or professional acceptance steps.

Source basis:
LPC/FIC operational framework.

### LP-04 — Protect confidential intake

Principle:
Collect and expose only the information necessary for the intake purpose; legal lead data requires strict client isolation.

Source basis:
POPIA and professional confidentiality.

### LP-05 — Conveyancing has dependent stages

Principle:
Communicate transfer progress using real stages and dependencies rather than unsupported fixed-date promises.

Source basis:
Deeds framework, SARS and official title-deed guidance.

### LP-06 — Estate content must be compassionate and procedural

Principle:
Explain general estate steps without personalised succession conclusions or guaranteed timelines.

Source basis:
Master of the High Court and Administration of Estates Act.

### LP-07 — AML controls are not optional friction

Principle:
Applicable customer due diligence and risk controls form part of lawful professional onboarding.

Source basis:
FIC guidance.

### LP-08 — Fee claims need conditions

Principle:
Price, free-consultation and fixed-fee claims should disclose material scope and exclusions.

Source basis:
Consumer-information principles and professional standards.
