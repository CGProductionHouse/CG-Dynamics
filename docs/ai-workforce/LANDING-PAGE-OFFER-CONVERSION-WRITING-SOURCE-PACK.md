# Landing Page, Offer and Conversion Writing Source Pack

Last researched: 2026-07-26
Status: Review-gated research source pack
Division: AI Workforce, Client Intelligence

## Purpose

Give CG Dynamics a practical, source-backed system for turning campaign attention into relevant action through clearer offers, stronger landing pages, better forms and truthful conversion writing.

This pack does not promise conversion lifts. It defines how to create, test and diagnose conversion experiences without confusing clicks, submissions, qualified leads, sales and retained revenue.

## Core operating model

Audience intent
→ ad promise
→ landing-page message match
→ relevant proof
→ clear offer
→ low-friction action
→ valid confirmation
→ qualified downstream outcome

## Primary source register

### Google Ads: ad and landing-page alignment

Source: Google Ads Help, “Optimize your ads and landing pages”
Current source state: reviewed July 2026

Finding:

Google advises that the landing page should closely match the ad, keywords, promotion and call to action. A user who clicks an ad expects to find what the ad promised without searching through unrelated information.

Safe candidate claim:

Campaign landing pages should preserve message, offer and action continuity from the originating ad.

Do not overclaim:

- Do not say message match guarantees conversion.
- Do not say Quality Score is a direct business-profit score.
- Do not select a page only because it has the highest traffic.

Source URL:
https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6238826

### Google Ads: destination experience

Source: Google Advertising Policies, “Destination experience”
Current source state: reviewed July 2026

Finding:

Ad destinations should be functional, useful, safe and easy to navigate. Misleading experiences, abusive interstitials and obstructive pop-ups can create policy problems and poor user experience.

Safe candidate claim:

A paid landing page must function correctly, communicate its purpose clearly and avoid obstructive or deceptive interaction patterns.

Do not overclaim:

- Do not say every modal is prohibited.
- Do not treat passing policy review as proof the page converts.
- Do not hide material terms behind interaction traps.

Source URL:
https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16427615

### Google Ads: landing-page experience definition

Source: Google Ads glossary, “Landing page”

Finding:

Landing-page experience considers usefulness, relevance, ease of navigation and whether the page meets expectations created by the ad.

Safe candidate claim:

Landing-page review should assess usefulness, relevance, navigation and promise fulfilment separately.

Source URL:
https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14086

### W3C: accessible forms

Sources:

- W3C WAI Forms Tutorial
- W3C Labeling Controls
- W3C Validating Input

Finding:

Forms should use clear labels, meaningful instructions, accessible controls and useful error feedback. W3C also notes that users generally prefer simple forms and that irrelevant or excessive questions can increase abandonment.

Safe candidate claim:

Ask only for data needed for the current transaction or qualification step, label every field clearly and provide actionable validation feedback.

Do not overclaim:

- Do not remove required compliance or qualification fields merely to shorten a form.
- Do not use placeholder text as the only field label.
- Do not treat a technically submitted form as a qualified lead.

Source URLs:
https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/
https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/labels/
https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/validation/

### Google Ads conversion confirmation

Source: Google Ads glossary, “Conversion page”

Finding:

A conversion page or confirmed event should be reached only after the user completes the valued action. Tracking should not fire merely because the user viewed or started the form.

Safe candidate claim:

Conversion events must correspond to a completed action and retain the exact event definition.

Do not overclaim:

- Do not treat a thank-you-page load as proof the lead is genuine.
- Do not treat a submitted lead as a customer.
- Do not treat a platform conversion as accounting revenue.

Source URL:
https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/61132

## Offer definition

An offer is the complete reason and mechanism for taking the next step.

Store:

- Audience
- Buying situation
- Problem or desire
- Promised value
- Product or service
- Scope
- Price or pricing basis
- Conditions
- Availability
- Proof
- Risk-reduction mechanism
- Required action
- Fulfilment path
- Exclusions
- Expiry or review date

An offer is not merely a discount.

Offer types include:

- Direct purchase
- Book a table or stay
- Request a quote
- Schedule a consultation
- Request a site visit
- Download a useful guide
- Register a team or attendee
- Apply or enquire
- Request a demonstration
- Get a sample
- Join a waitlist
- Claim a verified promotion
- Start a trial
- Request a call-back

## Offer-quality questions

- Is the value clear to the intended audience?
- Does the offer solve a real buying situation?
- Is the action proportionate to the person’s current intent?
- Are terms and exclusions visible?
- Is urgency real?
- Is scarcity verified?
- Is the fulfilment team ready?
- Is the offer economically and operationally sustainable?
- Is the proof relevant to the claim?
- Can the result be measured downstream?

## Offer strength model

Offer strength is not a single score, but the AI may review:

- Relevance
- Specificity
- Perceived value
- Credibility
- Friction
- Risk
- Urgency
- Distinctiveness
- Fulfilment confidence

The agent must explain the evidence for each judgement and identify unknowns.

## Prohibited false-pressure patterns

Do not create:

- Fake countdown timers
- Invented “only one left” claims
- Permanent “today only” offers
- Hidden renewal or subscription terms
- Preselected paid add-ons
- Misleading before/after comparisons
- False price anchors
- Unavailable lead magnets
- A free claim with compulsory undisclosed payment
- A form that implies guaranteed acceptance

## Recommended page structure

The exact order should match audience and offer, but a common structure is:

1. Immediate promise and relevance
2. Clear explanation of what is offered
3. Proof and reason to believe
4. Who it is for and use cases
5. What happens next
6. Objection handling
7. Terms, price or qualification where material
8. Primary action
9. Additional trust and contact information

Do not force every page into the same visual template.

## Above-the-fold requirements

The initial viewport should usually make the following understandable:

- What this is
- Who it is for
- Why it matters
- What action is available
- Whether the ad promise is present

The initial viewport does not need to contain every detail, but must not conceal material qualifications.

## Conversion-writing principles

### Specific before clever

Prefer language that identifies the relevant outcome, audience, product or next step.

Weak:

“Unlock your potential.”

Stronger when supported:

“Request a site assessment for your warehouse security system.”

### One primary next step

A campaign landing page should have one primary action. Secondary paths may exist where user intent genuinely differs, such as call versus form, but the hierarchy must be clear.

### Explain the next step

Users should know:

- What happens after submission
- Who will contact them
- Expected response window if verified
- What information they need
- Whether the step is a quote, application, booking request or confirmed booking

### Use proof next to the claim

Proof types:

- Demonstration
- Product specification
- Professional registration
- Verified review
- Case study
- Process evidence
- Before/after with lawful evidence
- Current stock or availability
- Independent certification
- Real project imagery

Do not place a general testimonial beside an unrelated claim and imply it proves that claim.

### Address objections truthfully

Common objections include:

- Price
- Time
- Fit
- Risk
- Complexity
- Availability
- Trust
- Switching effort
- Approval requirements
- Delivery or service area

Answer only with verified client facts.

## Headline system

Generate headline concepts from different strategic routes:

- Outcome
- Problem recognition
- Audience qualification
- Product or service specificity
- Use case
- Proof
- Comparison
- Location
- Timing
- Risk reduction

Do not generate twenty cosmetic variants of one headline and call that strategy.

Headline review:

- Is it relevant to the originating ad?
- Is it supported?
- Is the subject clear?
- Is the intended audience likely to recognise the buying situation?
- Does it avoid generic superlatives?
- Does it leave room for the page to prove the claim?

## CTA system

CTA text should name the actual next step.

Examples:

- Request a quote
- Check available dates
- Book a consultation
- Register your team
- Download the specification guide
- Ask about delivery
- Start your application

Use “Buy now” only where the person can actually complete a purchase.
Use “Book now” only where the action creates a confirmed booking or clearly state “Request booking”.

## Form design system

### Field strategy

Separate:

- Required to contact
- Required to route
- Required to qualify
- Required by law
- Nice to know later

Move non-essential questions out of the first interaction.

### Field-label rules

- Use visible labels.
- State expected format where needed.
- Mark required fields clearly.
- Give useful error messages.
- Preserve entered information after validation errors where safe.
- Make buttons descriptive.
- Ensure keyboard and assistive-technology use.

### Sensitive data

Do not collect health, financial, identity, child or other sensitive information through a generic marketing form unless the lawful purpose, security and industry workflow have been reviewed.

## Confirmation state

After valid submission, show:

- What was received
- What happens next
- Expected next-step timing where verified
- Reference or summary where useful
- Alternative urgent contact only when appropriate

Do not display a success state before the backend confirms receipt.

## Funnel truth

Page view
≠ engaged visit
≠ CTA click
≠ form start
≠ form submission
≠ valid lead
≠ qualified lead
≠ quote
≠ sale
≠ retained revenue

Each metric must preserve its definition and source.

## Landing-page metrics

Possible measures:

- Ad clicks
- Valid landing-page sessions
- Engaged sessions
- CTA clicks
- Form starts
- Form completions
- Form error rate
- Call clicks
- Booking starts
- Completed bookings
- Purchases
- Qualified leads
- Cost per qualified lead
- Downstream conversion rate
- Retained revenue where verified

Do not optimise only for submission volume where lead quality is poor.

## Valid test examples

- Proof-first hero versus outcome-first hero
- Short initial form versus staged qualification
- Quote CTA versus consultation CTA for the same offer
- Case-study proof versus process proof
- Price shown versus request-price where both are operationally truthful

Do not call it a clean test where audience, offer, campaign objective, page, budget and follow-up process all changed.

## Diagnostic model

### High clicks, low valid sessions

Check:

- Page availability
- Redirects
- Mobile loading
- accidental clicks
- tracking mismatch

### Valid sessions, low action

Check:

- Ad-page mismatch
- unclear offer
- weak proof
- wrong CTA
- unsuitable audience
- mobile friction
- hidden terms

### Many starts, few completions

Check:

- Form length
- validation errors
- unclear questions
- sensitive requests
- technical failures
- unexpected qualification

### Many submissions, few qualified leads

Check:

- Audience targeting
- promise accuracy
- qualification clarity
- fraud or spam
- offer mismatch
- staff follow-up

### Qualified leads, low sales

Do not automatically blame the landing page. Check:

- Sales response time
- quote quality
- pricing
- product fit
- stock
- service capacity
- follow-up
- competitor context

## Non-negotiable safeguards

- No fake urgency or scarcity.
- No guaranteed outcome without evidence.
- No ad-page promise mismatch.
- No click or submission renamed as revenue.
- No inaccessible unlabeled forms.
- No sensitive data collected without reviewed purpose.
- No experiment winner from one shallow metric.
- No unreviewed AI page published automatically.
