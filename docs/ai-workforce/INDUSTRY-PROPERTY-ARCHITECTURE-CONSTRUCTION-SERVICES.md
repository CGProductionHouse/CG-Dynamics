## Industry structure

The AI must separate these journeys:

1. Architectural professional services
   - feasibility;
   - briefing;
   - concept and design development;
   - statutory submission;
   - technical documentation;
   - procurement support;
   - contract administration and close-out where appointed.

2. Property development
   - site and rights assessment;
   - concept and feasibility;
   - funding and approvals;
   - design and procurement;
   - construction;
   - sales/leasing and occupation.

3. Residential construction and renovation
   - brief and budget;
   - professional appointments;
   - approvals;
   - contractor procurement;
   - construction;
   - inspections, completion and defects.

4. Contractor procurement
   - capability and class of work;
   - public versus private procurement;
   - tender eligibility;
   - programme, quality, health and safety;
   - payment and completion evidence.

5. Property sales and leasing
   - listing and disclosure;
   - enquiry and qualification;
   - viewing;
   - offer or lease application;
   - finance, legal and transfer dependencies;
   - occupation and after-sales.

6. Planning and land-use applications
   - current zoning and rights;
   - proposed use;
   - application pathway;
   - municipal process;
   - public participation where required;
   - decision and conditions.

A house-design enquiry, contractor quote, development opportunity, property viewing and rezoning application are not equivalent leads.

## Source register

### Source P1 — Architectural Profession Act and SACAP registration

Publisher: South African Council for the Architectural Profession / South African Government
Canonical sources:

- https://www.sacapsa.com/faq/registrations
- https://www.gov.za/documents/architectural-profession-act

Source type: statutory professional-regulation source
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: verify registration categories, scope rules and current status

Verified findings:

- A person may not practise in an architectural registration category unless registered with SACAP.
- Architectural titles and representations are regulated.
- Any person providing architectural services to the public must be correctly registered for the work represented.

Marketing implications:

- “Architect”, “Professional Architect”, “Professional Senior Architectural Technologist”, “Professional Architectural Technologist”, “Professional Architectural Draughtsperson” and candidate titles are not interchangeable.
- Staff biographies must match current registration category and status.
- A candidate professional must not be presented as a registered professional architect.
- Registration is evidence of professional status, not proof that every project will be approved, completed on time or delivered within budget.

Prohibited overclaims:

- “Guaranteed plan approval.”
- “Architect” where the person is not registered in the relevant category.
- “Council-approved designer” without exact evidence.
- “No architect or professional required” as a universal claim.

### Source P2 — Council for the Built Environment mandate

Publisher: Council for the Built Environment
Canonical page: https://www.cbe.org.za/Pages/What-We-Do.asp
Source type: statutory built-environment regulator
Rights treatment: official_reference

Verified findings:

- The CBE coordinates and oversees South Africa’s built-environment professional councils.
- Its mandate includes protecting public interest, supporting professional standards, health, safety, environmental protection and sound governance.

Marketing implications:

- Built-environment credentials, competence and professional category should be verified at the correct council.
- Professional titles and registration numbers are operational facts, not decorative credibility claims.
- Professional-registration claims should retain source, category and last-verified date.

### Source P3 — SACPCMP professional registration

Publisher: South African Council for the Project and Construction Management Professions
Canonical sources:

- https://mypcm.sacpcmp.org.za/Register-Who
- https://mypcm.sacpcmp.org.za/Register-Categories
- https://mypcm.sacpcmp.org.za/About_Regulatory

Source type: statutory professional regulator
Rights treatment: official_reference

Verified findings:

- Professional Construction Managers and Professional Construction Project Managers are regulated registration categories.
- A person may not practise under those professional titles unless registered in the relevant category.
- Candidate and professional categories are distinct.

Marketing implications:

- “Project manager”, “construction manager”, “PrCPM” and “PrCM” must not be conflated.
- Project-management content should distinguish coordination, professional services, physical construction management and contractor responsibilities.
- “Turnkey” does not erase professional, contractor, statutory or client responsibilities.

### Source P4 — Spatial Planning and Land Use Management Act 16 of 2013

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/spatial-planning-and-land-use-management-act
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal/planning risk flag
Freshness: verify amendments and municipal by-laws

Verified findings:

- SPLUMA provides the national framework for spatial planning and land-use management.
- Municipal planning tribunals and local application procedures form part of the land-use decision system.
- Land-use applications and decisions operate across national principles, provincial frameworks and municipal schemes/by-laws.

Marketing implications:

- Ownership does not automatically permit any proposed use.
- Zoning, consent use, rezoning, subdivision, consolidation, removal of restrictions, building-plan approval and environmental authorisation are distinct processes.
- An application submitted is not an approval.
- Approval conditions, appeals and related authorisations may affect implementation.

Prohibited overclaims:

- “Any property can be rezoned.”
- “Guaranteed subdivision.”
- “Planning approval in 30 days” without exact municipal/process evidence.
- “No public participation required” as a universal statement.

### Source P5 — National Building Regulations and Building Standards Act

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/national-building-regulations-and-building-standards-act-16-apr-2015-1302
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; technical/legal risk flag

Verified findings:

- Building erection and approval operate within a formal regulatory framework.
- Building-plan approval, professional design, product compliance, inspection and occupation are separate states.

Marketing implications:

- “Approved”, “compliant”, “safe”, “structural” and “ready for occupation” require exact evidence.
- A rendered image or concept design is not an approved building plan.
- Municipal approval does not prove correct construction or final occupancy status.
- A completed-looking project image does not prove final completion, occupation or defect-free status.

### Source P6 — NHBRC mandate and home-builder registration

Publisher: National Home Builders Registration Council
Canonical sources:

- https://www.nhbrc.org.za/about/
- https://www.nhbrc.org.za/registration-process/
- https://www.nhbrc.org.za/home-builders-inspection-process/

Source type: statutory home-building regulator
Rights treatment: official_reference

Verified findings:

- Persons in the business of home building must be registered with the NHBRC.
- Home enrolment, inspections and consumer-protection processes are separate from municipal approval and professional appointments.

Marketing implications:

- “NHBRC registered”, “NHBRC enrolled” and “NHBRC inspected” are different claims.
- A registered builder is not automatically proof that a specific home is enrolled.
- An inspection at one stage is not proof of full compliance at every stage.

### Source P7 — cidb Register of Contractors

Publisher: Construction Industry Development Board
Canonical sources:

- https://www.cidb.org.za/contractors/register-of-contractors/overview/
- https://www.cidb.org.za/clients/procurement-guides/applying-register-of-contractors/
- https://portal.cidb.org.za/RegisterOfContractors/

Source type: statutory construction-procurement register
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: verify grade, class and active status online

Verified findings:

- The cidb Register of Contractors supports public-sector construction procurement.
- Contractors are graded from 1 to 9 according to financial and works capability.
- Classes of works and tender-value limits matter.
- Public-sector clients must apply the required grading in procurement.
- A contractor’s status should be verified from the active online register.

Marketing implications:

- A cidb grade is not a universal quality score.
- A grade does not prove suitability outside the registered class of works.
- A historical grade or certificate image is not current proof of active registration.
- Home builders fall under a different regulatory context and may be exempt from the cidb Register of Contractors.

Prohibited overclaims:

- “cidb approved for all projects.”
- “Grade 9 means highest workmanship quality.”
- “Registered contractor” without current grade/class/status evidence.

### Source P8 — Statistics South Africa P5041.1 building statistics

Publisher: Statistics South Africa
Canonical page: https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P5041.1&page_id=1854
Source type: official recurring building statistics
Rights treatment: official_reference; retain month and category
Freshness: monthly

Verified findings:

- P5041.1 reports selected private-sector building plans passed and buildings completed as reported by local government institutions.
- The May 2026 release was published on 23 July 2026.
- Residential, non-residential and additions/alterations are separate categories.
- Plans passed and buildings completed are separate measures and may move differently.

Safe uses:

- Provide dated market context by category and comparison basis.
- Describe planning/completion pipeline movement without projecting it directly onto a client.

Prohibited overclaims:

- “Plans passed increased, therefore construction revenue increased by the same amount.”
- “National completions prove this architect’s pipeline is growing.”
- “Building activity is up” without defining category, period and measure.

### Source P9 — Consumer Protection Act

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/consumer-protection-act
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal-risk flag

Marketing implications:

- Fees, scope, exclusions, timelines, deliverables and dependencies should be communicated clearly.
- “Free consultation”, “fixed fee”, “turnkey”, “all inclusive”, “guaranteed completion” and “no hidden costs” require exact conditions.
- Renderings, property images and portfolio claims must not materially misrepresent what was designed, built or delivered.
- Testimonials and project outcomes need permission and factual support.

### Source P10 — Professional and project-role separation

Source families:

- SACAP;
- CBE and professional councils;
- SACPCMP;
- cidb;
- NHBRC;
- municipal planning/building-control sources;
- project-specific appointments and contracts.

Knowledge rule:

- Client, architect, engineer, quantity surveyor, project manager, contractor, subcontractor, property practitioner, developer and municipality have different roles.
- The AI must not attribute one party’s statutory or contractual responsibility to another without evidence.
- “Full service” is a scope claim and must be tied to an actual appointment.

### Market reality

Property, architecture and construction purchases are infrequent, high-value and
often stressful. Buyers seek trust, proof, process clarity and risk reduction
before engaging.

The AI must separate these journeys: architectural professional services;
property development; residential construction and renovation; contractor
procurement; property sales and leasing; building-material procurement for
projects; facilities and maintenance management.

### Campaign and ad angles

**Process before project** — Show how the practice or firm works from first
contact to completion. Make the process visible, predictable and reassuring.

**Proof through completed work** — Present real completed projects with scope,
team role, challenges, solutions and outcomes. Do not present concept images as
completed work.

**Trust and credentials** — Use verified professional registrations, insurance,
industry association memberships and relevant completed projects. Avoid implying
unique capability without evidence.

**Risk reduction** — Explain how the firm manages budget, programme, quality,
safety, variations, defects and dispute resolution. Show transparent fee or
pricing structures where applicable.

**Local knowledge** — Emphasise familiarity with local authorities, bylaws,
municipal processes, subcontractor networks and site conditions. Do not claim
exclusive relationships.
