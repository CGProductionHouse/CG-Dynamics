## Purpose

Create a trustworthy automotive industry layer for marketing strategy, social content, paid ads, lead generation, website reporting and client reporting across:

- new-vehicle dealerships;
- used-vehicle dealerships;
- commercial vehicles and fleets;
- workshops and service centres;
- parts and accessories;
- tyre and fitment businesses;
- body repair and related after-sales services.

This industry pack does not classify any client from its name. Client classification still requires official client evidence.

## Industry structure

The automotive customer journey is not one market. The AI must separate:

1. New vehicle retail
   - passenger cars;
   - light commercial vehicles;
   - medium/heavy commercial vehicles;
   - new-energy vehicles;
   - fleet and business purchases.

2. Used vehicle retail
   - trade-in;
   - finance approval;
   - vehicle history and condition;
   - warranty and after-sales expectations.

3. Ownership and after-sales
   - scheduled servicing;
   - maintenance;
   - repair diagnosis;
   - warranty work;
   - parts replacement;
   - tyres, brakes, batteries and wear items.

4. Fleet and commercial operations
   - uptime;
   - payload and application fit;
   - fuel and total operating cost;
   - service-network support;
   - parts availability;
   - driver and road-safety considerations.

5. Automotive retail trust
   - transparent pricing;
   - clear exclusions;
   - authorisation before additional work;
   - safety and product compliance;
   - complaint and dispute pathways.

Source type: official industry statistical reference

Current verified findings:

- The 2026 manual is presented by naamsa as the official source of key automotive data in South Africa.
- South African vehicle production increased from 600,473 units in 2024 to 618,077 in 2025.
- Vehicle exports reached 414,271 units in 2025.
- Automotive export value reached R291.0 billion in 2025.
- Automotive exports represented 15.6% of total South African exports in 2025.
- South Africa accounted for 50.3% of African vehicle production and 46.5% of African vehicle sales in 2025.
- naamsa identifies policy support, export demand, localisation, new-energy transition, logistics, infrastructure and adaptability as major industry-growth factors.

Safe candidate knowledge:

- South Africa's automotive sector is economically important, export-oriented and sensitive to policy, logistics, technology and consumer affordability.
- Automotive content should distinguish local retail demand from manufacturing and export performance.
- A national production or export trend must not be presented as proof that one dealership is growing.

Prohibited overclaims:

- "The automotive market is booming everywhere."
- "Exports prove local consumers are buying more vehicles."
- "Every dealership benefits equally from national production growth."

Verified scope:

- The Competition Commission maintains Guidelines for Competition in the South African Automotive Aftermarket.
- The guidelines are intended to promote competition and consumer choice across servicing, repairs, insurance and parts-related aftermarket activity.

Marketing implications:

- Do not imply that consumers always lose manufacturer warranty merely by using an independent service provider.
- Do not make blanket "dealer-only" service claims without exact, current legal and warranty support.
- Distinguish manufacturer warranty conditions, service-plan conditions, insurance terms and general consumer rights.
- Independent workshop marketing may discuss accreditation, capability, transparency and consumer choice without making unverified attacks on franchised dealers.

Prohibited overclaims:

- "Any workshop can service any car without affecting anything."
- "Manufacturer parts are unnecessary."
- "Independent servicing can never affect a claim."
- "Dealers are legally required to honour every repair from any provider."

### Source A6 — Implied warranty of quality

Publisher: South African Government / automotive industry-code material
Reference: https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/36155gen113a.pdf
Source type: official code material referencing CPA section 56
Rights treatment: official_reference; human/legal review required before production activation

Verified narrow rule from the cited material:

- Goods carry an implied warranty that they comply with the quality requirements contemplated in section 55, subject to the Act and applicable exceptions.
- Within six months after delivery, a consumer may have statutory remedies for goods that fail the applicable quality requirements.
- A repaired item that fails again within the specified period may trigger further remedies under the Act.

Important limitations:

- The exact remedy depends on facts, transaction type, exclusions, alterations, misuse, proof and current law.
- Do not turn this into a universal "six-month no-questions refund" claim.
- Marketing content must not replace legal review or case-specific advice.

### Source A7 — NRCS tyre compulsory specifications

Publisher: National Regulator for Compulsory Specifications
Canonical page: https://www.nrcs.org.za/business-units/automotive/tyres
Source type: official safety and product-compliance guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: re-verify compulsory specifications and linked standards

Verified findings:

- Pneumatic tyres for passenger and commercial vehicles and trailers in South Africa must conform to compulsory safety standards.
- Passenger-car tyres fall under VC8056 / ECE Regulation 30.
- Commercial-vehicle tyres fall under VC8059 / ECE Regulation 54.
- Required sidewall information includes size, tread pattern, load/speed index, approval mark, manufacture date and construction/type details.
- Homologation exists to reduce the risk of non-compliant tyres entering the market.

Marketing implications:

- Tyre marketing may educate around correct size, load/speed rating, application and compliance markings.
- "Road legal", "approved", "safe" and "commercial grade" must be linked to exact product evidence.
- A tyre's age, condition, tread, pressure, load, fitment and use all matter; one compliance mark does not prove current roadworthiness.

Prohibited overclaims:

- "E-mark means this tyre is safe in every condition."
- "This tyre cannot fail."
- "Any tyre of the same diameter is suitable."

Useful researched distinctions:

- A warranty, service plan and maintenance plan are not interchangeable.
- Extended warranties generally focus on specified mechanical/electrical risk and often exclude routine servicing, consumables and wear.
- Work beyond the authorised scope should not be presented as automatically chargeable.
- Accreditation and documented processes can support trust and dispute recourse.

## Buyer needs and decision drivers

### New passenger vehicle buyer

Typical decision areas:

- affordability and finance structure;
- monthly ownership cost;
- reliability and warranty;
- fuel or energy consumption;
- safety features;
- technology and comfort;
- resale and trade-in;
- service-plan coverage;
- availability and delivery time;
- dealership trust and after-sales support.

Content that adds value:

- model comparison by real use case;
- transparent finance examples;
- feature demonstrations;
- ownership-cost explanations;
- delivery and handover stories;
- service-plan and warranty explanations;
- customer questions answered plainly.

### Used vehicle buyer

Higher-risk questions:

- prior ownership and service history;
- accident/repair history where known;
- mileage and condition;
- inspection evidence;
- finance and affordability;
- warranty scope and exclusions;
- tyre/brake/battery condition;
- trade-in process;
- after-sales recourse.

Trust content should be evidence-led, not cosmetic:

- inspection process;
- documented history;
- actual condition disclosures;
- clear warranty terms;
- named contact and follow-up path;
- no vague "perfect condition" language without support.

### Workshop/service customer

Core anxieties:

- surprise costs;
- poor diagnosis;
- unauthorised work;
- inferior or unsuitable parts;
- repeated failures;
- loss of mobility;
- unclear timelines;
- weak communication;
- uncertainty about warranty/recourse.

High-value content:

- what a service includes;
- service versus safety inspection;
- diagnosis before repair;
- authorisation process;
- old-parts return policy where applicable;
- OEM, equivalent and aftermarket part distinctions;
- progress updates;
- warranty and workmanship terms;
- maintenance by vehicle age/mileage/use.

### Commercial vehicle and fleet buyer

Primary decision areas:

- vehicle/application fit;
- payload and body configuration;
- uptime and service support;
- total cost of ownership;
- parts supply;
- driver safety;
- finance and replacement cycle;
- route and duty cycle;
- fuel/energy efficiency;
- residual value;
- fleet reporting.

Marketing should avoid applying passenger-car language to business fleet decisions.

## Candidate industry Skill Cards

All cards enter `needs_review`.

### AUTO-01 — Separate market context from client performance

Principle:
National automotive production, exports and motor-trade sales provide context, but they do not prove one dealership's demand, revenue or lead quality.

Sources:
naamsa 2026 Trade Manual; Stats SA P6343.2.

### AUTO-02 — Treat automotive lead types as different commercial events

Principle:
A test-drive request, finance lead, trade-in lead, service booking, tyre quote and parts enquiry have different intent and value. Report them separately before aggregating.

Evidence basis:
Industry journey structure plus lead-measurement truth rules.

### AUTO-03 — Use transparent finance language

Principle:
Vehicle finance content should make the cash price, deposit, instalment, term, interest assumptions, residual/balloon and relevant fees clear enough for the audience to understand the offer.

Risk:
Legal/compliance review required for each live offer.

### AUTO-04 — Explain warranty, service and maintenance separately

Principle:
Do not use warranty, service plan, maintenance plan and extended warranty as interchangeable terms. State coverage and exclusions from the actual contract.

Sources:
CPA/code materials; RMI/MIWA supporting guidance.

### AUTO-05 — Authorisation and transparency build workshop trust

Principle:
Workshop marketing should emphasise diagnosis, quoted scope, customer authorisation, progress communication and documented workmanship rather than unsupported "best mechanic" claims.

### AUTO-06 — Tyre fitment is application-specific

Principle:
Tyre size, load/speed rating, compliance marking and vehicle/application requirements must be checked together. A matching diameter or brand reputation alone is insufficient.

Source:
NRCS tyre guidance.

### AUTO-07 — Accreditation is evidence, not immunity

Principle:
Accreditation or association membership can support process and recourse claims, but does not prove every job is faultless or remove the need for evidence.

### AUTO-08 — Commercial-vehicle marketing centres uptime and application fit

Principle:
Fleet and truck buyers need operational fit, uptime, support and ownership-cost evidence; passenger-car lifestyle messaging alone is inadequate.

### AUTO-09 — Used-stock claims need item-level evidence

Principle:
Claims about condition, history, mileage, inspection, ownership or accident status must be supported for the specific vehicle.

### AUTO-10 — Safety education must avoid guarantees

Principle:
Maintenance and tyre content may explain risk reduction and compliance, but should not promise that a product or service prevents every accident or failure.
