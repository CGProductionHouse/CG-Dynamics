# Community, Sport and Entertainment Human Marketing Goldmine

Last updated: 2026-08-06
Status: Researched repository pack; all derived knowledge and skill cards remain review-gated.
Scope: Community organisations, grassroots sport, professional sport, clubs, leagues, fan communities, ticketed entertainment, sponsorship, memberships and local events.

## Purpose

This pack exists to help CG Production House create marketing that turns belonging into action:

- ticket purchases;
- memberships and registrations;
- fixture attendance;
- repeat attendance;
- supporter participation;
- sponsorship value;
- community involvement;
- volunteer and donor response;
- event discovery;
- creator and fan participation.

It does not treat sport or entertainment as generic poster production. The work must understand rituals, rivalries, local identity, anticipation, pride, access, affordability and the social reason people attend together.

## Core human truths

People rarely attend only because an event exists. They attend because:

- their people will be there;
- the occasion means something locally;
- the atmosphere promises to be worth leaving home for;
- they can participate, not only watch;
- they understand exactly when, where and how to join;
- the event feels safe, credible and well organised;
- they do not want to miss a moment others will talk about;
- the team, artist, school, town or community represents part of who they are.

Useful customer language to research:

- “Who else is going?”
- “Is it family friendly?”
- “Where do we park?”
- “Can I buy at the gate?”
- “What time does the main game/start act begin?”
- “Is there food and a bar?”
- “Will it be worth the drive?”
- “Can the kids join?”
- “Where do I register?”
- “What do members actually get?”
- “How do we support the team?”

## Audience groups

### Spectators and fans

Need:

- a reason to care;
- simple ticketing;
- clear fixture or programme information;
- atmosphere proof;
- social proof that others are attending;
- transport, parking and venue clarity;
- repeated reminders near the event.

### Participants and members

Need:

- visible progression;
- belonging;
- coaching or programme quality;
- age, level and eligibility clarity;
- real schedules and fees;
- proof that organisers are active and reliable.

### Parents and families

Need:

- safety;
- supervision;
- logistics;
- developmental value;
- honest costs;
- family-friendly facilities;
- trustworthy organisers.

### Sponsors and partners

Need:

- audience fit;
- credible attendance and reach data;
- visible activation opportunities;
- content rights and deliverables;
- community value;
- measurable outcomes beyond logo placement.

### Local communities

Need:

- representation;
- access;
- respect for local culture and language;
- practical benefit;
- a visible role in the event, not only passive targeting.

## Proven and useful campaign references

### Carling Black Label Cup and fan control

The core idea gave supporters an active role in team and match decisions instead of treating them as passive viewers. Later Carling Cup work continued using voting, participation and fan energy as campaign mechanics.

Reported evidence:

- the 2023 Fak’Ugesi campaign reportedly achieved a 36% increase in unique voters;
- WARC reports a 51% sales increase and stronger match attendance and excitement;
- the result remains campaign-reported evidence and must not be generalised as proof that any voting mechanic will cause the same outcome.

Marketing lesson:

The strongest sports activations give fans a meaningful action connected to the competition itself.

Sources:

- https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/the-carling-black-label-cup-and-the-campaign-that-changed-fan-engagement-950334a
- https://www.warc.com/en/article/ab-inbev-sa%3A-carling-cup-2023-%E2%80%93-fak%27ugesi-%28bring-the-energy%29-735c3c7370324e949b697d75dd04ad90

### Rugby World Cup Sevens fan experience

Moonsport describes an eight-month campaign combining digital content, ticket sales, sponsorship activation, stadium presentation, artists, DJs, competitions and a fan village.

Reported evidence:

- more than 150,000 attendees;
- two of three days sold out;
- near-capacity attendance for most of the three-day event.

Marketing lesson:

For major sport, the entertainment surrounding the fixture can be part of the product. Marketing must sell the complete day, not only the match.

Source:

- https://www.moonsport.tv/work/rugby-world-cup-sevens

### All Blacks vs Springboks at Twickenham

Fifty Three Six used phased media, expat targeting, fan-page audiences, key buying dates, updated ticket messaging and retargeting.

Reported evidence:

- more than 80,000 attendance;
- 9.5x media ROAS;
- campaign-attributed ticket sales accounted for 11% of attendance.

Marketing lesson:

Long-ticketing windows need phased demand creation, not one launch burst and last-minute panic.

Source:

- https://www.53six.ie/case-studies-post/all-blacks-vs-springboks-campaign

### Jazz in the Lights

The campaign marketed a cultural event through digital ticket conversion, community gathering and heritage storytelling.

Marketing lesson:

Local entertainment performs better when the campaign sells the social and cultural occasion, not only the performer list.

Source:

- https://cxke.co.za/portfolio/jazzinthelights/

### Carling Black Label Varsity Cup engagement platform

The campaign combined match predictions, game mechanics, location check-ins and retail rewards across 30 matches.

Reported evidence:

- 11,444 registrations;
- 13,886 unique logins;
- 12,713 game plays;
- 3,044 vouchers won.

Marketing lesson:

A season can become a repeated participation system rather than a collection of disconnected match posters.

Source:

- https://www.socialwiiv.com/Case-Study-Carling-Black-Label-Varsity-Cup.html

### Coca-Cola Fan Can Be a Bok

The 2026 activation turns fandom into a rare match-day experience, including team access, collectible packaging and a direct fan role.

Marketing lesson:

Experiential prizes work when they provide access money normally cannot buy and connect directly to fan identity.

Source:

- https://www.coca-cola.com/za/en/media-center/Coca-Cola-South-Africa-Gives-Fans-a-Chance-to-Live-Like-a-Springbok-on-Match-Day

### Betway Catch a Million

MSCSPORTS describes a mechanic where spectators could win by taking a clean one-handed catch during SA20 matches.

Marketing lesson:

The best stadium activations use something naturally possible in the sport and make the fan part of the live action.

Source:

- https://mscsports.co.za/

### KFC Micro Soccer

YONDER reports that a packaging-linked AR football game increased engagement, return on ad spend and online basket value.

Reported evidence:

- 135% increase in engagement rate;
- 132% increase in ROAS;
- 21% increase in online ordering basket value.

Marketing lesson:

Technology is valuable only when it creates a simple, playful action linked to an existing customer moment.

Source:

- https://www.yonder.co.za/kfc-micro-soccer

## Content engine

### Before the event or fixture

- why this match or event matters;
- rivalry and history;
- player, performer or participant profiles;
- training and preparation;
- venue walkthroughs;
- first-timer guides;
- ticket and registration explainers;
- fan predictions;
- local supporter stories;
- “who are you bringing?” participation posts;
- countdown moments based on real milestones;
- sponsor-supported access or transport information.

### During the event

- arrivals and fan outfits;
- live atmosphere;
- behind-the-scenes preparation;
- crowd reactions;
- key moments;
- fan challenges;
- partner activations;
- food, family and social moments;
- quick interviews;
- practical live updates.

### After the event

- result and highlight recap;
- supporter reactions;
- thank-you content;
- best fan moments;
- volunteer and staff recognition;
- sponsor proof;
- next fixture or event path;
- membership or season conversion;
- photo galleries and searchable recaps.

## Human commercial structures

### The town shows up

1. Open on recognisable local people preparing.
2. Show the journey to the venue.
3. Build sound and atmosphere.
4. Reveal the event through human reactions.
5. End with a clear invitation and exact date.

### The ritual

1. A supporter repeats a familiar pre-match ritual.
2. Others recognise and join it.
3. The ritual leads into the live occasion.
4. The brand or event supports the ritual without hijacking it.

### The first time

1. Follow a first-time participant or spectator.
2. Show their uncertainty honestly.
3. Let real people guide them.
4. Reveal the moment they understand the appeal.
5. End with a low-friction next step.

### The fan decides

1. Present a real decision, vote, prediction or challenge.
2. Explain the consequence clearly.
3. Show participation visibly.
4. Close the loop after the event.

### The community made it happen

1. Introduce the local need or opportunity.
2. Show organisers, volunteers, clubs or partners doing the work.
3. Show the real outcome.
4. Invite continued participation.

## Paid media directions

### Ticketed events

Use:

- broad awareness early;
- performer, team and interest audiences;
- local radius audiences;
- website and video retargeting;
- cart or checkout retargeting;
- buyer lookalikes only where data quality is strong;
- phased creative linked to real buying moments;
- final availability messaging based on truthful stock.

Do not:

- run one identical poster for eight weeks;
- use false “almost sold out” claims;
- optimise only for clicks when ticket purchases can be measured;
- ignore mobile checkout friction.

### Memberships and registrations

Use:

- search ads for exact sport, age group, town and programme;
- parent and participant testimonial content;
- trial-session offers;
- lead qualification by age, area, level and availability;
- remarketing to incomplete registrations.

### Sponsorship campaigns

Use:

- audience and community proof;
- activation footage;
- measurable participation;
- sponsor-role clarity;
- post-event delivery reporting;
- content rights and reusable assets.

## Giveaways and activations

Useful prizes:

- team or artist access;
- signed or match-used items with verified provenance;
- season tickets;
- training sessions;
- family event packages;
- transport and accommodation where relevant;
- kit or equipment linked to participation;
- community-club grants;
- youth coaching experiences.

Strong activations:

- supporter voting;
- prediction leagues;
- fan cams;
- local-hero nominations;
- school or club challenges;
- skills competitions;
- community relay events;
- fixture passport systems;
- sponsor-funded access days;
- open training sessions;
- supporter storytelling booths;
- live mural, chant or anthem creation;
- mobile registration stations.

## Photography and video direction

Prioritise:

- faces and reactions;
- groups arriving together;
- scale and atmosphere;
- recognisable local context;
- action with a clear subject;
- preparation and effort;
- volunteers and organisers;
- family participation;
- authentic supporter rituals;
- sponsor integration inside real activity.

Avoid:

- empty venue shots presented as atmosphere;
- only podium or handover images;
- endless logo walls;
- generic stock crowds;
- content that excludes the actual community;
- overproduced emotion that feels staged.

## Reviews and reputation

Monitor themes around:

- ticketing;
- parking;
- queues;
- safety;
- bathrooms;
- food and beverage;
- programme timing;
- communication;
- refunds;
- family friendliness;
- organiser professionalism;
- coaching quality;
- fairness and inclusion.

A viral highlight cannot repair repeated operational complaints. Marketing must report recurring friction to organisers rather than burying it.

## Measurement model

### Events

Report:

- ticket sales;
- paid attendance;
- complimentary attendance separately;
- capacity percentage;
- ticket revenue;
- cost per purchase;
- channel-attributed purchases;
- checkout abandonment;
- repeat buyer rate where available;
- arrival or scan rate;
- refund and cancellation rate;
- geographic demand;
- sponsor activation participation;
- post-event membership or next-event conversion.

### Clubs and programmes

Report:

- enquiries;
- qualified enquiries;
- trial attendance;
- registrations;
- paid memberships;
- retention;
- attendance frequency;
- member referrals;
- age and programme fit;
- source-to-registration conversion.

### Community campaigns

Report:

- verified participants;
- volunteers;
- donations or resources delivered;
- partner involvement;
- attendance;
- repeat participation;
- geographic reach;
- outcome evidence;
- unresolved limitations.

Do not call reach, video views or registrations “attendance”. Do not call a sponsor logo impression “community impact”.

## Anti-sludge language standard

Reject unless directly supported and made specific:

- “more than a game”;
- “where passion comes alive”;
- “unforgettable experience”;
- “bringing communities together”;
- “feel the energy”;
- “the ultimate fan experience”;
- “something for everyone”;
- “a celebration of talent”;
- “making memories”;
- “uniting through sport”.

Replace with concrete human detail:

- what people will do;
- who they will meet;
- what access they receive;
- why this fixture matters;
- what changes because they participate;
- what the town, club or community recognises as meaningful.

## Claim-risk register

Never publish without evidence:

- largest or biggest event;
- fastest-growing league;
- sold out;
- record attendance;
- number-one club;
- guaranteed selection or development outcomes;
- safest event;
- official partner status;
- charity impact totals;
- athlete or artist endorsement;
- ticket scarcity;
- audience demographics;
- sponsor reach or value.

## Rights and safeguarding

- Obtain appropriate consent for identifiable children and vulnerable participants.
- Confirm music, broadcast, athlete, artist, team-mark and venue rights.
- Do not reuse broadcast footage merely because it appears online.
- Confirm competition rules, prize fulfilment and eligibility.
- Separate alcohol-brand work from minors and youth participation.
- Do not publish private injury, medical or disciplinary information.
- Respect team, league and sponsor category exclusivity.
