# AI Workforce — Healthcare, Wellness and Personal Care Industry Library

Last updated: 2026-07-26
Status: Actual researched industry pack; all production knowledge remains review-gated.
Territory: South Africa.

## Purpose

Create a trustworthy industry layer for:

- medical and dental practices;
- psychology and counselling services;
- physiotherapy, podiatry and biokinetics;
- dietetics and nutrition;
- optometry and related regulated services;
- pharmacies and pharmaceutical services;
- allied and complementary health practitioners;
- medical-aesthetic and device-led services;
- wellness, fitness and personal-care businesses;
- beauty, skincare and non-medical treatment businesses.

This pack supports marketing strategy, social content, paid media, appointment generation, website intelligence, lead reporting, reputation and compliance-risk review. It does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, treatment recommendations or legal advice.

## Industry structure

The AI must distinguish these journeys:

1. Regulated clinical care
   - practitioner registration;
   - scope of practice;
   - informed consent;
   - confidentiality;
   - consultation, assessment, diagnosis and treatment stages.

2. Allied and complementary healthcare
   - exact statutory council and discipline;
   - registered qualification and scope;
   - evidence limits;
   - referral and escalation boundaries.

3. Pharmacy and health-product retail
   - pharmacy and premises status;
   - medicine scheduling;
   - prescription versus non-prescription products;
   - pharmacist services;
   - product and promotional restrictions.

4. Medical devices and aesthetic procedures
   - device classification and regulatory status;
   - practitioner competence;
   - indication, risks and limitations;
   - before/after evidence and consent;
   - adverse-event and complaint pathways.

5. General wellness and personal care
   - non-clinical service scope;
   - realistic benefits;
   - no diagnosis or cure claims;
   - clear separation from regulated professional care.

6. Appointment and retention journey
   - enquiry;
   - triage or suitability check;
   - appointment requested;
   - appointment confirmed;
   - attended;
   - treatment or service accepted;
   - follow-up;
   - repeat care or maintenance.

A click, form submission, booked appointment, attended consultation, diagnosis and completed treatment are not equivalent outcomes.

## Source register

### Source HW1 — National Health Act 61 of 2003

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/acts/national-health-act-61-2003-23-jul-2004
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal and clinical risk flag
Freshness: verify amendments and applicable regulations

Verified findings:

- The Act establishes the national framework for South Africa's health system.
- Health establishments, users, health records and service delivery operate within a formal legal framework.
- Health information and health records require protection and controlled disclosure.

Marketing implications:

- A patient story, testimonial, image, diagnosis, treatment detail or result must not be published merely because staff know the patient.
- Booking and marketing systems should collect only information needed for the stated purpose.
- A public enquiry form is not an appropriate place for detailed clinical history unless the practice has a secure, lawful intake process.
- General health education must remain separate from personalised medical advice.

- Booklet 19: Ethical billing practices;
- Booklet 20: Ethical use of AI, published in September 2025.

Safe candidate knowledge:

- Professional marketing must preserve dignity, confidentiality, informed consent and truthful representation of qualifications and services.
- Social-media use does not remove professional duties.

Prohibited overclaims:

- “Guaranteed cure.”
- “Best doctor in South Africa” without defensible substantiation.
- “No consultation needed.”
- “AI diagnosis approved by the practitioner” where no practitioner reviewed it.
- Publishing a patient image or outcome without valid, specific authority.

### Source HW4 — AHPCSA registration and advertising restrictions

Publisher: Allied Health Professions Council of South Africa
Canonical sources:

- https://ahpcsa.co.za/faqs/
- https://ahpcsa.co.za/practitioners/
- https://ahpcsa.co.za/associations/

Source type: statutory allied-health regulator
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: verify current regulations and notices

Verified findings:

- The AHPCSA regulates registered allied-health disciplines including chiropractic, homeopathy, naturopathy, phytotherapy, therapeutic massage therapy, aromatherapy, reflexology and others.
- Public registers are updated monthly.
- The AHPCSA FAQ states that regulated practitioners may not advertise in the ordinary promotional sense and may not make unlawful health or curative claims.
- Council registration is a legal requirement, not a voluntary professional “membership”.

Marketing implications:

- Allied-health content requires exact profession, registration and current rules.
- “Member of Council” is not an appropriate claim merely because a person is registered.
- Non-registered qualifications must not be presented as though they are statutory professional credentials.
- Curative claims require extreme caution and may be prohibited.

### Source HW5 — SAHPRA advertising of medicines and health products

Publisher: South African Health Products Regulatory Authority
Canonical page: https://www.sahpra.org.za/document/guidelines-for-advertisement-of-medicines-and-health-products/
Document: SAHPGL-INSP-RC-07, Version 2, updated 23 June 2022
Source type: official regulatory guideline
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: verify replacement version before activation

Verified scope:

- The guideline addresses advertising requirements for medicines and health products.
- Promotional information should align with the Medicines Act and applicable regulations.
- Advertising that is misleading, inappropriate or unsupported can contribute to irrational or incorrect use.

Marketing implications:

- Product claims must match the product's lawful status and approved evidence.
- Prescription, scheduled and over-the-counter products cannot be treated as one marketing category.
- Influencer posts, testimonials, captions and landing pages are still advertising when they promote a health product.
- “Clinically proven”, “doctor recommended”, “safe”, “natural”, “side-effect free” and “works for everyone” require exact evidence and lawful context.

Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: device-classification guideline updated 28 February 2025; clinical-evaluation and investigation guidance updated 9 September 2025; reliance guideline updated 25 February 2026

Verified implications:

- Medical devices and IVDs are classified by risk and intended use.
- Safety, quality and performance claims require product-specific evidence.
- A device being sold or used does not prove every promoted indication is authorised or clinically supported.

Prohibited overclaims:

- “SAHPRA approved” without exact product and regulatory evidence.
- “Zero risk.”
- “Permanent result” without support.
- “Clinically proven” based only on a supplier brochure.
- “Medical grade” as an undefined prestige label.

### Source HW10 — POPIA and special personal information

Publisher: Information Regulator South Africa
Canonical sources:

- https://inforegulator.org.za/popia/
- https://eservices.inforegulator.org.za/priorauthorisation/default.aspx

Source type: privacy regulator guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: current portal and guidance should be checked

Verified findings:

- Medical information is personal information and can constitute special personal information.
- Organisations processing personal information are responsible parties and must comply with lawful processing conditions.
- Certain high-risk processing, linking of identifiers, foreign transfer or special-information scenarios can require prior authorisation.

Marketing and lead implications:

- Health-condition audiences, diagnosis lists, patient testimonials and treatment histories are high-risk marketing data.
- A patient enquiry does not create permission to use the person's condition for retargeting, a lookalike audience or a case study.
- Lead exports, spreadsheets and WhatsApp groups must not expose health information unnecessarily.
- Client reporting should use aggregated counts and never surface identifiable patient data.

## Candidate review-gated Skill Cards

### HWSC-01 — Health outcomes require clinical evidence

A booking, consultation, product purchase or testimonial does not prove diagnosis, efficacy or long-term outcome.

### HWSC-02 — Professional titles require current registration

Use exact regulator, category and current registration status. Do not collapse practitioner, intern, candidate, therapist and support roles.

### HWSC-03 — Social media does not remove confidentiality duties

Patient content requires valid, specific authority and professional review. De-identification must be genuine, not cosmetic.

### HWSC-04 — Health-product advertising requires exact regulatory context

Keep product, indication, scheduling, evidence, audience, territory and review date attached to every claim.

### HWSC-05 — Device availability is not efficacy proof

A device being marketed or used does not prove every promoted indication, permanent result or universal safety claim.

### HWSC-06 — Wellness must not impersonate clinical care

Non-clinical businesses may describe their actual service and customer experience but must not diagnose, prescribe or claim cures.

### HWSC-07 — Medical leads contain sensitive information

Minimise collection, isolate access and avoid ad-platform audience use unless a lawful, reviewed basis exists.

### HWSC-08 — Appointment stages must remain separate

Enquiry, requested, confirmed, attended, accepted and completed are different states.

### HWSC-09 — Medical-scheme context is not coverage confirmation

Industry membership or a patient's scheme status does not prove a service is covered or payable.

### HWSC-10 — Proposed rules are not current rules

Store enacted, amended, draft and proposed professional or pharmacy rules as separate legal states.

## Review and activation checklist

Before activating any healthcare or wellness knowledge:

- verify regulator and current source;
- confirm profession and scope;
- confirm whether the content is health education, advertising or personalised advice;
- check patient confidentiality and consent;
- verify product/device/scheduling status;
- verify claim evidence;
- check account, platform and paid/organic context;
- remove sensitive lead or patient data;
- retain researched and last-verified dates;
- route legal or clinical uncertainty to qualified review.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- No diagnosis or treatment advice.
- No cure, guaranteed result or zero-risk claim.
- No patient story, image or testimonial without lawful authority.
- No practitioner title without current registration evidence.
- No health-product or device claim without exact source and regulatory status.
- No sensitive patient data in marketing audiences or client reports.
- No proposed rule presented as current law.
- No client classified from its name.
- No unreviewed knowledge in normal production answers.
