## Purpose

This pack exists to help CG Production House market agriculture clients better.

It focuses only on material that improves:

- strategy;
- positioning;
- social content;
- commercial scripts;
- photography and video briefs;
- paid advertising;
- SEO and search intent;
- websites and landing pages;
- field days, trade shows, demonstrations and local activations;
- promotions, giveaways and partnerships;

- client reporting and creative optimisation.

It does not exist to teach farming, bookkeeping, tax, procurement or generic agricultural operations.

## Human truth at the centre

Farmers and agricultural buyers do not respond well to empty inspiration, glossy corporate language or generic “innovation” claims.

Strong agriculture marketing usually earns attention by doing one or more of the following:

- proving practical value in the field;
- showing a real farmer, dealer, agronomist or operator speaking plainly;
- respecting the pressure, risk and pride involved in agricultural work;
- translating technical value into yield, time, reliability, cost, control or confidence;
- demonstrating rather than claiming;
- using local language, climate, crops, livestock, soil and work rhythms;
- treating the farmer as experienced and intelligent;
- making the brand useful before making it impressive.

## Campaign evidence and human lessons

### 1. John Deere — The Farm Must Go On

Source: WARC campaign case summary.
Canonical source: https://www.warc.com/content/article/john-deere-the-farm-must-go-on/en-gb/136440

Reported results:

- raised $306,000 for Farm Rescue;
- increased John Deere brand trust by 10%;
- increased searches for Farm Rescue by 75%.

Human insight:

Agricultural work continues through illness, weather, market pressure and crisis. The campaign did not lead with machinery features. It made the audience feel the consequences when a farmer cannot keep going, then gave people a practical way to help.

Creative structure:

1. Start with a recognisable human pressure.
2. Show the wider consequence beyond one farm.
3. Use real people and grounded scenes.
4. Give the brand a credible role in solving the problem.
5. End with a specific action, not a vague emotional line.

Transferable use for CG Production House:

- farmer-support campaigns;
- harvest-season community initiatives;
- dealership service campaigns built around keeping operations moving;
- emergency-parts or maintenance storytelling;
- sponsorships that produce real value for farmers rather than branded noise.

Do not copy:

- American crisis language without local evidence;
- sentimental music and slow-motion farming footage without a real idea;
- charity positioning where the brand is not making a meaningful contribution.

### 2. Molatek — Farmer First Education

Source: Ads of the World campaign listing.
Canonical source: https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/farmer-first-education

Campaign approach:

- digital-first explainer series;
- technical livestock knowledge translated into accessible content;
- commercial and emerging farmers addressed through useful education;
- brand positioned as a knowledgeable partner rather than only a feed supplier.

Human insight:

Trust is earned by being useful. A farmer is more likely to remember a brand that helped solve a feeding, condition, growth or seasonal problem than a brand that repeatedly says it cares.

Creative structure:

1. Open with a real farmer question.
2. Show the consequence of getting it wrong.
3. Let a credible person explain the principle simply.
4. Use visual proof, animals, feed, condition or field context.
5. End with one practical next step.

Transferable use:

- monthly expert series;
- “what to check this week” content;
- myth-versus-reality videos;
- short farmer questions answered by an agronomist, technician, veterinarian or dealer specialist;
- downloadable seasonal checklists tied to lead capture;
- WhatsApp-first educational clips for rural audiences.

### 3. Absa AgriBusiness — Cultivating Credibility through Conversations

Source: Ads of the World campaign listing.
Canonical source: https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/cultivating-credibility-through-conversations

Campaign approach:

- converted a static agricultural report into podcast and vodcast conversations;
- used experts and spoken explanation rather than expecting farmers to read a dense report;
- designed for audiences who are often travelling or working while listening.

Human insight:

Format matters. Valuable information fails when it is delivered in a way that does not fit the audience’s day.

Transferable use:

- short expert audio episodes for WhatsApp;
- in-cab podcast clips;
- “market in five minutes” updates;
- filmed dealer or agronomist conversations;
- voice-note style content in Afrikaans and English;
- content repurposing from one long conversation into reels, quote cards, articles and email summaries.

### 4. Seed Consultants — Better to Best

Source: Meyocks campaign case study.
Canonical source: https://www.meyocks.com/work/seed-consultants-integrated-campaign/

Reported results:

- 95% site engagement rate;
- more than 2% ad click-through rate.

Campaign approach:

- targeted row-crop farmers in a defined region;
- positioned regional agronomic knowledge and strong genetics together;
- integrated print, digital, video and paid search;
- avoided gimmicks and centred yield and local service.

Human insight:

Farmers may value relationships, but the commercial promise still needs to connect to performance. “We know you” is weak unless it leads to a better recommendation, better support or a better outcome.

Transferable use:

- local dealer campaigns combining product proof and specialist support;
- region-specific crop and soil messaging;
- search campaigns tied to crop, product, problem and location;
- farmer testimonial videos that explain why a recommendation worked in that exact region;
- landing pages built around local conditions rather than generic national copy.

### 5. Sasol at NAMPO — On-the-ground activation connected to lead generation

Source: MO Agency case study.
Canonical source: https://www.mo.agency/case-studies/fueling-sasols-lead-generation-campaign

Campaign approach:

- used NAMPO as the physical launch point;
- connected fuel and lubricant relevance directly to tractors, trucks and farm equipment;
- combined on-site activity with a paid lead-generation campaign.

Human insight:

Agricultural trade shows should not be treated as isolated event coverage. The stand, demonstration, staff interaction, QR journey, lead form, retargeting and follow-up should operate as one campaign.

Transferable use:

- pre-event teaser ads;
- appointment booking before the show;
- QR-linked product comparisons;
- filmed demonstrations;
- lead capture by product need;
- retargeting based on stand interaction;
- post-event sales content using real questions asked at the stand;
- “we met you at NAMPO” follow-up sequences.

### 6. John Deere India — Regional and vernacular localisation

Source: Goldmine case study.
Canonical source: https://goldmineltd.com/our-work/john-deere-creating-content-that-connects-across-regions-and-realities/

Reported results from the agency case study:

- 40–50% increase in market share;
- 20% increase in lead conversion.

Use with caution:

These are agency-reported figures and require independent verification before being treated as universal proof.

Human insight:

Agriculture is local. Crop type, climate, machinery use, language, visual culture and decision-making differ by region. One national message often becomes generic and emotionally distant.

Transferable use in South Africa:

- Afrikaans, English, Sesotho or other relevant language variants;
- Free State-specific farming context;
- crop and livestock-specific campaigns;
- regional weather, soil and seasonal references;
- local dealer staff and customer faces;
- avoid stock footage that does not resemble the actual farming environment.

### 7. John Deere customer stories and precision-ag proof

Sources:

- https://www.deere.com.au/en/campaigns/youve-got-what-it-takes/learn-more/customer-stories/
- https://www.deere.com/en/news/all-news/see-spray-technology-across-5-million-acres/

Observed pattern:

- real operators explain what changed;
- the story connects technology to planning, efficiency, control and measurable outcomes;
- product proof is shown through use, not adjectives.

Human insight:

Complex agricultural technology becomes credible when the farmer explains the before, the change and the result in ordinary language.

Recommended testimonial structure:

1. What was difficult before?
2. What did the farmer try?
3. What changed in the work?
4. What evidence is available?
5. What would the farmer tell another farmer?

## Commercial script patterns that work

Do not copy existing commercial scripts verbatim. Use the structure and human insight.

### Script pattern A — The day starts before the ad does

Best for:

- machinery;
- irrigation;
- tyres;
- parts;
- fuel;
- service support.

Structure:

1. Open in the middle of real work.
2. Show the pressure without explaining everything.
3. Let the operator identify the problem in one plain sentence.
4. Demonstrate the product or service doing its job.
5. Show the practical result.
6. End on a direct, credible line.

Avoid:

- dramatic sunrise footage with no story;
- “built for those who never stop” clichés;
- fake mud, fake farmers or polished clothing that destroys credibility.

### Script pattern B — One farmer explains it to another

Best for:

- feed;
- seed;
- irrigation;
- precision agriculture;
- agricultural finance;
- technical services.

Structure:

1. Real question from one farmer.
2. Short explanation from a trusted peer or specialist.
3. Visual demonstration.
4. One limitation or condition stated honestly.
5. Clear next step.

Why it works:

It feels like useful advice, not a brand lecture.

### Script pattern C — The cost of waiting

Best for:

- maintenance;
- spare parts;
- disease or pest response marketing;
- irrigation repair;
- tyre replacement;
- insurance or finance lead generation.

Structure:

1. Show the small warning sign.
2. Show what delay can cost in time, yield or disruption.
3. Introduce the practical response.
4. End with urgency tied to reality, not fake scarcity.

### Script pattern D — The proof is in this field

Best for:

- seed trials;
- equipment demonstrations;
- fertiliser or feed trials;
- irrigation systems;
- precision tools.

Structure:

1. Name the location and conditions.
2. Explain what was tested.
3. Show the process.
4. Present verified evidence.
5. State what the result does and does not prove.
6. Invite a field visit, demo or consultation.

### Script pattern E — The family, team or community behind the operation

Best for:

- heritage brands;
- dealerships;
- family farms;
- cooperatives;
- local suppliers.

Structure:

1. Introduce people through work, not posed interviews.
2. Show roles and relationships.
3. Connect the product or service to continuity, trust or succession.
4. Avoid turning farmers into sentimental symbols.

## Giveaways and promotions that fit agriculture

Good agriculture giveaways should be useful, relevant and connected to the client’s offer.

Ideas:

- farm safety kit;
- service voucher;
- machinery inspection;
- soil or water assessment where the client is qualified;
- branded workwear of genuine quality;
- fuel or parts voucher;
- field-day access;
- product trial;
- livestock or crop management workshop seat;
- toolbox or practical equipment;
- customer story competition judged on a real farming challenge;
- “nominate a farmer who helped the community” campaign with a meaningful prize.

Avoid:

- generic hamper competitions unrelated to the client;
- engagement bait with no lead value;
- prizes that attract people outside the target market;
- forced tagging mechanics that create low-quality audiences;
- fake scarcity or unclear terms.

## Anti-cliché language standard

Reject phrases such as:

- passion meets purpose;
- growing together, unless tied to a specific programme and proof;
- innovation for tomorrow;
- feeding the future;
- rooted in excellence;
- empowering farmers;
- solutions that matter;
- trusted partner in growth;
- where tradition meets innovation;
- more than just a supplier.

These lines may only be used when replaced by specific evidence and human meaning.

Preferred language:

- plain;
- specific;
- locally recognisable;
- useful;
- respectful;
- evidence-led;
- spoken like a real person.

Example transformation:

Weak:

> We are your trusted partner in agricultural excellence.

Better:

> When the pivot stops at 14:00, you need the part before tomorrow — not a promise next week.

Weak:

> Innovation that empowers farmers.

Better:

> Spray only where the weeds are, and keep the rest of the field out of it.
