# Marketing Library — Research Scope

Last updated: 2026-08-06
Status: Canonical research inclusion standard.
Owner: CG Production House.

## Purpose

The Marketing Library exists to help CG Production House market clients better.

Every source, research pack, knowledge item and skill card must directly improve at least one of these outcomes:

- stronger marketing strategy;
- better content ideas and creative execution;
- more effective online advertising;
- stronger social media performance;
- better websites and landing pages;
- improved SEO and local discovery;
- better campaigns, promotions and giveaways;
- stronger lead generation and conversion;
- clearer audience understanding and positioning;
- better client reporting and optimisation;
- stronger on-the-ground activations that support the client’s brand and online presence.

CG Production House specialises in social media management, campaign strategy, paid media, websites, branding, graphic design, photography, videography, 3D/animation and creative production. Research must support those services.

## Non-negotiable human standard

Only research that helps us create marketing people can recognise, relate to, trust, remember or act on belongs in this Library.

The Library must learn from real human persuasion, not generic AI marketing language.

Prioritise:

- proven campaigns and ads from the industry;
- commercials that generated attention, demand, enquiries, bookings or sales;
- real scripts, story structures, hooks, offers and calls to action;
- books written by credible practitioners, strategists, copywriters, salespeople, filmmakers, psychologists, researchers or industry experts;
- interviews with customers, founders, sales staff, service staff, creators and campaign teams;
- customer reviews, comments, search queries, objections, frustrations and buying language;
- cultural, local and community insight;
- human behaviour and decision-making evidence;
- real examples of promotions, giveaways, sponsorships, launches, activations and events;
- campaign post-mortems, case studies and documented performance where the evidence is credible;
- scripts and concepts that sound like an actual person speaking to another person.

Reject:

- “passion meets purpose” language;
- empty inspiration;
- generic brand-manifesto copy;
- corporate filler;
- AI-generated slogan lists without evidence;
- abstract frameworks that do not produce a usable campaign, script, offer, idea or decision;
- content that could apply unchanged to every industry;
- language no customer, salesperson or staff member would naturally use;
- made-up case studies or unsupported claims of campaign success.

The test is simple: would this help CG Production House create work that feels specific, human and commercially useful for this client or industry? If not, exclude it.

## Inclusion test

A resource belongs in the Library only when the answer to at least one of these questions is yes:

1. Does it help us understand how customers in this industry discover, compare, trust and choose a business?
2. Does it show what successful advertising, offers, scripts, campaigns or creative work looks like in this industry?
3. Does it help us create better social posts, videos, photography, graphics, campaigns or storytelling for this industry?
4. Does it help us plan or optimise Meta, Google, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn or another relevant advertising channel?
5. Does it help us identify useful SEO keywords, search intent, local-search behaviour, website content or landing-page opportunities?
6. Does it help us design promotions, competitions, giveaways, partnerships, events, activations or community marketing that suit the industry?
7. Does it reveal real customer language, objections, motivations, fears, desires or trust signals?
8. Does it help us improve reviews, reputation, social proof, enquiries, bookings, sales or lead quality?
9. Does it help us measure marketing performance truthfully and improve future work?
10. Does it identify a marketing claim, platform rule or industry restriction that directly affects what we may advertise or publish?

If none apply, exclude it.

## Required research areas per industry

Each industry pack must focus on practical marketing intelligence.

### Successful advertising and campaign evidence

Research:

- notable ads and campaigns in that industry;
- campaign objective;
- target audience;
- central human insight;
- offer or promise;
- hook;
- story or creative structure;
- media/channel used;
- call to action;
- available performance evidence;
- what appears transferable;
- what is brand-, market- or budget-specific;
- why people likely connected with it;
- what would make the same idea feel fake or generic.

Do not label an ad “successful” merely because it is famous, won an award or has many views. Record the evidence available and its limitations.

### Commercials, scripts and storytelling

Research real, lawfully available examples of:

- television commercials;
- radio commercials;
- short-form video ads;
- testimonial ads;
- founder or staff-led ads;
- demonstration scripts;
- comparison ads;
- offer-led sales ads;
- local and community-driven scripts;
- humour, tension, relief, pride, belonging, aspiration, trust and problem-solution structures.

For each useful example, extract the structure rather than copying protected text:

- opening situation;
- first line or hook type;
- person speaking;
- customer tension or desire;
- proof or demonstration;
- emotional turn;
- offer;
- CTA;
- pacing;
- visual or audio devices;
- why the language feels natural.

Do not ingest or reproduce copyrighted scripts beyond lawful quotation limits. Store metadata, analysis and structure unless rights permit more.

### Books and practitioner knowledge

Prioritise books and long-form sources that improve:

- advertising;
- copywriting;
- sales psychology;
- customer research;
- creative strategy;
- direct response;
- storytelling;
- brand memory;
- behavioural science;
- local marketing;
- retail, hospitality, automotive, property or other industry-specific selling;
- film, photography and visual communication;
- offers, promotions and conversion.

For modern copyrighted books:

- store bibliographic metadata;
- store human-created notes and original analysis;
- do not ingest full copyrighted text without rights;
- record the practical principle, example and limitation;
- link each useful principle to a real marketing application.

Historical or public-domain books must still be checked against modern evidence before their claims are treated as current.

### Audience and buyer behaviour

- primary customer groups;
- customer needs, frustrations and motivations;
- buyer journey and decision stages;
- trust signals;
- objections;
- emotional and practical stakes;
- seasonal behaviour;
- local and regional differences where useful;
- B2B decision-makers and buying groups where relevant;
- exact words customers use in reviews, comments, forums, calls, enquiries and search.

### Positioning and messaging

- strongest value propositions;
- useful terminology and real customer language;
- common weak or generic claims to avoid;
- proof points required for credibility;
- emotional and functional messaging angles;
- differentiation opportunities;
- brand-tone opportunities suited to the industry;
- lines that sound human when spoken aloud;
- claims that require demonstration rather than adjectives.

### Content and creative

- social content pillars;
- short-form video concepts;
- long-form video opportunities;
- photography shot lists;
- graphic and carousel ideas;
- educational, entertaining, promotional and trust-building formats;
- behind-the-scenes opportunities;
- staff, customer, product, process and location stories;
- user-generated content and testimonial opportunities;
- platform-native creative patterns;
- content risks and clichés to avoid;
- ideas built from real industry rituals, moments, tensions and customer experiences.

### Advertising

- suitable campaign objectives;
- useful audience strategies;
- search-intent groups;
- offer and lead-magnet ideas;
- campaign structures;
- retargeting opportunities;
- creative testing ideas;
- landing-page requirements;
- likely lead-quality problems;
- reporting metrics that matter;
- platform and industry advertising restrictions;
- examples of winning hooks, offers, demonstrations and proof mechanisms where credible evidence exists.

### SEO, websites and local discovery

- industry keyword themes;
- customer search questions;
- service, product, location and problem-based search intent;
- comparison and “best/near me/cost/how/why” intent where relevant;
- website page opportunities;
- FAQ and educational-content opportunities;
- Google Business Profile opportunities;
- review and reputation strategies;
- local citations and directory opportunities where legitimate;
- technical or content issues that commonly weaken conversion;
- customer language that should shape headings, landing pages and calls to action.

### Promotions, giveaways and on-the-ground marketing

- practical giveaway ideas;
- seasonal promotions;
- referral concepts;
- loyalty and retention ideas;
- local partnerships;
- sponsorships;
- pop-ups, demonstrations, open days, tastings, launches, workshops or events;
- in-store or on-site content opportunities;
- QR, signage and print-to-digital connections;
- community and experiential ideas that can feed social content and paid campaigns;
- real examples of what people in that industry actually participate in, talk about and share.

Every idea must state:

- who it is for;
- why they would care;
- what action it should create;
- how it connects to content or ads;
- what proof, prize, partner or experience makes it credible;
- what would make it feel cheap, manipulative or irrelevant.

### Measurement and optimisation

- meaningful marketing outcomes;
- lead stages;
- booking, enquiry, quote, purchase or footfall signals;
- channel-specific metrics;
- attribution limits;
- campaign learning questions;
- benchmarks only where credible and properly qualified;
- what should trigger a strategy or creative change;
- how to distinguish attention from persuasion and activity from commercial outcome.

### Marketing-specific compliance

Include only rules that directly affect marketing, advertising, content, claims, consent, platform eligibility, customer data used for campaigns, promotions or published material.

Examples that belong:

- whether an alcohol brand may target a certain audience;
- whether a medical practice may publish a patient testimonial;
- whether a promotion needs material terms;
- whether a platform prohibits a product category;
- whether a claim needs substantiation;
- whether customer data may be uploaded for remarketing.

Examples that do not belong:

- bookkeeping workflows;
- payroll;
- tax filing;
- general VAT administration;
- procurement administration;
- staff rostering;
- internal stock-control procedures with no marketing effect;
- broad legal summaries unrelated to advertising or content;
- operational process documentation that does not influence customer experience, marketing truth or campaign delivery.

## Source priority

Prefer sources that produce practical human marketing insight:

1. Documented campaigns, case studies and ad libraries with credible evidence.
2. Real commercials, scripts, interviews and creative breakdowns from identifiable human creators or teams.
3. Books and long-form work by credible practitioners and researchers.
4. Customer reviews, comments, search behaviour, interviews and first-party voice-of-customer evidence.
5. Credible market, audience and consumer research.
6. Search-trend, keyword and demand evidence.
7. Peer-reviewed advertising, communication and consumer-behaviour research.
8. Official platform documentation and policy where it affects campaign execution.
9. Industry bodies and regulators where they affect marketing claims or audience communication.
10. Reputable industry publications with clear methodology.
11. Verified client evidence, kept client-specific.
12. CG Production House campaign observations, retained as client-specific learning unless independently supported.

AI-generated articles, listicles, anonymous summaries and generic SEO blogs are not primary sources.

## Required source record for campaigns and ads

For every campaign, ad or commercial used as research, record:

- brand;
- industry;
- country/market;
- year;
- campaign or ad name;
- agency/creator where known;
- objective;
- audience;
- channel;
- human insight;
- offer or promise;
- creative structure;
- source URL;
- evidence of performance;
- evidence limitations;
- transferable lesson;
- non-transferable context;
- rights state;
- review state.

## Output standard

Research must be turned into usable marketing outputs, not stored as an academic summary.

Each industry pack should produce:

- audience profiles;
- buyer journey;
- real customer language;
- messaging angles;
- examples of proven campaign structures;
- commercial and short-form script frameworks;
- content pillars;
- video ideas;
- photography ideas;
- graphic/poster/carousel ideas;
- campaign and ad ideas;
- offer and promotion ideas;
- SEO keyword and search-intent groups;
- website and landing-page opportunities;
- local marketing ideas;
- giveaway and activation ideas;
- partnership ideas;
- seasonal opportunities;
- trust and review strategies;
- reporting and optimisation guidance;
- marketing-specific risks;
- review-state skill cards.

Every suggested output must be specific enough to brief a writer, designer, photographer, videographer, media buyer or client manager.

## Writing standard

All derived marketing guidance must:

- sound natural when spoken aloud;
- use concrete language;
- name the customer, situation, tension, product, service or proof;
- avoid vague adjectives as substitutes for evidence;
- avoid generic lines that could fit any business;
- avoid corporate theatre and AI-sounding inspiration;
- preserve local language, humour and cultural context where appropriate;
- be smart without trying to sound clever;
- be emotionally aware without becoming manipulative;
- keep the human being at the centre of the idea.

## Exclusion rule

Do not add a source merely because it is authoritative or related to the industry.

Authority is not relevance. Fame is not proof. Views are not sales. Awards are not automatically effectiveness.

A source that does not help CG Production House understand people, develop a real idea, write a stronger script, build a better offer, produce better creative, advertise, optimise, report or protect client marketing must not enter the active Marketing Library.

## Client phase

After reusable industry research is complete, work through the live active-client directory one client at a time.

For each client:

- verify the exact business, services, locations and audience;
- identify the correct industry packs;
- research the client’s website, Google presence, social channels, competitors and public proof;
- collect real customer language from reviews, comments, search and enquiries;
- identify current SEO and content gaps;
- identify relevant campaign, commercial, book and script references;
- propose client-specific campaigns, ads, giveaways, partnerships, shoots and on-the-ground activations;
- preserve exact-client isolation;
- link all client knowledge by client id;
- do not carry former clients into the active knowledge base.
