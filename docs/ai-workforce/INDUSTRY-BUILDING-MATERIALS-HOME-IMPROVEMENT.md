## Purpose

Create a trustworthy industry layer for:

- timber and boards;
- steel and structural products;
- cement, concrete and masonry;
- stone, marble, granite and engineered surfaces;
- paint, coatings and waterproofing;
- hardware and tools;
- roofing, insulation and cladding;
- plumbing and related building products;
- trade supply and retail home improvement.

This pack supports marketing strategy, content, paid media, website journeys, lead generation and client reporting. It does not replace engineering, architectural, regulatory or product-specific advice.

## Industry structure

The AI must distinguish these buying contexts:

1. Consumer renovation
   - smaller project;
   - price and inspiration-led;
   - needs clear quantities, finish and application guidance.

2. Builder and contractor procurement
   - repeat trade customer;
   - specification, stock, delivery, credit and project timing matter.

3. Architect/designer specification
   - appearance, performance, compatibility, documentation and sample approval matter.

4. Engineer/fabricator procurement
   - exact grade, dimensions, tolerances, certification and design responsibility matter.

5. Developer/project procurement
   - scale, programme, cost, compliance, quality assurance and supply continuity matter.

6. Maintenance and repair
   - diagnosis, compatibility, urgency and correct preparation matter.

A hardware-store visit, structural-steel enquiry and stone-countertop quotation are not equivalent leads.

## Source register

### Source B1 — National Building Regulations and Building Standards Act 103 of 1977

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/national-building-regulations-and-building-standards-act-16-apr-2015-1302
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal and technical risk flag only
Freshness: verify amendments, regulations and commencement

Verified findings:

- The Act promotes uniformity in law relating to the erection of buildings within local-authority areas.
- It provides for prescribed building standards.
- Building approval, occupancy and control operate within a formal regulatory framework.

Marketing implications:

- “Compliant”, “approved”, “building-standard”, “structural” and “safe” require exact support.
- A product being sold does not prove it is suitable for every building, location or application.
- Building-plan approval, product compliance and correct installation are separate matters.
- Content should state when professional design or municipal approval may be required.

Prohibited overclaims:

- “No plans needed.”
- “Approved for every building.”
- “Engineer-free structural solution.”
- “Guaranteed to pass inspection.”

### Source B3 — NHBRC mandate and registration

Publisher: National Home Builders Registration Council
Canonical pages:

- https://www.nhbrc.org.za/about/
- https://www.nhbrc.org.za/registration-process/
- https://www.nhbrc.org.za/home-builders-inspection-process/
- https://www.nhbrc.org.za/know-your-rights/

Source type: statutory home-building regulator
Rights treatment: official_reference / metadata_and_link_only
Freshness: verify fees, manuals and procedural updates

Verified findings:

- The NHBRC’s mandate includes protecting housing consumers and ensuring builders comply with prescribed standards in the Home Building Manual.
- A person in the business of home building must be registered with the NHBRC and renew membership annually.
- NHBRC inspection considers compliance with applicable deemed-to-satisfy requirements and approved drawings/specifications.
- A deviation may result in a non-compliance being issued.

Marketing implications:

- A supplier must not imply that product purchase itself enrols a home or registers a builder.
- “NHBRC compliant” needs exact evidence and context.
- Builder registration, home enrolment, product suitability, workmanship and municipal approval are distinct.
- Consumer content may explain these distinctions without making project-specific compliance conclusions.

### Source B4 — SANS 10400 and standards ecosystem

Publishers: South African Bureau of Standards and NHBRC references
Canonical sources:

- https://www.sabs.co.za/standards-technical-committee
- NHBRC Home Building Manual references

Source type: national standards framework
Rights treatment: standards metadata/reference only unless licensed; do not copy protected standards text

Verified findings:

- Active SABS technical committees cover metal/composite structures, timber structures, masonry, geotechnical design, coatings, water systems, drainage, plumbing components and pipe systems.
- Standards are application-specific and updated over time.

Marketing implications:

- Never state “SABS approved” merely because a product category has a standard.
- Distinguish a standard, tested conformity, certification mark, supplier declaration and compulsory specification.
- Store exact standard number, edition/date and evidence when making a standards claim.

Rights boundary:

- SANS and commercial handbooks may be copyrighted and licensed.
- Record metadata and lawful excerpts only; do not ingest protected full text without permission.

### Source B5 — Statistics South Africa Construction Materials Price Indices P0151.1

Publisher: Statistics South Africa
Canonical page: https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P0151.1&page_id=1854
Source type: official monthly price-index statistics
Rights treatment: official_reference; retain month, series and table identity
Freshness: monthly

Verified findings:

- Stats SA publishes monthly construction-material price indices and downloadable time series.
- The May 2026 release was published on 25 June 2026.

Safe uses:

- Describe price movement for exact material categories and periods.
- Support procurement-context reporting and time-sensitive market commentary.

Limitations:

- An index is not a supplier quotation.
- National or category movement does not prove one client increased prices or margins.
- Index changes do not automatically equal retail-price changes for every SKU.
- Month-on-month and year-on-year comparisons must remain separate.

## Candidate review-state Skill Cards

### BM-01 — Product suitability is application-specific

Principle:
A product can only be recommended after intended use, environment, dimensions and required performance are known.

### BM-02 — Standard, certification and approval are different

Principle:
Record the exact standard, current edition and product evidence instead of using vague “SABS approved” language.

### BM-03 — Draft regulation is not current law

Principle:
Clearly label Bills and consultation notices as proposals until enacted.

### BM-04 — Price index is not a quotation

Principle:
Use Stats SA material indices for market context, not client-specific retail pricing or margin conclusions.

### BM-05 — Compliance does not replace installation quality

Principle:
A compliant product can still fail if selected, stored, prepared or installed incorrectly.

### BM-06 — Structural claims need competent review

Principle:
Do not infer load capacity or structural suitability from a merchant product listing.

### BM-07 — Quote and order are separate outcomes

Principle:
Report submitted enquiries, quotes and confirmed orders separately.

### BM-08 — Natural material varies

Principle:
Samples and images illustrate appearance but may not guarantee identical natural stone or timber pattern/colour.

### BM-09 — Safety instructions must come from current product documents

Principle:
Use current technical and safety data rather than invented chemical-product instructions.

### BM-10 — Sustainability requires chain-specific evidence

Principle:
Do not apply a general forestry, recycled-content or environmental claim to a specific product without proof.

### Market reality

The buying journey is often split across several people: homeowner or property
owner; contractor or installer; architect, designer or specifier; quantity
surveyor or procurement person; developer or project manager; store buyer or
trade customer; do-it-yourself consumer.

Marketing must distinguish retail convenience from specification-led project
sales. A homeowner may be choosing by look, confidence and budget. A contractor
may care more about stock, lead time, compatibility, technical support and
margin. A professional specifier may require standards, documentation and proven
performance.

### Buyer pains

- Uncertainty about product suitability
- Wrong quantities and costly wastage
- Products being out of stock at the wrong time
- Delayed deliveries affecting contractors and project programmes
- Poor colour, finish or material selection
- Confusion between product grades and specifications
- Unclear installation requirements
- Hidden accessories or additional materials needed
- Fear of premature failure
- Lack of trustworthy technical advice
- Difficulty comparing whole-project cost rather than unit price
- Poor coordination between homeowner, contractor and supplier
- Inconsistent quality between batches or suppliers
- Returns and warranty disputes

### Buying triggers

- New build or renovation start
- Contractor appointment
- Approved plans or finalised specification
- Seasonal maintenance
- Water damage, roof failure, cracking or wear
- Property sale preparation
- Store promotion or trade day
- Product failure or replacement
- New colour scheme or design change
- Project-budget approval
- Stock availability becoming urgent
- Need to comply with a technical or municipal requirement

### Audience roles

- Homeowner planning a once-off project
- DIY buyer needing simple guidance
- Contractor needing repeat supply
- Architect or designer specifying a product
- Quantity surveyor comparing cost and availability
- Developer managing multiple units
- Trade reseller
- Facilities or maintenance manager
- Property investor improving resale or rental value

### Campaign and ad angles

**Confidence before purchase** — Explain how to choose the right product for the
use case. Show differences between grades, finishes, sizes and systems. Avoid
oversimplified claims such as "best" or "maintenance-free" without evidence.

**Project planning** — Help buyers calculate quantity, accessories, lead time and
delivery. Use checklists and planning tools that reduce waste and rework.

**Proof through use** — Show real installations, product application and
completed projects. Include surface preparation, installation method and
limitations where relevant.

**Trade reliability** — Emphasise stock visibility, delivery, account service,
technical help and repeat supply where true. Separate trade offers from retail
promotions.

**Design inspiration linked to action** — Use visual inspiration, but connect it
to product names, finish, quantity, preparation and availability. Do not leave
the buyer with attractive content but no practical next step.

### Landing-page structure

1. Name the project type or problem.
2. Identify the buyer and use case.
3. Explain suitable product options.
4. Show proof, specifications and limitations.
5. Add quantity, preparation, delivery and installation information.
6. Answer stock, return, warranty and lead-time questions.
7. Use one clear action: quote, sample, consultation, trade account or order enquiry.

### Objection handling

- "It is too expensive" → compare lifecycle, waste, failure risk and total installed cost.
- "The cheaper product looks the same" → explain grade, suitability, warranty and use-case difference.
- "I do not know how much I need" → provide quantity support.
- "My contractor will decide" → give contractor-ready documentation and shared consultation.
- "I need it immediately" → show honest stock and delivery options.
- "I am worried about the colour or finish" → offer samples and real-project references.
