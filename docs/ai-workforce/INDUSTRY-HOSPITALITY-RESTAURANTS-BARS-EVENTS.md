## Purpose

Create a trustworthy industry layer for:

- restaurants;
- coffee shops and takeaways;
- bars and pubs;
- event venues;
- caterers;
- casual dining and family venues;
- nightlife and entertainment venues;
- food-and-beverage-led hospitality businesses.

This pack supports marketing strategy, social content, paid media, bookings, local discovery, event promotion, website intelligence, lead reporting and reputation management. It does not replace legal, food-safety, liquor-licensing or health advice.

## Industry structure

The AI must separate these journeys:

1. Everyday dining
   - discovery;
   - menu consideration;
   - location and convenience;
   - booking or walk-in;
   - spend and repeat visit.

2. Occasion dining
   - birthdays, celebrations, date nights and group bookings;
   - capacity, deposits, dietary needs and event coordination.

3. Bar and nightlife
   - age eligibility;
   - entertainment schedule;
   - responsible alcohol communication;
   - transport and safety;
   - event-specific capacity and rules.

4. Events and functions
   - enquiry;
   - date and capacity check;
   - package selection;
   - quote and deposit;
   - final numbers;
   - execution and post-event follow-up.

5. Takeaway and delivery
   - menu accuracy;
   - preparation and delivery time;
   - packaging;
   - order correctness;
   - platform commission and attribution.

6. Catering
   - guest count;
   - menu and dietary requirements;
   - venue and logistics;
   - service scope;
   - quote, deposit and final confirmation.

A table booking, food order, event enquiry, liquor promotion and venue-hire lead are not equivalent outcomes.

## Source register

### Source H1 — Statistics South Africa P6420 Food and Beverages

Publisher: Statistics South Africa
Canonical description: https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P6420&SCH=5407&page_id=1861
Archive: https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P6420&SCH=73027&page_id=1866&page_no=1
Source type: official monthly economic statistics
Rights treatment: official_reference; retain month, category and comparison basis
Freshness: monthly

Verified findings:

- P6420 covers a sample of public and private enterprises producing meals and drinks for immediate consumption.
- The survey includes restaurants, takeaways, coffee shops, caterers and bars.
- The release is monthly and should be used as time-sensitive market context.

Safe candidate knowledge:

- Food-and-beverage industry movement should be reported with the exact month, category and comparison basis.
- National survey movement does not prove one venue's foot traffic, profitability or customer demand.
- Current-price income, physical customer counts and margin are different measures.

Prohibited overclaims:

- "Restaurant demand grew by X%, therefore this venue should grow by X%."
- "National bar income proves this campaign worked."
- "Industry sales equal customer visits."

### Source H2 — National Department of Health Food Control

Publisher: National Department of Health
Canonical pages:

- https://www.health.gov.za/food-control/
- https://www.health.gov.za/food-control-regulations/
- https://www.health.gov.za/food-control-home/

Source type: official food-safety and labelling regulator
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: monitor regulation updates

Verified findings:

- Food control in South Africa is based on food-safety, labelling and related regulations.
- The framework applies to formal and informal food handling.
- The Department's functions include food legislation, public information, imported-food control support and municipal-health enforcement support.
- Food-safety regulation includes hygiene, microbiology, labelling, additives, contaminants and nutrition-related rules.

Marketing implications:

- "Safe", "healthy", "allergen free", "gluten free", "sugar free", "organic", "fresh" and nutrition claims require evidence.
- Social content must not encourage unsafe food handling for entertainment.
- Behind-the-scenes kitchen content should not expose poor hygiene or unsafe practices.
- A menu image is not evidence of ingredient, allergen or nutrition status.

### Source H3 — R638 General Hygiene Requirements for Food Premises

Publisher: National Department of Health
Canonical register: https://www.health.gov.za/food-control-regulations/
Regulation: R638 of 22 June 2018
Source type: official food-hygiene regulation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal/health risk flag

Verified scope:

- R638 governs general hygiene requirements for food premises, transport of food and related matters.
- Food premises, handling, storage, transport and hygiene operate within a regulated system.
- Municipal health services are involved in enforcement and monitoring.

Marketing implications:

- Do not claim a premises is "health approved", "certified hygienic" or "fully compliant" without current evidence.
- Food-safety content should use verified procedures rather than improvised advice.
- A once-off inspection, certificate or clean kitchen photo does not guarantee every service period is compliant.

### Source H4 — Foodborne disease prevention and response

Publisher: National Department of Health
Canonical page: https://www.health.gov.za/outbreaks-foodborne-disease/
Source type: official public-health guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference

Verified findings:

- Foodborne-disease investigation can involve epidemiological, laboratory and environmental work.
- Control measures can include recall, closure and public-health advisories.
- Prevention includes hygiene, adequate cooking/storage, avoiding cross-contamination and staff training.

Marketing and crisis implications:

- Do not diagnose or publicly assign blame before facts are established.
- A suspected food-safety incident requires escalation, evidence preservation and professional/regulatory handling.
- Public responses should prioritise safety, honesty and verified updates, not defensive promotional messaging.

### Source H5 — Liquor Act 59 of 2003 and licensing context

Publishers: South African Government / SAPS official resource index
Official index: https://www.saps.gov.za/services/flash/main_liquor.php
Act listing: https://www.saps.gov.za/resource_centre/acts/acts.php
Source type: legislation and official licensing information
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal-risk flag
Freshness: national and provincial law must be checked

Verified findings:

- South Africa has national liquor legislation and provincial liquor frameworks.
- Manufacturing/distribution and retail/on-consumption licensing contexts differ.
- Provincial legislation and licence conditions matter to venue operations.

Marketing implications:

- A venue must not imply it may sell, deliver or serve liquor outside its actual licence scope.
- Trading-hour, age, delivery and event-liquor claims require local verification.
- "Licensed venue" should be tied to current premises and licence details.

### Source H6 — Liquor Amendment Bill B21-2025 status

Publisher: Parliament of South Africa
Canonical page: https://www.parliament.gov.za/bill/2325059
Source type: current parliamentary bill
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: critical

Verified finding:

- The Liquor Amendment Bill B21-2025 proposes major restrictions on liquor advertising, promotion, product placement and event support.
- It is a bill, not automatically current enacted law.

Knowledge rule:

- Do not present proposed restrictions as current law.
- Monitor parliamentary stage and enactment status.

### Source H7 — Advertising Regulatory Board Code

Publisher: Advertising Regulatory Board
Canonical page: https://www.arb.org.za/The_Codes/
Source type: South African advertising self-regulation
Rights treatment: official_reference / code metadata and lawful excerpts
Freshness: dynamic; current online version must be checked

Verified findings:

- The ARB administers the South African Code of Advertising Practice.
- The Code is updated over time and includes general rules plus sector appendices.
- Current appendices include Alcohol, Food & Beverage, Social Media and Gambling.
- Advertising should be legal, decent, honest and truthful, with substantiation for objective claims.

Marketing implications:

- Promotions, prices, competitions, food claims and alcohol communication need current code review.
- Influencer or creator content must not disguise advertising.
- "Best", "number one", "healthiest", "award-winning" and comparative claims require support.

### Source H8 — Responsible alcohol communication

Publishers: ARB sector appendix / Aware.org.za
Canonical sources:

- https://www.arb.org.za/The_Codes/
- https://www.aware.org.za/

Source type: advertising code and industry harm-reduction source
Rights treatment: official/supporting_reference

Verified principles for review:

- Alcohol communication should be directed at adults and should not encourage harmful or irresponsible consumption.
- Responsible-use messaging and age eligibility matter.
- Alcohol marketing should not present intoxication, social success, sexual success, courage or performance as guaranteed outcomes of drinking.
- Promotions should avoid pressure, coercion and youth appeal.

Limitations:

- Exact code provisions must be reviewed from the current appendix before activation.
- Industry self-regulation does not replace legislation or licence conditions.

### Source H9 — Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/consumer-protection-act
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal-risk flag

Hospitality implications:

- Menu prices, portion/product descriptions, promotion conditions, deposits and cancellation terms should be clear.
- "Free", "bottomless", "unlimited", "all you can eat", "half price" and "special" require material conditions.
- A photo should not materially misrepresent the product served.
- Hidden compulsory charges, ambiguous service fees and misleading availability claims create risk.
- Booking, event and delivery promises should match operational capacity.

### Market reality

Hospitality purchases are occasion-driven. Customers choose based on the
combination of food, drink, atmosphere, convenience, social proof, event
relevance, price, availability and expected experience.

The same venue may serve several distinct audiences: everyday local diners;
families; couples; business lunches; sports viewers; nightlife audiences;
private-event organisers; tourists; regulars; corporate groups. Marketing should
not treat all audiences as one generic "people looking for a good time" group.

### Buyer pains

- Not knowing what the venue feels like before arriving
- Unclear menu, pricing or availability
- Difficulty booking
- Slow responses to enquiries
- Fear that the venue will not suit the occasion
- Uncertainty about parking, safety, accessibility or family suitability
- Poor consistency between advertised and real experience
- Limited proof for private events
- Confusion about specials and conditions
- Concern about service quality during busy periods
- Lack of dietary or practical information
- Event information being scattered across social posts
- Customers forgetting to return after one visit

### Campaign and ad angles

**Experience before booking** — Show atmosphere, menu, service style and practical
information through photos, video and real operational detail. Avoid stock imagery
that does not match the actual venue.

**Occasion targeting** — Create content and offers that fit how people actually
choose the venue. Sunday lunch, date night, birthday booking and business meeting
need different creative approaches.

**Ease and confidence** — Make the booking process, special requests, dietary
needs and event enquiry steps visible and simple. Address common worries around
parking, accessibility, minimum spend and cancellation.

**Social proof and discovery** — Use genuine reviews, ratings and user-generated
content. Ensure the venue can be found and assessed without a phone call.

**Return and frequency** — Build loyalty programmes, reminders and reasons to
return beyond a one-off promotion. Treat a regular differently from a first-timer.

### Landing-page structure

1. Communicate the experience and occasion fit.
2. Show practical information: location, hours, parking, menu style, price range.
3. Provide social proof: ratings, reviews, real photos and coverage.
4. Explain the booking or enquiry process.
5. Answer common concerns: dietary, safety, kids, payment and cancellation.
6. Offer one clear action: book, enquire, view menu or make a reservation.
7. Present the reason to return: loyalty, events, new menu, seasonal offering.
