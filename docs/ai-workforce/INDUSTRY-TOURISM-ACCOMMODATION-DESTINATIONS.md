# AI Workforce — Tourism, Accommodation and Destination Industry Library

Last updated: 2026-07-26
Status: Actual researched industry pack; all production knowledge remains review-gated.
Territory: South Africa.

## Purpose

Create a trustworthy industry layer for:

- hotels, guest houses, lodges, B&Bs and self-catering properties;
- game lodges, nature reserves and destination properties;
- conference and events accommodation;
- tour operators and registered tourist guides;
- attractions, experiences and destination businesses;
- travel planners and tourism information services;
- local destination marketing.

This pack supports strategy, content, paid media, websites, bookings, reviews, seasonality, occupancy, lead reporting and reputation management. It does not replace legal, immigration, safety, transport, consumer or tourism-regulatory advice.

## Industry truth model

The AI must keep these journeys separate:

1. Accommodation discovery
   - destination search;
   - property and room-type comparison;
   - availability check;
   - rate and policy review;
   - booking request or direct booking;
   - payment or deposit;
   - arrival and check-in;
   - occupied stay;
   - checkout;
   - cancellation, no-show or refund;
   - review and repeat visit.

2. Tour or experience booking
   - enquiry;
   - date/capacity check;
   - guide and transport verification;
   - quote or itinerary;
   - deposit/payment;
   - confirmed participant count;
   - completed experience;
   - cancellation or incident handling.

3. Group, conference or wedding accommodation
   - enquiry;
   - room block and venue availability;
   - package and quotation;
   - contract/deposit;
   - rooming list;
   - final numbers;
   - delivery;
   - reconciliation.

4. Destination marketing
   - awareness;
   - itinerary planning;
   - referral to properties/activities;
   - booking handoff;
   - actual visitor arrival only where verified.

A website session, availability search, enquiry, OTA referral, provisional booking, paid booking, occupied room and retained revenue are different states.

## Source register

### Source T1 — Tourism Act 3 of 2014

Publisher: National Department of Tourism / South African Government
Canonical sources:

- https://www.tourism.gov.za/AboutNDT/Ministry/News/Pages/Tourism_Act_no_3_of_2014.aspx
- https://tkp.tourism.gov.za/Research/Policy/Pages/Tourism-Act.aspx

Source type: legislation and official explanatory source
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: review amendments and regulations

Verified findings:

- The Act provides for sustainable tourism development and promotion.
- It supports responsible tourism, quality tourism products and services, and national tourism marketing.
- It establishes the statutory basis for the Tourism Grading Council of South Africa.
- It provides for tourism complaints handling and regulates tourist guiding.

Safe candidate knowledge:

- Tourism quality, grading, guiding and complaints operate within a formal statutory framework.
- A private badge, award or association membership is not the same as statutory registration or TGCSA grading.

### Source T2 — Statistics South Africa P6410 Tourist Accommodation

Publisher: Statistics South Africa
Canonical sources:

- https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P6410&SCH=74311&page_id=1856
- https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P6410&SCH=6198&page_id=1866

Source type: official monthly industry statistics
Rights treatment: official_reference; preserve release month, category and comparison basis
Freshness: monthly

Verified April 2026 findings:

- Total tourist-accommodation income increased 5.6% year-on-year in nominal terms.
- Accommodation income increased 6.5% year-on-year.
- Stay-unit nights sold increased 9.0%.
- Average income per stay-unit night sold decreased 2.3%.
- Seasonally adjusted accommodation income decreased 1.1% month-on-month.

Interpretation rules:

- Income, room/stay-unit nights sold and average income per night are distinct measures.
- Nominal income growth is not real-volume growth or profit growth.
- National or category movement does not prove one property's occupancy, rate performance or campaign success.
- Year-on-year, month-on-month and three-month comparisons must never be blended.

### Source T3 — Statistics South Africa P0350 International Tourism

Publisher: Statistics South Africa
Canonical archive:

- https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P0350&SCH=73970&page_id=1866&page_no=1

Source type: official international-tourism statistics
Rights treatment: official_reference; retain month, traveller category, country/region and comparison basis
Freshness: monthly

Verified scope:

- The archive includes monthly international-tourism releases, with May 2026 listed as the latest release at the research date.
- Arrival, departure, purpose-of-visit and traveller categories must remain tied to the source methodology.

Limitations:

- Border movements or tourist arrivals do not equal bookings at one establishment.
- International arrivals do not prove domestic demand, occupancy, spend or profitability.
- Market-level visitor numbers cannot be attributed to one campaign without first-party evidence.

### Source T4 — Tourism Grading Council of South Africa

Publisher: TGCSA, a business unit of South African Tourism
Canonical sources:

- https://www.tourismgrading.co.za/about-the-tgcsa/what-is-the-tgcsa/
- https://www.tourismgrading.co.za/get-graded/what-is-star-grading/
- https://www.tourismgrading.co.za/about-the-tgcsa/news-media-and-events/statistics/

Source type: official tourism quality-assurance body
Rights treatment: official_reference; logo and marks require authorised use
Freshness: verify live grading and validity

Verified findings:

- TGCSA is the officially recognised South African quality-assurance body for graded tourism accommodation and venues.
- Star grading follows an independent quality assessment for the relevant property category.
- A property's category matters when interpreting its star level.
- TGCSA is the body authorised to award official tourism quality stars.

Marketing implications:

- Never infer grading from price, appearance, reviews or self-description.
- Do not display TGCSA stars or logo without current evidence and authorised use.
- A five-star guest house and a five-star hotel are assessed within category-specific expectations.
- Grading is not a guarantee that every individual stay will be defect-free.

### Source T5 — Grading validity and renewal

Publisher: TGCSA
Canonical supporting page:

- https://www.tourismgrading.co.za/about-the-tgcsa/news-media-and-events/latest-news/its-official-government-travellers-to-stay-in-star-graded-establishments/

Verified finding:

- TGCSA states that star grading is valid for one year and renewed through membership payment and follow-up assessment.

Knowledge rule:

- Store grading level, category, property, evidence URL/document, award date, expiry/renewal date and last verification date.
- Never use an old plaque, image or website statement as current proof.

### Source T6 — Tourist-guide regulation and registration

Publisher: National Department of Tourism
Canonical sources:

- https://tkp.tourism.gov.za/touristguide/Pages/Legislative-Matters.aspx
- https://itks.tourism.gov.za/content/page/26/registration-requirements
- https://itks.tourism.gov.za/view-registered-tourist-guides

Source type: official registration and legislative guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: verify current registration, province, category and expiry

Verified findings:

- Tourist guiding is regulated under the Tourism Act.
- Guides must complete recognised training and register with the relevant Provincial Registrar.
- Registered guides receive a registration number, badge and identification card.
- Guide categories and permitted geography/specialisation matter.
- The official register exposes validity dates and categories such as nature, culture and adventure.

Marketing implications:

- “Registered guide” requires current official verification.
- A guide's registration for one province, site or category does not automatically authorise every itinerary or activity.
- Association membership does not replace statutory guide registration.
- Safety, first-aid and activity-specific requirements must be verified separately.

### Source T7 — Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical source:

- https://www.gov.za/documents/consumer-protection-act

Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; legal-risk flag

Tourism implications:

- Prices, taxes, mandatory fees, deposits, cancellation rules, package inclusions and material restrictions should be clear.
- Marketing images and room descriptions should not materially misrepresent the booked product.
- “Free”, “all-inclusive”, “last room”, “best rate”, “no cancellation” and “guaranteed” require exact evidence and conditions.
- A property policy cannot simply be presented as overriding statutory consumer rights.
- Supplier-caused cancellation, consumer cancellation, force majeure and no-show states must remain distinct.

### Source T8 — Electronic Communications and Transactions Act

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical source:

- https://www.gov.za/documents/electronic-communications-and-transactions-act

Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference

Online-booking implications:

- Online suppliers must preserve accurate identity, contact, product/service, price and transaction information.
- A booking engine, OTA page and direct website should not conflict on material terms.
- Confirmation pages and emails should preserve the exact booked dates, room/product, guest count, amount, taxes/fees and cancellation terms.

### Source T9 — POPIA and traveller information

Publisher: Information Regulator / South African Government
Canonical source family:

- https://inforegulator.org.za/popia/
- https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act

Source type: privacy legislation and regulator guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference; privacy-risk flag

Tourism-data implications:

- Guest names, contact details, identity/passport data, travel dates, location, dietary/accessibility needs, payment data and companion information can be personal or sensitive information.
- Passport or identity collection for operational/legal purposes does not create marketing consent.
- Guest Wi-Fi, CCTV, access logs, vehicle registrations and booking histories require defined purpose, access and retention controls.
- Children and health/accessibility information need stronger safeguards.
- OTA-provided customer data must be used within the lawful and contractual purpose.

### Source T10 — Tourism complaints framework

Publisher: National Department of Tourism
Canonical sources:

- https://itks.tourism.gov.za/content/page/30/legislation-%26-requirements
- https://www.tourism.gov.za/ResourceCentre/Pages/default.aspx

Verified findings:

- The Tourism Act provides for a Tourism Complaints Officer.
- Complaints may be recorded, acknowledged and referred to the appropriate body.
- Different disputes may belong with tourism, consumer, equality, safety or other authorities.

Marketing and service-recovery rule:

- Do not publicly dismiss a complaint as false before checking booking, service and incident evidence.
- Do not reveal guest details when responding to public reviews.
- Separate public response, private resolution, refund decision and regulatory escalation.

## OTA and direct-booking truth

The AI must not assume:

- an OTA reservation equals retained revenue;
- a direct booking is always more profitable;
- an OTA referral created the first customer touchpoint;
- commission is the only distribution cost;
- a booking-engine confirmation means the guest stayed;
- a cancelled room automatically returned to every channel's inventory;
- property-management, channel-manager, OTA and analytics totals will match without reconciliation.

Required reconciliation dimensions:

- booking ID and channel ID;
- property and room type;
- status history;
- gross amount;
- taxes/fees;
- commission;
- payment/refund;
- arrival/departure;
- occupied stay;
- currency;
- source/campaign;
- last update timestamp.

All integrations must paginate fully, process updates/cancellations idempotently and preserve source status history.

## Reporting model

Report separately:

- reach and engagement;
- website sessions;
- availability searches;
- no-availability searches;
- booking starts;
- confirmed bookings;
- gross booking value;
- payment success;
- direct/OTA/channel mix;
- cancellations and no-shows;
- refunds;
- occupied room/stay nights;
- average daily rate only when definition is consistent;
- occupancy only with valid available-unit denominator;
- revenue per available room/unit only with correct scope;
- commission and distribution cost;
- retained net booking value where verified;
- group enquiries, quotes and confirmed groups;
- review volume/rating with limitations;
- repeat guests where identity resolution is lawful.

Definitions:

- Occupancy requires occupied units divided by available units for the same property, unit scope and period.
- Average daily rate requires room/accommodation revenue divided by occupied/sold room nights under a defined method.
- Revenue per available room requires a consistent room-revenue and available-room denominator.
- Gross booking value is not net revenue, cash received or profit.
- National tourism arrivals are not property bookings.

## Claim-risk register

High-risk claims include:

- “officially five-star” without current TGCSA evidence;
- “best hotel/lodge/guest house” without substantiation;
- “lowest price guaranteed” without enforceable comparison terms;
- “last room” or “selling fast” without live inventory evidence;
- “all inclusive” with hidden exclusions;
- “free cancellation” without date/time and rate-plan conditions;
- “fully refundable” without payment-method and deadline terms;
- “safe area” or “crime free”;
- “guaranteed wildlife sighting”;
- “registered guide” without current official verification;
- “wheelchair accessible” without exact facility evidence;
- “eco-friendly/sustainable” without defined practices and evidence;
- “minutes from” without route/time basis;
- “award-winning” without exact award, category and year;
- “government approved” where no such approval exists.

## Candidate review-gated Skill Cards

### TSC-01 — Booking stages are not interchangeable

An enquiry, provisional hold, confirmed booking, paid booking, occupied stay and retained revenue are different states.

### TSC-02 — Market tourism statistics are context

Stats SA tourism and accommodation data must retain date, category, methodology and comparison basis and cannot prove one property's performance.

### TSC-03 — Star grading requires live evidence

TGCSA category, star level and validity must be verified before publication.

### TSC-04 — Guide registration is scoped

Tourist-guide registration, province/site, category, specialisation and validity must match the advertised service.

### TSC-05 — Occupancy needs the correct denominator

Occupied rooms or units cannot be presented as occupancy without the matching available inventory and period.

### TSC-06 — Gross booking value is not profit

Taxes, fees, discounts, commission, cancellations, refunds and operating costs affect retained value.

### TSC-07 — Availability and scarcity must be live

“Last room”, sold-out and date availability claims require current inventory evidence.

### TSC-08 — Traveller data is purpose-bound

Identity, passport, itinerary, companion and accessibility information collected for a stay is not general marketing permission.

### TSC-09 — Reviews require privacy-safe responses

Public replies should acknowledge and resolve without disclosing booking or guest information.

### TSC-10 — Seasonal experiences are not guarantees

Weather, wildlife, flowers, snow, water levels and natural events require dated, conditional language.
