# AI Workforce — B2B Manufacturing, Engineering and Industrial Supply Industry Library

Last updated: 2026-07-26
Status: Actual researched industry and marketing-activation pack; all production knowledge remains review-gated.
Territory: South Africa, with clearly labelled international B2B platform research where local evidence is unavailable.

## Purpose

Create a practical marketing layer for:

- manufacturers;
- engineering firms;
- industrial suppliers and distributors;
- machinery and equipment providers;
- component, consumable and spare-parts suppliers;
- fabrication and processing businesses;
- technical service and maintenance providers;
- project, tender and procurement-led businesses.

This pack is designed to help CG create better ads, videos, landing pages, campaigns, lead funnels and reporting. Compliance and technical truth are guardrails, not the sole purpose.

## Marketing outcome hierarchy

The AI must separate:

1. awareness among relevant buying-group members;
2. technical interest;
3. specification or product-fit evaluation;
4. sample, site-visit or demonstration request;
5. RFQ or quotation request;
6. approved-vendor or tender participation;
7. proposal or bid submission;
8. order or appointment;
9. delivery, commissioning or implementation;
10. repeat order, maintenance or after-sales relationship.

A brochure download, enquiry, RFQ and awarded order are not equivalent outcomes.

## Source register

### Source M1 — Statistics South Africa P3041.2 Manufacturing: Production and Sales

Publisher: Statistics South Africa
Canonical archive: https://www.statssa.gov.za/?PPN=P3041.2&page_id=1866
Source type: official monthly manufacturing statistics
Rights treatment: official_reference
Freshness: monthly

Verified findings:

- The latest available release at the time of research is May 2026.
- P3041.2 reports manufacturing production and sales using defined categories and comparison bases.
- Monthly, year-on-year and seasonally adjusted measures are different.

Marketing use:

- use current sector context when genuinely relevant;
- preserve exact month, category and comparison basis;
- do not infer one supplier's demand, pipeline or profitability from national manufacturing movement.

Prohibited overclaims:

- "Manufacturing is booming, so this campaign will generate sales."
- "National output growth proves this supplier is growing."
- "Industry sales equal client revenue."

### Source M2 — Engineering Council of South Africa

Publisher: Engineering Council of South Africa
Canonical sources:

- https://www.ecsa.co.za/
- https://www.ecsa.co.za/ecsa-registration/

Source type: statutory professional regulator
Rights treatment: official_reference; professional-title risk flag

Verified findings:

- ECSA regulates the engineering profession under the Engineering Profession Act 46 of 2000.
- ECSA is the authorised body for titles such as Pr Eng, Pr Tech Eng, Pr Techni Eng and Pr Cert Eng.
- Professional, candidate and specified categories are distinct.

Marketing implications:

- never call a person a Professional Engineer unless the exact current registration is verified;
- candidate and professional titles must not be collapsed;
- company capability does not prove every staff member holds a regulated title;
- technical authority must be tied to the actual person, scope and evidence.

### Source M3 — Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/occupational-health-and-safety-act
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference; safety-risk flag

Verified scope:

- the Act addresses health and safety at work and hazards arising from plant and machinery;
- safety claims around equipment, installation, operation and maintenance require evidence and context.

Marketing implications:

- do not claim equipment is "100% safe";
- do not show unsafe PPE, guarding, lifting, welding or machine practices for visual effect;
- do not imply that one certificate proves safe installation, operator behaviour and maintenance;
- technical demonstrations should include real operating conditions and limitations.

### Source M4 — National Treasury and public procurement guidance

Publishers: National Treasury / Office of the Chief Procurement Officer
Canonical sources:

- https://ocpo.treasury.gov.za/legislation/guidelines/default.aspx
- https://www.treasury.gov.za/legislation/pfma/supplychain/default.aspx

Source type: official public-procurement guidance
Rights treatment: official_reference

Verified findings:

- public procurement uses formal supply-chain and bid processes;
- functionality, pricing, eligibility and preference criteria may all matter;
- a marketing lead does not replace vendor registration, tender compliance or bid evaluation.

Marketing implications:

- tender-oriented content should help buyers verify capability, scope, evidence and documentation;
- "government approved" and "preferred supplier" claims require exact evidence and current scope;
- being on a database does not prove contract award.

### Source M5 — LinkedIn 2025 B2B Marketing Benchmark

Publisher: LinkedIn, with Ipsos research
Canonical page: https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/marketing-collective/2025-b2b-marketing-benchmar-the-video-influence-effect-starts-with-trust
Source type: official platform research with disclosed methodology
Rights treatment: official_reference; international evidence

Verified findings:

- Ipsos surveyed 1,500 senior B2B marketers across six countries.
- 94% agreed trust is important to B2B success.
- 78% reported using video, and more than half planned to increase investment.
- The manufacturing subsample was 73 respondents, so manufacturing-specific conclusions must remain cautious.

Marketing implications:

- video should make technical competence visible rather than merely look polished;
- human experts, process footage, demonstrations and customer proof can reduce perceived risk;
- do not generalise the international sample as South African buyer behaviour without qualification.

### Source M6 — LinkedIn buying-committee research

Publisher: LinkedIn
Canonical page: https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/content-marketing/which-departments-influence-buying-decisions-in-your-industry
Source type: official platform research and synthesis

Verified finding:

- LinkedIn reported an average of 4.6 departments influencing manufacturing buying decisions in the cited research.

Marketing implication:

- content should give operations, engineering, procurement, finance, safety and leadership different reasons to support the purchase.

### Source M7 — LinkedIn/Bain Buyability research

Publisher: LinkedIn, referencing joint work with Bain
Canonical page: https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/research-and-insights/the-principles-of-buyability-why-strong-deals-stall-and-what-separates-the-vendors-who-get-chosen
Source type: current B2B research framework
Rights treatment: official_reference; international evidence

Verified concept:

- B2B buyers must often defend a decision internally, not merely prefer a product.
- Collective confidence across a buying group matters.

Marketing implication:

- ads and landing pages should help an internal champion justify the decision with proof, risk controls, implementation detail and commercial logic.

### Source M8 — Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008

Publisher: South African Government
Canonical page: https://www.gov.za/documents/consumer-protection-act
Source type: legislation
Rights treatment: official_reference

Marketing implications:

- objective product, warranty, performance and comparative claims require support;
- advertised pricing, inclusions, exclusions and availability must be clear;
- product imagery must not materially misrepresent configuration or scope;
- warranties and guarantees must state who provides them and under what conditions.

## Marketing activation layer

### High-value ad-angle families

#### 1. Downtime and continuity

Useful angle:

- expose the operational cost of waiting for parts, support or repair;
- show how availability, diagnosis, stock or service reduces delay.

Proof required:

- real support model;
- actual stock or lead-time evidence;
- service-area evidence;
- measured case study where claiming outcomes.

Example hook patterns:

- "The cheapest component is expensive when production stops."
- "What happens after the machine is installed matters more than the handover photo."
- "Three questions to ask before choosing an industrial supplier."

Do not claim:

- zero downtime;
- guaranteed immediate response;
- universal savings.

#### 2. Technical fit and application risk

Useful angle:

- help buyers avoid buying the wrong specification;
- demonstrate selection criteria rather than merely listing features.

Proof assets:

- engineer explanation;
- sizing or selection guide;
- compatibility table;
- application footage;
- sample specification sheet.

Hook patterns:

- "Same size does not mean same application."
- "Before you quote this part, check these four operating conditions."
- "Why this specification fails in one environment and works in another."

#### 3. Total cost, not purchase price

Useful angle:

- explain maintenance, energy, consumables, lifespan, service and downtime.

Proof assets:

- lifecycle-cost calculator;
- maintenance schedule;
- assumptions table;
- comparison using like-for-like conditions.

Hook patterns:

- "The invoice price is only one line in the real cost."
- "A cheaper unit can cost more by month six."

Do not present modelled savings as guaranteed savings.

#### 4. Capability and proof

Useful angle:

- make invisible competence visible.

Proof assets:

- workshop and machinery footage;
- process control;
- testing;
- inspection;
- staff expertise;
- completed application;
- traceable case study;
- certification with exact scope.

Hook patterns:

- "This is what happens before your order leaves our floor."
- "A look inside the quality check buyers normally never see."
- "From drawing to delivery: how this component is produced."

#### 5. Procurement confidence

Useful angle:

- reduce the internal effort required to approve a supplier.

Proof assets:

- vendor pack;
- compliance checklist;
- warranty summary;
- standard lead-time table;
- references;
- implementation plan.

Hook patterns:

- "Everything procurement needs before adding a new supplier."
- "The documents that delay industrial orders—and how to prepare them early."

#### 6. After-sales and service

Useful angle:

- focus on support after purchase.

Proof assets:

- service team;
- parts process;
- maintenance programme;
- support escalation;
- customer training;
- geographic coverage.

Hook patterns:

- "The sale ends. The support should not."
- "What our service team checks before a breakdown becomes a shutdown."

#### 7. Education and authority

Useful angle:

- answer technical questions buyers search before contacting sales.

Formats:

- myths versus reality;
- specification explainers;
- failure analysis;
- maintenance tips;
- standards explained carefully;
- application selection;
- engineer interviews.

This content supports search, LinkedIn, email nurture and sales enablement.

## Video and visual strategy

High-value formats:

- machine/process walkthrough;
- technical expert to camera;
- application demonstration;
- installation sequence;
- quality-control close-ups;
- before/after process explanation;
- maintenance or fault diagnosis;
- customer site case study with permission;
- animated system diagram;
- procurement or selection checklist;
- staff competence and workshop capability.

Visual priorities:

- show scale;
- show tolerances and detail;
- show operators safely;
- show the product in its real environment;
- show proof labels, test instruments or drawings only where disclosure is safe;
- avoid generic stock footage when specific capability is the selling point.

Do not expose:

- client-confidential drawings;
- plant vulnerabilities;
- serial numbers where sensitive;
- security controls;
- proprietary process detail;
- unsafe working practice;
- unapproved customer sites.

Useful offers:

- site assessment;
- demonstration;
- quote or RFQ support;
- specification checklist;
- maintenance audit;
- lifecycle-cost worksheet;
- procurement document pack;
- spare-parts readiness review;
- training session;
- webinar or technical breakfast.

Weak generic offer:

- "Contact us for all your engineering needs."

Stronger offer:

- exact audience;
- exact problem;
- clear deliverable;
- low ambiguity;
- realistic next step.

## Landing-page framework

Recommended structure:

1. exact application and buyer problem;
2. clear solution category;
3. operating conditions or fit;
4. key proof;
5. product/service scope;
6. technical documents;
7. implementation and support;
8. relevant case study;
9. commercial next step;
10. clear RFQ, consultation or demonstration CTA;
11. limitations, lead time and geographic scope where material.

For high-consideration purchases, the page should help the user brief colleagues internally.

## Ad and content measurement

Early indicators:

- target-account reach;
- relevant job-function reach;
- video completion among target audience;
- technical document engagement;
- search-term relevance;
- landing-page engagement;
- repeat visits;
- named-account activity.

Mid-funnel indicators:

- qualified technical enquiries;
- RFQs;
- demonstrations;
- site visits;
- samples;
- procurement-pack requests;
- quote progression;
- buying-group contacts per opportunity.

Commercial indicators:

- opportunity value;
- quote-to-order rate;
- sales-cycle length;
- won revenue;
- gross margin where authorised;
- repeat orders;
- service or maintenance revenue;
- loss reason;
- influenced pipeline with documented attribution method.

Do not optimise only for cheap leads. A low-cost enquiry with no application fit is not better than a higher-cost RFQ from a genuine buyer.

## Candidate Skill Cards

All remain `needs_review`.

### Card: Market technical fit before features

Candidate rule:

- industrial buyers need application relevance, operating conditions and implementation evidence before generic feature lists.

Limitation:

- exact buying criteria vary by product and account.

### Card: Build ads for buying groups

Candidate rule:

- create different proof for technical, operational, procurement, financial and safety stakeholders rather than targeting one imagined decision-maker.

Evidence:

- LinkedIn buying-committee research and B2B benchmark.

Limitation:

- buying-group composition must be verified per client and deal type.

### Card: Make competence visible

Candidate rule:

- use real process, expert, testing, service and application footage to reduce buyer uncertainty.

Limitation:

- never expose confidential or unsafe information.

### Card: Measure industrial marketing through pipeline stages

Candidate rule:

- report qualified enquiries, RFQs, quote progression, orders and repeat business separately.

Limitation:

- attribution requires CRM and sales feedback.
