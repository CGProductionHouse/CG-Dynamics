# AI Workforce — Brand Strategy and Creative Effectiveness Source Pack

Last updated: 2026-07-26
Status: Research complete for review-gated knowledge use.
Purpose: Give CG Dynamics a practical evidence base for building memorable brands, stronger advertising creative, clearer briefs, more useful testing and better long-term campaign decisions.

## 1. Why this pack exists

CG does not only need content that looks polished. It needs work that:

- is noticed;
- is correctly attributed to the client;
- communicates one useful idea;
- fits a real buying situation;
- is memorable after exposure;
- supports both immediate response and longer-term demand;
- can be repeated without becoming visually inconsistent;
- produces measurable movement toward business outcomes.

This pack turns brand and creative-effectiveness research into practical planning rules for:

- ads;
- reels and short-form video;
- campaign concepts;
- landing pages;
- design systems;
- scripts;
- content calendars;
- creative testing;
- client reporting;
- AI-generated recommendations.

It does not treat awards, likes, novelty or visual polish as proof of commercial effectiveness.

## 2. Core operating model

```text
Business objective
→ buying situation
→ audience and problem
→ single-minded proposition
→ proof
→ distinctive brand signals
→ creative idea
→ platform execution
→ exposure and memory
→ action
→ verified business outcome
```

The AI must not skip directly from a generic business goal to a caption or design.

## 3. Evidence hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. Peer-reviewed marketing-science research.
2. Large effectiveness databases with disclosed methods.
3. Official platform experiments with disclosed samples and controls.
4. Reputable industry bodies and transparent research programmes.
5. Verified client-specific campaign evidence.
6. Practitioner frameworks, clearly labelled as interpretation rather than universal fact.

Books under copyright may be indexed by title, author, edition and high-level thesis. Their full text must not be ingested without rights evidence.

## 4. Source register and actual findings

### 4.1 Ehrenberg-Bass Institute — distinctive brand assets

Source family:

- Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, evidence-based marketing resources.
- Jenni Romaniuk, public article: `Brands of Distinction`.
- Institute research-services pages for distinctive assets.

Canonical public source:

- https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/brands-of-distinction

Actual finding:

Brand identity elements can include colours, fonts, logos, characters, celebrities, shapes, sounds and jingles. They become useful distinctive assets only when buyers have learned to associate them with the brand and when those assets are sufficiently unique relative to competitors.

The Institute distinguishes asset strength by fame and uniqueness. A brand element is not automatically a strong asset merely because the company uses it consistently.

Safe operational claim:

- Distinctive assets can improve brand recognition and attribution when they are both known and uniquely linked to the brand.

Unsafe overclaim:

- Any repeated colour, font or logo will automatically improve sales.

Practical CG use:

- Record each client's candidate assets.
- Assess whether an asset is recognisable and unique before using it as a substitute for the brand name.
- Keep weak assets close to the brand name while they are being built.
- Do not remove the logo early just because a visual style looks distinctive internally.

Required asset record:

```text
asset_type
asset_description
approved_source
fame_evidence
uniqueness_evidence
competitor_conflicts
usage_rules
last_reviewed_at
review_state
```

Limitations:

Much of the Institute's deeper research is sponsor-only or contained in copyrighted books. Public summaries support the high-level rule but do not permit copying proprietary methods or book text into the library.

### 4.2 IPA Effectiveness Databank — long and short effects

Sources:

- IPA, `The Long and the Short of It` summary presentation.
- IPA Effectiveness Databank overview.
- IPA, `Marketing in the Era of Accountability`.

Canonical public sources:

- https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/documents/the-long-and-the-short-of-it-presentation
- https://ipa.co.uk/awards-events/effectiveness-awardsv1/ease
- https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/publications-reports/marketing-in-the-era-of-accountability

Actual finding:

The IPA work distinguishes short-term activation effects from longer-term brand effects. The public summary emphasises broad prospect reach, balancing emotional and rational communication, integrating brand and activation, and measuring both short- and long-term outcomes.

Safe operational claim:

- Immediate-response activity and longer-term brand building should be measured separately because they do not produce the same effects on the same timescale.

Unsafe overclaim:

- Every client should use one universal brand-versus-activation budget split.

Practical CG use:

Every campaign brief must state whether it is primarily:

- activation;
- brand building;
- mixed;
- retention;
- reactivation;
- event attendance;
- product launch;
- reputation/trust.

A campaign may support more than one objective, but it must have one primary measurement frame.

Limitations:

The IPA Databank contains award entries and case-study data, not a random sample of every campaign. Findings are highly useful for direction but should not be presented as a guaranteed formula for every South African SME.

### 4.3 IPA — short-termism and collapse in creative effectiveness

Sources:

- `The Crisis in Creative Effectiveness`.
- `Selling Creativity Short`.

Canonical sources:

- https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/publications-reports/the-crisis-in-creative-effectiveness/
- https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/publications-reports/selling-creativity-short-creativity-and-effectiveness-under-threat

Actual finding:

Peter Field's analyses of the IPA Databank found that short-term evaluation and insufficient investment behind creative work weakened the effectiveness advantage associated with creatively awarded campaigns.

Safe operational claim:

- A strong idea can still fail commercially when it is underfunded, run too briefly, fragmented across too many small executions or judged only on immediate response.

Unsafe overclaim:

- Creatively awarded work is always effective.

Practical CG use:

Do not abandon a coherent campaign concept after one or two posts because the first post did not outperform every previous post. Review:

- exposure;
- platform delivery;
- audience fit;
- brand attribution;
- message comprehension;
- frequency;
- landing-page continuity;
- lead quality;
- time horizon.

### 4.4 WARC/LIONS effectiveness research — creative commitment

Sources:

- WARC, `The Effectiveness Code`.
- LinkedIn/WARC/LIONS, `The B2B Effectiveness Code` summary.

Canonical sources:

- https://www.warc.com/en/content/article/warc-exclusive/the-effectiveness-code/en-gb/133006
- https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/trends-tips/why-commitment-matters-the-b2b-effectiveness-code-a-framework-for-b2b-creative-effectiveness

Actual finding:

The Effectiveness Code analysed 4,863 effectiveness-award entrants and winners from 2011 to 2019. Its public summary links greater creative commitment with larger media investment, longer duration and more channel support.

The B2B-specific analysis reviewed 435 case studies from WARC and LIONS databases and argues that B2B effectiveness suffers when campaigns are too short, narrowly targeted and overly rational.

Safe operational claim:

- Strong creative normally needs sufficient time, budget and distribution to have a fair chance of building memory and producing business effects.

Unsafe overclaim:

- More spend, longer duration and more channels always cause growth.

Practical CG use:

Before judging creative, record:

```text
campaign_duration
planned_spend
actual_spend
channels
frequency
creative_versions
primary_objective
brand_asset_consistency
landing_page_match
business_outcome_window
```

### 4.5 WARC — creativity is not automatically effectiveness

Sources:

- WARC, `The Health of Creativity`, third edition.
- WARC marketing-effectiveness overview.

Canonical sources:

- https://page.warc.com/the-health-of-creativity-third-edition-2024.html
- https://prod1.warc.com/

Actual finding:

WARC compares creatively awarded and effectiveness-awarded work. Its public material reports that only a minority of creatively awarded ideas also receive effectiveness recognition, while highly awarded creative work is more likely to overlap with effectiveness success.

Safe operational claim:

- Creative recognition and business effectiveness overlap, but one does not prove the other.

Unsafe overclaim:

- An award, viral view count or impressive design proves business impact.

Practical CG use:

Creative review requires two scorecards:

1. Creative quality.
2. Business effectiveness.

They may influence each other, but they must not be merged into one score.

### 4.6 LinkedIn and MAGNA — controlled B2B ad testing

Source:

- LinkedIn and MAGNA Media Trials, 2024 B2B creative research summary.

Canonical source:

- https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/research-and-insights/new-research-the-creative-tactics-that-drive-better-b2b-ad-performance-on-linkedin

Method disclosed publicly:

- More than 1,700 LinkedIn users.
- Participants recruited from a nationally representative online panel.
- Controlled testing of 67 Sponsored Content ads.
- Single-image and video formats.
- Business decision-maker outcomes measured.

Actual finding:

The study found that both display and video ads could improve brand outcomes. Ads perceived as creative were associated with stronger consideration and engagement than less creative ads in that tested environment.

Safe operational claim:

- In the tested LinkedIn environment, creative quality and emotional interest were associated with stronger B2B brand and consideration responses.

Unsafe overclaim:

- A creative B2B ad is 40% more likely to create a purchase in every market or platform.

Practical CG use:

B2B creative should not only list product features. It should seek a human tension, consequence, character, story or distinctive point of view while retaining technical credibility.

Limitations:

- LinkedIn environment.
- Platform-funded research.
- Tested ads and audience may not represent South African SMEs.
- Consideration is not a verified sale.

### 4.7 Thinkbox — attention is not one simple metric

Source:

- Thinkbox, `Giving Attention a Little Attention`, independent review by Dr Ali Goode.

Canonical source:

- https://www.thinkbox.tv/research/reports/giving-attention-a-little-attention-download-the-white-paper

Actual finding:

The review compares commercial attention measurement with academic cognitive-science approaches and warns that attention is complex. Different attention measures do not necessarily represent the same cognitive process or predict the same outcome.

Safe operational claim:

- Viewability, watch time, visual attention, memory and persuasion are related but distinct.

Unsafe overclaim:

- A longer watch time proves the viewer understood, remembered or was persuaded by the ad.

Practical CG use:

Separate:

```text
served
viewable
started
watched_3_seconds
watched_50_percent
completed
clicked
recalled
correctly_attributed
acted
converted
```

### 4.8 IPA — trust-building advertising

Sources:

- IPA, `The Commercial Value of Trust`.
- IPA, `Trust-building advertising works`.

Canonical sources:

- https://ipa.co.uk/initiatives/effworks/effworks-ft-reports/trust/
- https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/documents/trust-building-advertising-works

Actual finding:

The 2026 IPA material draws on more than 25 years of Effectiveness Databank evidence and reports that campaigns producing very large trust effects are more likely to show substantial commercial gains.

Safe operational claim:

- Trust can be a commercially important brand outcome and should be measured when it is strategically relevant.

Unsafe overclaim:

- Any testimonial, professional tone or claim of trustworthiness creates trust.

Practical CG use:

Trust-building proof can include:

- transparent process;
- verified credentials;
- real staff expertise;
- clear pricing or conditions;
- service recovery;
- warranty clarity;
- customer evidence with permission;
- consistent delivery;
- visible accountability.

## 6. Brand strategy framework for CG clients

### 6.1 Brand truth

Before positioning work, collect:

- exact products and services;
- geography;
- capacity;
- margins or commercial priorities where authorised;
- customer types;
- proof;
- constraints;
- competitors;
- reasons customers choose the business;
- reasons they hesitate;
- brand history;
- current visual and verbal assets.

Do not invent a brand truth because it sounds premium.

### 6.2 Category entry points and buying situations

The AI should identify real situations in which a buyer may enter the category.

Examples:

- urgent breakdown;
- planned upgrade;
- seasonal event;
- new business launch;
- compliance requirement;
- gift purchase;
- family celebration;
- monthly convenience;
- risk avoidance;
- status or identity;
- cost reduction;
- replacement purchase.

The output must distinguish researched buying situations from assumptions.

### 6.3 Positioning record

```text
client_id
category
priority_audience
buying_situation
customer_problem
alternative_or_competitor
single_minded_proposition
reason_to_believe
proof_assets
brand_tone
mandatory_assets
forbidden_claims
reviewed_by
reviewed_at
```

### 6.4 Single-minded proposition

A proposition should express one useful reason to care.

Weak:

- Quality service you can trust.
- Your one-stop solution.
- Excellence every day.

Stronger structure:

```text
For [audience in buying situation],
[brand] helps achieve [specific outcome]
through [credible mechanism or advantage],
proved by [evidence].
```

This is a planning tool, not necessarily final ad copy.


### 7.2 Asset rules

- Consistency does not mean every post must look identical.
- Variation should occur inside a recognisable brand system.
- A trending template must not erase client attribution.
- The brand should be identifiable before the final logo frame where practical.
- Music and characters can be assets only when rights and usage consistency are secured.
- Do not claim uniqueness without competitor evidence.

## 8. Creative brief standard

Every meaningful campaign should have:

```text

priority_audience
buying_situation
human_tension
single_minded_proposition
reason_to_believe
proof
mandatory_brand_assets
call_to_action
platform_and_format
measurement_window
primary_metric
secondary_metrics
forbidden_claims
```

### 8.1 Human tension

A human tension is not generic demographics. It is a conflict or concern that makes the message matter.

Examples:

- I want the cheaper option, but I am afraid it will fail.
- I need to book now, but I do not trust the venue yet.
- I know I need help, but I do not want to feel incompetent.
- I want to join, but I do not know anyone.
- I want premium quality, but I need to justify the price.

Tensions must be grounded in client evidence, sales conversations, customer research or clearly labelled hypotheses.

## 9. Creative idea ladder

Use this review ladder:

1. **Information** — states a fact.
2. **Benefit** — explains a customer outcome.
3. **Demonstration** — proves the benefit.
4. **Story** — dramatises the tension and outcome.
5. **Platform** — repeatable campaign idea supporting many executions.
6. **Distinctive platform** — repeatable idea strongly linked to the client through owned assets.

Higher is not automatically better for every task. An urgent stock ad may only need a clear benefit and proof. A long-term campaign should normally seek a repeatable platform.

## 10. Creative quality scorecard

Score each item from 1 to 5, with evidence notes.

### 10.1 Strategic clarity

- Is the objective clear?
- Is there one main message?
- Does the audience understand why it matters?

### 10.2 Brand attribution

- Is the client identifiable?
- Are approved brand assets used correctly?
- Could the same work run unchanged for a competitor?

### 10.3 Attention value

- Is there a reason to notice?
- Does the opening create curiosity, consequence or relevance?
- Does visual or verbal clutter compete with the message?

### 10.4 Emotional or human value

- Is there a person, tension, story, humour, surprise, pride, relief or aspiration?
- Does the emotion fit the brand and buying situation?

### 10.5 Proof

- Is the promise substantiated?
- Is the demonstration genuine?
- Are conditions visible?

### 10.6 Platform fit

- Does the execution fit the feed, duration, sound environment and user expectation?
- Is it adapted rather than merely resized?

### 10.7 Action clarity

- Is the next step appropriate?
- Does the landing page continue the same promise?

### 10.8 Repeatability

- Can the idea produce multiple non-repetitive executions?
- Will repeated use build memory rather than random visual noise?

## 12. Video creative system

### 12.1 Opening

The first seconds should establish at least one of:

- consequence;
- question;
- visual change;
- tension;
- recognisable person;
- product in use;
- surprising detail;
- direct category relevance.

A loud transition is not automatically a strong hook.

### 12.2 Middle

Deliver one coherent idea through:

- demonstration;
- explanation;
- sequence;
- contrast;
- story;
- reaction;
- proof.

### 12.3 Brand linkage

Use brand cues throughout where practical:

- location;
- product;
- uniform;
- colour;
- staff member;
- recurring format;
- sonic cue with rights;
- on-screen name.

### 12.4 Ending

End with the correct next action:

- remember;
- save;
- enquire;
- book;
- buy;
- register;
- request a quote;
- visit;
- return for the next episode.

Not every brand-building video needs a hard sales CTA.

## 13. Static-ad and poster system

A strong static execution normally needs:

- one dominant message;
- readable hierarchy;
- visible brand attribution;
- a real product, person or proof asset where useful;
- limited supporting detail;
- conditions for price, availability or promotion;
- platform-safe dimensions and text size.

Do not confuse decorative complexity with effectiveness.

## 14. Landing-page continuity

The landing page must preserve:

- the same promise;
- the same offer;
- the same product or service;
- the same price and conditions;
- the same audience context;
- recognisable brand assets;
- the correct CTA.

Track:

```text
ad_impression
ad_click
landing_view
engaged_visit
primary_action_start
primary_action_complete
qualified_outcome
verified_business_result
```

## 15. Creative testing protocol

### 15.1 Define the question

Examples:

- Does a demonstration outperform a static product claim for qualified enquiries?
- Does staff-led video improve watch time and correct brand attribution?
- Does price-first creative increase low-quality leads?

### 15.2 Test one meaningful variable

Possible variables:

- proposition;
- proof type;
- opening;
- character;
- format;
- CTA;
- offer;
- brand cue intensity.

Do not claim to have tested one variable when audience, placement, budget and landing page also changed materially.

### 15.3 Preselect metrics

Primary metric must match the question.

Examples:

- attention test: qualified watch or recall measure;
- response test: completed lead or purchase;
- brand test: correct attribution, awareness or consideration;
- lead-quality test: qualified lead rate;
- ecommerce test: contribution after refunds, not only purchase events.

### 15.4 Result states

```text
planned
running
insufficient_data
undecided
positive
negative
mixed
invalidated
applied
not_applied
superseded
```

### 15.5 Never declare a winner only from CTR

Higher CTR may reflect:

- curiosity;
- misleading wording;
- price sensitivity;
- low-intent audiences;
- accidental taps;
- poor qualification.

Reconcile with downstream outcomes.

## 16. Brand-building versus activation reporting

### Brand-building indicators

- reach within relevant audience;
- frequency;
- video completion with limitations;
- correct brand attribution;
- distinctive-asset recognition;
- direct traffic trend;
- branded search trend;
- awareness or consideration research;
- repeat exposure;
- trust indicators.

### Activation indicators

- qualified clicks;
- landing-page action;
- submitted lead;
- qualified lead;
- booking;
- purchase;
- quote;
- order;
- cost per verified outcome.

Do not report activation metrics as proof of long-term brand growth or brand metrics as proof of immediate revenue.

## 18. Review-gated candidate Skill Cards

Purpose:
Evaluate whether proposed brand elements are likely to improve attribution.

Required evidence:

- approved brand source;
- usage history;
- fame evidence;
- uniqueness evidence;
- competitor conflicts.

Limitation:
Do not call an element distinctive merely because the design team likes it.

### Card 2 — Single-Minded Proposition Builder

Purpose:
Turn client truth and buyer tension into one useful planning proposition.

Limitation:
Must not invent product advantages.

### Card 3 — Creative Brief Builder

Purpose:
Build a complete campaign brief before generating executions.

Limitation:
No client-specific output without exact client retrieval.

### Card 4 — Brand Attribution QA

Purpose:
Check whether the work is recognisably the client's.

Limitation:
Visual consistency is not proof of customer recognition.

### Card 5 — Creative Effectiveness Review

Purpose:
Score strategy, attribution, attention, emotion, proof, platform fit, action and repeatability.

Limitation:
The score is a review aid, not a prediction of sales.

### Card 6 — Creative Testing Planner

Purpose:
Define one useful test, one primary metric and a valid interpretation rule.

Limitation:
No causal claim from uncontrolled historical comparison.

### Card 7 — Brand and Activation Balance

Purpose:
Clarify whether work is designed for immediate response, future demand or both.

Limitation:
No universal budget split.

### Card 8 — Campaign Platform Builder

Purpose:
Develop a repeatable creative idea rather than isolated posts.

Limitation:
Must preserve client truth and rights.

## 20. Non-negotiable boundaries

- No creative award treated as commercial proof.
- No virality treated as brand growth.
- No logo use treated as correct attribution without evidence.
- No universal brand-versus-activation budget formula.
- No CTR-only winner.
- No aesthetic preference presented as research.
- No copyrighted book ingestion without rights.
- No client positioning invented from name or industry alone.
- No unreviewed candidate knowledge in normal production answers.
- No campaign claim stronger than the evidence.
