# Creator and Influencer Partnership Source Pack

Last researched: 2026-07-26
Status: Review-gated research source pack
Division: AI Workforce, Client Intelligence, Operations Hub

## Purpose

Give CG Dynamics a safe, practical system for planning, briefing, producing, approving, publishing, boosting and measuring creator or influencer work.

This is a marketing execution pack, not a legal opinion and not a creator-directory database.

## Core operating principle

A creator partnership is not merely a person with followers posting a product.

The useful operating model is:

Audience fit
→ credible creator role
→ clear commercial relationship
→ truthful first-hand content
→ correct platform disclosure
→ rights and usage terms
→ measurable audience response
→ verified downstream outcome

## Source register

### TikTok commercial content disclosure

Source: TikTok for Business, “How to turn on the Commercial Content Disclosure setting in TikTok”
Current source state: updated January 2026

Finding:

TikTok requires the commercial content disclosure setting when a post promotes a brand, product or service. The creator must choose whether the post promotes their own brand or branded content for another party. TikTok warns that missing disclosure can result in restriction or removal.

Safe candidate claim:

TikTok branded or commercial content should use TikTok’s native disclosure setting and still comply with applicable law and policy.

Do not overclaim:

- Do not say a native platform label automatically satisfies every jurisdiction.
- Do not say disclosure guarantees distribution.
- Do not say a post is non-commercial merely because no cash changed hands.

Source URL:
https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-to-turn-on-the-commercial-content-disclosure-setting-in-tiktok

### TikTok brand partner tag and paid amplification

Source: TikTok for Business, “About the Brand Partner tag in content disclosure settings”
Current source state: updated April 2026

Finding:

Tagging a brand partner makes the content visible in TikTok Content Suite and can pre-authorise Spark Ads usage for a default period. TikTok also provides post analytics to the tagged partner.

Safe candidate claim:

Creator posting permission and paid-media usage permission should be recorded separately, including authorisation duration.

Do not overclaim:

- Do not assume a creator post can be boosted forever.
- Do not treat tagging as transfer of copyright ownership.
- Do not assume analytics access proves sales or incrementality.

Source URL:
https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-the-brand-partner-tag-in-content-disclosure-settings

### TikTok commercial-content quality

Source: TikTok for Business, “About TikTok's content quality standard for creator commercial content”
Current source state: updated May 2026

Finding:

TikTok evaluates commercial creator content on user experience, authenticity and useful first-hand product or service representation. Commercial content should feel credible and provide actual value rather than appearing mechanically inserted.

Safe candidate claim:

Creator content should be based on a genuine role, actual experience or demonstrable use wherever the format implies personal experience.

Do not overclaim:

- Do not fabricate first-hand use.
- Do not script a testimonial the creator cannot honestly support.
- Do not assume “natural-looking” content is automatically truthful.

Source URL:
https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-tiktoks-content-quality-standard-for-creator-commercial-content

### Meta branded content

Sources:

- Meta/Facebook Help, “What is branded content on Facebook…”
- Instagram Help, “What is considered branded content”

Finding:

Meta treats funded or influenced promotional creator content as branded content and requires disclosure. Exchange of value can include gifted products or services, not only cash payment. Meta’s paid partnership label helps users understand the commercial relationship, but the poster remains responsible for legal compliance.

Safe candidate claim:

Paid, gifted or otherwise value-exchanged creator content on Facebook or Instagram should use the platform’s paid partnership tools where applicable and include any additional disclosure required by the context.

Do not overclaim:

- Do not assume a gifted stay or meal is “organic” merely because there was no fee.
- Do not hide the disclosure after “more”.
- Do not rely only on a profile bio.

Source URLs:
https://www.facebook.com/help/233665870791913/
https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/616901995832907/

### FTC endorsement disclosure principles

Sources:

- FTC, “Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers”
- FTC Endorsements, Influencers and Reviews guidance

Jurisdiction note:

These are United States sources. They are valuable comparative guidance and platform-facing best practice but must not be presented as South African law.

Finding:

The FTC treats money, gifts, discounts, family, employment and other material relationships as relationships that may need disclosure. Disclosures should be clear, hard to miss and placed with the endorsement. In video, disclosure is stronger when present in the content itself rather than only in the description.

Safe candidate claim:

CG should treat any meaningful value or relationship that could affect how an audience interprets an endorsement as a disclosure trigger unless current local review confirms otherwise.

Do not overclaim:

- Do not label FTC guidance as POPIA, CPA or ARB law.
- Do not rely only on ambiguous tags such as #collab.
- Do not assume followers already know the relationship.

Source URLs:
https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers
https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews

### South African advertising and social-media review

Primary source family:

- Advertising Regulatory Board Code and appendices
- Consumer Protection Act
- Sector-specific advertising rules where applicable

Operating rule:

Before activation, a reviewer must verify the current ARB social-media, testimonial and sector-specific rules. The ARB code is dynamic and must retain a review date.

No AI output may state that one disclosure format is universally sufficient for all South African campaigns without a current source check.

## Creator role types

The system should distinguish:

- Customer advocate
- Expert or professional
- Staff creator
- Founder or owner
- Entertainer
- Athlete or team personality
- Community figure
- Reviewer
- Demonstrator
- Host or presenter
- Event correspondent
- Affiliate
- UGC producer whose content posts only on the brand account

These roles create different trust, rights and evidence requirements.

## Creator selection model

Do not rank creators by follower count alone.

Store:

- Creator identity and platform
- Audience geography
- Audience language
- Audience category fit
- Average reach range
- Median recent views where available
- Engagement quality
- Comment relevance
- Brand-safety history
- Previous category partnerships
- Apparent audience-age fit
- Content craft strengths
- On-camera style
- Reliability and turnaround
- Rights willingness
- Paid amplification willingness
- Exclusivity conflicts
- Disclosure compliance history
- Pricing model
- Evidence date

## Audience-fit questions

- Does the creator reach the people who can realistically buy, attend, enquire or influence the decision?
- Is the audience local enough for the client’s service area?
- Does the creator naturally speak about the category?
- Would the audience understand why this person is involved?
- Is the creator trusted for the role the campaign asks them to play?
- Does the creator’s tone fit the brand without erasing the creator’s own voice?

## Partnership objective types

- Awareness
- Distinctive brand recognition
- Product education
- Venue discovery
- Event attendance
- Community participation
- Lead generation
- Sales or tracked purchase
- Review or demonstration
- Content asset production
- Retargeting asset creation
- Reputation or credibility

One partnership may support more than one objective, but one primary objective must control the brief and reporting.

## Brief standard

Every creator brief should include:

- Client and campaign
- Primary objective
- Target audience
- Buying or participation situation
- Required truth or proposition
- Required proof
- Creator’s role
- Content format
- Platform
- Required disclosure
- Required brand tag
- Mandatory facts
- Forbidden claims
- Restricted products or actions
- Music and footage rights
- Filming consent
- Draft and approval process
- Posting date/window
- Organic usage period
- Paid usage period
- Territory
- Exclusivity
- Raw-footage requirement, if contractually agreed
- Reporting requirement
- Compensation and payment trigger

## Creative principle

Use a constraint-light brief.

The brand should control:

- Truth
- Objective
- Required proof
- Risk boundaries
- Rights
- Deliverables

The creator should retain meaningful control over:

- Natural wording
- Performance style
- Familiar format
- Audience-native pacing
- Personal delivery

Over-scripted creator content can weaken credibility. Under-briefed content can create factual, rights and compliance failures.

## Honest endorsement rules

The AI must not draft or approve:

- A personal-experience claim where the creator did not use the service or product
- A guaranteed result
- A hidden commercial relationship
- A review that the client edited into a materially different opinion
- A claim beyond the creator’s competence
- A health, financial, legal or technical claim unsupported by the relevant industry pack
- A “customer” testimonial from an employee without disclosure
- Fake spontaneous reactions

## Rights matrix

Track each right separately:

- Creator may publish organically
- Brand may repost organically
- Brand may edit
- Brand may create cutdowns
- Brand may use still frames
- Brand may use in paid social
- Brand may use in Spark Ads or partnership ads
- Brand may use on website
- Brand may use in email
- Brand may use in-store or venue
- Brand may use on other platforms
- Territory
- Start date
- End date
- Exclusivity
- Music restrictions
- Third-party appearance restrictions

A creator invoice, post tag or WhatsApp approval is not automatically a full rights licence.

## Music and third-party material

Creator content must pass the existing Music Copyright Platform Rights System.

Before paid usage, confirm:

- Track source
- Commercial-use permission
- Paid-ad permission
- Platform and territory
- Duration
- Embedded broadcast or background music
- Third-party clips, memes, match footage or screenshots

A creator’s ability to post a sound organically does not prove the brand may boost or reuse it commercially.

## Staff creator rules

Staff-led content should state or make clear the person’s relationship where that relationship could affect audience interpretation.

Store:

- Employment role
- Consent
- Whether participation is voluntary
- Compensation outside normal duties, if any
- Image and voice usage period
- Exit process
- Whether old content may remain after employment ends

Do not pressure employees into sensitive, humiliating, alcohol-heavy or personally risky content.

## Creator campaign workflow

1. Define objective and audience.
2. Identify creator role needed.
3. Shortlist using audience and content fit.
4. Screen brand-safety and conflicts.
5. Confirm commercial and rights terms.
6. Create evidence-led brief.
7. Confirm disclosure and music plan.
8. Produce draft or supervised shoot.
9. Review truth, rights, brand fit and platform compliance.
10. Publish with disclosure.
11. Capture platform and downstream results.
12. Reconcile payment, rights expiry and asset reuse.
13. Record learning without universalising one result.

## Measurement model

Separate:

Creator post published
→ valid reach
→ engaged viewing
→ meaningful response
→ profile or destination action
→ tracked enquiry or purchase
→ qualified lead or retained order

Possible metrics:

- Reach
- Engaged views
- Average watch time
- Completion rate
- Saves
- Shares
- Relevant comments
- Profile visits
- Link clicks
- Code use
- Landing-page visits
- Submitted leads
- Qualified leads
- Bookings
- Purchases
- Retained revenue where verified
- Cost per qualified outcome

Do not treat:

- Follower count as guaranteed reach
- Reach as unique cross-platform reach
- Code use as fully incremental sales
- Last-click sales as proof the creator caused all demand
- Comments as purchase intent without qualification

## Creator code and attribution safeguards

Promo codes and custom links can help identify response, but they have limits:

- Codes may leak beyond the creator’s audience.
- People may see creator content and convert through another route.
- Existing customers may use the code.
- Platform attribution windows may over- or under-count.
- Organic and paid amplification can overlap.

Use creator attribution as evidence, not automatic incrementality.

- First-use or first-visit experience
- Guided demonstration
- “What I noticed” review

- Expert reacts or explains
- Challenge with truthful conditions
- Event correspondent

- Before/after where lawful and substantiated
- Audience Q&A
- Local guide or itinerary
- Comparison using fair, supported criteria

## Non-negotiable safeguards

- No hidden value exchange.
- No fake testimonial.
- No follower-count-only selection.
- No paid reuse without explicit rights.
- No music assumed licensed.
- No gifting treated as automatically non-commercial.
- No creator content activated without current platform and sector review.
- No creator result generalised into a universal formula.
