# AI Workforce — Community, Sport and Entertainment Industry Library

Last researched: 2026-07-26
Status: Research complete; candidate knowledge remains review-gated.
Primary market: South Africa.

## Purpose

This library helps CG plan stronger marketing for:

- community sport clubs and leagues;
- fan-engagement competitions;
- amateur and professional sporting events;
- bars and venues running sport-linked entertainment;
- live music and entertainment events;
- community festivals and activations;
- memberships, registrations and ticketed attendance;
- sponsors and supplier partners;
- recurring venue entertainment programmes.

It is designed to produce practical:

- campaign strategies;
- ad concepts and hooks;
- social content;
- event launch plans;
- ticket and registration funnels;
- sponsorship offers;
- fan-retention programmes;
- landing pages;
- post-event reporting.

Safety, alcohol, gambling, copyright, privacy and event-authorisation rules remain guardrails. They are not the sole purpose of the library.

## 1. Authoritative South African source map

### 1.1 Safety at Sports and Recreational Events Act 2 of 2010

Official source family:

- Department of Sport, Arts and Culture Act page;
- SAPS event-risk documents and section 6(3) application materials;
- applicable municipal Joint Operations Committee processes.

Research finding:

The Act creates a risk-based event-safety framework. Event organisers may need formal risk categorisation, safety plans, venue-capacity controls and coordination with responsible authorities.

Marketing implication:

- Do not market an event as approved before the relevant approval exists.
- Do not imply that ticket availability proves legal capacity.
- Do not publish a final venue capacity from an early planning estimate.
- Campaign launch dates should account for approval lead time.
- Safety arrangements can become trust content only after they are confirmed.

Limitations:

Exact municipal and provincial processes vary. A national Act does not replace local by-law, noise, liquor, health, traffic or venue requirements.

### 1.2 National Sport and Recreation Act 110 of 1998

Official source family:

- Department of Sport, Arts and Culture legislation pages;
- Parliament's legislation compendium.

Research finding:

The Act addresses the national sport and recreation framework and relationships among government, federations and recognised sport bodies.

Marketing implication:

- A club, league or event must not imply national federation recognition without evidence.
- “Official”, “national”, “provincial” and “championship” claims require exact authority.
- Sponsor decks must distinguish private competition status from federation-sanctioned status.

### 1.3 SAPS event-risk categorisation

Official source:

- SAPS event-risk document portal.

Research finding:

SAPS publishes event-risk materials, including section 6(3) application resources and operational contact information.

Marketing implication:

Event promotion should preserve these states:

```text
concept
→ venue hold
→ authority applications submitted
→ categorisation received
→ safety plan approved
→ public launch
→ tickets or registrations open
```

The system must not jump from concept directly to “confirmed event” when approvals remain outstanding.

### 1.4 Municipal event compliance

Example official source:

- City of Tshwane Events Compliance and JOC guidance.

Research finding:

Municipalities may require event organisers to present safety plans and applications to a Joint Operations Committee before the event. The exact lead time and supporting documents vary.

Marketing implication:

- Build approval milestones into campaign calendars.
- Avoid paid-media spend before a material approval risk is resolved.
- Maintain an authority-status field rather than a generic “confirmed” flag.

### 1.5 Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008

Research finding:

Ticket, registration, prize, promotion and service claims must be clear, fair and not misleading.

Marketing implication:

Campaign material should clearly disclose, where relevant:

- event date and venue;
- ticket inclusions and exclusions;
- age restrictions;
- refund and cancellation terms;
- competition entry rules;
- prize conditions;
- sponsor fulfilment conditions;
- capacity or availability basis;
- material changes.

Unsafe claims include:

- “non-refundable under all circumstances” without legal review;
- “guaranteed prize” where sponsor fulfilment is unconfirmed;
- “sold out” as false urgency;
- “free” where unavoidable fees apply;
- “official championship” without authority.

### 1.6 Advertising Regulatory Board Code

Official source:

- ARB current online Code and appendices.

Research finding:

The ARB Code is dynamic and includes general advertising rules plus relevant appendices for alcohol, gambling and social media.

Marketing implication:

- Re-check current online rules before publishing regulated event campaigns.
- Influencer and sponsor content must be identifiable as commercial communication where required.
- Gambling, alcohol and prize promotions require category-specific review.
- Do not rely on an old downloaded appendix as permanently current.

### 1.7 Liquor and responsible-alcohol source family

Relevant sources:

- Liquor Act and applicable provincial licensing sources;
- ARB alcohol appendix;
- responsible-use sources already captured in the Hospitality library.

Research finding:

An event may involve alcohol without making alcohol consumption the primary participation mechanic.

Marketing implication:

Safer creative focuses on:

- atmosphere;
- food;
- people;
- team identity;
- entertainment;
- venue experience;
- responsible adult enjoyment.

Higher-risk creative includes:

- speed-drinking;
- chugging challenges;
- rewards tied directly to consumption volume;
- intoxication as the punchline;
- young-looking participants with alcohol;
- alcohol trade or giveaway calls to action without review.

### 1.8 Gambling and prediction mechanics

Relevant sources:

- National Gambling Act and provincial gambling authority sources;
- ARB gambling appendix;
- promotion and competition law review where prizes are involved.

Research finding:

A free fan prediction game, promotional competition, betting product and paid-entry game are not the same legal category.

Marketing implication:

Before promoting a prediction or prize mechanic, store:

```text
entry fee
purchase requirement
chance versus skill
prize source
prize value
age restriction
winner method
terms location
territory
licensing review state
```

Never label a mechanic “just for fun” as a substitute for legal classification.

### 1.9 POPIA and participant privacy

Research finding:

Registrations, team rosters, captain contact details, attendance records, photographs, video, challenge results and leaderboard data may contain personal information.

Marketing implication:

- Separate operational registration from marketing consent.
- Use clear image/video notices and consent processes.
- Do not publish phone numbers or private team details.
- Do not reuse registration lists for unrelated sponsor marketing without a lawful basis.
- Child and youth data requires stricter handling.

### 1.10 Copyright and music

Canonical cross-platform source:

- `MUSIC-COPYRIGHT-PLATFORM-RIGHTS-SYSTEM.md`

Marketing implication:

Every event edit, hype video, recap and sponsor reel must resolve:

- platform;
- account type;
- organic versus paid use;
- track source;
- exact licence;
- duration;
- territory;
- proof certificate;
- embedded broadcast footage;
- team, league and sponsor marks.

A song being available in an app does not create universal commercial rights.

### 1.11 Broadcast and sports-footage rights

Research finding:

Broadcast footage, commentary audio, match graphics and league marks may be protected even when filmed from a television inside a venue.

Marketing implication:

Safer content uses:

- crowd reactions;
- original venue footage;
- staff and fan commentary;
- score references created from verified facts;
- original graphics that do not copy protected broadcasts.

Do not assume that a short broadcast clip is automatically fair use or commercially licensed.

## 2. Business and marketing model

### 2.1 Common commercial outcomes

- Ticket revenue.
- Registrations.
- Membership growth.
- Table or venue bookings.
- Food and beverage sales.
- Sponsor value.
- Merchandise sales.
- Repeat attendance.
- Community participation.
- Database growth with lawful consent.

### 2.2 Funnel truth

```text
impression
→ engaged viewer
→ event-page visit
→ registration or ticket start
→ completed registration or paid ticket
→ checked-in attendee
→ active participant
→ repeat attendee or member
```

Do not rename:

- event-page views as attendance;
- RSVPs as paid tickets;
- registrations as check-ins;
- checked-in attendees as repeat customers;
- sponsor logo impressions as sponsor sales;
- bar sales as campaign-attributed revenue without reconciliation.

### 2.3 Audience segments

#### Existing loyal fans

Needs:

- belonging;
- tradition;
- status;
- recognition;
- consistent updates;
- easy return participation.

#### Casual social participants

Needs:

- low-friction entry;
- social proof;
- clarity on what happens;
- reassurance that beginners are welcome;
- strong atmosphere.

#### Competitive participants

Needs:

- transparent rules;
- credible scoring;
- fair fixtures;
- visible progression;
- worthwhile prizes;
- recognition.

#### Families and supporters

Needs:

- timing;
- safety;
- access;
- food and seating information;
- age suitability;
- parking and transport clarity.

#### Sponsors

Needs:

- audience fit;
- visible deliverables;
- brand safety;
- measurable exposure;
- activation opportunities;
- proof after the event.

#### Venue customers

Needs:

- easy booking;
- clear special or package;
- atmosphere proof;
- certainty on date, time and location;
- compelling reason to arrive early and stay.

Proof assets:

Useful for:

- bars;
- fan parks;
- community screenings;
- festivals.

Proof assets:

- crowd reactions;
- food;
- service;
- seating;
- entertainment;
- authentic recap footage.

#### Recognition

Angle:

“Your team deserves its moment.”

Useful for:

- awards;
- table-of-the-week content;
- member spotlights;
- sponsor activations.

Proof assets:

- names;
- photos;
- results;
- prize handovers;
- verified achievements.

#### Scarcity and occasion

Angle:

“One date. One room. One final.”

Use only when:

- date is confirmed;
- venue is confirmed;
- real capacity exists;
- availability is current.

Avoid fabricated urgency.

### 3.2 Hook library

Candidate frameworks:

- “You have watched together. Now compete together.”
- “The scoreboard is only half the story.”
- “Your table name could be on top next week.”
- “This is not another quiet match night.”
- “Four friends. One team. A full season of bragging rights.”
- “Think you know the final score? Prove it.”
- “The best seat in the venue is the one your team owns.”
- “From first whistle to final leaderboard.”
- “Your sponsor logo should do more than sit on a poster.”

These remain frameworks until exact event facts are retrieved.

### 3.4 Video formats

#### Fast launch video

Structure:

1. Strong event moment.
2. What it is.
3. Who can join.
4. Date or season.
5. Exact action.

#### Rules explainer

Use:

- one rule per visual beat;
- real interface or physical demonstration;
- plain language;
- no dense legal terms in the main cut;
- full terms linked separately.

#### Reaction-led hype

Use original reactions rather than unlicensed broadcast footage.

#### Leaderboard update

Structure:

- biggest mover;
- current top teams;
- one surprising stat;
- next fixture;
- CTA to attend or participate.

#### Sponsor proof recap

Show:

- activation delivered;
- audience interaction;
- visible brand placement;
- prize handover;
- verified reach or attendance with scope labels.

### 3.5 Offer structures

- Free team registration.
- Early-registration benefit.
- Group or table package.
- Season membership.
- Family package.
- Sponsor-supported prize.
- Bring-a-friend mechanic.
- Captains’ briefing or launch night.
- Limited merchandise bundle.
- Priority booking for returning teams.

Every offer must specify eligibility, inclusions, dates, capacity and exclusions.

### 3.6 Landing-page structure

1. Event or league promise.
2. Date, venue and status.
3. Who it is for.
4. How it works.
5. Exact registration or ticket requirements.
6. Fixtures or programme.
7. Prizes and sponsor conditions.
8. Venue experience.
9. Safety, age and accessibility information.
10. Terms, refunds and privacy.
11. Clear CTA.
12. Contact or support route.

### 3.7 Retargeting stages

#### Viewed content, no page visit

Use:

- simple concept explanation;
- strongest atmosphere proof;
- clear date and CTA.

#### Visited page, no completion

Use:

- friction removal;
- rules summary;
- participant proof;
- deadline only when real.

#### Registered or purchased

Use:

- preparation details;
- venue logistics;
- team identity;
- add-on opportunities;
- sharing tools.

#### Attended

Use:

- recap;
- result;
- recognition;
- next event;
- membership or season conversion.

## 5. Sponsor marketing system

### Sponsor package components

- naming rights;
- category exclusivity;
- jersey or team placement;
- fixture or challenge sponsorship;
- on-site activation;
- prize sponsorship;
- content integration;
- database communication where lawfully permitted;
- post-event proof report.

### Sponsor funnel

```text
prospect
→ sponsor conversation
→ proposal
→ negotiated scope
→ signed agreement
→ assets received
→ activation delivered
→ evidence captured
→ report issued
→ renewal discussion
```

A logo appearing once is not proof that the full sponsor package was delivered.

### Sponsor reporting

Report separately:

- contracted deliverables;
- delivered deliverables;
- attendance scope;
- content reach;
- engagement;
- activation participation;
- leads only where valid consent and source exist;
- sales only where sponsor data confirms them.

## 7. Failure patterns

- Advertising before critical approvals.
- Unclear rules.
- Overly complicated participation.
- No reason to return after one event.
- Sponsor logos without integrated value.
- Only publishing polished posters and no human moments.
- Relying on broadcast clips for hype.
- Making alcohol consumption the event mechanic.
- Calling an RSVP an attendee.
- Using false scarcity.
- Failing to record prize fulfilment.
- No post-event conversion path.
